## What landed, and why it needs a pass

[`bill-text-s8`](/MOO/issues/MOO-42#document-bill-text-s8) is at **store revision 61**. Thirteen substitutions in §1, all inside new § 51.1-142.4 — subdivisions **G 4**, **D 1** and subsection **M 2**. Five carry the Policy Director's answers of 12 October 2026; eight dispose [MOO-161](/MOO/issues/MOO-161)'s counsel pass, which returned 1 fatal, 9 serious, 6 moderate and 4 minor findings on store revision 58.

**Why this pass exists.** This subdivision has now had three cures for the same defect, and **each of the two previous unreviewed cures drew a fatal finding from the next pass** — MOO-126 F1 against store revision 45's deletion of the deeming, MOO-161 F1 against store revision 58's freestanding disapplication. **The cure below is the third attempt and it has had no adversarial reading at all.**

## The thing to attack first

**E1 answers MOO-161's fatal finding by SUBTRACTION.** Store revision 58 read: *"... where clause (i) of this subdivision applies, § 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death and the Board shall determine the benefit payable under this subdivision under § 51.1-162 ..."*. MOO-161 F1 held that, because clause (i) is simply *"dies"* and its only carve-out is a member who retired in a retirement that is **not** a qualifying retirement, the sentence reached **the death of a retiree years into retirement** and switched off § 51.1-163 (*"Death after retirement"*), forfeiting her unrecovered contributions where no spouse, minor child or parent survived her.

The cure does two things and adds nothing: **(i)** it deletes *"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death and"*, on the ground — MOO-161's own citation check and its null result N1 — that **§ 51.1-163 opens *"If a member dies after the effective date of retirement"* and cannot reach a pre-effective-date death of its own force**, reaching one only by the deeming in § 51.1-165 C 1, § 51.1-165 C 2 and § 51.1-165.01 F, of which the sentence's own notwithstanding already displaces the first and third and deliberately preserves the second; and **(ii)** it confines the sentence to the window, in the words subdivision D 1's certification already uses.

**Eight things to attack, in order:**

1. **Is the deletion safe?** With *"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply"* gone, is there **any** route by which § 51.1-163 still governs a post-notification, pre-effective-date death of an electing member, notwithstanding the two displacement limbs? N1 says there are exactly three deeming provisions. **Find a fourth, or a route through §§ 51.1-207 / 51.1-218, or a route through § 51.1-165.01 C 2 (the 24× partial lump sum), and the cure fails.**
2. **Is the window airtight, and does it now exclude someone it should cover?** The condition is *"and the member dies after the Board has received the notification of the member's effective date of retirement required by subdivision D 1 and before that date."* Consider: a member who never files the notification; a member who files and then **separates from service** before dying; a member who **cancels** under subdivision G 5 and dies; a member who files a notification stating a date that has already passed (subdivision D 1 expressly allows a late-filed notice). **For each, say what the act now gives her survivor and whether that is worse than store revision 58, or than present law.**
3. **Does the C 2 greater-of now work?** MOO-161 F2 said it could not be performed because its input had been switched off. Is it performable now? MOO-161 F3 said it still has **no measure and no payee rule** — that is on the Director's card and is deliberately NOT cured here. **Say whether leaving F3 open is survivable in the text as it now stands, or whether the greater-of is so indeterminate that the sentence should not stand without it.**
4. **E10 + E11, the certification's remedy.** An incomplete certification is now *"a failure of the Board for the purposes of subsection M"*, with a 30-day period that runs from the subdivision G 4 death notice, and M 2's failure limb is widened to reach *"a failure of the Board to state in a certification or notice a matter this section requires to be stated in it"*, with its filing window split three ways. **Attack: does the new ground swallow M 2's second sentence (which already deems a certification a determination)? Is the three-way window coherent? Does the deferred period work where the member is alive and the G 4 notice will never be given?**
5. **E12 + E13, the narrowed class.** *"Other person to whom this section requires a notice to be given"* is replaced in both of M 2's class lists by *"person who claimed any benefit payable on the death of a member and whom the Board determined is not entitled to it, or employer that paid on a member's behalf."* **Attack: who loses a right they had at store revision 60 and should not have lost?** Note that subsection L confines the Program to state employment, and that § 51.1-124.3 defines *"beneficiary"* as *"any person entitled to receive benefits under this chapter."*
6. **E7, E8, E9 — the compelled propositions of law.** Three of D 1's six propositions were wrong and are restated. **Re-read § 51.1-162 A, B and C and § 51.1-165 C 2 against the new words and tell me whether any of the six is still inaccurate, incomplete or misleading to a member who will rely on it.** This is a writing the Board must give before it may accept her money.
7. **E6, the release and the reinstatement (the Director's answers).** The release now fires where **no allowance is payable at all**, restores to the $3,000,000 limitation **only so far as nothing has been paid under subsection N**, and a released amount is **reinstated** where the determination is reversed on review. **Attack the arithmetic and the timing: can an amount be released twice, reinstated without having been released, or restored to a limitation it was never charged against? Does the reinstatement collide with *"no certified subsidy is settled more than once"*?**
8. **E2, E3, E4, E5 — the notice machinery.** A fiduciary addressee for a minor; the waiver (§ 51.1-162 C, 90 days) split from the election (§ 51.1-162 B, no period); the rider extended to the **amount**; *"that date"* resolved as *"the earlier of those dates"*; a 30-day state-agency duty to report the death; the 90-day redetermination run from when the Board learns of the death. **Attack the clocks against each other and against § 51.1-162 C's 90 days from death.**

## Standing traps in this bill

- **§ 51.1-165 and § 51.1-165.01 each have a subdivision C 2.** Never read or write *"subdivision C 2"* without the section number.
- **§§ 51.1-162, 51.1-163, 51.1-165 and 51.1-165.01 are not amended by this act.** The displacements live only in § 51.1-142.4, so a reader of § 51.1-165 alone will not find them. That is a known, recorded risk — not a new finding.
- **A citation count is not a cross-reference check.** MOO-154's §5.33 counted § 51.1-165.01's citations and never read its subsection F, which is how one fatal finding survived two passes.
