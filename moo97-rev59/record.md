## 3. The fatal finding, in full, and why the cure is a subtraction

**What store revision 58 did.** Its predecessor read: *"the Board's receipt of a written notification of the member's effective date of retirement given under subdivision D 1 shall not cause § 51.1-163 to apply to the member's death."* That formulation was **self-limiting** — it spoke to the consequence of one pre-effective-date event. Store revision 58 replaced it with a freestanding ***"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death"***, governed only by *"where clause (i) of this subdivision applies."*

**Why that was fatal.** Clause (i) of subdivision G 4 is *"if the member, after the Board accepts the election, (i) dies."* Its only carve-out is *"Clause (i) of this subdivision does not apply where the member has retired in a retirement that is **not a qualifying retirement** before the date of death."* By negative implication a member who **has** retired in a qualifying retirement is **not** carved out — so the sentence reached the death of a retiree years into retirement and switched off § 51.1-163, *"Death after retirement"*, which returns to her family the excess of her own contributions over the allowances she lived to draw. On the reviewer's facts: twenty-nine years of service, three years purchased under this act, retirement on a **basic** allowance (so § 51.1-163's *"unless ... optional benefits"* proviso does not bite), death fourteen months later, no spouse, no minor child, no surviving parent, a brother as designated beneficiary. **§ 51.1-162 B does not pay him — no qualifying relation. § 51.1-162 A does not pay him — she did not die *"before retirement."* § 51.1-162 C does not pay him — her death was not compensable. He receives nothing and $122,000 of her money stays in the trust fund.** An act whose entire case is that forfeiting purchased military service is wrong would have forfeited more than the defect it was curing.

**Why the cure is to take words out.** The reviewer's own citation check is the authority: **§ 51.1-163 opens *"If a member dies after the effective date of retirement"* and cannot reach a pre-effective-date death of its own force at all.** It reaches one only by deeming, and there are exactly three deeming provisions — § 51.1-165 C 1, § 51.1-165 C 2 and § 51.1-165.01 F. **E1's notwithstanding already displaces the first and the third, and the second (C 2) is deliberately preserved.** So the disapplication was never load-bearing: it did no work in the window it was written for, and did damage outside it. **It is deleted**, and the operative sentence is **confined to the window** in the words D 1's own certification already uses.

**What the deletion also cures.** **F2.** C 2 is a conditional pair *both of whose limbs are defined by reference to § 51.1-163* — the first as the measure, the second as the comparator. With § 51.1-163 declared inapplicable, C 2's first limb commanded payment under a section the same sentence had switched off, and its second limb could not be triggered because the comparison had no input. *"Nothing in the preceding sentence reduces a benefit payable under subdivision C 2"* forbade a reduction; it did not supply the missing figure. **Restoring § 51.1-163 as a measure makes the greater-of performable again.** What it does **not** supply is a basis of comparison or a payee — that is **F3**, and F3 is on the Director's card.

**Why this is not a policy choice made by drafting.** A fatal finding may not be resolved by drafting choice, and this one is not resolved by one. Every answer the Director has given on this branch speaks to the death of a member who has filed her § 51.1-153 notice and has not yet reached her effective date: answer **(d)** of 12 October (the measure), answer **(f)** of 12 October (the § 51.1-165 C 1 displacement), and the D 1 certification, which has said since store revision 54 that it is *"where a member ... dies after the Board has received the notification of the member's effective date of retirement required by this subdivision and before that date."* **The reach beyond that window was an artefact of how store revision 58 was cut, not an agreed policy**; restoring the window restores the agreed scope. **The wider alternative is nonetheless drafted at §7 item 128 and is not enacted**, so that the Director can take it if the intention was wider than the record shows.

**What it costs.** Nothing that the act had been priced on. The reviewer's fiscal challenge (its §4) is structural rather than numerical: the act's claim to discipline is that every cost flows through the *certified subsidy*, which is capped at $3,000,000 in aggregate and $170,000 per election, switched off by subsection K and funded by subsection N. **A death benefit is none of those things.** F1's class was *"every electing member who retires on a basic allowance and dies in retirement"*, and the reviewer's own order-of-magnitude bound on it was **$3.6M to $90M depending on cohort size** — against a priced program of $758,048. **F1's cure removes that tranche entirely.** What remains is the C 2 greater-of, which the reviewer priced at **about one case every three years** at $50k–$150k per case: inside model noise, and it is the only benefit change the act now makes.

## 6. Ambiguities resolved — fourteen, and none of them chose a policy

1. **The disapplication is deleted rather than carved.** F2 offered both. Deletion is enacted because N1 and the citation check show the words were never load-bearing, and a carve-out would have preserved the words that caused F1.
2. **The window is stated in D 1's words.** The fix direction read *"a written notification ... given under subdivision D 1 and before the effective date of retirement stated in it."* D 1 already says *"the notification of the member's effective date of retirement required by this subdivision and before that date."* **The act may not use two formulations for one concept**; the enacted words are D 1's, with *"this subdivision"* → *"subdivision D 1."*
3. **Confinement is treated as restoring the agreed scope, not as choosing policy** — §3 above states the reasoning in full, and the wider alternative is on the card rather than in the text.
4. **The release trigger's second branch needed a date and the card stated none.** The Director's words fire the release *"because no such allowance is payable to any person"* — a case with no election to date from. The enacted period runs from **the Board's determination that no such allowance is payable**, on the model of every other period in the subdivision. A duty with no deadline is unenforceable.
5. **F9(a)(i)'s limitation attaches to *restore*, not to *release*.** The comma before *"only to the extent"* is dropped so the clause reads restrictively on the restoration. **The release still happens in full** — it must, or the charge against the limitation never lifts; what is limited is how much returns to the $3,000,000.
6. **F6's certification states C 2's payee by cross-reference and names nobody.** The fix direction ended *"to the person entitled to it under the provision under which the greater benefit is determined"*, which is a payee rule — and the payee rule is F3, which is the Director's. The enacted proposition says only that each of C 2's two benefits is *"payable to the person entitled to it under the provision under which it is determined"*: a statement of existing law, not a direction to pay anyone.
7. **F17's *"that person"* is given an antecedent.** The fix direction opened *"of any right of that person under subsection C of § 51.1-162"*, and in this sentence there is no such antecedent once the old waiver clause is replaced. The enacted words are *"of any right under subsection C of § 51.1-162 of a person entitled to any benefit payable on the member's death"*, and the election limb then says *"of any right of such a person."*
8. **F13's *"the preceding clause"* is rejected for *"the earlier of those dates."*** This subdivision uses *"clause"* for its enumerated clauses (i), (ii), (iii) and (a), (b), (c); a *"preceding clause"* reference would have been a second meaning for one word.
9. **F14 is enacted as to the rider only.** The rider now reaches *"the amount of any benefit payable to that person"*, so the Board is not compelled to state a figure it does not have. The companion — a further notice with a fresh review period where the Board later determines the amount differently — is a **new duty on the Board**, was not agreed, and is on the card.
10. **F9's clock is stated once.** The 30-day length is in D 1 (*"no later than 30 days after the date on which the notice required by subdivision G 4 is given to that person"*) and M 2 admits the ground and defers the window (*"in the case of a failure to state a matter, within the period provided by the provision that requires the matter to be stated"*). **One clock, one place, no conflict** — and M 2's existing two filing windows had to be disambiguated (*"in the case of such a failure"* → named by kind) because there are now three failure kinds.
11. **F8's narrow class keeps the paying employer expressly** — §4(b) above. It also keeps the rejected claimant, who was the whole object of the amendment, and relies on § 51.1-124.3's definition of *"beneficiary"* (*"any person entitled to receive benefits under this chapter"*) for the person actually entitled, exactly as the reviewer's fix direction does.
12. **F12's fallback addressee is a notice addressee and nothing more.** *"And, where there is none, to the person to whom the Board pays or would pay the benefit"* identifies someone to notify where a minor has no fiduciary; **it names no payee and moves no money.**
13. **F15's base is stated as creditable service *including* the purchased credit**, which preserves null result N3: where the member cancelled under G 5 and the purchased credit is a null set, *"including"* nothing leaves § 51.1-162 to compute on her ordinary creditable service. *"Computed **on** the service credit credited under this subdivision"* would have computed on nothing; *"taking account of"* was operable but stated no base.
14. **The state agency's death-notice duty is confined to state agencies and drafted on G 4's own involuntary-separation model.** G 4 already pairs a state agency's duty to notify with a Board duty to report every failure *"of which the Board becomes aware"* in the subsection J report; the new sentence uses the same pairing and the same words. **A duty on a local employer would need an express grant under Dillon's Rule and a judgment that is not the drafter's** — and subsection L excludes local employment from the Program in any event. The 30 days is the Director's figure, not mine.

**Two findings declined, with reasons, so that neither is re-run as new.**

- **F16 — declined on the reviewer's own analysis.** Deeming a certification omission a reviewable matter puts it inside M 2's tolling sentence, so a member could buy time by pointing at a trivial omission. M 2 caps *"the aggregate of all tolling under this subsection"* at **120 days** and denies tolling to any second request on the same determination or failure. **The cap holds, the exposure is 120 days, and the cost of closing it exceeds it.** Note that F9's move to the *failure* limb does not remove the lever, because the failure limb is inside the tolling sentence too — the cap is doing the work either way.
- **F21 — declined because the cure widens what F8 narrows.** The new class is confined to notices required by *"this section"* while M 2's tolling sentence reaches periods under *"this section or ... subdivision B 4 f of § 51.1-142.2."* Extending the class to § 51.1-142.2 notices would add a class for symmetry's sake in the same revision that removes one for having no boundary. **Flagged for consistency only, by the reviewer's own rating, and left alone.**

## 7. The seven drafted answers — word for word, and none of them enacted

**Each is one substitution against the live text of store revision 61.** Three of them move money between named people and I have not picked one. All seven are §7 item 128 in [`bill-text-s8-item-list`](/MOO/issues/MOO-42#document-bill-text-s8-item-list).

### (1) F3 — the greater-of has no measure and no payee rule. The sharpest thing in the pass after F1

Subdivision G 4's second sentence directs the Board to pay *"the benefit it provides or the benefit determined under the preceding sentence, whichever is greater."* The two sides are different kinds of money payable to different people. On the reviewer's facts — contingent annuitant **mother**, surviving **husband**, designated beneficiary **sister** — there are three candidate payees, one direction to pay the greater, and no basis of comparison. Three answers, any combination.

**(a) State the measure.** FIND *"whichever is greater."* in that sentence; READ:

> whichever is greater<u>, the two being compared on the basis of actuarial equivalence as of the date of the member's death determined on the assumptions then in use by the Board</u>.

**(b) State the payee.** Same sentence; READ:

> whichever is greater<u>, and the Board shall pay it to the person to whom the provision under which the greater benefit is determined requires it to be paid</u>.

**(c) Confine the greater-of to the case where the payees coincide.** FIND the whole sentence *"Nothing in the preceding sentence reduces a benefit payable under subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165, and where that subdivision applies the Board shall pay the benefit it provides or the benefit determined under the preceding sentence, whichever is greater."*; READ:

> Nothing in the preceding sentence reduces a benefit payable under subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165 <u>in a case in which that subdivision requires benefits to be paid in accordance with subsection B of § 51.1-162</u>, and <u>in such a case</u> the Board shall pay the benefit it provides or the benefit determined under the preceding sentence, whichever is greater.

**(a) and (b) are cumulative; (c) is an alternative to both.** (c) is the smallest and it takes a right from the designated beneficiary, because C 2's first limb is then displaced outright. **(b) names a payee, which is why it is the Director's and not the drafter's.**

### (2) F7 — the compelled notice of a right that may already have expired. The reviewer's highest-value repair after F1

§ 51.1-162 C's waiver must be made *"within ninety days after the death of the member."* The G 4 notice is due, at the outer limit, 90 days after the Board **learns** of the death (60 to credit, 30 to notify). **Every day the Board takes to learn of the death is a day by which the Commonwealth's own notice of the right can post-date the expiry of it** — and there is no remedy, because the Board gave the notice inside the period the act allows. Add after the J-hook sentence in subdivision G 4:

> <u>Where the Board gives the notice required by this subdivision later than 30 days after the death of the member, the period stated in subsection C of § 51.1-162 within which a waiver must be made does not expire before the thirtieth day after the notice is given to the person entitled to make it.</u>

**It overrides a period stated in another section of the Code**, which is why it is the Director's. The state agency's new 30-day death-notice duty, enacted in this revision, shortens the exposure and does not close it.

### (3) F10 — whether a State Police, VaLORS or Judicial Retirement System member is in this Program at all

§ 51.1-142.2's purchase right runs to *"any member in service."* Subsection L excludes teachers and employees of political subdivisions and **does not exclude the sister systems**. § 51.1-200 applies Chapter 1 to the State Police Officers' Retirement System *"except as otherwise provided."* § 51.1-163 directs payment *"in the same manner as provided in subsection A of § 51.1-162, subsection A of § 51.1-207, or subsection A of § 51.1-218, in accordance with the retirement plan covering such member"* — and the act's death branch names **only § 51.1-162**, which Chapter 2 displaces for a SPORS member.

**(a) Generalise.** In subdivision G 4, FIND *"under § 51.1-162 as on the death of a member in service"*; READ:

> under § 51.1-162<u>, § 51.1-207, or § 51.1-218, as applicable to the retirement plan covering the member,</u> as on the death of a member in service

— with the same change to the in-service disapplication that follows it, and with D 1's six propositions reframed as *"the provisions of this title governing the benefit payable on the death of a member in service under the retirement plan covering the member."* **That is a substantial rewrite of the certification and it should not be cut until the research answer is in.**

**(b) Confine the Program.** Add to subsection L:

> <u>No member of the State Police Officers' Retirement System, of the Virginia Law Officers' Retirement System, or of the Judicial Retirement System is eligible to make an election under this section.</u>

**(b) is cheaper, it is one sentence, and it is a scope decision rather than a conforming one.** **Routed to the Statutory Researcher** for the complete list of sister-system death-benefit sections and whether Chapters 2, 2.1 and 3 displace § 51.1-162 by their own force or only through § 51.1-200's *"except as otherwise provided."* **Neither form should be enacted before that answer.**

### (4) F11 — say expressly that a death benefit is outside the cap, or leave it to inference

Every cost control in the act is addressed to the *certified subsidy*: subdivision D 2's $3,000,000 aggregate and $170,000 per-election limits, subsection K's kill-switch, subsection N's appropriation of *"the amount settled."* **A benefit determined under subdivision G 4 is none of those things**; it is paid by the retirement system and falls on the trust fund and, through it, on the employer contribution rate. Add to subsection N:

> <u>Nothing in this section charges a benefit payable under § 51.1-162 against the limitations stated in subdivision D 2.</u>

**It changes nothing and discloses everything**, which is both the argument for it and the argument against it. F1's cure removes the large exposure; what remains is the C 2 greater-of, which the reviewer priced at about one case every three years. **Whether or not this is enacted, the fiscal note must not present the $3,000,000 cap as the act's maximum exposure.**

### (5) F14's companion — a supplemental notice where the Board later determines an amount differently

Entitlement can be clear while the amount is not: whether a surviving spouse takes the § 51.1-162 B allowance or the richer § 51.1-162 C allowance can turn on a Workers' Compensation award made months later. This revision defers the notice until the amount is determined. The drafted companion adds, after the rider:

> <u>and where the Board after giving the notice determines the amount of a benefit differently, it shall give that person a further notice stating the determination and the reasons for it no later than 30 days after making it, and the period within which that person may seek review under subsection M of that determination runs from the date that further notice is given</u>

**It is a new duty on the Board and was not agreed.**

### (6) M 2 — make lateness reviewable after it is cured

The failure limb closes *"at any time before the Board makes the determination, gives the notice, or makes the payment,"* so a member whose crediting was 30 days late has no remedy for the lateness from the moment it is cured. The reviewer's words, in M 2's first sentence:

> or, in the case of a failure to make a determination, to give a notice, or to make a payment, at any time before the Board makes the determination, gives the notice, or makes the payment<u>, or, where the Board has made the determination, given the notice, or made the payment late, within 30 days after it does so</u>

**Small, and it is a new right.**

### (7) F8's alternative — keep the general notice class, gated

This revision replaced *"other person to whom this section requires a notice to be given"* with two named classes, in both of subdivision M 2's class lists, because the open formula reached the public through subsection K. If the Director wants the general formula back, the gated form is:

> or other person to whom this section requires a notice <u>of a determination affecting that person</u> to be given

**Not enacted; the narrow form is in the text.** The gated form keeps the Chairmen out (their notices are information-only and two of them are expressly anonymised) and keeps the public out (subsection K's notice is of a fact, not of a determination affecting a person) — but it does so by a judgment a court would have to make, where the narrow form does it on the face of the act.

### Still open and not re-asked here

- **§7 item 60's (a), (b) and (c)** — the elected-option class whose contingent annuitant is a sibling, an adult child or an unmarried partner. Subdivision G 4 routes her death to § 51.1-162, and § 51.1-162 B pays only a spouse, minor child or parent. **She is still unanswered, and MOO-161 does not change that.**
- **§7 item 122's five unenacted answers** — F5's two forms (the ERF residual: the Commonwealth prices the subsidy on unreduced retirement while subsection E's second limb may leave the survivor reduced), F9(a)(ii) (recovering the cash from the trust fund), F12's 90-day election period, F15's payee where no personal representative qualifies, and the § 51.1-165 C 2 displacement. **Item 122 is marked in the item list with what was enacted and what stays open.**
- **§7 items 106, 107 and 126** are untouched by this revision.

## 9. Three sentences for the Editor

1. The bill no longer switches off the Code provision that refunds a deceased retiree's unrecovered contributions to her family — it leaves that provision alone and speaks only to a death in the window between a member's retirement notice and her retirement date.
2. Where a member of the program dies in that window, the Retirement System must determine her survivor's benefit as it would on a death in service, taking her purchased military service into account, and must tell her survivors in writing what they are entitled to, how it was computed, what they may waive or elect, and by when.
3. The six statements the bill compels the Retirement System to give a member before it takes her money now match the statutes they describe, and the people who may ask the Board to reconsider a decision are named — a rejected claimant and an employer that paid — rather than left to a formula that, on its face, included the general public.
