### The fatal finding, and why the cure takes words out instead of adding them

**[MOO-161](/MOO/issues/MOO-161) F1.** Store revision 58's freestanding *"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death"* was governed only by *"where clause (i) of this subdivision applies"* — and clause (i) is *"dies"*, whose only carve-out is a member who retired in a retirement that is **not** a qualifying retirement. By negative implication it reached **a member who retired in a qualifying retirement on a basic allowance and died years later as a retiree**, and switched off the provision that returns her unrecovered contributions to her family. On the reviewer's facts: no spouse, no minor child, no surviving parent, a brother as beneficiary — **he receives nothing and $122,000 of her own money stays in the trust fund.** An act whose case for existing is that forfeiting purchased military service is wrong would have forfeited more than the defect it was curing.

**The citation check is what makes the cure a subtraction.** § 51.1-163 is captioned *"Death after retirement"* and opens *"If a member dies after the effective date of retirement"* — **it cannot reach a pre-effective-date death of its own force at all.** It reaches one only by deeming, and there are exactly three deeming provisions: § 51.1-165 C 1, § 51.1-165 C 2 and § 51.1-165.01 F. The sentence's own notwithstanding already displaces the first and the third, and preserves the second deliberately. **So the disapplication was never load-bearing.** It is deleted, and the sentence is confined to the window in subdivision D 1's own words — *"after the Board has received the notification of the member's effective date of retirement required by subdivision D 1 and before that date."* That cures **F1** and, because § 51.1-163 is a measure again, **F2** — the C 2 greater-of that could not be performed because its input had been switched off.

**I treated the confinement as restoring the agreed scope, not as choosing a policy**, and the record says so at §3 and §6. Your answers (d) and (f) and the D 1 certification all speak to the member who has filed her § 51.1-153 notice and not yet retired; the reach beyond that window was an artefact of how store revision 58 was cut. **The wider alternative is drafted and not enacted**, in case the intention was wider than the record shows.

### Three statements of law the act compelled the Board to make were wrong

The certification a member must be given **before the Board may accept her money** stated that § 51.1-162 **C** pays *"a surviving spouse"* — it pays a spouse, **minor children and parents** (F4), so an unmarried member with a child was being told the work-related death allowance could not reach her child. It stated that § 51.1-162 **A** pays contributions where no allowance is payable under *"subsection B or C"* — A's condition is **subsection B alone** (F5), so the act compelled the Board to certify the opposite of the statute and handed a future plaintiff his own best evidence. And it described subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165 as entitling the contingent annuitant, which is true of **one of its two limbs**, while suppressing C 2's in-service condition (F6). **All three now state the law as the Code states it, by cross-reference rather than by paraphrase, and name no payee** — because who is paid is F3, and F3 is yours.

**The incomplete-certification remedy could not work and now can.** Deeming the omission a *determination* gave the review right to the only person who had the certification — the member, who is not adversely affected while alive — and ran the clock from a document her survivors never saw. It is now a **failure** of the Board; M 2's failure limb reaches a notice or certification given on time with a required matter left out; and the period runs 30 days from the G 4 death notice, the first writing the person adversely affected ever receives.

**The reconsideration class is narrowed because it had no boundary.** *"Other person to whom this section requires a notice to be given"* reached, on the face of the act, **the public** — subsection K requires the Board to *"give public notice"* — and the two money-committee Chairmen, whose notices are information-only. Both of M 2's class lists now name the **rejected claimant** and the **employer that paid on the member's behalf** expressly. **This is not a reversal of store revision 59's reasoning:** §6.0-59 kept the six-class list precisely so as not to strip those two of the Article 5 right, and they are exactly who is kept. What goes is the accidental.

### Your five answers, as enacted

A **30-day duty on the employing state agency** to report the death, with a subsection J line, drafted on G 4's own involuntary-separation model and confined to state agencies. The **90-day redetermination** run, in the death case, from the date the Board learns of the death. **One drafting judgment you should see:** your release trigger's second branch — *"because no such allowance is payable to any person"* — has **no election to date the 60 days from**, so the enacted words run that branch from the Board's determination that no allowance is payable. A duty with no deadline is unenforceable; the alternative was to leave it undated. It is §6 item 4 of the record.
