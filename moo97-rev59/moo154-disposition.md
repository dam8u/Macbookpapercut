## 3. Corrections — three against the review, two against me, two against my own sweep

**(a) F7's premise was mine, and it fails on the words.** This issue's own work list reasoned that D 1's day-for-day extension carried the consequence of an incomplete certification because *"an incomplete statement is a late statement."* The extension sentence attaches to *"that written statement"* — the statement of earliest and final qualifying retirement dates required by the **definition of "final qualifying retirement date" in subsection A** — and the certification is a different writing. **The duty had no consequence at all**, not a weak one. The review is right and the premise is withdrawn.

**(b) F3's cancellation case needs no confinement of (f), and I did not take the fix direction for it.** The reviewer proposed adding *"and whose election has not been cancelled under subdivision 5."* With *"taking account of"* in place, the computation limb no longer points at a base that can be null: § 51.1-162's own computation runs on creditable service, and the credited months are taken account of in it, whether they were credited under G 4 or under G 5. **Confining (f) would have pushed the cancelling member back out to § 51.1-165 C 1 — a loss — to cure a defect that the smaller fix already closes.**

**(c) F13(b) does not hold on the live body, and the reason is store revision 55.** The review worked from store revision 54 (document revision `d453a9ee`). Subdivision M 2's second sentence — added as store revision 55's E5, out of [MOO-130](/MOO/issues/MOO-130)'s M1 — now provides that *"every determination, certification, redetermination, recertification, reduction, cancellation, rescission, restoration, **crediting of service credit**, placement on a waiting list, expiration of a reservation, denial of an election, and refusal to accept an election under this section is a determination of the Board for the purposes of this subsection."* M 2's first limb reaches *"a failure of the Board to make a determination … within a period required by this section."* **So a failure to credit within E2's 60 days is already reviewable and no words were written for it.** F13(a) and (d) are real and enacted; (b) is a null.

**(d) The reviewer's F1 fix direction named *"subsection F of § 51.1-165.01."* The enacted words name one limb of it.** Subsection F does two things, and only one of them is adverse: it routes the death to § 51.1-163, **and** it provides that *"the requirement that the member be in service shall not apply."* The second limb is the member's friend, and it is the authority F2's cure borrows. Displacing the whole subsection would have discarded the limb the act wants.

**(e) The reviewer's F1 fix direction also named subdivision C 2. C 2 is preserved instead.** See §6 item 2. The displacement alternative is §7(9) and is one substitution away.

**(f) Two counts in my own §5.37 were predictions and were wrong; the sweep is corrected in the document.** I published *"§ 51.1-165 bare 5 → 6"* and *"§ 51.1-162 6 → 11."* Re-counted mechanically over §1 of the written body: **bare § 51.1-165 is 5 → 7** and **§ 51.1-162 is 6 → 9**, because three of the new references say *"that section"* rather than repeating the citation and because the C 2 preservation sentence names § 51.1-165 twice. The corrected figures and a one-line note are in §5.37. **A predicted count is not a count** — the same lesson §5.33's failed assertion taught, in a smaller way.

**(g) §5.33's assertion that *"§ 51.1-165 is the only one whose operation the act alters"* was false when it was written**, and MOO-154's F1 is the finding that caught it. The stub §5.33 now carries the correction, and §5.37 states the rule it produces: **the citation count is not the cross-reference check; reading the cited section is.**

## 7. Ambiguities resolved — nine, and none of them chose a policy

1. **F1's notwithstanding names a limb, not a subsection.** See §3(d). *"So much of subsection F of § 51.1-165.01 as requires benefits to be paid in accordance with § 51.1-163"* — twenty-one words that keep subsection F's disapplication of the in-service requirement alive and take away only its routing to a refund.
2. **Subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165 is preserved, not displaced — the judgment to attack first.** C 2 is a *saving* provision: it pays the **greater** of § 51.1-163 and § 51.1-162 B to a spouse, minor child or parent named under a joint and last-survivor option. Naming it in the notwithstanding, as the fix direction proposed, would take an existing right from a named class so that the act reads more simply. The enacted second sentence pays the greater of the two, so **no member is worse off than under present law**, and the conflict between an unrepealed saving provision and a later flat command is resolved on the face of the act. **The alternative is §7(9) below and is one substitution.**
3. **F13(b) is a null and the reason is a sentence that landed after the review.** See §3(c).
4. **F7's premise was mine and is withdrawn.** See §3(a). What replaces it is procedural and the section's own: a determination for subsection M, and a line in the subsection J report. **It is not a new remedy and it moves no money.**
5. **The certification is not given a new verb.** D 1's third sentence already reads *"The Board's certification shall state the matters required by…"*, so the enacted opener is *"The Board's certification shall also state"* — the same actor, the same formula, one clause shorter than the fix direction's *"The Board shall state in the Board's certification."* **Where the surrounding statute has a form, it beats the better sentence.**
6. **F6(c) is cured by stating § 51.1-162 C, not by deleting *"in every other case."*** A member about to file can act on the information, and it is the one case in which the purchased credit cannot change the benefit at all, because § 51.1-162 C measures on average final compensation and not on creditable service. **That consequence is a fiscal fact and it is routed, not drafted around.**
7. **F11 is cured in the addressee and nowhere else.** *"Each person entitled"* goes into the addressee list, because § 51.1-162 B divides an allowance among more than one minor child and among more than one parent, and a definite singular identifies no addressee where two persons are entitled. The **release** half, and F10's asymmetry with the no-election case, are one drafted answer at §7(4) — widening a release widens what the Commonwealth pays under subsection N.
8. **F8's and F13(d)'s cure is one clause, built as a floor and never a setter.** The notice period runs from *"the earlier of"* the date of crediting and the last day on which crediting was required, so **a Board that credits late cannot extend its own notice period**; and the alternative limb starts a fresh 30 days as to a person whose entitlement the Board determines later. Neither limb can pull a period forward against the Board; both can only bind it.
9. **F12 is cured by making the statement conditional, not by inventing a period.** § 51.1-162 **B** states none; the 90 days is in § 51.1-162 **C** and waives a C allowance to take a B allowance. Fixing a period would be a determination with no standard anywhere in the act. **It is drafted as an answer at §7(5) instead of taken.**

## 8. The nine drafted answers — word for word, and none of them enacted

**Four of these nine decide who bears a loss. I have not picked one.** Each is one substitution against the live text of store revision 58.

### (1) F5 — the unreduced-retirement-eligibility bar. The ERF residual, and the largest item here

The problem: subsection E's last sentence has two limbs, and G 4's saving (*"Subsection E does not prevent the crediting of service credit required by this subdivision"*) reaches only the **crediting** limb. The second limb — *"except for the purposes of the definition of 'eligible member' in subsection A, no member shall be treated as having reached his unreduced retirement eligibility date by reason of an election before payment in full is received"* — stands unqualified in the death case. Clause (v) of *"eligible member"* means **every** member in G 4 is one whose unreduced eligibility depends on the purchase. So the survivor's § 51.1-162 B allowance may be computed **with** the § 51.1-155 A 2 or A 3 reduction, while *"certified subsidy"* priced the subsidy on *"the member's retirement on the member's anticipated earliest qualifying retirement date"* — unreduced retirement. **The Commonwealth pays for the unreduced outcome and the survivor is paid the reduced one.**

**Form A — every member whom the credited service carries over the line.** FIND *"Subsection E does not prevent the crediting of service credit required by this subdivision."* READ:

> Subsection E does not prevent the crediting of service credit required by this subdivision<u>, and does not prevent the member from being treated, for the purposes of § 51.1-162, as having reached his unreduced retirement eligibility date where the service credit credited under this subdivision causes him to have reached it</u>.

**Form B — only where payment was completed before death.** Same FIND. READ:

> Subsection E does not prevent the crediting of service credit required by this subdivision<u>, and, where the member completed payment for the whole of the service credit stated in the Board's certification before the date of death, does not prevent the member from being treated, for the purposes of § 51.1-162, as having reached his unreduced retirement eligibility date where that service credit causes him to have reached it</u>.

**A costs more than B. B leaves the partial payer reduced** — and the partial payer is the case G 4's own crediting sentence was written for. **The figure is the Fiscal & Impact Modeler's and neither form is priced.**

### (2) F9(a) — the $3,000,000 cap stops measuring Commonwealth money

Subsection N pays the settled amount to the retirement system **on settlement**, which G 4 fixes at the date of death; the release sentence then restores that amount to the cap, and nothing moves it back from the retirement system. Over the Program the Commonwealth's cash exposure becomes **the cap plus every released-and-already-paid amount, with no stated bound.**

**(i) Restore only what has not been paid.** READ: *"…shall release the amount settled under this subdivision and shall restore it to the amount available to be reserved under the limitation stated in the first sentence of subdivision D 2 <u>only to the extent the amount settled has not been paid to the retirement system under subsection N</u>, no later than 60 days after the election…"* **Keeps the cap a cash limit; gives the Program less capacity back than the current words promise.**

**(ii) Recover the cash.** Add: *"<u>and the retirement system shall repay to the appropriation from which it was paid so much of the amount settled as has been paid to it under subsection N, no later than 60 days after the release</u>."* **This takes money back out of the trust fund after settlement; [MOO-96](/MOO/issues/MOO-96)'s F-3 and Va. Const. art. X, § 11 both bear on it, and it should not be enacted without counsel on that point.**

**(iii) Leave it, and disclose it.** No edit. The cap then bounds *reservations* and not cash, and that must be stated in the fiscal note and in the memo's risk section. **This is a decision, not a default.**

### (3) F9(b) — a survivor who wins her review has nothing left to be paid out of

G 4 forbids a second settlement (*"no certified subsidy is settled more than once under this subdivision or under subdivision 1"*) and its reinstatement machinery is keyed to a released **reservation**, not to a released **amount settled**.

> <u>Where an amount released under the preceding sentence would not have been released had a determination of the Board been as it stands after reconsideration under subsection M or after judicial review, the Board shall reinstate the amount released, together with any contingency amount released with it, no later than 30 days after the determination is reconsidered or reviewed, and the rule of this subdivision that no certified subsidy is settled more than once does not prevent the settlement of an amount so reinstated.</u>

**It restores a right and it costs what the right costs.**

### (4) F10 and F11's release half — the election case and the no-election case

Under § 51.1-162 **A** there is no election; A operates automatically where the relation test fails. So the unmarried member with no minor child and no surviving parent — identical economics, no allowance ever payable — frees nothing from the cap, while the electing survivor's case does.

**FIND** *"Where the person entitled to any benefit payable on the member's death elects under § 51.1-162 to be paid the member's accumulated contributions or a lump sum in lieu of any other benefit under that section,"* **READ**:

> <u>Where no retirement allowance is payable under § 51.1-162 on the member's death, whether because the person entitled to any benefit payable on the member's death elects under § 51.1-162 to be paid the member's accumulated contributions or a lump sum in lieu of any other benefit under that section or because no such allowance is payable to any person,</u>

**It also cures the two-minor-children case**, where one guardian elects and an allowance is still payable to the other child: the release no longer fires while an allowance remains payable. **It widens what the cap gives back, so it interacts with (2) and should be decided with it.**

### (5) F12's alternative — state a period in G 4 for the § 51.1-162 B election

> <u>A waiver or election under § 51.1-162 in respect of a death to which this subdivision applies shall be made no later than 90 days after the Board gives the notice required by this subdivision, and the Board shall act upon it no later than 30 days after it is made.</u>

**It closes (2)(iii)'s open-ended driver and it takes a right from a survivor who is slow.** Ninety days is § 51.1-162 C's own figure, which is why it is the one proposed; the numeral would be mine and that is why it is here.

### (6) F13(c) — nothing requires anyone to tell the Board the member died

> <u>A state agency that employs or last employed a member whose election the Board has accepted shall give the Board written notice of the member's death no later than 30 days after the agency learns of it, and the Board shall state in the report required by subsection J every failure of a state agency to give that notice of which the Board becomes aware.</u>

Drafted on the model of G 4's own involuntary-separation limb, which pairs *"learns of"* with exactly this duty and this report. **It is a new duty on employing agencies. It is confined to state agencies, because a duty on a local employer needs an express grant and a Dillon's Rule judgment that is not mine to make.**

### (7) F13(e) — a deadline that can expire before the Board can begin

The redetermination runs *"no later than 90 days after that date"* — the date of death. Where the Board learns of the death later than that, it is already late, and G 4's escape sentence covers a recertification that *"becomes required after the redetermination has been made,"* not a deadline that expired before the Board knew.

**FIND** *"The Board shall, no later than 90 days after that date, redetermine"* **READ**:

> The Board shall, no later than 90 days after that date <u>or, where that date is the date of the member's death, no later than 90 days after the date on which the Board learns of the death</u>, redetermine

**It is a timing change to a period you set, and it only ever moves the deadline later.**

### (8) F15's payment side — a refund payable to an estate that may never exist

G 4 directs the refund and the notice to *"the member's estate"*, and nothing in the act, in Chapter 1 or in Title 64.2 requires a personal representative to qualify.

> <u>Where no personal representative of the member's estate has qualified, the Board shall pay the amount payable to the estate under this subdivision, and shall give the notice required by this subdivision, to the person who received the member's accumulated contributions under § 51.1-162 or, where no person has received them, to the person who would be entitled to receive them under subsection A of that section.</u>

**It names a payee, which is why it is yours and not mine.** It also makes that person a notice addressee, and therefore — by E7 and E8 — a person who may seek review under subsection M.

### (9) F1's alternative — displace subdivision C 2 instead of preserving it

**READ for E1's opening**, in place of what is enacted: *"Notwithstanding subdivision<u>s</u> C 1 <u>and C 2</u> of § 51.1-165 and so much of subsection F of § 51.1-165.01 as requires benefits to be paid in accordance with § 51.1-163, …"*, with the second sentence (*"Nothing in the preceding sentence reduces a benefit payable under subdivision C 2…"*) **deleted**, and the certification's C 2 limb deleted with it.

**Simpler by about 230 characters, and it takes an existing right** from a spouse, minor child or parent named under a joint and last-survivor option whose refund of contributions would have been the greater of the two. **One substitution either way.**

### Still open and not re-asked here

**§7 item 60's (a), (b) and (c)** — the elected-option class whose contingent annuitant is a sibling, an adult child or an unmarried partner. (f) routes her death to § 51.1-162, and § 51.1-162 B pays only a spouse, minor child or parent, so **she is still unanswered**, and MOO-154 does not change that. **Item 106's three alternatives for the certification's wording are superseded by E6** and should be read against the words now enacted. **Item 107** is store revision 55's and is untouched.

## 10. Three sentences for the Editor

**This revision fixes a hole that would have made the bill's death-benefit promise false for most of the state employees who rely on it: the text displaced one provision that sends a pre-retirement death to a refund of the member's own contributions, but not the twin provision that does the same thing for anyone who takes a partial lump sum, which is the ordinary way a Virginia state employee retires. It also stops the Board being required to tell a member four things about her own death benefit, three of which were not the law, and it gives two people the act hands a notice — a rejected claimant and an employer that paid part of the member's cost — the right to ask the Board to think again. What it does not yet do is guarantee that the service credit the Commonwealth paid to accelerate actually produces the unreduced allowance it was priced on; that question, and three about who bears a loss, are in front of the Policy Director.**
