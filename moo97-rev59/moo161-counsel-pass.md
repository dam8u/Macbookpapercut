# MOO-161 — Counsel pass on store revision 58's eight substitutions

**Internal risk analysis, not legal advice.** I am not counsel of record and nothing here is a legal opinion the Lab or the Commonwealth can rely on. `bill-text-s8` is an internal working draft: not a Division of Legislative Services bill, not reviewed by DLS, and not legally sufficient for introduction.

**Scope.** E1–E8 only, read against subdivisions G 4, D 1 and subsection M 2 of new § 51.1-142.4 whole (document title still reads "store revision 57"; the revision-58 strings are present in the body — I verified each of the eight against the live text before attacking it). I did not re-run MOO-154's F1 diagnosis, the enacted findings, the nine on the Director's card, or the §7 item 60 choice.

**Counts: 20 findings — 1 fatal, 9 serious, 6 moderate, 4 minor.** Plus 9 null results and one answer to the F13(b) question. Memo-risk-section marks on F1, F7, F8 and F11.

---

## 1. The strongest case against, in the opposition's voice

> *Mr. Chairman, this is the fourth year this patronage has come back to us, and each year it is longer. Look at what the drafter has now done to pay for it. Subdivision G 4 says — in words of one syllable — that where a member of this program dies, "§ 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death." Section 51.1-163 is captioned "Death after retirement," and it is the provision that returns to a dead retiree's family the contributions she made out of her own paycheck and did not live long enough to draw back. This bill repeals that protection for every member who takes the subsidy. A twenty-nine-year state employee who buys three years of military credit under this act, retires on a basic allowance, dies fourteen months later unmarried and childless with a hundred and twenty-two thousand dollars of her own money still in the fund, and whose only surviving relative is a brother — her brother gets nothing. Not a reduced benefit. Nothing. The section that would have paid him is switched off by name, and the section the bill substitutes, § 51.1-162, pays only where the member "dies before retirement." She did not. The drafter will tell you he meant this to operate in the two months between a retirement notice and a retirement date. I invite him to show the Committee the words that say so, because they are not in the bill.*
>
> *And understand what is being traded. The bill's whole claim to fiscal discipline is a three-million-dollar cap in subdivision D 2 and a kill-switch in subsection K. Neither reaches one dollar of what I have just described, because a death benefit is not a "certified subsidy." The cap caps the subsidy. The appropriation in subsection N funds the subsidy. The benefit change rides free, on the trust fund, uncapped, unappropriated, and unpriced — and the fiscal note the Committee was handed says seven hundred fifty-eight thousand dollars.*
>
> *Then look at what the bill compels the Retirement System to tell a member in writing before it will accept her money. Six propositions of law. Two of them are wrong. It compels the Board to tell a member that § 51.1-162 C pays "a surviving spouse" when the statute pays a spouse, minor children **and** parents — so the widowed father of a trooper's child is told in an official certification that his child has no claim. It compels the Board to tell her that her accumulated contributions are paid only where neither subsection B nor subsection C pays, when subsection A's own condition is subsection B alone. The bill makes the Board the author of a misstatement of law, requires the member to rely on it before she may participate, and then — in the same breath — provides that leaving one of these statements out is a "determination" she may appeal. She has no grievance while she is alive, and she is dead by the time anyone does.*
>
> *Last, subsection M. The bill extends the right to reconsideration and to Administrative Process Act review to "other person to whom this section requires a notice to be given." Subsection K of the same bill requires the Board to "give public notice." The drafter has, on the face of his own text, conferred a statutory review right on the general public over the administration of a retirement benefit. I do not say a court will give it to them. I say the Commonwealth will pay lawyers to argue about it.*

If that brief cannot be answered on the text, the eight substitutions are not finished. F1, F5, F4 and F8 above are each confirmed below.

## 2. Findings

### FATAL

#### F1 — E1 · `"where clause (i) of this subdivision applies, § 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death"`

**Category:** scope / wrong class. **Mark for the memo's risk section.**

The words at fault are the whole of the first limb, and specifically the deletion of the BEFORE text's window. BEFORE read: *"the Board's receipt of a written notification of the member's effective date of retirement given under subdivision D 1 shall not cause § 51.1-163 to apply to the member's death."* That formulation was **self-limiting** — it spoke only to the consequence of the Board's receipt of the notification, which is a pre-effective-date event. E1 replaced it with a freestanding, unconditional **`"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death"`**, governed only by `"where clause (i) of this subdivision applies."`

Clause (i) of G 4 is `"if the member, after the Board accepts the election, (i) dies."` The only carve-out in the subdivision is: *"Clause (i) of this subdivision does not apply where the member has retired in a retirement that is **not a qualifying retirement** before the date of death."* By negative implication a member who **has** retired in a qualifying retirement is **not** carved out. So clause (i) applies to the death of an electing member who retired in a qualifying retirement and died years later as a retiree.

**Authority.** § 51.1-163 is captioned **"Death after retirement"** and opens: *"If a member dies after the effective date of retirement, any excess of his accumulated contributions as of the effective date of his retirement, over the total retirement allowances received by him, shall be paid in the same manner as provided in subsection A of § 51.1-162, subsection A of § 51.1-207, or subsection A of § 51.1-218, in accordance with the retirement plan covering such member, **unless the retirement allowance is then being paid in accordance with any of the optional benefits provided for in § 51.1-165 or § 51.1-165.01**."* § 51.1-162 A opens: *"If a member dies **before retirement**, and if no benefits are payable under subsection B, the amount of his accumulated contributions shall be paid to the designated beneficiary or to a surviving relative according to the order of precedence set forth in this section."* § 51.1-162 B requires that *"a member dies in service."* Expressio unius as to the non-qualifying-retirement carve-out.

**The affected class is precise:** an electing member who retires in a **qualifying** retirement **on a basic (non-optional) allowance** — for whom § 51.1-163 is live, because the "unless ... optional benefits" proviso does not bite — and who then dies in retirement.

**Concrete failure scenario.** Member purchases 3 years of prior active-duty credit under the act in 2030. She retires in a qualifying retirement on 1 July 2038 on a basic allowance, aged 61, with $140,000 of accumulated contributions. She dies 1 September 2039 having received 14 monthly payments totalling $18,000. She is unmarried, has no minor child, and both parents are dead; her designated beneficiary is her brother.
- Under current law: § 51.1-163 pays the brother $122,000 (the excess of accumulated contributions over allowances received).
- Under E1 as written: clause (i) applies (she died after the Board accepted the election; she did not retire in a non-qualifying retirement). `"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death."` § 51.1-162 B does not pay — no spouse, no minor child, no parent. § 51.1-162 A does not pay by its own terms — she did **not** die "before retirement." § 51.1-162 C does not pay — her death was not compensable.
- **Result: the brother receives nothing. $122,000 of the member's own contributions is forfeited to the trust fund.** The act, sold as an anti-forfeiture repair, forfeits more than the defect it was curing.

**Second scenario, same clause, opposite direction.** Same member, but survived by a spouse. § 51.1-163 would have paid the $122,000 refund. E1 instead directs the benefit `"under § 51.1-162 as on the death of a member in service"` with E1's own in-service disapplication removing B's `"dies in service"` gate — so the spouse takes a **lifetime monthly survivor allowance** instead of a one-time refund. That is a windfall, it is a trust-fund liability, and F11 shows it is outside every cap in the bill.

**Why E1 made this worse rather than inheriting it.** Two reasons. First, BEFORE's first limb was inherently confined to the notification window; AFTER's is not. Second, E1 **added** `"and for the purposes of § 51.1-162 the requirement that the member be in service shall not apply"` — which removes the single textual obstacle that previously made `"as on the death of a member in service"` hard to apply to a retiree. The second limb retains a weak limiter (`"the benefit payable under this subdivision"`), but the first limb — the one that does the damage in scenario 1 — has none.

**Fix direction (one clause).** Restore the window expressly on the face of the (f) sentence: `"where clause (i) of this subdivision applies and the member dies after the Board has received a written notification of the member's effective date of retirement given under subdivision D 1 and before the effective date of retirement stated in it, ..."`. A narrower alternative is `"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death where the member dies before the effective date of retirement"`, but the first is better because it also aligns the operative text with what E6 already compels the Board to certify — see F9's note on the mismatch.

### SERIOUS

#### F2 — E1 · the greater-of is unperformable, because the preceding sentence removes its input

`"Nothing in the preceding sentence reduces a benefit payable under subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165, and where that subdivision applies the Board shall pay the benefit it provides or the benefit determined under the preceding sentence, whichever is greater."`

**Authority — § 51.1-165 C 2 verbatim:** *"If (i) the death of the member occurs prior to the effective date of retirement but after the Board has received written notification of the member's effective date of retirement; (ii) the member died while in service; (iii) at the time of the member's death the election for payment of the member's retirement allowance was a joint and last-survivor optional retirement benefit; and (iv) the member named his spouse, minor child, or parent as the contingent annuitant under the joint and last-survivor optional benefit, then **benefits shall be paid in accordance with the provisions of § 51.1-163**. However, if such contingent annuitant or annuitants are the same person or persons who would receive a monthly benefit under subsection B of § 51.1-162 and the monthly benefit under such subsection **would be greater than the benefit provided in accordance with the provisions of § 51.1-163**, then retirement benefits shall be paid in accordance with the provisions of subsection B of § 51.1-162."*

C 2 is not a provision with one benefit. It is a conditional pair, and **both of its limbs are defined by reference to § 51.1-163** — the first as the operative measure, the second as the comparator. E1's preceding sentence says `"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death."` So:

- C 2's first limb commands payment "in accordance with the provisions of § 51.1-163" — a section the same sentence has declared inapplicable to this death.
- C 2's second limb cannot be triggered at all, because triggering it requires computing "the benefit provided in accordance with the provisions of § 51.1-163" and comparing it. No input, no comparison, no trigger.
- `"Nothing in the preceding sentence reduces a benefit payable under subdivision C 2"` forbids a reduction. It does **not** supply the missing § 51.1-163 figure. A non-reduction clause cannot restore a measure that has been switched off.

This is the circularity the Director asked about, and it is worse than a double comparison: **it is a comparison that cannot be performed.** The Board is left to infer that § 51.1-163 remains available *as a measure* for C 2 purposes while being inapplicable *as a provision* — a distinction the text does not draw.

**Concrete failure scenario.** Member elects a joint-and-last-survivor option naming her husband as contingent annuitant, and also purchases credit under the act. She notifies the Board of a 1 June effective date, remains in service, and dies 3 May. C 2's four conditions are all met. The Board must determine "the benefit it provides." Limb 1 says pay per § 51.1-163; the act says § 51.1-163 does not apply. Limb 2 says compare § 51.1-162 B against the § 51.1-163 figure; there is no § 51.1-163 figure. The Board has no computable C 2 benefit to put on one side of E1's greater-of, and the greater-of is the only thing standing between the husband and whatever the Board decides.

**Fix direction.** Carve the disapplication rather than leaving it absolute: `"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death, except that for the purposes of subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165 the benefit payable in accordance with § 51.1-163 shall be determined as though this sentence had not been enacted."` Simpler and cleaner: delete `"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply to the member's death"` altogether and rely on the two notwithstanding limbs plus the affirmative § 51.1-162 direction — § 51.1-163 does not reach a pre-effective-date death of its own force anyway (see F1's authority and Null N1), so the disapplication was never load-bearing and it is the source of F1 and F2 both.

#### F3 — E1 · the greater-of has no measure and no payee rule

`"the Board shall pay the benefit it provides or the benefit determined under the preceding sentence, whichever is greater"`

**No measure.** The C 2 limb produces, on its first branch, a **lump sum** (accumulated contributions, since allowances received are nil before the effective date). The (f) sentence produces, where § 51.1-162 B or C applies, a **lifetime monthly allowance**. `"Whichever is greater"` states no basis of comparison — no present value, no actuarial equivalence, no discount rate, no mortality basis. C 2 has the same gap internally, but the General Assembly left one undefined comparison; E1 stacks a second on top of it and gives the Board no rule for either.

**No payee rule — and the payees differ.** This is the sharper half. The three candidate benefits have three different statutory payees:
| Limb | Payee |
|---|---|
| C 2 limb 1 (§ 51.1-163) | § 51.1-163 pays "in the same manner as provided in subsection A of § 51.1-162" → **the designated beneficiary or a surviving relative by order of precedence** |
| C 2 limb 2 (§ 51.1-162 B) | **the first qualifying person** in the order spouse → minor children → parents |
| (f) (§ 51.1-162 as on death in service) | § 51.1-162 A, B or C's payee as the case may be |

E1 says "the Board shall pay ... whichever is greater." It does not say **to whom.** Where the two limbs point at different people, a direction to pay the greater amount does not identify the recipient.

**Concrete failure scenario.** Member elects a joint-and-last-survivor option and names her **mother** as contingent annuitant. She is survived by her mother and by a husband. She dies in service after notification, before the effective date. Her designated death beneficiary of record is her sister.
- C 2 conditions (i)–(iv) are met (a parent was named).
- C 2's "however" limb is **not** engaged: § 51.1-162 B pays the *first qualifying person*, which is the **husband**, not the mother; so the contingent annuitant is not "the same person who would receive a monthly benefit under subsection B."
- So C 2's benefit is the § 51.1-163 measure — payable, through § 51.1-162 A's manner, to **the sister**.
- The (f) benefit is the § 51.1-162 B allowance — payable to **the husband**.
- E1 directs the Board to pay "whichever is greater." A lump sum to the sister, or an allowance to the husband. **Nothing in the act tells the Board how to compare them or which of the three women and one man is to be paid.** All four have a colourable claim and a statutory right under M 2 to reconsideration of whatever the Board decides, and under the Article 5 savings clause to judicial review of it in the Circuit Court of the City of Richmond.

**Fix direction.** Two separate repairs are needed. (a) State the measure: `"... whichever is greater, the two being compared on the basis of actuarial equivalence as of the date of the member's death determined on the assumptions then in use by the Board."` (b) State the payee: `"and the Board shall pay it to the person to whom the provision under which the greater benefit is determined requires it to be paid."` If the Director prefers not to open the payee question, the alternative is to confine the greater-of to the case where the payees coincide — which is C 2's "however" limb — and let C 2's first limb be displaced outright.

#### F4 — E6 · proposition 5 is inaccurate: § 51.1-162 C is not limited to a surviving spouse

`"that subsection C of § 51.1-162 provides a retirement allowance to a surviving spouse where the member's death results from a cause compensable under the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act (§ 65.2-100 et seq.)"`

**Authority — § 51.1-162 C** opens *"If a member dies in service from a cause compensable under the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act ..."* and provides an allowance for the member's **surviving spouse, minor children, and parents** — not for a surviving spouse alone. (Verified against the section; see §3 below.)

The proposition is therefore a **compelled misstatement of law in a document the Board must give the member before it may accept her election**, and the misstatement is adverse: it tells a member with no spouse that the workers'-compensation death allowance is unavailable to her survivors, when it is available to her minor children and to her parents.

**Concrete failure scenario.** An unmarried corrections officer with a seven-year-old daughter purchases credit under the act in 2031. Her D 1 certification, as E6 compels, states that § 51.1-162 C "provides a retirement allowance to a surviving spouse." She reads it, concludes the work-related death allowance cannot reach her child, and designates her sister as death beneficiary to take the contributions refund instead. She notifies the Board of a 1 October 2040 effective date and dies in service on 2 September 2040 of a cause later held compensable. Her daughter is entitled to a § 51.1-162 C allowance. Nobody in the family knows it, because the only writing the Commonwealth ever gave the member on the subject said the allowance was for spouses. The Board's own J report will record no failure, because the Board stated exactly what the act told it to state.

**Fix direction.** `"that subsection C of § 51.1-162 provides a retirement allowance, where the member's death results from a cause compensable under the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act (§ 65.2-100 et seq.), to the member's surviving spouse, minor children, or parents as that subsection provides."` Do not enumerate C's internal priority or its Social Security adjustment in the certification — a cross-reference is safer than a paraphrase, and every paraphrase in this sentence is a fresh exposure.

#### F5 — E6 · proposition 6 is inaccurate: § 51.1-162 A's condition is subsection B alone

`"and that where no allowance is payable under subsection B or C of § 51.1-162, subsection A of that section provides for the payment of the member's accumulated contributions"`

**Authority — § 51.1-162 A verbatim:** *"If a member dies before retirement, and if no benefits are payable under **subsection B**, the amount of his accumulated contributions shall be paid to the designated beneficiary or to a surviving relative according to the order of precedence set forth in this section."* Subsection A's condition does not mention subsection C. I checked this twice against the section and it held both times.

Subsection **B**, by contrast, *does* contain the cross-condition — it applies *"if no benefits are payable under subsection C of this section."* So the Code's actual structure is: C displaces B; A's condition is B's non-payment only. Where C pays and B therefore does not, **A's literal condition is satisfied** and A requires payment of the accumulated contributions *in addition to* C's allowance.

E6 compels the Board to certify the opposite. The practical consequences run both ways and both are bad: if VRS administers the act as certified, it underpays the C class by the whole accumulated-contributions amount; if VRS administers the Code as written, it has given every member a certification that misstates her own survivors' entitlement.

**Concrete failure scenario.** Member dies in service of a compensable cause, survived by her husband. The Commission makes an award; § 51.1-162 C allowance commences. Her designated beneficiary — her adult son — claims the accumulated contributions under § 51.1-162 A, correctly pointing out that no benefit is payable under subsection B because C displaced it. The Board denies the claim, citing the proposition this act compelled it to certify. The son seeks reconsideration under M 2 and then an Article 5 appeal in the Circuit Court of the City of Richmond, pleading that the Board applied a statement of law the General Assembly wrote into § 51.1-142.4 D 1 instead of applying § 51.1-162 A's actual condition. **The act has supplied the plaintiff with his own best evidence.**

**Fix direction.** Either track A's condition exactly — `"and that where no benefit is payable under subsection B of § 51.1-162, subsection A of that section provides for the payment of the member's accumulated contributions"` — or, if the Lab's intent is that the C class not also take a refund, that is a substantive amendment to § 51.1-162 A and belongs in the bill's amending sections, not in a compelled certification. **It cannot be achieved by telling the member the law is other than it is.**

#### F6 — E6 · proposition 2 misdescribes C 2's payee and omits two of its conditions

`"that subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165, where it applies, entitles the person named as contingent annuitant to the benefit that subdivision provides or to the benefit so determined, whichever is greater"`

Three inaccuracies, from the C 2 text quoted at F2:

1. **The payee is wrong on C 2's first limb.** C 2's first limb pays "in accordance with the provisions of § 51.1-163," which pays "in the same manner as provided in subsection A of § 51.1-162" — i.e. to the **designated beneficiary or a surviving relative by order of precedence**. The contingent annuitant *qua* contingent annuitant is entitled to nothing under that limb. C 2 "entitles the person named as contingent annuitant" only on its second limb, and only because condition (iv) and the "however" clause there happen to coincide. The certification states as a flat proposition what is true of one branch of two.
2. **C 2's in-service condition is suppressed.** Condition (ii) is `"the member died while in service"` — and E1's in-service disapplication is expressly confined `"for the purposes of § 51.1-162,"` so it does **not** reach C 2. A member who separates from service in March with a 1 June effective date and dies in April is outside C 2 entirely. The certification's `"where it applies"` is technically true but is read by a member who has just been told, in the immediately preceding clause, that the in-service requirement is beside the point.
3. **C 2's own greater-of is not disclosed.** "The benefit that subdivision provides" is itself the greater of two figures. A member reading the certification would understand C 2 to provide one benefit and the act to provide a floor under it. It is two comparisons, not one.

**Concrete failure scenario.** Member elects a joint-and-last-survivor option naming her husband, purchases under the act, and is told by the certification that C 2 "entitles the person named as contingent annuitant" to the greater benefit. Relying on that, she does not update her death-beneficiary designation, which still names her late mother's estate. She leaves service on 31 March with a 1 June effective date, and dies on 20 April — **not in service.** C 2 does not apply at all. Her husband takes whatever § 51.1-162 B gives him; the certification's assurance that his status as contingent annuitant secured him the greater benefit was never true for her facts, and the one condition that would have told her so — `"the member died while in service"` — was the one the certification left out.

**Fix direction.** `"that where the member dies while in service and subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165 otherwise applies, the Board shall pay the benefit that subdivision provides, itself determined under that subdivision as the greater of the benefit payable in accordance with § 51.1-163 and the benefit payable under subsection B of § 51.1-162, or the benefit determined under subdivision G 4, whichever is greater, to the person entitled to it under the provision under which the greater benefit is determined."* Long, but every clause of it is load-bearing, and F3's payee gap has to be closed in the operative text before it can be honestly certified here.

#### F7 — E3c + E3b · the compelled notice can be given after the § 51.1-162 C waiver period it must describe has expired

**Mark for the memo's risk section.**

`"no later than 30 days after the earlier of the date on which it credits the service credit and the last day on which this subdivision requires it to be credited"` — read with the crediting deadline `"no later than 60 days after it learns of the member's death"` and with the notice content `"of any right of the person entitled to any benefit payable on the member's death to waive that benefit or to elect another benefit in its place under § 51.1-162 and, where § 51.1-162 states a period within which the waiver or election must be made, of that period."`

**Authority.** § 51.1-162's only waiver right is in **subsection C**, and it states a period: a C beneficiary may *"waive his rights to the allowance by written notification to the Board within **ninety days after the death of the member**"* so as to make the subsection B benefits available. (The separate **election** right is in subsection B — *"If the person elects in writing, the amount of the member's accumulated contributions or lump sum payment shall be paid to him exclusively, in lieu of any other benefits under this section"* — and states **no** period.)

Now compose the clocks. The C waiver runs **90 days from the death**. The G 4 notice is due at the outer limit **90 days from the date the Board *learns* of the death** (60 to credit + 30 to notify). The two coincide exactly only if the Board learns of the death on the day it occurs. **Every day of delay in the Board learning of the death is a day by which the compelled notice of the waiver right can post-date the expiry of that right.** E3c did not create this — it tightened the ceiling, which is why I rate it serious rather than fatal — but E3c is where the period now lives, the fix belongs in the same words, and the defect is now *determinate* rather than open-ended, which makes it provable against the Commonwealth.

**Concrete failure scenario.** Member dies in service on 1 March 2041 of a compensable cause, survived by her husband and by a minor child from an earlier marriage. The employing agency reports the death to the Board on 25 March. The Board credits the purchased service credit on 24 May (day 60). The G 4 notice is due 23 June. **The § 51.1-162 C waiver expired on 30 May.** The husband — who would have been better off waiving the C allowance so that the § 51.1-162 B benefit became available to the child — receives, on 23 June, a notice from the Commonwealth telling him he has a right to waive, and stating a 90-day period that ran out 24 days before the letter was written. He has no remedy: the Board gave the notice within the period G 4 allows, so there is no "failure ... within a period required by this section" to take to M 2, and nothing in the act tolls § 51.1-162 C.

**Aggravating sub-point.** E7/E8 give the newly-added notice recipients a right to seek reconsideration, but M 2's tolling sentence still tolls only *"any period within which **the member or applicant** must act under this section or under subdivision B 4 f of § 51.1-142.2."* A survivor or disappointed claimant who files for reconsideration tolls nothing for herself, and the § 51.1-162 C period is not a period "under this section" in any event. The new review right is therefore granted on terms that let the underlying substantive right expire while the review is pending.

**Fix direction.** The clean repair is a tolling provision in the act: `"Where the Board gives the notice required by this subdivision later than 30 days after the death of the member, the period stated in subsection C of § 51.1-162 within which a waiver must be made does not expire before the thirtieth day after the notice is given to the person entitled to make it."` That requires an amendment to § 51.1-162 or an express override in § 51.1-142.4; either way it is a substantive clause, not a drafting tidy, and it is the single highest-value repair in this list after F1. A weaker fallback is to accelerate the notice (30 days after the Board learns of the death, not after crediting) — but that collides with F14.

#### F8 — E7/E8 · the new class is facially unbounded, because subsection K requires a notice to the public

**Mark for the memo's risk section.**

`"or other person to whom this section requires a notice to be given"` — in both the reconsideration limb and the Article 5 savings clause.

I counted the notice duties in § 51.1-142.4. The persons to whom the section requires a notice to be given, who are not already in M 2's list, are:

| # | Recipient | Source | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | each person entitled to any benefit payable on the member's death | G 4 death notice | **cure — intended** |
| 2 | any person who claimed a benefit and whom the Board determined is not entitled to it | G 4 death notice | **cure — intended, and the whole point of the amendment** |
| 3 | any employer that paid on the member's behalf | G 4 death notice; refund sentences | problem — see F19 |
| 4 | the Chairmen of the House Committee on Appropriations and the Senate Committee on Finance and Appropriations | four express `"written notice ... to the Chairmen"` duties in D 3, D 5 and G 4 | problem — see F20 |
| 5 | **the public** | **subsection K: `"and shall give public notice of that fact"`** | **unbounded** |

Item 5 is the finding. Subsection K requires the Board to give a notice; the person to whom it is required to be given is the public at large. On the face of the text, every member of the public is "a person to whom this section requires a notice to be given," and is therefore within both the reconsideration class and the Article 5 savings class. The class is not merely wide — **it has no boundary that can be stated.**

The `"adversely affected"` / `"aggrieved by"` gates are the Lab's answer to this, and they are a real answer on the merits. They are not an answer to the cost of litigating it, and they are weaker here than they look, because M 2's second sentence expressly makes `"refusal to accept an election"` a determination of the Board, and subsection K is a provision that commands the Board to **refuse all elections** for a fiscal year.

**Concrete failure scenario, with plaintiff, claim and venue.** The General Assembly does not appropriate the certified amount for FY2035. The Board, as K requires, accepts no election filed on or after 1 July 2034 and gives public notice. A public-employee association whose members include several hundred people who intended to file brings (a) a request for reconsideration under M 2 within 30 days of the public notice, pleading that it is a "person to whom this section requires a notice to be given" under the clause E7 added and is aggrieved by the Board's "refusal to accept an election," a determination under M 2's second sentence; and (b) on denial, a petition for Article 5 review under § 2.2-4026 in the **Circuit Court of the City of Richmond**. The Board's standing defence is good but it is a defence — it must be briefed, and the association's answer is that the General Assembly wrote its class into the statute by name in 2029 and is presumed to have meant something by it.

**Fix direction.** The cure E7/E8 were reaching for is item 2 — the disappointed claimant — and item 2 alone needs new words, because **"beneficiary" is defined in § 51.1-124.3 as "any person entitled to receive benefits under this chapter,"** which already covers item 1. So replace the open formula with the narrow one that does the work: `"A member, retiree, beneficiary, applicant, personal representative of a deceased member's estate, or person who claimed any benefit payable on the death of a member and whom the Board determined is not entitled to it,"` in both occurrences. If the Director wants to keep the general formula, gate it on individualised notice: `"or other person to whom this section requires a notice of a determination affecting that person to be given."`

#### F9 — E6 · the consequence sentence gives the review right to the one person who cannot be aggrieved

`"A failure to state in the Board's certification every matter required by the preceding sentence is a determination of the Board for the purposes of subsection M"`

The Director asked whether deeming an omission a *determination* works given that M 2's 30-day clock runs `"after the notice of the determination is given"` and no notice is given of an omission, and whether M 2's failure limb covers it better. The answer to the first is no, but **the clock is not the worst of it**, and the answer to the second is "better, but not as drafted."

**The timing defect is a person defect.** D 1's first sentence requires the certification **before** the Board may accept the election. The omission is therefore complete years before it can hurt anyone, and the matters omitted are *all* about what happens on the member's death. So:
- At the moment of the omission the only person who has received the certification is **the member**, and she is not `"adversely affected"` by it — nothing has happened to her.
- At the moment someone *is* adversely affected — the member is dead and a survivor has been paid the wrong benefit or none — the person adversely affected **never received the certification**, has no notice of the deemed determination, and is confronted with a determination made, on the Board's reading, a decade earlier.

Whichever reading of the clock a court adopts, the right lands on the wrong person. If the clock runs from the certification, it expired while the only holder of the right had no grievance. If it never starts, the Board's answer is that the condition precedent in M 2's first limb (`"filed within 30 days after the notice of the determination is given"`) is unsatisfiable, so no request is ever properly filed.

**Does the failure limb cover it better?** M 2's failure limb runs to `"a failure of the Board to make a determination or to give a notice within a period required by this section,"` reviewable `"at any time before the Board makes the determination or gives the notice."` An incomplete certification is **neither**: the Board made the determination and gave the certification, on time. It is a notice given within its period but defective in content. **As drafted, neither limb reaches it** — which is why the deeming sentence was written, and the deeming sentence picked the limb that fits worse.

**Note on redundancy, and on what the deeming sentence displaces.** M 2's second sentence already provides that `"every determination, **certification**, redetermination, ..."` is a determination for the purposes of the subsection. The certification is therefore *already* a reviewable determination with a clock that runs from when it is given. E6's deeming sentence recharacterises the **omission** as a separate determination of which no notice is given, and in doing so arguably displaces the cleaner reading already available.

**Concrete failure scenario.** The Board's 2030 certification to an unmarried member with a minor child omits the subsection C proposition. She retires-notifies in 2041, dies in service in 2041 of a compensable cause before her effective date. No one ever tells the family that § 51.1-162 C exists. In 2042 the child's guardian seeks reconsideration of the omission. The Board answers: the deemed determination was made in 2030; the guardian is not a person to whom the certification was given; and M 2's 30-day period, measured from the only notice ever given of it, expired in 2030.

**Fix direction.** Put it in the failure limb and widen the limb to fit, then fix the clock to the death notice. Two edits:
- In M 2's first sentence, after `"or by a failure of the Board to make a determination or to give a notice within a period required by this section,"` add `"or by a failure of the Board to state in a certification or notice a matter this section requires to be stated in it,"`.
- In D 1, replace the deeming sentence with: `"A failure to state in the Board's certification every matter required by the preceding sentence is a failure of the Board for the purposes of subsection M, and the period within which a person adversely affected by it may seek review under subsection M runs from the date on which the notice required by subdivision G 4 is given to that person."`

**Separate, bounded, gaming note (see also F16).** Deeming the omission a *determination* puts it inside M 2's tolling sentence, so a member can toll her own final qualifying retirement date by filing a reconsideration request pointing at a trivial certification omission. This is bounded — M 2 caps `"the aggregate of all tolling under this subsection"` at 120 days and denies tolling to any second request on the same determination — so I rate it moderate, not serious. But note that F9's recommended move to the *failure* limb does not remove it, because the failure limb is also inside the tolling sentence; capping it is the 120-day aggregate's job and the aggregate does that work.

#### F10 — E1 and E6 · both hard-code § 51.1-162, and the purchase program is not confined to Chapter 1 members

`"the Board shall determine the benefit payable under this subdivision under § 51.1-162 as on the death of a member in service"` (E1) and, in E6, all six propositions, every one of which is framed in terms of § 51.1-162.

**Authority.** § 51.1-163 itself proves the point: it directs payment *"in the same manner as provided in subsection A of § 51.1-162, **subsection A of § 51.1-207, or subsection A of § 51.1-218**, in accordance with the retirement plan covering such member."* The pre-/post-retirement death scheme has three parallel tracks. § 51.1-200 provides that *"the provisions of Chapter 1 (§ 51.1-124.1 et seq.) of this title shall apply to and govern the operation of the State Police Officers' Retirement System,"* subject to *"Except as otherwise provided"* — so § 51.1-142.2's purchase right reaches a SPORS member, while Chapter 2's own death provisions displace § 51.1-162 for her. Chapter 2.1 (VaLORS) and Chapter 3 (JRS) raise the same question. § 51.1-142.2's own eligibility words are the unqualified `"Any member in service."`

Subsection L of the act excludes *teachers and employees of political subdivisions* — it does **not** exclude SPORS, VaLORS or JRS members. So the drafter has addressed the Dillon's Rule / local-cost question (see Null N8) and left the sister-system question open.

**Concrete failure scenario.** A State Police officer purchases three years of prior active-duty credit under the act, notifies the Board of a 1 July effective date, and dies in service on 10 June. Under current law § 51.1-165 C 1 routes her death to § 51.1-163, which routes it to **subsection A of § 51.1-207** — her plan's provision. E1 displaces C 1 and commands the Board to determine the benefit `"under § 51.1-162,"` a section that Chapter 2 displaces for her. The Board must either apply § 51.1-162 to a member it does not govern, or apply § 51.1-207 in the teeth of an express statutory command to apply § 51.1-162. Either way her survivor's benefit is determined by the Board's choice rather than by the act, and the certification she was given under E6 described a section that never governed her.

**Fix direction.** Generalise in both places: `"under § 51.1-162, § 51.1-207, or § 51.1-218, as applicable to the retirement plan covering the member, as on the death of a member in service"` — and in E6, frame the propositions as `"the provisions of this title governing the benefit payable on the death of a member in service under the retirement plan covering the member."` **Hand-off:** Statutory Researcher should confirm the complete list of sister-system death-benefit sections and whether Chapters 2, 2.1 and 3 displace § 51.1-162 by their own force or only by § 51.1-200's "except as otherwise provided." If the Lab's intent is that the program be confined to Chapter 1 members, that is a one-sentence addition to subsection L and is the cheaper fix.

#### F11 — E1 · the benefit change rides outside every cost control in the act

**Mark for the memo's risk section.** This is also the fiscal assumption challenge; see §4.

Both of E1's sentences change a **benefit payable from the retirement system**. Every cost control in § 51.1-142.4 is addressed to the **certified subsidy**:
- subdivision D 2: the Board shall not reserve where the total `"would exceed $3,000,000"`, and shall not accept an election whose certified subsidy `"exceeds $170,000"`;
- subsection K: the Board accepts no election for a fiscal year for which the General Assembly does not appropriate the amount the Board certified as necessary to fund `"the certified subsidies the Board expects to reserve in that fiscal year and to administer this section"`;
- subsection N: `"Upon the settlement of a certified subsidy under subdivision G 1 or G 4, the Commonwealth shall pay **the amount settled** to the retirement system from an appropriation made for that purpose."*

A death benefit determined under E1 is not a certified subsidy and is not an "amount settled." **It is not charged against the $3,000,000 limitation, is not capped at $170,000, is not within the subsection K kill-switch, and is not funded by subsection N.** It falls on the trust fund, and through the fund on the employer contribution rate.

In the narrow C 2 case this is small (see §4). Coupled with **F1**, it is not small at all, because F1's class is not "deaths in a 60-to-90-day window" but "every electing member who retires on a basic allowance and dies in retirement" — which is, eventually, most of the cohort.

**Fix direction.** Fixing F1 removes the large exposure. For the residue, say so expressly rather than leaving it to inference: add to subsection N `"Nothing in this section charges a benefit payable under § 51.1-162 against the limitations in subdivision D 2,"` and tell the Fiscal & Impact Modeler to carry the benefit delta as a separate, uncapped line rather than inside the subsidy. **Do not** let the fiscal note present the $3,000,000 cap as the act's maximum exposure while E1 stands as written.

### MODERATE

#### F12 — E3b · `"to each person entitled"` now squarely requires notice to a minor child, and runs her clock from it

E3b's change from `"the person entitled"` to `"each person entitled to any benefit payable on the member's death"` is correct and necessary (see Null N4). But § 51.1-162 B and C both make a **minor child** a person entitled to a benefit, so the notice is now unambiguously required to be given *to the minor*, and the sentence ends: `"the period within which such a person may seek review under subsection M of a determination stated in that notice runs from the date the notice is given to that person."` Subsection A defines giving as mailing to the person or electronic transmission to an address `"that person has provided to the Board."* A four-year-old provides no address and files no reconsideration request; her 30-day M 2 period runs from a letter addressed to her.

**Failure scenario.** Member dies in service survived by a seven-year-old daughter and no spouse. The Board computes the § 51.1-162 B allowance on the wrong average final compensation. The notice is mailed to the child at the member's last address. Thirty days pass. The guardian, appointed four months later, requests reconsideration; the Board answers that the period ran from the date the notice was given to the person entitled, and expired.

**Fix.** `"to each person entitled to any benefit payable on the member's death or, where that person is a minor or is under a disability, to that person's guardian, conservator, committee, or other fiduciary and, where there is none, to the person to whom the Board pays or would pay the benefit"` — and make the review period run from the date the notice is given to the person entitled to act.

#### F13 — E3c · `"that date"` has two antecedents

`"... after the earlier of the date on which it credits the service credit and the last day on which this subdivision requires it to be credited **or, as to a person whose entitlement or non-entitlement ... the Board determines after that date**, no later than 30 days after that determination"`

`"That date"` can refer to the earlier-of composite or, on nearest-antecedent reading, to `"the last day on which this subdivision requires it to be credited"` (day 60). The two readings diverge whenever the Board credits early.

**Failure scenario.** Board learns of the death on 1 April, credits on 10 April (earlier-of = 10 April; notice due 10 May), and determines on 5 May that a claimant is not entitled. Composite reading: the determination is after 10 April, so the rider applies and that claimant's notice is due 4 June. Nearest-antecedent reading: the determination is *before* 31 May (day 60), so the rider does not apply and her notice was due 10 May — five days after the Board first knew her status. The Board that reads it the second way is late and must self-report under J; the Board that reads it the first way may be late as to a claimant who had a reviewable determination made about her.

**Fix.** `"after the date determined under the preceding clause"` in place of `"that date."`

#### F14 — E3c · the rider covers *entitlement* but not *amount*

The rider extends the notice period only `"as to a person whose **entitlement or non-entitlement** to any benefit payable on the member's death the Board determines after that date."* But the notice must also state `"the benefit payable to the member's survivor or beneficiary **and of the computation of it**."* Where entitlement is clear and the **amount** is not, the Board must state a figure it does not have.

**Failure scenario.** Member dies 1 March of a suspected work-related cause, survived by her husband. Entitlement is not in doubt — he is the surviving spouse either way. Whether he takes the § 51.1-162 **B** allowance or the richer § 51.1-162 **C** allowance depends on an award under the Workers' Compensation Act that the Commission does not make until October. The Board credits on 10 March; the notice is due 9 April and must state "the benefit payable ... and the computation of it." The Board must either state a B figure that October will supersede — and start his 30-day M 2 clock running against a determination it is about to change — or miss the deadline and self-report under subsection J.

**Fix.** Extend the rider to the amount, and require a supplemental notice with a fresh review period: `"or, as to a person whose entitlement or non-entitlement to any benefit payable on the member's death, or the amount of any benefit payable to that person, the Board determines after that date, no later than 30 days after that determination; and where the Board after giving the notice determines the amount of a benefit differently, it shall give that person a further notice stating the determination and the reasons for it no later than 30 days after making it, and the period within which that person may seek review under subsection M of that determination runs from the date that further notice is given."`

#### F15 — E1 · `"taking account of"` leaves the computation base undefined

`"computed taking account of the service credit credited under this subdivision"`

E3's predecessor — `"computed **on** the service credit credited under this subdivision"` — was worse, and the change is an improvement (see Null N3 on the G 5 case, where `"taking account of"` is what makes the sentence operable). But `"taking account of X"` is a direction not to ignore X. It is not a statement of the base. It does not say that the base is the member's **total creditable service**, and § 51.1-162 B and C compute on creditable service and average final compensation. A Board could satisfy `"taking account of"` by any computation that gives the purchased credit some weight.

**Failure scenario.** Member with 26 years of membership service purchases 3 years. She dies in the window. The Board computes the § 51.1-162 B allowance on 29 years — correct. Four years later, under budget pressure, the Board's procedures under M 1 compute instead on 26 years plus a flat actuarial addition for the 3, producing less. Both computations "take account of" the purchased credit. Nothing in the act says which is right, and M 1 expressly gives the Board the procedures power.

**Fix.** `"computed on the member's creditable service, including the service credit credited under this subdivision."*

#### F16 — E6 · the deemed determination is a tolling lever (bounded)

Covered as the gaming note at F9. Rated moderate because M 2 caps `"the aggregate of all tolling under this subsection"` at 120 days and denies tolling to a second request on the same determination. **Null-adjacent conclusion: the threshold is gamed, but the gaming is capped, and the cap holds.** A member who wants more time to complete payment can buy up to 120 days by pointing at a trivial certification omission; she cannot buy more. No fix recommended — the cost of closing it exceeds the exposure.

#### F17 — E3a · the notice duty still conflates two different rights held by two different people

`"of any right of the person entitled to any benefit payable on the member's death to waive that benefit or to elect another benefit in its place under § 51.1-162 and, where § 51.1-162 states a period within which the waiver or election must be made, of that period"`

E3a's conditional is sound and necessary (Null N2). What it does not fix is that **"the waiver or election" is two rights with different holders and different timing:**
- the **waiver** is § 51.1-162 **C**'s, belongs only to a C allowance recipient, and has a **90-day** period;
- the **election** is § 51.1-162 **B**'s — *"If the person elects in writing, the amount of the member's accumulated contributions or lump sum payment shall be paid to him exclusively, in lieu of any other benefits under this section"* — belongs to a B recipient, and has **no** period.

So the singular `"that period"` is correct for C and inapplicable to B, and the notice as drafted invites the Board to state one period for both or neither. Combined with F7, the risk is that the only period that matters is the one most likely to be mis-stated.

**Fix.** Split them: `"of any right of that person under subsection C of § 51.1-162 to waive the allowance and of the period stated in that subsection within which the waiver must be made, and of any right of that person under subsection B of § 51.1-162 to elect to be paid the member's accumulated contributions or a lump sum in lieu of any other benefit under that section."`

#### F18 — E4 · singular `"the period"` after E3c made the period person-specific

`"shall state in the report required by subsection J every failure to give the notice required by the preceding sentence within **the period** this subdivision allows for it."* E3c made the preceding sentence allow **several** periods — the primary earlier-of period, plus a distinct period as to each person whose entitlement the Board determines late. #### F19 — E7/E8 · a state-agency employer is now in the reconsideration and Article 5 classes

#### F20 — E7/E8 · the two money-committee Chairmen are in the new class

Four express duties give `"written notice ... to the Chairmen of the House Committee on Appropriations and the Senate Committee on Finance and Appropriations"* (D 3's lapse notice, D 5's waiting-list determination notice, G 4's reinstatement notice, and the subsection J report). Two of those notices are expressly anonymised (`"which notice shall not identify any member"`). They are information-only notices to legislators, and the Chairmen are now, textually, within both the reconsideration class and the Article 5 class. The `"adversely affected"` / `"aggrieved"` gates will defeat any actual claim, so this is nuisance rather than exposure — but it is precisely the "notice for information only" case the Director asked about, and it is the second reason (after F8's subsection K point) to narrow the formula rather than defend it.

#### F21 — E7/E8 · asymmetry with M 2's own tolling reference

The new class is confined to notices required by `"this section."* M 2's tolling sentence, by contrast, reaches periods `"under this section or under subdivision B 4 f of § 51.1-142.2,"* and D 1 requires the certification to state `"the matters required by subdivision B 4 c of § 51.1-142.2."* A person entitled to a notice required by § 51.1-142.2 rather than by § 51.1-142.4 is outside the new class though the subsection reaches across to § 51.1-142.2 for other purposes. Low consequence; flagged for consistency only.

## 4. Fiscal assumption challenge

**The load-bearing assumption.** Not a number in `model-s8-s9` — the structural assumption underneath it: **that every cost of this act flows through the certified subsidy, and is therefore capped at $3,000,000 in the aggregate and $170,000 per election, switched off by subsection K if unappropriated, and funded by subsection N.** That is what makes a $758,048 fiscal note credible for a program that touches death benefits.

**The test.** Does any of E1's operative effect fall outside the certified subsidy? Read D 2, K and N against E1. D 2 caps *reservations of certified subsidies*. K conditions acceptance on an appropriation for *"the certified subsidies the Board expects to reserve."* N funds *"the amount settled."* E1 changes a **benefit payable from the retirement system** — not a subsidy, not an amount settled.

**The result: the assumption is wrong, and the exposure is unbounded by the act's own caps.** Two tranches.

*Tranche 1 — the C 2 greater-of (E1 sentence 2). De minimis; I could not break the fiscal case on it.* The class is electing members who (a) die after notification and before the effective date, (b) die in service, (c) had a joint-and-last-survivor election, and (d) named a spouse, minor child or parent. The window is the gap between notification and effective date — order 60–90 days. Scoping on a cohort of N electing retirements a year, mortality at ages 60–62 of roughly 0.6%, a 75-day average window and perhaps 40% of electors holding a qualifying J&S election: expected cases ≈ N × 0.0005. Even at N = 600 that is **about one case every three years**, and the per-case delta between a contributions refund and a § 51.1-162 B allowance is order $50k–$150k. **Expected annual cost well inside model noise on a $758,048 program. No change to the conclusion.** The C 2 preservation is a sound policy choice on the numbers; its problems are F2, F3 and F6, which are drafting, not cost.

*Tranche 2 — F1. This one breaks the conclusion.* F1's class is not a 75-day window. It is **every electing member who retires in a qualifying retirement on a basic allowance and then dies in retirement** — i.e. eventually most of the cohort. For each, E1 as written replaces the § 51.1-163 unrecovered-contributions benefit with a § 51.1-162 in-service death benefit, in-service requirement disapplied. The delta per member is large in **both** directions and both are costly to the Commonwealth's position:
- survived by a spouse/minor child/parent → a **lifetime survivor allowance** in place of a one-time refund (cost);
- survived by no one in that class → **nothing at all**, where § 51.1-163 would have paid the unrecovered contributions (litigation and, on any realistic reading, legislative repair).

**Scoping arithmetic — mine, not the model's, and explicitly an order-of-magnitude bound rather than a price.** Inputs stated so they can be challenged: cohort 3,000 members over the program's life (the $3,000,000 aggregate cap divided by an assumed $1,000 average certified subsidy is an upper bound on cohort size; at a $5,000 average it is 600, at $25,000 it is 120 — **I did not verify the model's average subsidy and the result scales linearly with it**); 60% dying with a surviving spouse, minor child or parent; average present-value delta between a § 51.1-162 B survivor allowance and the § 51.1-163 refund of $50,000. That yields an undiscounted lifetime exposure of **$90M at a 3,000 cohort, $18M at 600, $3.6M at 120.** Against a priced program of $758,048, **even the smallest of those is more than four times the entire fiscal note, and none of it is charged against the $3,000,000 cap or funded by subsection N.**

**What the answer becomes if the assumption is wrong:** the act's stated maximum exposure stops being $3,000,000 and becomes unbounded. **What it becomes if F1 is fixed:** the assumption holds, tranche 2 vanishes, tranche 1 stays inside noise, and the $758,048 note is defensible. **The whole of the fiscal challenge therefore resolves to a single clause** — the missing window in E1's first limb.

**Hand-off to Fiscal & Impact Modeler.** I did not re-run `model-s8-s9`; the eight substitutions change no input to it, and that is the point — the exposure sits outside the model's boundary, not inside it with a wrong value. Two asks: (1) confirm the average certified subsidy so the cohort size above can be replaced with a real number; (2) carry the E1 benefit delta as a separate uncapped line, and do not let the fiscal note present the $3,000,000 cap as the act's maximum exposure while E1 stands as written.

## 5. Null results — attacked and could not break

These are reported as nulls deliberately. Each was a real attempt.

**N1 — There is no unnamed third provision of Chapter 1 routing this death to § 51.1-163.** I expected to find one and did not. The reason is structural, and it is the most useful thing I learned: **§ 51.1-163 cannot reach a pre-effective-date death of its own force at all**, because it opens *"If a member dies after the effective date of retirement."* It reaches one only by deeming, and there are exactly three deeming provisions — § 51.1-165 C 1, § 51.1-165 C 2, and § 51.1-165.01 F. Two are named in E1's notwithstanding; the third is C 2, which E1 deliberately preserves. **The Director's two passes between them have found all three.** The residual risk is not a missing Chapter 1 provision but a missing *plan* — see F10 on §§ 51.1-207 and 51.1-218.

**N2 — E3a's conditional is sound and is doing real work.** `"where § 51.1-162 states a period within which the waiver or election must be made"` was the right shape: § 51.1-162 **C**'s waiver states a period (90 days from death), § 51.1-162 **B**'s election states none. Without the conditional, the Board would be compelled to state a period that does not exist for the B election. **Holds.** (The residue is F17's conflation of the two rights and F7's timing, not the conditional itself.)

**N3 — E1's `"taking account of"` keeps the G 5 cancellation case operable, and the Director's refusal to confine (f) to uncancelled elections was right.** Member cancels under G 5 and dies a month later. Clause (i) still applies — she died after acceptance, and the only carve-out is for non-qualifying retirees. But the crediting was done by G 5, so **`"the service credit credited under this subdivision"` is a null set.** Under the BEFORE wording, `"computed **on**"` a null set computes to zero and the sentence would have been either inoperable or catastrophic. `"Taking account of"` a null set simply leaves § 51.1-162 to compute on her ordinary creditable service. **The sentence remains operable and gives the right answer.** The alternative — confining (f) to uncancelled elections — would indeed have pushed her back out to § 51.1-165 C 1 and a refund, which is the result MOO-154's F1 condemned. **The Director's judgment on this point survives the attack.** (F15's base-definition point stands separately and is moderate.)

**N4 — E3b's `"each person entitled"` is correct and necessary.** § 51.1-162 B's recipients can be plural (minor children), and C's can be plural. `"The person entitled"` would have invited a Board to notify one of several. **Holds.** (F12's minor-child problem is a consequence of getting the plural right, not a reason to undo it.)

**N5 — E5's added `"under that section"` is accurate and improves the clause.** I initially scored this as a defect and was wrong. § 51.1-162 B's election clause reads *"in lieu of any other benefits under **this** section"*; E5's `"a lump sum in lieu of any other benefit **under that section**"` tracks it exactly, and the addition removes a real ambiguity — without it, `"a lump sum in lieu of any other benefit"` could have been read to pick up a § 51.1-165.01 lump sum, which is void in this window. **Holds, and the added words are the right added words.**

**N6 — E1's in-service disapplication does reach every place in § 51.1-162 where being in service matters, including subsection C.** § 51.1-162 **B** requires *"a member dies in service"*; § 51.1-162 **C** opens *"If a member dies in service from a cause compensable under the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act."* Both are gated on the same words, and `"for the purposes of § 51.1-162 the requirement that the member be in service shall not apply"` reaches both. **Holds.** Two notes, neither a defect: (a) the disapplication is expressly confined `"for the purposes of § 51.1-162,"` so it has **no** effect outside that section — including none on § 51.1-165 C 2's condition (ii), which is correct and is why F6's point 2 matters; (b) the act's disapplication is accordingly **broader** than the Code's existing ones in C 1 and F, which disapply in-service only in aid of a § 51.1-163 routing. That breadth is what makes subsection C available to a member who had already separated, which is a benefit expansion — deliberate, defensible, and small enough to live inside tranche 1 of §4.

**N7 — E2 holds.** `"and shall state in the report required by subsection J every failure to credit it within that period"` — `"that period"` has one unambiguous antecedent (`"60 days after it learns of the member's death"`), and subsection J exists and requires an annual report. Worth knowing but not a defect: J's report is due **1 December each year**, so a January failure surfaces up to eleven months later. The J hook is an **oversight mechanism, not a remedy**; the remedy is M 2, and per the F13(b) answer below M 2 reaches it.

**N8 — the single-object and Dillon's Rule lenses are clean on these eight edits.** All eight amend one program in one title on one subject; no single-object problem under Va. Const. art. IV, § 12. No local duty is created — subsection L provides that *"Nothing in this section imposes any duty or cost upon a county, city, or town, upon any other political subdivision of the Commonwealth, upon a local school board, or upon any local retirement system,"* and excludes teachers and employees of political subdivisions from eligibility, so no Dillon's Rule express-grant question arises. No taking, no compelled speech of a private party (E6 compels a **state agency**, not a person, to state propositions of law — the defect there is accuracy, F4 and F5, not the First Amendment), no retroactivity, and no delegation-without-standards problem in these eight: M 1 confines the Board's procedures power to enumerated subjects. **I looked for a constitutional tripwire in E1–E8 and did not find one.**

**N9 — E6's new anchor `"The Board's certification"` is an improvement and is properly anchored.** The BEFORE text's `"the writing required by this subdivision"` was genuinely ambiguous — D 1 requires at least three writings (the certification, the member's written acknowledgment of obligations, and the written statement of qualifying retirement dates). `"The Board's certification"` picks up the term already used two sentences earlier (*"The Board's certification shall state the matters required by subdivision B 4 c ..."*), and `"shall **also** state"` sequences correctly after it. **Holds.**

**N10 — E3c's `"earlier of"` cannot shorten the period in an ordinary case.** I tried to break this and could not. The two candidates are the actual crediting date and day 60. Where the Board credits early, the earlier-of picks the crediting date — which is exactly what the BEFORE text (`"30 days after it credits"`) already did. Where the Board credits late, the earlier-of picks day 60 and caps the notice at day 90, which is the intended anti-self-extension effect. **There is no combination in which the earlier-of yields a date earlier than the BEFORE text would have yielded.** The construct does what it was built to do. (F13's antecedent ambiguity and F14's amount gap are separate; F7's collision with the § 51.1-162 C waiver is a pre-existing problem that E3c tightened rather than caused.)

**N11 — the comma placement in E7/E8 works.** `"... personal representative of a deceased member's estate, or other person to whom this section requires a notice to be given, adversely affected by a determination ..."` — the trailing comma keeps `"adversely affected by a determination"` as a qualifier on the whole list rather than on the last item only, matching the BEFORE text's structure. Same in the Article 5 limb with `"aggrieved."` **Holds** — and those two gates are the only thing containing F8.

**N12 — `"subdivision C 2"` is never ambiguous in the enacted text.** § 51.1-165 and § 51.1-165.01 **each** have a subdivision C 2 (§ 51.1-165.01 C 2 is the 24×-monthly partial lump sum). I checked every occurrence of `"C 2"` in the enacted text: all of them carry the section number (`"subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165"`). **Holds** — but it is a live trap for the next edit, and worth a standing note to Drafting Specialist: in this bill, never write `"subdivision C 2"` without the section.

## 6. The F13(b) question — the Director's reading is right, with one qualification

The Director asked me to say if his reading is wrong. **It is not wrong.** M 2's second sentence provides that *"every determination, certification, redetermination, recertification, reduction, cancellation, rescission, restoration, **crediting of service credit**, placement on a waiting list, expiration of a reservation, denial of an election, and refusal to accept an election under this section is a determination of the Board for the purposes of this subsection, whether or not another provision of this section expressly provides for review of it."* So a crediting is a determination, and a **late** crediting is reachable two ways:
- **the failure limb** — crediting is a deemed determination, G 4 requires it within 60 days, so a failure to credit within 60 days is `"a failure of the Board to make a determination ... within a period required by this section,"* reviewable `"at any time before the Board makes the determination"`; and
- **the determination limb** — once credited, the crediting is a determination of which the G 4 notice is given, reviewable within 30 days of that notice.

**The qualification.** The failure limb's window closes the moment the Board cures: `"at any time **before** the Board makes the determination."* So a member whose credit was made on day 90 has no failure-limb remedy from day 90 onward, and must use the determination limb within 30 days of the G 4 notice — where what is reviewable is the **crediting**, not its lateness. **Lateness as such ceases to be independently reviewable the instant it is cured.** That is a narrow gap, it does not disturb the Director's conclusion that a late crediting is already reviewable, and I am not re-reporting F13(b). If the Director wants lateness itself reviewable after cure, the words are: in M 2's first sentence, `"or, in the case of such a failure, at any time before the Board makes the determination or gives the notice **or, where the Board has made the determination or given the notice late, within 30 days after it does so**."`
