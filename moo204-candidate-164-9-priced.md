## 2. The movement in days

The model reproduces every published day figure from the register before it computes
anything — fourteen locks, all passing, as a gate (§0 of the transcript).

### 2.1 The endpoint, on the reconsideration class

| Timing | F | D | notice delay | base | MOO-164 cut | candidate 164-9 | 164-9 − cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **prompt** — acts, decides and gives notice the same day | 3 | 3 | 0 | 33 | 33 | 33 | **0** |
| **MOO-141's worked facts** — token act day 7, decision day 85 | 7 | 85 | 0 | 37 | 55 | 115 | **+60** |
| **worst lawful** — token act day 0, decision on the last allowed day, notice at the backstop | 0 | 90 | 18 | 30 | 48 | 120 *(clips branch)* / 138 *(aggregate-only)* | **+72 / +90** |

**18 + 60 = card (f)'s 78, and the model checks that identity rather than repeating
it.** The cut repairs 18 days of the 78; candidate 164-9 closes the remaining 60.

**Two things on that table that matter more than the 60.**

- **In the prompt case the movement is exactly zero, on both clause (ii) branches and
  for both classes of request.** A Board that decides and tells her costs nothing under
  candidate 164-9. That is the same asymmetry MOO-164 §2.2 recommended Edit 164-1 on,
  carried sixty days further, and it is the single most favourable fact in this issue.
- **The definition class is structurally smaller.** M 2's tolling sentence covers two
  classes, and 164-9's cap keys to *"the period allowed by this section for acting upon
  the request"* — 90 days for a reconsideration request, 30 for a definition request
  (MOO-164 §2.4). So for the definition class the cap is 30 + 48 = **day 78**, not 138,
  the movement is at most 30 days, and the retention always covers it.

### 2.2 The one construction question, reported on both branches

Whether M 2 clause (ii)'s 120-day aggregate clips a **single** request's endpoint — the
branch MOO-164 §2.2 states, *"clause (ii)'s 120-day aggregate still binds on top of
it"* — or bounds only the sum across requests, **is Statutory Researcher's and I have not
decided it.** Both branches are carried everywhere they matter.

**They agree in the central case** (day 115 either way, because 115 is inside 120) and
**they agree in the dollars**. They diverge only at the outer bound, 120 against 138, and
that divergence decides one thing: §4's coverage margin.

## 3. MOO-140's C2 — the movement, and whether the answer changes

### 3.1 The answer to your question, in the form you put it

**It changes C2's figure in the central case. It does not change C2's answer. And it does
not change C2's published figure either.**

The route is MOO-164 §2.2's and I did not re-derive it: a longer tolling lengthens *"the
period allowed to the member by subsection M within which to request reconsideration"*,
G 2's notice floor is keyed to that period, so the determination takes effect later — and
**no retirement allowance is paid until the determination takes effect.**

| | cut | candidate 164-9 | movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| prompt timing | 33 days | 33 days | **+0** |
| MOO-141's worked facts | 55 days | 115 days | **+60** |
| worst lawful timing | 48 days | 120 days | **+72** |
| **maximum, scanned over every lawful timing and both branches** | | | **+72 days** |

**The clip that bounds it, and this is why the bound does not move.** G 4 caps the
non-running of G 2's clocks by reason of M 2 requests at **an aggregate of 120 days**
(MOO-140 §2.3(b)). The tolling can therefore only lengthen the C2 window up to that
ceiling, whatever M 2's own endpoint does. On the aggregate-only branch 164-9's endpoint
reaches day 138 — and **the last 18 of those days cannot lengthen the C2 window at all**,
because G 4 has already stopped counting.

### 3.2 The 300-day bound does not move, and the reason is arithmetic rather than judgment

The card (h) outer bound on the zero-allowance window, as corrected at MOO-164 §2.3 on
[MOO-187](/MOO/issues/MOO-187) F5 and now **determinate** because **m8-a is enacted at
store revision 68**, is

```
60 (G 2 notice) + 30 (her own window) + 120 (clause (ii)'s aggregate) + 90 (m8-a's cap on the exclusion) = 300 days
```

**Candidate 164-9 touches no term in it.** It does not change G 2's notice period, her own
window, clause (ii)'s aggregate, or m8-a's cap. The third term **is already at its ceiling
in the bound**, so moving a single request's endpoint *toward* that ceiling cannot raise
the sum. **The bound stays at 300 days.**

### 3.3 Nothing in MOO-140 has to be re-cut, and the binding rule is still satisfied

MOO-140 §4's memo-ready C2 text already states the limb **at its cap**: *"a
reconsideration request of her own stops every one of these clocks for up to a further
120 days, with no allowance while it runs."* Candidate 164-9 takes the single-request
realisation from **55 days to 115 days of a figure already published at 120.**

**So no artifact changes.** And MOO-140 §2.4's binding drafting rule — the figure may
appear only welded to *"where the Board meets each of its own deadlines"* in the same
sentence — is **untouched and still satisfied**, because 164-9 adds no case in which the
figure could appear unconditioned.

**One thing I will not pretend.** The +60 days arises in a case where the Board **does**
meet its own deadlines: on MOO-141's facts it acts on day 7 and decides on day 85, inside
the 90. So this is not movement hiding in the Board's lateness; it is movement in the
compliant case, and it is already inside the published 120.

**This is the question nobody has asked, and it is the thing I would want a counsel pass

out. Scanned over every first-action day from 0 to 120, at the worst decision and notice

| Class / branch | margin on the worked facts | worst margin over the scan |
|---|---|---|
| reconsideration, clause (ii) clips | cut **+72** → 164-9 **+12** | cut **+72** → 164-9 **0** |

## 6. The chain — does candidate 164-9 lengthen any link?

**No. And this was F-6's first named trigger, so it is answered three ways rather than
one.**

**(a) Structurally.** MOO-170's model is imported unchanged. Its inputs are
`RETENTION_DAYS_PER_REQUEST`, `CHAIN_IS_UNCAPPED`, `CHAIN_LINK_DAYS`, and MOO-163's
first-action and decision bands. **Every one is a retention quantity, a Board-timing
quantity or a chain-geometry quantity. M 2's tolling endpoint is not among them**, so no
value of it can change any output of that model. The chain's links are **retention
periods** measured from a Board action; candidate 164-9 is five words inside the
**tolling** sentence's cap.

**(b) Numerically.** MOO-170's sweep is re-run at the board's 120-day retention and at
**five link lengths — 33, 40, 120, and candidate 164-9's own 115 and 138** — on a shared
draw set, 20,000 draws, seed 20261012, on the high cell, which is the only cell where
order decides anything. **Every saturated cell is identical across all five.** If the days
dimension carried any fiscal warrant, they would differ.

**The reproduction check that makes this load-bearing:** the same re-run gives
**2.800 mean and 11 in the worst draw** on the `skip_ahead` / `removed` cell, against
MOO-179 §2.2's published *"2.80 members mean ... with 11 members in the worst draw"*, and
`back_of_queue_gated` **flat at 0.00**. **Reproduced exactly.** So the sweep I am drawing
the invariance conclusion from is the sweep the board's figure was decided on.

**(c) The number of requests, which is the live dimension.** Pass 18's F-6, as recorded in
MOO-170's register, finds M 2's **tolling** sentence carries *"(i) no second or subsequent
request … [tolls]"* while the **retention** sentence carries neither limit. So a second
request **never tolls in the first place**, and her ability to file one does not depend on
a tolled period being live. **A longer tolling endpoint cannot buy her another request.
The number of requests is unreachable from candidate 164-9.**

**And one claim I am not making, because my own model falsifies it.** It is **not** true
that the chain is already saturated at a single 120-day retention: at k = 1 the
displacement in this re-run is **0.339 members**, rising to **2.800** at the unbounded
limit. The chain still does work at the board's figure. **The answer above rests on the
dimension argument, not on a saturation argument** — a saturation argument would have been
wrong, and it would have been the easy thing to write.

**So F-6's first named trigger does not fire, and MOO-179's decline of F-6's cap stands
without re-reading.**

## 11. Sensitivity

**No dollar driver passes the 10%-move / 25%-swing test.** Each moved 10% from the
central row, one at a time:

| Driver, +10% | per member | swing |
|---|---|---|
| central row | $184 | — |
| monthly allowance | $203 | +10.0% |
| member discount rate | $201 | +9.3% |
| the movement itself | $205 | +11.2% |
| the base window | $200 | +8.8% |

The member-side figure is close to linear in all four, and a 10% move in a linear driver
moves the answer 10%. **On the aggregate rather than the per-member figure, a 10% move in
the count moves it exactly 10%, and the count is the input with no source — which is why
the aggregate is given at two significant figures with the ceiling row beside it.**

**The thing this answer is actually sensitive to is not a dollar input at all.** It is the
clause (ii) construction question: it decides whether the coverage margin in §4.3 bottoms
out at **0 days or at −18 days**. **The dollars are identical on both branches.** That
question is Statutory Researcher's.

**And the dominant driver of the whole family is still upstream and unchanged.** MOO-179
§5: *"A 10% move in the cohort count moves the answer more than a 10× move in chain
length does."* If MOO-49's clause (vi) lands **broad**, roughly 130 members, the cap binds
in every scenario and **MOO-163, MOO-170, MOO-179 and this issue reopen together.**
Candidate 164-9 does not change that and **adds no trigger of its own** — which is itself
part of why it is cheap.

## 12. External sanity check — reported, including where it is weak

**Check 1 — against the loss C2 already discloses. Agreement.** MOO-140 §4 states that
her first payment *"is made whole from her effective date of retirement, but it carries
no interest for the months she waited."* The window she already waits, on the cut and at
the central timing, is **175 days**, and the time value of that wait on the same formula
is **$209**. Candidate 164-9 adds **$184** — **+88% of a loss already in the memo-ready
text.** That is the right order for a +60-day movement on a 175-day window.
**Caveat stated: this is an internal consistency check, not an external one.** It
validates that the increment is the right size relative to the disclosed base; it cannot
validate the base.

**Check 2 — against the test Virginia actually applies to a VRS bill. Agreement.** A bill
related to the Virginia Retirement System carries a VRS Board impact statement under
**§ 30-19.1:7**, and the test that statement applies is *"the financial impact of the
proposed bill upon the general fund and the various local governments"* that participate,
the policy implications for the systems administered by the Board, and the impact on local
government independent retirement systems. Candidate 164-9 changes the date on which a
determination takes effect, and therefore the date of a first payment the chapter already
requires to be made whole. It creates no new benefit, no new duty, no new position and no
new system. **On the test § 30-19.1:7 sets, the answer is no impact on the general fund
and no impact on a participating locality. The convention agrees with the three $0 state
lines.**
*(law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title30/chapter1/section30-19.1:7/, retrieved 12 October
2026; last amended 2010, c. 410.)*

**Where check 2 is weak, stated rather than buried:** it is a **convention** check, not a
quantitative one. It validates the three $0 state lines and the no-mandate finding.
**It cannot validate the member-side time value**, because a Virginia fiscal impact
statement does not price a member's forgone interest at all — the quantity has no home in
the instrument.

**Check 3 — no external count anchor exists, and that is unchanged since MOO-163.**
MOO-71 §3 establishes that no published count of § 51.1-142.2 military purchases exists.
There is therefore **no** other state's reported cost to scale by population and **no**
prior Virginia fiscal impact statement for a comparable bill. **I am not manufacturing
one.** The aggregate is reported with its ceiling row beside it so a reader can see what
the arithmetic does without the judgment.

## 13. Administrative cost, biennium timing, unfunded local mandate

**Administrative cost: $0 ongoing, $0 one-time, 0 positions, 0 systems — conditionally.**

Candidate 164-9 is five words into an existing clause and **introduces no concept**: *"the
period allowed by this section for acting upon the request"* is the formula Edits 164-3,
164-4 and 164-6 already introduce, so the Board is already being asked to compute it. It
states no new duty, requires no new notice, and changes no record the Board does not
already keep. **There is nothing to build.**

**The condition, and it is a gate rather than a footnote.** 164-9's cap is measured from
the **expiration** of the period allowed for acting. At the enacted base, M 2's 90-day
period is the **only one of the section's five *"shall act upon"* duties with no stated
commencement** (MOO-164 §15.4, candidate 164-10). Without a commencement the expiration
date is not computable from the text and the Board would have to pick one case by case.

- **Candidate 164-10 supplies it** — *"within 90 days after the request is filed"*, +27
  characters — and the Policy Director's disposition §10 **takes it**.
- **So 164-9's $0 administrative line holds if 164-10 is applied and does not hold if it
  is not.** Without 164-10, 164-9 converts a determinate 48-day cap into a per-case
  interpretation question, and that is an administrative cost I have not priced because
  there is no reason to incur it.
- **This is an ordering constraint on the cut, not a cost: apply 164-10 with 164-9, or
  apply neither.**

**Biennium timing.** Virginia budgets in two-year cycles and a July 1 effective date
splits a programme year. Candidate 164-9 moves a first payment later by up to 60 days at
the central timing, so for a member whose window straddles 30 June the cash falls in the
following fiscal year. **That is a cash shift, not a cost** — the amount is unchanged and
the chapter requires it in full. **Magnitude at the central estimate: under $1,800 of
nongeneral-fund cash over the whole 48-month pilot.** No general fund line carries it,
because a retirement allowance is paid from the trust.

**Unfunded local mandate: no, and the finding is express rather than inferred.**
Subsection L of the section **disclaims any duty or cost upon a locality** — that is what
subsection L is, as corrected at MOO-140 §0.1 item 1. Candidate 164-9 imposes no duty on
any locality, changes no local contribution rate, and changes no date on which a locality
must do anything. **Δ local = $0 in every fiscal year, in every scenario, as an
identity.** For the § 30-19.1:7 statement the sentence is: no impact on the general fund,
no impact on a participating locality, and no impact on a local government independent
retirement system.

## 14. The recommendation

**Take candidate 164-9, and take it with candidate 164-10.**

**Why, in the Director's own terms.** It is five words. It adds no concept. It costs the
Commonwealth nothing in any year, costs a locality nothing, costs the trust nothing in
cost and under $1,800 in cash across four years, and costs the member under two hundred
dollars of forgone interest at the central estimate. **It moves no figure in MOO-140,
MOO-163, MOO-170, MOO-177, MOO-179 or MOO-180 that has to be re-cut.** It does not move
the 300-day card (h) bound. It does not lengthen a link in the chain and does not fire
F-6's trigger. And in the case where the Board decides and tells her, **it costs nothing
at all.** It would make MOO-164 §0.1's original claim true.

**Three things to carry with the decision, and the first is the only one that is new.**

1. **The coverage margin at §4.3.** Candidate 164-9 creates an interval — up to 18 days,
   on one construction branch, in the late-notice case — in which her periods to act are
   still tolled after her retained filing date has lapsed. **$0, and a coherence defect.**
   Route it to whoever next opens Edit 3's retention sentence, and to counsel. **Do not
   fix it in this cut:** the 120 days is a board figure.
2. **The 164-10 gate.** Apply 164-10 with 164-9, or apply neither.
3. **The clause (ii) construction question** at §2.2 goes to Statutory Researcher. It
   does not change a dollar and it does change §4.3's worst case from 0 days to −18.

**What this does not decide.** Whether the 60 days is worth having is a policy call and I
have priced it, not taken it. MOO-164's two decisions are not reopened, route (c),
candidate 164-7 and card (c) are untouched, and **nothing here is applied to the store.**
**This does not block MOO-164's revision 6.**
