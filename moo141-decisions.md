## 1. What I accept without change

**The call at §0.2.** G 4's *"first acts upon it"*, M 2's *"acts upon the request"* and the definition's
*"last acts upon a request"* are three phrases for one event. Fixing the **event** once in subsection A's
final interpretation paragraph, and leaving each provision its own **endpoint**, is right. The reason is
the one I gave in [MOO-140](/MOO/issues/MOO-140) and pass 16's F-4 confirmed: **G 4 tolls the Board's
periods and M 2 tolls the member's, and they protect different parties.** A patch that collapsed them
would have had to take a side nobody has agreed to take.

**Edit 2's withdrawal, which was the best single judgment in either pass.** Striking *"first"* from one of
two parallel limbs joined by *"and"* would have armed *Forst v. Rockingham Poultry Marketing Coop.*,
222 Va. 270, 278 (1981), **inside the very subdivision the patch was drafted for**. Dropping it leaves a
patch that deletes no word of the bill. One edit beats two.

**The three withdrawals at §1.3.** *"Her extra 30 days of tolling costs her 30 days of allowance,"*
*"no endpoint moves,"* and *"M 2 needs no amendment"* are all properly withdrawn. Withdrawing a reason you
have already relied on is harder than finding a new one, and it was done three times here.

**The disagreement at §6.6 is handled correctly.** Pass 17's F-6 two-request sign flip could not be
reproduced, and the monotonicity argument — `charged(X)` and `excluded(X)` both non-decreasing, so
`min(120, ·)` of their sum is non-decreasing — is sound on its face. **More to the point, the error runs
the safe way**: if F-6 is right, the patch is *better* for the member than claimed. Stating the worse case
and carding the tie-break is the right order of operations. It goes to pass 18 as a precision question,
not a risk question.

## 2. The four decisions, with reasons

### 2.1 Card (g) — re-key the backstop to the decision

**Decision: re-key.** Draft the candidate text at §5.5 — *"no later than 15 days after **it gives its
decision upon the request**."*

**And it is a smaller call than revision 3 priced it.** Revision 3 put this as a trade worth 0 against up
to 12 months, and asked me to choose the Board's certainty over the member's extension. Read the live
sentence again:

> *Where the Board's action upon a request determines a member's final qualifying retirement date under
> this definition, the Board shall state that date in its decision upon the request, and **where the Board
> does not do so** it shall give the member written notice of that date no later than 15 days after it
> acts upon the request.*

**The duty is conditional on its own face, and the condition is the content of the decision.** Whether the
Board *"does not do so"* — does not state the date in its decision — cannot be known until the decision
issues. So the first-action reading asks the Board to perform, by day 23, a duty whose **trigger is not
yet determinable**. That is not a 12-month entitlement being taken away from the member; it is a reading
that cannot be made to work, and the subdivision D 1 lateness extension that rides on it would be a
windfall for a duty that could not have been performed.

**Two further reasons.**

- **The vocabulary is already there.** The next sentence keys the floor to *"the month in which the Board
  **gives a decision** or notice required by the preceding sentence."* The re-key borrows the definition's
  own formula instead of inventing one, which answers *Forst* and *Hubbard v. Henrico Ltd. P'ship*,
  255 Va. 335, 340 (1998), on its face.
- **An indeterminate provision is worse than either answer.** A clause whose cost to the system is
  unbounded between 0 and 12 months per member is a clause a fiscal impact statement cannot score, and an
  unscoreable retirement provision attracts an amendment from someone who is not in this room.

**What the member loses, stated plainly:** the chance that a court would read the backstop as
first-action-keyed and hand her a D 1 extension of up to 12 months. **What she keeps:** the decision-keyed
floor in the same definition, which Edit 1 cannot move and which is her real protection against a slow
Board. **This stays the one item in the memo's risk section**, as pass 17 routed it, and it should be
written up as a clarification of an unworkable reading rather than as a choice against her.

### 2.2 Card (k) — re-key the retention endpoint, (k-ii)

**Decision: (k-ii)** — *"retains its filing date for the purposes of subdivisions D 4 and D 5 until
**30 days after** the Board acts upon the request."*

**Why not (k-i), excepting the endpoint.** Preserving today's ambiguity inside the one subsection whose
ambiguity we are curing arms *Forst* **inside Edit 1 itself** — a reader asks why the rule governs three
endpoints in M 2 and not the fourth. And a queue needs certainty more than it needs length: an
indeterminate retention endpoint means the Board cannot know the order of its own waiting list, which is
challengeable by **every** applicant on it, not only by the one who asked for reconsideration.

**Why not simply accepting the first-action result.** It would let a bare acknowledgment letter cost an
applicant her place in the queue, and through subdivisions D 4 and D 5 that can move her from the
subsidised purchase to standard cost under subsection B of § 51.1-142.2. **A clarifying patch should not
reprice anybody as a side effect.**

**One divergence I flag once, which does not reopen the decision.** Subdivision D 3 carries the parallel
retention and runs it to the **substantive event**:

> *An application for which a request under this subdivision is pending retains its filing date for the
> purposes of subdivisions 4 and 5, and the Board shall act upon the application no later than 15 days
> after **the confirmation is obtained**.*

After Edit 1, (k-ii) resolves to **first action plus 30 days** — which can fall short of the decision in a
slow case and past it in a fast one, where D 3's analogue tracks the substantive event in both. The
decision stands as taken; **the comparison is a named target for pass 18**, and if it shows the two
retentions diverge in a way that matters it comes back to me as a card, not to the drafter as a redraft.

**Priced separately, and it does not block the drafting.** [MOO-163](/MOO/issues/MOO-163) asks Fiscal &
Impact Modeler the question I cannot answer: whether the choice between the three candidate dates moves
any number at all. My own expectation is that the aggregate is **zero**, because the queue does not create
slots — holding one applicant's place displaces whoever is behind her — so only the distribution moves.
**I have said so in terms, because I would rather have that confirmed or corrected than have a figure
manufactured to fill the gap.** It is a release gate, not a drafting blocker.

### 2.3 The *"month in which"* clause — keep

**Decision: keep, and the drafter's reasoning is accepted in full.** Dropping it would make Edit 1 govern
periods but not month-references, **which is the same split the item exists to close**, and would arm
*Forst* inside Edit 1. The smaller patch is not the better patch here.

Two things make keeping it safe. The clause has a **real and unique target** — of the fourteen
*"month in which"* phrases in enacted §1, exactly one is keyed to the Board's acting, and it is the
definition's reset. And §5.4's two-month movement is **neutralised downstream** by the decision-keyed
floor, which is keyed to the Board's *giving* a decision or notice and so cannot be moved by Edit 1. The
residual — where the Board gives neither — is [MOO-164](/MOO/issues/MOO-164)'s territory, not a new item.

### 2.4 Pass 18 — yes, widened to three targets

**Decision: commission it, and do not confine it to the six words.** Edits 2 and 3 are new operative text
and belong in the same pass; running pass 18 on revision 3's wording and then a pass 19 on the new edits
would spend two reviews to cover one draft. Scope:

1. **The six words at §6.3** — *"and last acts upon it"*.
2. **§5.2's arithmetic and card (l)** — the F-6 tie-break, framed as precision, with the monotonicity
   argument and the four cap models handed over.
3. **Edit 2 and Edit 3** — with card (g)'s condition-precedent reasoning and card (k)'s D 3 divergence
   named as the two things to attack.

**Both earlier passes earned this one.** Pass 16 caught a measuring rule that defined a verb carrying
duties; pass 17 caught an edit that armed *Forst* in its own subdivision and three endpoints nobody had
counted. A third pass on materially new text is cheap by comparison.
