# MOO-207 — f11-authority: the Virginia authority behind subdivision G 4's "notwithstanding" displacements

**Internal risk analysis. Not legal advice, not a legal opinion the organization may rely on, and nothing here states or implies that counsel has cleared the proposal.** Pre-decisional working material. Nothing in the draft bill is a Division of Legislative Services bill, nothing has been reviewed by DLS, and no part of it is legally sufficient for introduction.

Prepared 12 October 2026 by Red Team / Counsel Reviewer, on the Policy Director's commission out of [MOO-97](/MOO/issues/MOO-97) §7 item 132 answer (6).

**Bottom line.** The authority exists, it is Supreme Court of Virginia authority, and it is favourable — but it is not the authority [MOO-189](/MOO/issues/MOO-189) F11 named. The load-bearing case is ***Anderson v. Commonwealth*, 18 Gratt. (59 Va.) 295 (1868)**, not the "complete in itself" formulation. On that authority a "notwithstanding [named section]" clause in a new section is **not** an amendment of the named section for Article IV, Section 12 purposes; naming the section **helps**; and partial-limb displacement is, if anything, safer than whole-subdivision displacement. **No cure is required.** The live Article IV, Section 12 exposure in this bill is not the reenactment clause at all — it is the **one-object/title limb**, and that one does need something to travel to DLS.

## 1. The strongest case against, in the opposition's voice

> *Article IV, Section 12 exists for exactly one reason, and the Supreme Court of Virginia said so in Gilmore: to "prevent the legislature from accidentally or intentionally misleading the public regarding the precise action taken in a given enactment," and to give "clear notice to those affected by an enacted bill." Then ask who is affected here and what notice they get. A member elects the straight life option under § 51.1-165, files the notification subdivision D 1 of the new section compels her to file, and dies three weeks before her effective date. Her brother reads § 51.1-165 C 1. It tells him, in terms, that benefits "shall be paid in accordance with the provisions of § 51.1-163." He reads subsection F of § 51.1-165.01 and it tells him the same thing. Both statements are false in his case, and nothing on the face of either section — not a cross reference, not an "except as provided," not a catchline — tells him so. The General Assembly did not merely fail to warn him; it enacted a provision whose whole operative function is to make those two sentences untrue for a defined class, and then declined to put the two sections in the bill's title where a reader of the title would see them, and declined to reproduce them where a reader of the Acts of Assembly would see the change.*
>
> *And do not say "complete in itself." Virginia's own formulation of that idea, from Drewry, is an act that is "complete in itself, and perfectly intelligible, without reference to any thing beyond its own terms." Subdivision G 4 is the opposite of that by construction: it cannot be read at all without opening two other sections and locating a particular limb of each. Call that what it is. It is a surgical amendment of §§ 51.1-165 and 51.1-165.01, performed from a distance, with the scalpel held in a different section of the Code so that the reenactment clause never bites. Narrows tells you the Constitution "cannot be thus evaded" and that this Court will look to what an act is "in substance and effect," not to the label the drafter chose. Falls Church upheld an independent act on the express ground that "not a single provision in any of the sections cited was enlarged or diminished." Here two provisions are diminished, by name, on purpose. Falls Church is against them, not for them.*
>
> *Finally, the easy fix was available and was refused. Two conforming amendments, two sections into the title, two sections reproduced at length — the exact thing the Constitution asks for, at the cost of a few pages of underscores. A legislature that would not pay that price for the notice the Constitution requires has told you how much it valued the notice.*

That brief is serious and I could not make it go away by rereading the text. It goes away on the authority, below — but the third paragraph does not go away entirely, and the opposition's *Drewry* point lands squarely on MOO-189's reasoning rather than on the bill. See F2.

### 2.3 Where "complete in itself" actually lives in modern Virginia law

This matters, because it is the phrase MOO-189 F11 used. Of the fifty-four Virginia Reports cases in the corpus containing the words "complete in itself," **exactly one** uses it in an art. IV, § 12 (or predecessor) analysis: *Drewry* (1858). In modern Virginia law the phrase belongs to the **repeal-by-implication canon**, quoting 50 Am. Jur., Statutes § 543:

- ***City of South Norfolk v. City of Norfolk*, 190 Va. 591, 604–05, 58 S.E.2d 32 (1950)**: "Except where an act covers the entire subject-matter of earlier legislation, **is complete in itself**, and is evidently intended to supersede the prior legislation on the subject, a later act does not by implication repeal an earlier act unless there is such a clear, manifest, controlling, necessary, positive, unavoidable, and irreconcilable inconsistency and repugnancy … Moreover, **a statute is only repealed by the repugnancy of matter in a subsequent statute to the extent of such repugnancy, and if any part of the earlier act can stand as not superseded or affected by the later act, it is not repealed.**"
- ***Boulevard Bridge Corp. v. City of Richmond*, 203 Va. 212, 216–17, 123 S.E.2d 364 (1962)**: same quotation, same use.
- Cited in both: ***Scott v. Lichford*, 164 Va. 419, 422, 180 S.E. 393 (1935)** — "Repeal by implication is not favored."

So MOO-189's sentence — *"an act complete in itself that supersedes prior law by implication is not an amendment in that sense"* — welds the correct art. IV, § 12 conclusion to a phrase that, in Virginia, is doing different work. The conclusion survives. The citation does not. See F2.

## 3. The three questions, answered

### 3.1 Is a "notwithstanding [another section]" clause in a new section an amendment of the named section for art. IV, § 12 purposes?

**No, on the authority — and there is no Virginia case holding otherwise.** Three reasons, in descending strength:

1. **Direct rationale.** *Anderson* holds that a later provision which "operate[s] to overrule the legal effect of the former law, but … change[s] none of its terms" "did not 'amend' it in the sense of this provision of the constitution," and that cases where a later law "operate[s] to modify the construction and effect of the former law" "are not affected by this provision of the constitution." A "notwithstanding … as requires" clause changes none of the terms of § 51.1-165 C 1 or § 51.1-165.01 F. It modifies their effect in a defined class. That is *Anderson* on all fours.
2. **Negative authority.** *Gilmore* makes the question binary: is the law an "act revived," or a "section amended"? The bill revives nothing (no provision of either section has been repealed or expired). It therefore turns entirely on "section amended," and *Anderson*, *Price* and *Poindexter* all answer that an independent enactment that does not use amendatory language about a section is not an amendment of it.
3. **The failure cases do not fit.** Both decisions in Virginia that actually struck something under this clause — *Beale* and the invalid limb in *Poindexter* — turned on an act **using amendatory or revival words about a section it did not reproduce** ("to amend and re-enact an act entitled …"; "section 2038 … is hereby re-enacted"). Subdivision G 4 uses no amendatory verb and no revival verb anywhere, and the bill's title and enactment clause 1 claim nothing about either section.

**Honest limit: this is an answer by rationale, not by direct holding.** I found **no Virginia decision, at any level, in the corpus searched, construing a "notwithstanding [named section]" clause under art. IV, § 12 or its predecessors.** The gap is real and must be reported as a gap. What fills it is *Anderson*'s rationale plus the practice evidence at §3.4.

### 3.2 Is displacing part of a subsection treated differently from displacing a whole subdivision?

**No, and partial displacement is the safer of the two.** Both limbs in the current text are partial, so this is academic for the bill as it stands, but the answer is:

- *Anderson* expressly contemplates a later statute "inconsistent with **part** of a former statute" and puts that case on the same side of the line.
- *South Norfolk* states the rule in terms: "a statute is only repealed by the repugnancy of matter in a subsequent statute **to the extent of such repugnancy**, and if any part of the earlier act can stand as not superseded or affected by the later act, it is not repealed." A displacement drawn to a named limb leaves the rest of C 1 and F standing, by its own terms — which is what the canon would do anyway.
- *Gilmore* holds "section" means "the smallest distinct subdivision of a legislative act." Virginia therefore already reads this clause at sub-section granularity. A clause that operates at that same granularity is coherent with the constitutional unit, not in tension with it.

**There is no Virginia authority that treats whole-subdivision displacement worse, either.** But the drafting is better as it stands, and if anyone proposes to widen the clause back to the whole of C 1, that is a step away from the authority, not toward it.

### 3.3 Does the authority distinguish express, section-naming displacement from displacement by implication? Does naming help or hurt?

**The authority does not distinguish them for art. IV, § 12 purposes, and naming helps.**

- *Anderson* treats express inconsistency and implied modification identically: both "are not affected by this provision of the constitution."
- On the **only rationale the Court has ever given** for the clause, naming is affirmatively good. *Gilmore*: the purpose is "to prevent the legislature from accidentally or intentionally misleading the public regarding the precise action taken," and the notice function "is satisfied when the public is provided notice of every change made to a law." A clause that names the section, names the subsection, and names the limb displaces with maximum precision. A displacement left to implication would tell the public less, not more. **The opposition cannot simultaneously complain of inadequate notice and of excessive specificity.**
- **The one way naming can hurt is the one *Poindexter* and *Beale* identify: naming plus an amendatory or revival verb.** Had G 4 said "subdivision C 1 of § 51.1-165 is amended to read …" or "§ 51.1-165 C 1 is hereby re-enacted so as to …", it would be squarely inside *Poindexter*'s invalid limb. It says neither. **This is the single drafting constraint that must survive into the DLS request.** See §5.
- **A secondary way naming can hurt is not constitutional but practical:** naming the sections makes the displacement findable by anyone looking for it, including an opponent building a "hidden override" narrative. That is a messaging exposure, not an art. IV, § 12 exposure, and it belongs to the Editor, not here.

### 3.4 Settled Virginia legislative practice — in Title 51.1, verified

Practice is not authority, but a structurally identical provision that has stood unchallenged in the same chapter of the Code for twenty-five years is evidence about how the clause is understood.

**2001 Acts of Assembly, c. 804 (HB 2081), approved 4 April 2001** — title and text retrieved from legacylis.virginia.gov:

> "An Act to amend and reenact **§§ 51.1-206, 51.1-216 and 51.1-217** of the Code of Virginia and to amend the Code of Virginia by adding in Chapter 2.1 of Title 51.1 **a section numbered 51.1-221**, relating to law officers' retirement."

and in the body, in reenacted § 51.1-217:

> "A. A member shall receive an annual retirement allowance, payable for life, as follows: 1. Normal retirement. a. **Notwithstanding the provisions of §§ 51.1-155, 51.1-155.1 and 51.1-155.2**, for any employee commencing employment or reemployment on or after July 1, 2001, … the allowance shall equal …"

**§§ 51.1-155, 51.1-155.1 and 51.1-155.2 appear neither in the title nor in enactment clause 1, and none of them was amended by c. 804** — verified against each section's historical citation on law.lis.virginia.gov: § 51.1-155 carries "2001, cc. 689, 700" and no c. 804; §§ 51.1-155.1 and 51.1-155.2 carry no 2001 chapter at all.

That is the same act shape as ours — amend some sections, add one new section, displace named unamended sections of the same title from inside the amended/new text — enacted, codified, and in force since 2001. It remains the live text of § 51.1-217 A 1 a today.

**One style divergence, recorded because DLS will notice it.** The words "so much of" appear **zero times in the whole of Title 51.1** (verified against the full title text pulled from law.lis.virginia.gov on 12 October 2026). Virginia's house style in this title is "Notwithstanding the provisions of §§ X, Y and Z". The "notwithstanding so much of … as requires …" construction is more precise and I would not trade the precision away — it is what makes the displacement partial and therefore what makes §3.2 work. But expect DLS to want to restyle it, and the drafting request must say why it cannot be flattened to "Notwithstanding §§ 51.1-165 and 51.1-165.01" — that would widen the displacement to two whole sections and give away everything F2 of [MOO-189](/MOO/issues/MOO-189) bought. **This is a real risk of loss in drafting, and it is cheap to prevent with one sentence in the request.**

## 5. If the Director wants a cure anyway: the options, honestly costed

I do not recommend one. But the commission asks for the cheapest cure if the authority is adverse or unclear, and the Director may want belt and braces regardless. Ranked by cost:

1. **Do nothing to §§ 51.1-165 and 51.1-165.01. (Recommended.)** Cost: zero. Supported by *Anderson*, *Price*, *Poindexter*, *Kingan*, *Brown*, and settled Title 51.1 practice (2001 c. 804). **Spend the effort on F1 instead** — the title — which is where the pleadable claim actually is.
2. **Ask the Virginia Code Commission, after enactment, to carry a cross-reference note in §§ 51.1-165 and 51.1-165.01.** Cost: a letter, post-session. **This is not part of the bill and cannot be made part of it.** An editor's note is not enacted law and has no legal effect, so it cures nothing constitutionally — but it does cure the practical complaint in the opposition brief at §1 (the brother reading C 1 learns nothing), which is the complaint most likely to be made in a hearing room. Honest framing: this answers the **policy** objection, not the **constitutional** one.
3. **A true conforming amendment** — add "Except as provided in subdivision G 4 of § 51.1-142.4," to § 51.1-165 C 1 and to § 51.1-165.01 F. Cost, stated plainly because it is routinely understated:
   - Both sections go into the **title line** and into **enactment clause 1**, and both must be **reproduced at length with amendatory markup**. § 51.1-165 is a long section (three options, two subdivisions of subsection C, subsections D through the end).
   - It **widens** the art. IV, § 12 surface rather than narrowing it. Every added object in a title is a new germaneness question, and *Chippenham* is the case that severs on germaneness. Trading a reenactment-clause risk the authority says you do not have for an incremental one-object risk the authority says can actually bite is a bad trade.
   - It creates a **duplicate-amendment conflict** if any other 2027 bill amends § 51.1-165 — a live possibility for a frequently-amended VRS section (§ 51.1-155 took chapters in 2020 and 2023; § 51.1-165 is in the same chapter) — requiring reconciliation and risking an enrolling error.
   - It puts a long block of existing statutory text into the bill, where a transcription slip becomes enacted law.
   **If the Director orders this anyway, order it for both sections, not one.** A conforming amendment to § 51.1-165 alone would leave § 51.1-165.01 F as the sole unsignposted displacement and would make the omission look deliberate — strictly worse than doing neither.
4. **Recast G 4's clause as a direct amendment of C 1 and F** (move the rule into those sections). **Reject.** It would scatter one rule across three sections, and it is the shape *Poindexter* and *Beale* punish if the reproduction is anything less than perfect.

## 6. What must travel in the drafting request to Division of Legislative Services

A drafter's note is not enacted. Everything a DLS drafter must know has to be in the request. This is the checklist, for the Drafting Specialist to carry forward — **it is drafting direction, not drafted text; the Drafting Specialist writes the words.**

1. **The displacement, stated as a displacement.** The new section displaces, for a defined class, **only** so much of **subdivision C 1 of § 51.1-165** and of **subsection F of § 51.1-165.01** as requires benefits to be paid in accordance with § 51.1-163. Quote both target sentences in the request so the drafter can see exactly which words are being displaced and which survive.
2. **Neither § 51.1-165 nor § 51.1-165.01 is to be added to the title line or to the first enactment clause.** State that this is deliberate and state the ground: *Anderson v. Commonwealth*, 18 Gratt. (59 Va.) 295, 298–99 (1868). A DLS drafter's standing instinct is to conform; without this instruction the sections may be pulled into the title and reproduced, which is option 3 above, adopted by accident.
3. **The partial-limb form is substantive and must not be restyled** to "Notwithstanding §§ 51.1-165 and 51.1-165.01" or to "Notwithstanding the provisions of subdivision C 1 of § 51.1-165 …". Say what is lost: the first sentences of C 1 and of F, which void the member's option election where she dies before the Board receives her notification, must remain in force. (F4.)
4. **No amendatory or revival verb may be used about either section, anywhere in the bill** — not "is amended," not "is reenacted," not "shall not apply," not in the title, not in an enactment clause. The clause stays purely operative: "notwithstanding so much of … as requires …". Ground: *Poindexter v. May*, 98 Va. 143, 145 (1900); *Beale v. Pankey*, 107 Va. 215, 218 (1907), as explained in *District Road Board v. Spilman*, 117 Va. 201, 205–06 (1915).
5. **Request a title object covering death benefits** — e.g. adding "benefits on death of member" to the recited objects. This is F1 and it is the only item on this list that the authority says the bill actually needs. Flag it as the Lab's request, with the reason (germaneness; *Chippenham*).
6. **Distinguish references from displacements.** Subdivision G 4 also (a) preserves a benefit payable under **subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165** by a savings sentence, and (b) measures actuarial equivalence on the assumptions the Board uses under **§ 51.1-165**. Those are **references, not displacements**, and must not be swept into any conforming amendment or recast as overrides. Say so, or a tidy-minded drafter will conform them too.
7. **State the practice precedent** — 2001 Acts of Assembly, c. 804 — with its title and the § 51.1-217 A 1 a clause. It is the fastest way to settle the question with a drafter who has not met it before, because it is in the same chapter of the Code and has stood since 2001.
8. **Say what has not been reviewed.** This is internal risk analysis by the Lab's red team. It is not a legal opinion, DLS has not reviewed any of it, and the request should not imply that counsel has cleared the construction.

## 7. What I checked and found sound

Stated explicitly, because a review that reports only problems is not usable.

- **The reenactment-clause question itself is sound, on authority, and I could not break it.** *Anderson* is directly on point, favourable, never overruled, and never distinguished on a ground that reaches us. I looked specifically for a Virginia case holding that an act which expressly overrides a named unamended section violates the clause. **There is none in the corpus searched.** That is a null result and I report it as one.
- **The bill is not an "act revived."** On *Gilmore*'s definition — an act "which restores legal existence and force to a statute that has been expressly or impliedly repealed," including restoration of an expired act — nothing in the bill revives anything. Both halves of the clause have to be checked and only one of them was ever in play.
- **The title and enactment-clause mechanics are correct as drafted.** The title cites §§ 51.1-142.2 and 51.1-162 as amended and reenacted and § 51.1-142.4 as added; enactment clause 1 matches the title exactly. That satisfies *Parker v. Commonwealth*, 215 Va. 281 (1974), as stated in *Chippenham*, 219 Va. at 73. My F1 finding is about the **objects recited**, not about this mechanism.
- **A single act may amend some Code sections and add independent sections.** Verified in *Commonwealth v. Brown*, 91 Va. 762 (1895) (the oyster act: amend eight sections, repeal six, "and to add independent sections thereto") and in 2001 c. 804. The bill's structure is not novel and raises nothing on its own.
- **A new section may carve an operative exception out of existing law it does not amend.** Verified in *Kingan*, 198 Va. 820 (1957) — a new Tax Code § 188-b relieving a manufacturer of a wholesale merchants' licence tax, with no amendment of the taxing provisions, upheld.
- **1902 → 1971 continuity holds.** Every pre-1971 case above construes art. V, § 15 (1869), § 52 (1902), or art. IV, § 16 (1851), all in materially identical words. *Chippenham*, 219 Va. at 74, states that in the 1971 revision "no substantive changes were made in its wording." Without that bridge none of the old authority transfers, so I checked it rather than assuming it.
- **The displaced text is what the bill says it is.** Both target sentences read at source on 12 October 2026 and quoted at §0. The clause's words "as requires benefits to be paid in accordance with § 51.1-163" track the operative words of both targets exactly. No stranded or mis-designated cross reference in the clause.
- **No fiscal assumption is load-bearing on this question, and I did not manufacture one.** The commission is an authority question. Nothing in the answer turns on a number, no model is implicated, and I ran none. Recording this so the omission is not read as a gap in the review.
- **Scope held.** I did not re-review the substance of subdivision G 4, did not re-run [MOO-189](/MOO/issues/MOO-189)'s findings, and proposed no policy. Where the live text differs from the commission's description (F3) I report the difference and stop.
