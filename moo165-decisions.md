## 1. Q1 — the rate is wrong, and the bill's own cap proves it

**Decision: option (a). About nineteen state employees in all, over the four-year pilot. No per-year
rate exists and none can be named, because none is arithmetically available.**

### 1.1 The register is right, and there is a shorter proof than the one it gives

The register reproduces the authorised **78.1872 percent** central cap utilisation on the whole-pilot
reading and I have reproduced it independently: 18.3965 × $106,253 = $1,954,683 reserved, × the 20
percent contingency = $2,345,620, ÷ $3,000,000 = **78.1873 percent**. That is the authorised figure to
the last published digit. The whole-pilot reading is the published basis and there is no second basis.

**But the conclusive point does not need the participation model at all. It is in the bill.**

Subdivision D 2 forbids the Board to accept an election where the certified subsidy, **together with the
contingency amount and together with every certified subsidy and contingency amount previously reserved
and not released**, would exceed **$3,000,000**. That limitation is cumulative on its face, it is
expressly *"self-executing"*, and D 2's last sentence forecloses the only route round it — a zero or
negative certification *"shall not increase the amount available to be reserved for any other election."*

So on the central per-member certified subsidy of $106,253 plus its 20 percent contingency, the
**absolute ceiling on elections the Board may ever accept across the entire pilot is**

> $3,000,000 ÷ ($106,253 × 1.20) = **23.5, so twenty-three elections.**

Nineteen a year for four years is **seventy-four**. The sentence as I wrote it describes a program more
than three times the size of the one the bill authorises, and the bill forbids the Board to run it. This
is not a question of which basis to prefer. **One reading is impossible on the text.**

### 1.2 The error is mine and the Editor was right to hold it

The Editor was given the sentence *"as given and not paraphrased"* and declined to change the stated
scale of the program on their own arithmetic. **That is the correct instinct and I want it kept.** The
rule stands: where a figure in supplied text contradicts the authorised basis, hold it and route it — do
not reconcile it, even when the reconciliation looks obvious. It looked obvious here and it still needed
my signature, because the fix changes what the package claims the bill does.

### 1.3 The replacement sentence, and why the summary is now four sentences

**Sentence one is replaced and a new sentence two is inserted.** Sentences two and three of the original
become three and four, unchanged — the register has already cleared them as accurate on the live text.

> The bill creates a four-year pilot under which about nineteen state employees **in all** may buy credit
> for prior active-duty military service at a subsidised price, with the Commonwealth paying the
> difference up to a **$3,000,000 cap on what it may commit over the life of the pilot**. **The
> $3,000,000 is a ceiling, not an appropriation: the bill appropriates nothing, and in any year the
> General Assembly does not fund what the Board certifies as necessary, the program accepts no new
> elections.** A member who takes the subsidy must retire within a fixed window on a retirement that is
> not actuarially reduced, and if she does not, her purchased credit is cut back to what her own payments
> would have bought at the ordinary price. The pilot protects the member who dies, is approved for
> disability, is involuntarily separated, or is defeated by an error of the Board, and it expires after
> the pilot period with a report to the Governor and the money committees.

**Three words carry the Q1 fix:** *"in all."* Not *"over the four-year pilot"* — the sentence has already
said *"four-year pilot"* and the repetition would read as a correction of something, which invites the
question. *"In all"* resolves the rate in two words and reads as ordinary English.

**The register's four bindings on this statement all survive** and binding 2 is discharged: sentence one
is released. Binding 3 — the report's recipients are four, not two — still governs, so the compression
to *"the Governor and the money committees"* is available only on a page that has already named all four.
Binding 4's forward pointer to §8 still travels with it, and §3 below adds an entry to §8 that it points
to.

**This statement is still the one for memo §2 opening and the one-pager bullets**, and it still supersedes
MOO-90's for the opening.

## 2. Q2 — option (c): the bill appropriates nothing, and that is worth a sentence of its own

**Decision: my own words, as set out in §1.3's new sentence two. I am not taking option (d). The word
*"appropriation"* is withdrawn and may not be used of the $3,000,000 in any artifact.**

### 2.1 I verified it myself, at store revision 58

The register verified this at store revision 57. Store revision 58's eight substitutions fall in
subdivisions G 4, D 1 and M 2, so they do not touch it, and I confirmed the position directly in the live
body:

- **D 2** limits what the Board may **reserve** — *"would exceed $3,000,000"* — and nothing else.
- **D 3** requires, before any reservation, the written confirmation of the Director of the Department of
  Planning and Budget that *"funds appropriated and legally available for the purposes of this section
  are sufficient to fund the reservation"*, and requires **a further confirmation for each fiscal year in
  which any reservation remains in effect** — *"until that confirmation is obtained the Board shall
  accept no further election."*
- **Subsection K** provides that if the General Assembly *"does not appropriate, for any fiscal year,"*
  the amount the Board has certified as necessary, *"the Board shall accept no election filed on or after
  the first day of that fiscal year, and shall give public notice of that fact"* — while leaving accepted
  elections, made reservations and credited service untouched.
- The strings *"hereby appropriated"*, *"is appropriated"* and *"appropriation of"* appear **nowhere** in
  the document.

So the $3,000,000 is a **statutory ceiling on reservations whose funding depends on a separate annual
appropriation.** The register's reading is exactly right.

### 2.2 Why not option (b), and why not option (d)

**Option (b) — *"…up to a $3,000,000 cap, funded by annual appropriation"* — is accurate but it buries
the lead.** It reads as a note on plumbing. The fact a money-committee reader wants in the first
paragraph is not how the cap is funded; it is that **the bill asks for no money and switches itself off
if none comes.** That is a containment feature, and a package that mentions it only in a subordinate
clause is leaving its best fiscal argument on the table.

**Option (d) — keep *"appropriation"* as a deliberate simplification — I decline, and the reason is not
pedantry.** It is the one sentence in the summary that a committee staffer can correct out loud, and the
correction costs more than the word saves: it makes every other figure in the room look like it needs
checking. A simplification that cannot survive the first question is not a simplification.

### 2.3 The binding

**The word *"appropriation"* is not to be used of the $3,000,000 anywhere.** Available formulations, all
accurate on the text:

- *"a $3,000,000 cap on what the Commonwealth may commit"*
- *"a $3,000,000 ceiling on the Commonwealth's share, not an appropriation"*
- *"a $3,000,000 cap on reservations"* — where the audience knows what a reservation is, and nowhere else

**And the shut-off is stated with the cap, not separately.** The cap on its own invites *"and who pays for
it?"* The two facts answer each other and they travel together.

## 3. Q3 — option (b), reframed: C8 is retired and C19 replaces it

**Decision: C8 is retired as written. A new must-appear entry, C19, takes its place in §8, on pass 17's
S-5 / §7 item 118. I am putting S-5 on the must-appear list — §7 of the register says that instruction is
mine to give, and I am giving it.**

### 3.1 Why not option (a), and why not option (c)

**Option (a) — retire C8 and leave S-5 off — fails because the finding is not about the report.** I read
§7 item 118 in the live text. The reporting duty is the **third** of three consequences and the least of
them. The first two are:

1. **A single member can hold the program's capacity open indefinitely.** While any disability
   application is on file the Board is forbidden to make the subdivision 2 determination and no period
   runs. The 120-day aggregate caps **subsection M requests** and nothing else — disability applications
   are uncapped and uncounted. A member who applies, is denied, and applies again is never reduced. D 7's
   second sentence withholds release of her reservation while her vesting is undetermined, so her share
   of the $3,000,000 stays **committed and never spent.**
2. **Everybody behind her waits, and new acceptance stops.** The amount is charged against a $3,000,000
   cumulative limitation on a program whose absolute ceiling is about twenty-three elections (§1.1), so
   the waiting list in D 5 bears it; and until a fresh Department of Planning and Budget confirmation is
   obtained under D 3 the Board may accept **no further election** at all.

**That is a fiscal and distributive defect, not a reporting one**, and it is the same family as C12 and
as §7 item 85's appropriation-occupancy question. Something that can occupy a quarter of the program's
capacity on one member's repeat filings does not belong off the list.

**Option (c) — retire C8 and send item 118 to §9 as an open question — understates it the same way.** §9
is where questions go that the package has not answered. This one is answered: the reviewer names the
defect, names the mechanism, and recommends two specific repairs, **neither of which is drafted into the
bill.** A known defect with a known unenacted cure is a §8 risk. It can carry a pointer into §9 for the
choice between the two repairs, but its home is §8.

### 3.2 C19 — the entry, and the wording is fixed

**C19 — S-5: uncapped disability-application pendency lets one member occupy the cap, freeze the queue
and make the annual report perpetual — OPEN, §7 item 118, enacted nowhere. Must appear.**

Memo-ready text:

> **One member can hold the program's capacity open indefinitely, and the bill gives the Board no way to
> close it.** The bill's 120-day aggregate limit caps requests for review of the Board's decisions and
> nothing else. Disability applications are uncapped and uncounted: while any application is on file the
> Board is forbidden to cut back the member's purchased credit and no period runs, so a member who
> applies, is denied, and applies again is never cut back.
>
> **The consequence is fiscal before it is administrative.** The bill forbids the Board to release her
> reservation while her entitlement is undetermined, so her share of the $3,000,000 stays committed and
> is never spent. Because that limit is cumulative over the whole pilot, the applicants behind her go on
> the waiting list; and until the Department of Planning and Budget gives a fresh confirmation for the
> year, the Board may accept **no** further election.
>
> **The annual report to the Governor and the money committees also never ends**, because the condition
> that is supposed to end it can never be satisfied while any election remains undetermined.
>
> **The Lab's reviewer recommends two repairs — extend the 120-day aggregate to disability applications
> filed after the first, and give the reporting duty an outer date. Neither is in the bill.**

Citation to carry: pass 17 finding **S-5**, carded as **§7 item 118**, as adopted in
[`moo152-pass17-disposition`](/MOO/issues/MOO-42#document-moo152-pass17-disposition); the operative text
at D 3, D 7 and J 1 of the live body.

**Four bindings on C19.**

1. **No figure for the displacement.** How much capacity one member can hold, and how many applicants are
   displaced, is **not priced** and no number is authorised. The $3,000,000, the $170,000 per-election cap
   and the 120-day aggregate are the bill's own figures and are available. The twenty-three-election
   ceiling at §1.1 is available **as a cap arithmetic**, attributed as such, and is not to be presented as
   a forecast of participation.
2. **It is not netted against C12 or C18.** Three fiscal entries now sit in §8 — C12 on the cap and the
   non-completion population, C18 on the settlement count and the window, C19 on cap occupancy. They are
   three mechanisms. The memo states all three and nets none of them.
3. **MOO-90's C3 hold applies and this entry complies.** Subdivision G is not called an exception path, a
   rare case, an edge case or a backstop, and **no sentence in C19 leans on how few members reach it** —
   the point of the entry is that one member is enough.
4. **It does not say counsel has cleared anything.** The standing caveat travels with it.

### 3.3 The cure is recorded in §5 and is not stated in §8

**The register's standing rule that no artifact may say the terminal-date defect is cured stays in force
as to §8, and I am making it permanent there.**

Pass 17 verified that store revision 52's edits 11 to 13 cure both pass-12 grounds, and the repair is
visible on the face of J 1 — limb (a) now names in terms the two classes item 90 said were excepted from
any determination, cancellation under D 6 and release under D 7 with no credit given, and the
one-further-report mechanism is there. **I accept that the two pass-12 grounds are closed.**

**But the duty is still perpetual.** Only the route changed. A risk section that said *"the terminal-date
defect is cured"* and then carried C19 would be contradicting itself on the same page, and a reader who
stopped at the first clause would be misled. **The repair belongs in §5's carry-through table, where the
register already has it, and nowhere else.** That is the whole point of keeping a carry-through table: it
is where a cured entry goes to be recorded rather than advertised.

**C8 is retired as a must-appear.** No artifact states the pass-12 grounds, as grounds or as cured.
