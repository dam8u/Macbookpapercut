## 2. Findings, ranked

### SERIOUS

---

**F1 — Edit 164-1's 48-day cap is act-keyed, is reached by Edit 1's deeming, and binds in the central
case. Occurrence #15 is not closed, and §0.1's headline claim is false.** *(attack items 1 and 2)*

**The text it attacks.** Edit 164-1's READ, final clause:

> `… and in no case later than 48 days after the Board acts upon the request`

**The construction that makes it bite.** The MOO-141 patch's Edit 1 deems the Board to act upon a request
*"on the date on which the Board first acts upon it"* wherever *"a period begins or ends upon, or is
measured from, the Board's acting … upon a request."* The cap is a date measured from the Board's acting
upon the request. **So the cap is measured from the token first action, exactly as clause (a) is.** After
the cut, M 2's tolling sentence carries two act-keyed terms where it carried one.

**Worked case — MOO-141's own facts, `F = 7`, `D = 85`.**

| Day | Event |
|---|---|
| 0 | Member files a request for reconsideration of a G 2 determination |
| 7 | Board sends an acknowledgment. Deemed *"acts upon the request"* = **day 7** |
| 85 | Board gives its decision, within M 2's 90 days |
| — | limb (a) = day 37 · limb (b) = day 115 · **cap = day 55** |
| **55** | **Tolling of her final qualifying retirement date and of every period within which she must act ends — 30 days before the Board tells her anything** |

Add item 124 and it is worse, not better: a deemed denial on day 91 involves no notice at all, so limb (b)
never fires, and the endpoint is the cap at day 55 against a determination on day 91.

**The evidence.** Applying both patches to a copy of the live base and counting: **`acts upon` occurs
nine times, and occurrences #5 and #6 are both inside Edit 164-1's READ** — clause (a) and the cap. I
enumerated all nine. The cut's §7.2 predicts seven (F2).

**Why it matters more than the 18 days.** §1.1's table records *"M 2 tolling endpoint (#15) — Act-keyed
after this cut? **No — Edit 164-1** — Residual: **closed**"*, and §0.1 states that *"after this cut,
exactly one act-keyed endpoint remains where a token action can still cost anybody anything."* Both are
wrong. The residual at #15 is **60 of MOO-141's 78 days** — the cut repairs 18 and leaves 60 — and
§9.3 says so in terms (*"the cap is also what holds Edit 164-1 to the carded 18 days rather than to card
(f)'s 78"*). The document therefore contains both the true number and the false headline. **The false
headline is the one Decision 1's recommendation rests on**, and the Director is being asked to decline a
verb definition because only #17 survives. Two survive.

**Severity.** Serious, not fatal: the text never leaves the member worse off than today (N2), and the
60-day residual is a disclosed policy choice rather than a hidden one. It is serious because the
recommendation is argued on a classification row that is incorrect, and because the cost of being wrong
on Decision 1 is stated as *"$0 at the central estimate, queue position only"* when it is that plus up
to 60 days of lost tolling per request at #15.

**Fix direction** (Drafting Specialist writes it). Give the cap a second limb so it cannot expire before
the Board's own period does — e.g. *"and in no case later than 48 days after the later of (i) the Board's
acting upon the request and (ii) the expiration of the period allowed by this section for acting upon the
request."* On the worked facts that puts the endpoint at day 115 (limb (b)) with a bound at day 138,
bounded in the no-notice case, and it keeps clause (a) — so Edit 1's deeming still reaches the endpoint,
which is the property §9.11 item 1 exists to preserve. If the Director prefers the 18 days, then **§0.1,
§1.1's table and §1.2's "cost of being wrong" line must be restated**, because as written they are not
true.

**F3 — Edit 164-2's new limb identifies the obligation it tests by the one limb of it that never
conflicts. On that reading the 38 words are a no-op.** *(attack items 4 and 5)*

**The text it attacks.** Edit 164-2's READ:

> `or where the determination could not take effect within 120 days after the determination date
> consistently with the sentence of this subdivision that requires notice to be given no less than 30
> days before the determination takes effect,`

**The construction that makes it bite.** G 2's floor sentence imposes **two** requirements: notice *"no
less than 30 days, **and in no case less than the period allowed to the member by subsection M within
which to request reconsideration of the determination**, before the determination takes effect."* The new
limb's condition is that the determination *"could not take effect within 120 days … **consistently
with** the sentence … that requires notice to be given **no less than 30 days** before the determination
takes effect."* A reader takes the words after *"consistently with"* as describing **the requirement
being tested**. Tested against the 30-day limb alone, the condition is satisfied almost never: notice on
day 60 plus 30 days is day 90, inside the 120. **Edit 164-2 then lifts nothing, and card (h) is
uncured.**

The cut's §3.2 defends the 25-word repetition on the ground that *"that formula already appears in the
consequence clause four words later and the section does not cross-refer by paraphrase."* That is right
about the **consequence** clause, where the descriptor only identifies a sentence and the operative words
are *"the first day on which it may take effect under"* it, which import the whole sentence. It is wrong
about the **new limb**, where the descriptor is load-bearing. Borrowing a descriptor built for
identification and using it for a substantive test is the defect.

**Worked case.** Determination date day 0. Member has a definition request pending that tolls 100 days,
days 0–100. Notice day 55, on time. Her 30-day window runs from day 55, tolled to day 100, completes day
130. On the intended reading the determination *"could not take effect within 120 days … consistently
with"* the floor — 130 > 120 — the limb fires, the limit lifts, effect day 130. On the narrow reading the
test is day 55 + 30 = day 85 ≤ 120, the limb does not fire, the 120-day limit stands, **and the member is
back in the irreconcilable case the edit was drafted to remove.**

**Fix direction.** Name the requirement rather than the sentence — e.g. *"or where the determination
could not take effect within 120 days after the determination date consistently with the requirement of
this subdivision that notice be given no less than the period allowed to the member by subsection M
within which to request reconsideration of the determination before the determination takes effect."*
Longer than 38 words, and it is the only version that reaches the case.

**F4 — §2.3's arithmetic omits subdivision G 4's suspension of the very G 2 period it says is
irreconcilable. The conflict is real; the stated condition for it is wrong, and in a definable class the
pair is reconcilable and Edit 164-2 is unnecessary.** *(attack item 4)*

**The text it attacks.** §2.3: *"Conflict wherever `notice day + 30 + T > 120` and `notice day ≤ 60`,
i.e. wherever aggregate tolling `T` exceeds `90 − notice day` days."*

**What the sources actually say.** Subdivision G 4, second sentence, verified in the live base today:

> *"**No period stated in subdivision 2** or in this subdivision, whether it is a period within which the
> Board is required to act or **a period within which a determination is required to take effect**, runs
> while such an application is pending or while a request the filing of which tolls the running of the
> member's final qualifying retirement date under subsection M is pending, except that the aggregate …
> shall not exceed 120 days …"*

G 2's 120-day outer limit **is** a period within which a determination is required to take effect, and it
is stated in subdivision 2. **It does not run while a tolling request is pending, up to 120 days.** The
cut knows this — §7.3 cites the same sentence and §6.5 reproduces it — but §2.3's arithmetic proceeds as
though the 120 were a calendar figure.

**The corrected condition.** Conflict wherever `notice_day + 30 + T_after > 120 + S` and `notice_day ≤
60`, where `T_after` is tolling that falls **after** notice (not total tolling `T`) and `S` is G 4's
aggregate non-running of G 2's periods. §2.3 uses `T` for `T_after` and omits `S` entirely, overstating
the conflict twice.

**The worked case does reproduce** — I checked it rather than assuming: with tolling days 0–100 and
notice day 55, `T_after` = 45, so the floor is day 130 and the document's day 130 is right. But it
reproduces **only where `S` is small**, i.e. only where the Board makes a prompt token first action.
`S` is driven by **pendency** (filing → first action) and `T` by **tolling** (filing → act + 30, or up to
act + 48 after Edit 164-1). Where the Board does not act promptly, `S ≈ T` and the pair is
**reconcilable**: with tolling days 10–110 and notice day 55, the floor is day 140 and the limit, as
suspended, is day 220.

**So Edit 164-2's necessity is a function of the gap between G 4's pendency and M 2's tolling — which is
[MOO-141](/MOO/issues/MOO-141), the parent of the parent, and it is `blocked`.** The claim that *"the
member's floor stands only where the Board has breached its own 60-day notice duty"* is not established
on the stated arithmetic. It is established for the prompt-token-action class, which is the class
MOO-141 is about.

**Fix direction.** No text change to Edit 164-2 is required by this finding. §2.3 needs restating with
`S` in it, and the restated condition should say which class the cure is for. **This one must appear in
the memo's risk section** — a reviewer who reruns the arithmetic will get a different answer from the
document's.

**F5 — the 210-day bound does not hold. The zero-allowance window in Edit 164-2's new class is bounded
only by the Board's own delay in first acting, which is unbounded on the face of the text — and this cut
widens the exclusion that makes it so.** *(attack item 5)*

**The text it attacks.** §2.3: *"Absolute outer bound: `60 (notice) + 30 (her window) + 120 (clause
(ii)'s aggregate) = 210 days` after the determination date."* And §10.1, which repeats the 210 to the
Director.

**The construction that makes it bite.** M 2 clause (ii), as Edit 164-3 leaves it:

> `(ii) the aggregate of all tolling under this subsection shall not exceed 120 days, excluding any
> period after the expiration of the period allowed by this section for acting upon the request and
> before the Board acts upon the request`

The exclusion takes the Board's own lateness **out of** the 120-day aggregate. Tolling continues through
that excluded interval and does not consume the cap. **So `T` is not bounded by 120; it is bounded by 120
plus the Board's lateness.** Edit 164-3 extends that exclusion to the second class of request — §3.3 says
so approvingly (*"Pass 17's F-5 makes it bite more often"*) — so this cut makes the unbounded term reach
further.

**Worked case — 460 days, not 210.**

| Day | Event |
|---|---|
| 0 | G 2 determination date |
| 1 | Member files a request under the definition of *"final qualifying retirement date"*. Tolling starts |
| 31 | The definition's 30 days for acting upon it expire. The Board has done nothing |
| 55 | Board gives the G 2 notice — **on time**, so the existing late-notice exception does **not** fire. Her 30-day reconsideration window starts, tolled |
| 400 | Board first acts upon the definition request |
| 430 | Tolling ends (limb (a) / limb (b)). Counted toward clause (ii)'s 120: days 1–31 and 400–430 = **60 days**. Days 31–400 are **excluded** |
| **460** | Her tolled 30-day window completes. The floor forbids effect before this date |
| 240 | What the 120-day limit allows, G 4's suspension included and capped at 120 |
| **460** | **Effect date under Edit 164-2. Zero-allowance window = 460 days after the determination date** |

Arrears are paid in full under G 2's own sentence, so the loss is 460 days of liquidity and time value,
not the benefit. There is no ceiling in the construction: substitute day 4,000 for day 400 and the window
is 4,060 days.

**And the cut puts the same formula at two sites that do opposite things with it.** Edit 164-6 leaves
G 4 reading *"a failure of the Board to act upon such a request within the period allowed by this section
for acting upon the request **shall not extend that aggregate**."* Edit 164-3 leaves M 2 reading that the
same lateness is **excluded from** its aggregate — i.e. does extend the tolling. **G 4 caps the Board's
lateness out; M 2 lets it run.** §3.6 asserts *"no change of meaning whatever"* and §7.3 frames the pair
as *"one concept, one formula, three sites."* The concept is indeed one; the consequences are opposite,
and after this cut the opposition is textually symmetrical, which invites a harmonising construction in
either direction. §9.3's refusal to introduce *"the F-6 shape of defect"* while F-6 is open is not met:
the 48-day cap bounds the gap between the act and the endpoint, not the absolute date, and the absolute
date is the Board's first action.

**Fix direction.** Two choices, and it is the Director's. (i) State the true bound — `60 + 30 + 120 +
the Board's lateness` — and say that the lateness term is the Board's own and pre-existing. (ii) Bound
it, which means touching clause (ii)'s exclusion, which is a larger change than this cut and should be
carded rather than drafted here. **The 210 must not go to the board as an absolute bound.**

**F6 — *"The Board shall act upon a request for reconsideration within 90 days"* states no commencement.
It is the only one of the section's five *"shall act upon"* duties that does not, and Edits 164-3 and
164-4 both now key to when its period *"expired."*** *(attack items 6 and 7)*

**The text it attacks.** Subsection M 2, third sentence, verbatim from the live base:

> `The Board shall act upon a request for reconsideration within 90 days.`

Within 90 days **of what**? Nothing in the sentence, and nothing in M 2, supplies the start.

**What depends on it.** Three things this cut creates or relies on:

1. **Edit 164-3** — clause (ii) now excludes *"any period after **the expiration of the period allowed by
   this section for acting upon the request**."* If the period's start is not stated, its expiry is not
   stated, and the excluded interval has no left edge.
2. **Edit 164-4 / R-5** — the floor is *"the first day of the second month following the month in which
   **that period expired**."* Same defect, and this is the answer §2.4 gives to the no-decision
   residual.
3. **§2.4's agreement claim** — that R-5 and [MOO-42](/MOO/issues/MOO-42) §7 item 124 *"key to the
   identical date — the expiry of M 2's 90-day period."* Two provisions cannot key to the same date if
   neither text fixes it.

**The evidence that this is an outlier and not the section's style.** I enumerated all five *"shall act
upon"* duties in enacted §1. The other four all state their commencement: *"no later than 30 days after
the request is filed"* (the definition), *"within 30 days after the request is filed"* (D 3, the DPB
Director), *"no later than 15 days after the confirmation is obtained"* (D 3), *"no later than 15 days
after it acts upon the request"* (M 2). **Only the 90-day duty is bare**, and it is the one two of this
cut's six edits now depend on.

**Worked case.** Member files on 1 March. The Board does nothing. Is the period expired on 30 May
(filing), on some later date measured from the Board's receipt, or from the date the request became
complete? R-5's floor is *"the first day of the second month following the month in which that period
expired"* — 1 July on the filing reading, 1 August if expiry slips past 31 May. **A one-day difference in
the construction of the commencement moves her final qualifying retirement date by a month.**

**Severity.** Serious. Not fatal, because every ordinary canon reads *"within 90 days"* in a sentence
about a filed request as running from filing, and clause (ii)'s *"the expiration of the … period"*
presupposes a determinate expiry. It is serious because it is five words to fix, because two of this
cut's edits newly rely on it, and because it is the kind of thing Legislative Services returns a draft
over.

**Fix direction.** *"The Board shall act upon a request for reconsideration within 90 days **after the
request is filed**."* Five words, no figure moves, and it makes §2.4's agreement claim true. It is also
within card (i)'s territory without reopening card (i)'s merits: card (i) asks what **discharges** the
duty; this asks when the duty **starts**.

**F8 — Edit 164-1's limb (b) keys the **member's** tolling endpoint to notice given to **the filer**, who
need not be the member.** *(attack item 2)*

**The text.** `(b) 30 days after the Board gives **the person who filed the request** written notice of
its decision upon the request`

M 2's tolling sentence is unconditional on who files: *"**The filing** of a request for reconsideration
under this subsection … tolls the running of **the member's** final qualifying retirement date."* The
standing list, at store revision 62, has **eight** classes, including an employer that paid on the
member's behalf, a personal representative, and a court-appointed notice recipient in a death case.

**Worked case.** Day 0, the employer that paid on the member's behalf files a reconsideration request of
the G 2 determination. Day 7, token first action. Day 20, the Board gives the **employer** written notice
of its decision. Limb (a) = day 37, limb (b) = day 50, cap = day 55 → endpoint **day 50**. The member's
final qualifying retirement date resumed running on day 50 and **she was never told anything**. The
18 days §2.2 buys her are bought for the employer.

Never worse than today (today's endpoint would be day 37), which is why this is moderate and not serious.
But the stated benefit — *"18 of her 30 days can run before she can know the date she is now required to
retire by"* — is not delivered in the class where someone else files.

**Fix direction.** *"(b) 30 days after the Board gives the member and the person who filed the request
written notice of its decision upon the request"*, or key limb (b) to the member and let the cap carry
the filer case. §9.5's reasoning for naming the filer is sound as to **the actor and the recipient of the
duty**; it does not reach **whose clock** the limb is protecting.

**F9 — candidate (c-1) also narrows G 2's second use, *"was pending"*, and §4.1 prices only the first.**
*(attack item 8)*

**The text.** (c-1) changes *"For the purposes of **this subdivision**"* to *"**this subsection**"* in
G 4's pendency definition. G 2 uses the defined word **twice** in one sentence:

> *"Nothing in this subdivision prevents the payment of a retirement allowance to a member while an
> application … **is pending**, and the determination required by this subdivision shall take effect
> notwithstanding that a retirement allowance has been paid to the member while such an application
> **was pending**."*

§4.1 prices the first (the carve-out ends at first action). The second is the **validating** limb: it
preserves the determination's effect despite an allowance having been paid during pendency. Narrowed to
first-action pendency, **an allowance paid after the Board's first action is no longer covered by the
notwithstanding.**

**Worked case.** Disability application filed day 0; Board acknowledges day 5; allowance paid day 40;
G 2 determination takes effect day 90. Under (c-1) the payment on day 40 was not made *"while such an
application was pending"*, so the notwithstanding does not reach it — and G 2's third sentence requires
that *"the determination shall take effect **before** the first payment of any retirement allowance to
the member."* It did not. **The self-correction clause is attached to the floor sentence, not to this
one**, so there is no stated consequence, and a litigant on either side can argue the determination is
of no effect or that the payment was unlawful. That is a worse outcome than the carve-out narrowing
§4.1 prices.

**Fix direction.** If the Director selects (c-1), the *"was pending"* limb needs its own treatment. This
is a second reason to prefer (c-2), and it reinforces §4.2's recommendation on grounds §4.2 does not
state.

**F10 — candidate (c-2) puts a second, different definition of *"pending"* for the same application in
the same subsection, and borrows G 2's most overloaded verb to do it.** *(attack item 8)*

**The text.** (c-2)'s new sentence: *"For the purposes of this subdivision, such an application is
pending from the date on which the member files it until the date on which the Board **determines**
it."* G 4's definition, four thousand characters later in the same subsection: *"… until the date on
which the Board **first acts upon** it."*

**Two problems, both drafting rather than policy.**

1. **One word, two definitions, one subsection.** Subsection G would define *"pending"* for a disability
   retirement application twice, differently, in adjacent subdivisions. The result is operationally
   coherent and deliberate — the Board may **make** the G 2 determination once it first acts on the
   application (G 4), while it must keep **paying** until it determines the application (G 2) — but it
   reads as an error, and the natural judicial move on a word defined twice in one subsection is to
   harmonise. Which one gives way is then not the drafter's choice.
2. ***"Determines"* collides.** G 2 uses *"the determination"* and *"determines"* about twenty times for
   the G 2 reduction determination. *"[U]ntil the date on which the Board determines it"* invites the
   reading that the endpoint is the G 2 determination, which would make the carve-out circular — the
   allowance may be paid until the determination, and the determination is what the carve-out is an
   exception to.

**Fix direction.** *"until the date on which the Board **grants or denies** it"*, and an express
non-application — e.g. open (c-2)'s sentence *"Notwithstanding the definition in subdivision 4, and for
the purposes of this subdivision, …"* My own view agrees with §4.2's: **(c-2) over (c-1)**, for §4.2's
reason plus F9. These two repairs are the price of taking it.
