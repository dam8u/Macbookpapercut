## 1. The strongest case against, in the opposition's voice

> You have written a General Assembly statute that orders the Virginia Retirement System to tell a
> state employee, in the middle of a 4,500-word certification about purchase prices and valuation
> dates, that she *"is advised to review"* her beneficiary designation. Then you told her to do it
> *before completing payment* — a date that has nothing whatever to do with when her designation
> matters. Her designation matters on the day she dies. Payment is the day the Commonwealth
> finishes taking her money.
>
> And you did not tell her what to look *for*. Consider the employee who has never filed a
> designation at all. Today, § 51.1-162 B protects her: no designation, so the allowance goes to
> her spouse, then her minor children, then her parents, in that order, automatically. Your
> advisory prompts her to go and make one. She names her sister, because her sister is who she
> would want to have her money. Now she has a designation, her sister is alive, and her sister is
> not a spouse, a minor child or a parent — so § 51.1-162 B's first branch will not pay her sister
> and its second branch will not trigger. **Her mother, who would have been paid, now gets
> nothing.** Your sentence did that. You told her to look and you did not tell her what she was
> looking at, and the General Assembly will have enacted the instruction that cost her family the
> benefit.
>
> Then there is the machinery. You attached this piece of pastoral advice to a failure sentence
> that converts its omission into a contested case under the Administrative Process Act. A
> disappointed mother, denied a death benefit, receives the subdivision G 4 notice and has thirty
> days to petition the Board over a sentence of *advice* that was missing from a certificate her
> daughter received four years earlier. She will lose, and under § 2.2-4029 even if she wins no
> court may supply the designation, so she gets a remand to the very Board that denied her. You
> have built a grievance channel whose only possible output is a letter. You should have left it
> in the report to the money committees, where it does some good, and out of the courts, where it
> does none.

### F1 — SERIOUS — the advisory can cause the loss it exists to prevent

**What is wrong.** The advisory tells the member to *look*. It does not tell her what she is looking
at. § 51.1-162 B has two branches, and they treat the no-designation member and the
bad-designation member in opposite ways:

> *"a retirement allowance shall be paid to the person or persons designated as provided in
> subsection A of this section **if the person is** the member's (i) surviving spouse, (ii) minor
> child, or (iii) parent(s). **If no designation has been made**, or if the death of the designated
> person occurs prior to the death of the member and another designation has not been made, a
> retirement allowance shall be paid in the following order of precedence to the member's (a)
> surviving spouse, (b) minor children, or (c) parent(s)."*
> — § 51.1-162 B, verified at source 12 October 2026 (`law.lis.virginia.gov`, Title 51.1 ch. 1
> art. 9).

A member who has filed **no** designation is already fully protected: branch two pays her spouse,
then her minor children, then her parents. A member who has designated a non-qualifying person is
protected by **neither** branch: branch one will not pay a sibling, and branch two does not trigger
because a designation has been made and the designee is alive.

**So the statute rewards doing nothing and punishes doing the wrong thing, and the advisory urges
action without saying which.**

**Concrete failure scenario.** Ms R, 56, a state employee, has never filed a VRS beneficiary
designation. She is unmarried, has no minor children, and her mother is living. She buys 36 months
of military service with a certified subsidy; the Board's certification under D 1 includes the
advisory. She reads it, logs in, finds no designation on file, and — prompted to act — designates
her sister, who is who she would want to have her money. She dies in service eleven months later.
Branch one will not pay her sister. Branch two does not trigger. **No § 51.1-162 B allowance is
payable to anyone.** Her mother, who would have received a lifetime allowance had the certification
said nothing at all, receives nothing. The 137 characters are the but-for cause.

**Authority.** § 51.1-162 B, both branches, quoted above. The act already states the rule correctly
two items earlier, so this is not a gap in the Lab's understanding — it is a gap between what item
[3] says and what item [4] tells her to do about it.

**Direction for the fix.** Give the advice an object. One anchored substitution, +47 characters.

- **FIND** (resolves **1×** in §1, 2× in the document — the second is §0.0-65's block quote):
  `that the member is advised to review the member's designation of beneficiary under subsection A of § 51.1-162 before completing payment;`
- **Words I would use:**
  `that the member is advised to review the member's designation of beneficiary under subsection A of § 51.1-162, and the effect of that designation under subsection B of that section, before completing payment;`

`"that section"` is safe here: § 51.1-162 is named earlier in the same item (verified — see N2).

### F2 — SERIOUS — *"before completing payment"* is the wrong deadline, and the recorded reason for it is not true

**What is wrong.** §6.0-65 item 5 gives two reasons for the deadline. The first is right and the
second is wrong:

> *"it is the last moment at which the purchase can still be undone, **and the whole window during
> which re-designating changes the outcome**."*

The first limb is correct — subdivision G 5 lets the member cancel *"at any time before payment is
completed"*. The second limb is false. **Nothing in § 51.1-162 A fixes a designation at the
completion of payment.** A's own words contemplate indefinite redesignation: *"If no designation
has been made, **or the death of the designated person occurs prior to the death of the member and
another designation has not been made**, …"*. The designation is revocable until the member dies.

On the act's own dials the window in which redesignating changes the § 51.1-162 outcome runs from
the certification to the earlier of the member's death and her retirement:

| Leg | Authority | Length |
|---|---|---|
| certification → payment in full | subsection E | ≤ 12 months |
| → crediting | subsection E | ≤ 1 month |
| → earliest qualifying retirement date | definition in subsection A | ~1 month |
| → final qualifying retirement date | definition in subsection A | +12 months, +3 on Board approval |

**Up to about 27 months — and indefinitely for a member who does not retire**, since § 51.1-162
applies for as long as she is a member in service and subsection F attaches no consequence beyond
subsection G. *"Before completing payment"* ends the advice at the **first** of those four legs, at
most 12 months of 27, and for a prompt payer at roughly the tenth part of it.

**Concrete failure scenario.** Ms T receives the certification in March 2031, reads the advisory,
reviews her designation that week, confirms her husband, and pays in full in June 2031 — perfect
compliance. She and her husband divorce in November 2032; she removes him and names her adult son.
She has not yet retired. She dies in service in August 2033. Her son is not a minor child, so no
§ 51.1-162 B allowance is payable; her mother, living, is passed over because a designation has
been made and the designee is alive. The advisory expired by its own terms twenty-six months
earlier and the act never tells her to look again. **This is F10, reproduced after the F10 fix, on
a member who did everything the statute asked.**

**The further harm is the implicature.** A member reading *"review … before completing payment"*
can reasonably infer that completing payment does something to the designation. It does not.
Store revision 62 was cut to stop the certification telling her something untrue; revision 65
reintroduces an untrue suggestion by implication.

**Direction for the fix.** Either extend the advice or name the real terminus. Minimal form, same
FIND as F1 — and it composes with F1's fix, so one substitution can carry both:

- **Words I would use (both F1 and F2 together):**
  `that the member is advised to review the member's designation of beneficiary under subsection A of § 51.1-162, and the effect of that designation under subsection B of that section, before completing payment, and that the member may make or change that designation under that subsection at any time before the member's death;`

That is +203 characters, creates no duty, adds no defined term, invents no mechanism, and states
only what § 51.1-162 A already provides.

### F3 — SERIOUS — the channel. **This is the Director's question, and my answer is: keep J, drop M**

**The question as the Director put it.** D 1's failure sentence reaches *"every matter this
subdivision requires to be stated"*, so omitting a sentence that creates no right is now a defect
reviewable under subsection M and reportable under subsection J. The Director accepted this at
MOO-188 §3.4, did not except the advisory, and asked whether the channel is worth more argument
than the advice is worth.

**What I verified before answering.**

1. **The channel is live for the person it is meant to protect, not merely for the member.** D 1's
   failure sentence gives a non-member *"no later than 30 days after the date on which the notice
   required by subdivision G 4 is given to that person."* G 4 requires that notice to be given,
   among others, **"to any person who claimed any benefit payable on the member's death and whom
   the Board determined is not entitled to it."** So the passed-over mother is noticed, and M 2's
   standing class reaches her twice over — *"person who has claimed any benefit payable on the
   death of a member"* and *"person to whom this section requires a notice to be given in respect
   of a benefit payable on the death of a member."* **The survivor branch is not theoretical.**
2. **The remedy is nil.** § 2.2-4029: *"it may compel agency action unlawfully and arbitrarily
   withheld or unreasonably delayed **except that the court shall not itself undertake to supply
   agency action committed by the basic law to the agency**"*; relief is to *"suspend or set it
   aside and remand."* No court can make a designation for a dead member. Verified at source.
3. **The worst outcome is blocked twice.** A court will not construct an estoppel against the
   Board: equitable estoppel does not run against a governmental entity acting in its governmental
   capacity — *Segaloff v. City of Newport News*, 209 Va. 259, 163 S.E.2d 135 (1968), and its
   progeny. And § 2.2-4029 independently bars the court from supplying the agency action.
4. **Fee shifting exists but is probably defeated.** § 2.2-4030 A entitles a person who
   *"substantially prevails on the merits"* to costs and fees where *"the agency action was in
   violation of law"*, capped at **$25,000**; § 2.2-4030 C charges any award *"against the
   operating expenses of the agency for the fiscal year in which the assessment is made"* and bars
   reimbursement *"from any other source"* — so it lands on VRS administration, not on the trust or
   the appropriation subsection K contemplates. **The Board's answer is § 2.2-4027(iii): observance
   of required procedure matters only *"where any failure therein is not mere harmless error."*
   Omitting advice that creates no right is the paradigm of harmless error.** I think the Board
   wins this, and I am not willing to rate the fee exposure above moderate on its own.
5. **Frequency.** On MOO-183's published counts and my own dials (§4), expected deaths in service
   among purchasers over the whole pilot are **0.12 to 0.92**. The survivor branch is expected to
   fire **fewer than once**.

**Now the cost that is real, and it is not any of the above.**

**The advisory is the single most omittable item in D 1, and it carries a share of the failure
sentence's exposure out of all proportion to its content.** The other six items are long,
interlocking propositions of law which a certification template will lift as one block from the
statute; they are present or absent together. The advisory is short, separable, in a different
register, and is exactly the line a plain-language template editor rewrites as *"Remember to check
your beneficiary!"* — which, because it drops *"under subsection A of § 51.1-162"*, is arguably a
failure to state the matter at all. **The one item with no legal content is the one most likely to
generate the contested case.**

And the member branch of the clock has a use the advisory's content does not justify. M 2 provides
that *"the filing of a request for reconsideration … tolls the running of the member's final
qualifying retirement date and of any period within which the member or applicant must act,"*
capped at 120 days aggregate. A member who wants more time has, after revision 65, a seventh
ground on which to allege a defect in her certification. I rate this **marginal, not new** — the
tolling path already existed with six grounds, and the advisory adds a seventh. I say so rather
than inflate it.

**My answer to the Director.** The channel is cheap, but it is cheap for the same reason the advice
is cheap — the predicate event is rare. That is not the asymmetry that decides it. The asymmetry
that decides it is this: **M review's only possible output is a remand that cannot give the
claimant anything, while the J report gives the money committees precisely the fact the channel
would surface, at no litigation cost and with no claimant needed.** Reporting dominates review here
on both limbs.

**Direction for the fix (one added sentence, no enacted word touched).**

- **FIND** (resolves **1×** in §1):
  `and the Board shall state every such failure in the report required by subsection J. The member shall file the notice of retirement required by § 51.1-153`
- **Words I would use:**
  `and the Board shall state every such failure in the report required by subsection J. A failure to state the advice this subdivision requires to be stated is not a failure for the purposes of subsection M, and the Board shall state every such failure in the report required by subsection J. The member shall file the notice of retirement required by § 51.1-153`

**The counter-argument, stated honestly because the Director should weigh it.** An advisory excepted
from M review is enforceable by nobody: J reporting depends on the Board noticing and disclosing its
own omission, and there is no funded inspector. That is a real objection. My answer is that M review
depends on the same noticing, by a claimant who gains nothing from noticing, so J-only loses very
little — but the Director may legitimately conclude that an unenforceable advisory should not be
compelled at all, and that conclusion is theirs and not mine.

### F4 — MODERATE — the placement splits the series' only forward reference from its resolution

**What is wrong.** The series now runs: [1] G 4 / § 51.1-162 displacement — [2] § 51.1-165 C 2
greater-of — [3] *no other option governs* — [4] **§ 51.1-162 B's rule** — [5] **the advisory** —
[6] § 51.1-162 C's rule — [7] § 51.1-162 A's rule. (Numbering is mine; the series has seven items,
verified.)

Item [4] ends by pointing forward: *"only where no benefit is payable under **subsection C of that
section**"*. Item [6] is what explains subsection C. Before this revision those two sat adjacent.
**The advisory is now wedged between a forward reference and its resolution.** That is not a grammar
break — the referent still resolves, and I verified that no short-form referent crosses the
insertion (N2) — but it is a reading-order break, and reading order is what the Director's attack 4
asks about.

There is a second, independent reason the placement is wrong. The advisory is about the
**subsection A** designation. The only item in the series that says what subsection A does is item
[7], which now *follows* the advisory. A member reading in order is told to review her designation
under subsection A two items before she is told what subsection A provides.

**Concrete failure scenario.** A member reads item [4], hits *"subsection C of that section"*, and
reads on to find out what subsection C does. The next thing she reads is an instruction about her
beneficiary form. She loses the thread of the one proposition in the series that limits when the B
allowance is payable at all.

**Direction for the fix.** Move it to the end of the series, where it is adjacent to the subsection
A item and disturbs nothing. §6.0-65 item 2's recorded reasoning — that the advice must be *its own
item* and visibly advice — is preserved, not contradicted. Two anchored edits:

- **FIND 1** (1× in §1): `and no other designation has been made; that the member is advised to review the member's designation of beneficiary under subsection A of § 51.1-162 before completing payment; that subsection C of § 51.1-162`
  → `and no other designation has been made; that subsection C of § 51.1-162`
- **FIND 2** (1× in §1): `in the order of precedence that section states.`
  → `in the order of precedence that section states; and that the member is advised to review the member's designation of beneficiary under subsection A of § 51.1-162 before completing payment.`
- and the current closing item's `; and that where no benefit is payable` becomes `; that where no benefit is payable`.

**If the Director takes F1's and F2's wording, apply it here rather than to the current position.**

### F5 — MODERATE — advice with no route, and the cheapest nominal-compliance path defeats it

**What is wrong.** The advisory names no manner, no form, no office and no enclosure. § 51.1-162 A
says the designation *"must be made in a manner prescribed by the Board"* — so the route exists, but
it lives in Board procedure and nothing in § 51.1-142.4 requires the Board to put it in front of the
member at the moment it compels the advice. §6.0-65 item 7 says this is deliberate and that enclosing
her designation of record would be *"a new duty with an administrative cost that nobody has agreed."*
That is a fair answer on cost. It is not an answer to salience.

**The cheapest compliance path.** The Board drops the 137 characters into the certification template
verbatim, as paragraph 7 of 7, in the same typeface and the same register as the six propositions of
law that precede it. Nominal compliance is complete and unimpeachable. A member reading six dense
statements about §§ 51.1-162, 51.1-163, 51.1-165 and 51.1-165.01 encounters the advisory as more of
the same and does nothing. **The F10 harm recurs at exactly the prior rate, and the statute's own
compliance record shows the advisory was given every time.**

**The act knows how to make something stick and chose not to here.** Two sentences later in the same
subdivision: *"The Board shall not accept an election until the member has filed with the Board a
written acknowledgment of the member's obligations under subsections E, F, and G."* That machinery
already exists and is already a precondition to acceptance.

**Direction for the fix, with its trade-off stated.** The cheapest real fix is to add the designation
review to the existing acknowledgment, which costs the Board one line on a form it already collects.
**But that moves the advisory measurably closer to a member obligation, which is the thing MOO-188
§3.3 deliberately avoided.** I name the option; the trade is the Director's. A lighter alternative
that keeps the no-duty property: require the Board to state *"the manner prescribed by the Board
under that subsection for making or changing a designation"* alongside the advice — a Board duty, not
a member one.

### F6 — MODERATE — a contributory-fault argument, in the one forum where the Board decides

**What is wrong.** *"Is advised to review … before completing payment"* creates no duty (see N3). But
advice with a deadline attached is the raw material for a fault argument, and the General Assembly
will have enacted the words.

**Concrete failure scenario.** A survivor files an M 2 reconsideration on some adjacent ground — that
the certification's description of the death benefit was compressed to the point of misleading, say,
or that the Board's G 4 notice was late. In the Board's written reconsideration decision appears:
*"The certification advised the member to review her designation of beneficiary before completing
payment, as subdivision D 1 requires. She did not."* That sentence is legally irrelevant to her
entitlement — a § 51.1-162 B allowance is fixed by statute and is not reduced for the member's
inattention — but **M 2 reconsideration is decided by the Board, not by a court**, and the sentence
is rhetorically decisive in that forum. The claimant must then take it to circuit court to have it
disregarded.

**Why this is moderate and not serious.** There is no doctrinal footing for it. Virginia benefits
entitlements are statutory and are not subject to contributory fault, and the advisory confers no
right the member could waive. The risk is argumentative and confined to the administrative stage.
I decline to rate it higher.

**Direction for the fix.** If the Director wants it closed, one clause after the advisory:
`and that nothing in this subdivision requires the member to review the designation or affects any
benefit by reason of the member's not having reviewed it`. I would not spend the characters unless
F3's M-limb exception is declined, in which case it becomes worth more.

### F7 — MODERATE — the failure sentence catches omission and misses misstatement

**What is wrong.** D 1's failure sentence reaches *"a **failure to state** … every matter this
subdivision requires to be stated"*, and M 2's general limb reaches *"a **failure** of the Board to
state in a certification or notice a matter this section requires to be stated in it."* Both are
framed on absence. **Neither reaches a statement that is present and wrong.**

For the six propositions of law this matters less: they will be copied verbatim. For the advisory it
matters most, because the advisory is the item a plain-language rewrite will paraphrase rather than
drop.

**Concrete failure scenario.** The Board's vendor renders the advisory as *"Your beneficiary
designation on file with VRS will apply to this purchase."* That is present, so it is not an
omission; it is also wrong, because the designation on file may defeat the B allowance entirely. The
member is actively misled, there is no channel, and the J report records the matter as stated.
**The channel the Director is worried about catches the harmless case and misses the harmful one.**

**Direction for the fix.** This is a property of a sentence enacted at revision 62, not of the 137
characters, so I do not propose amending it inside this cut. If the Director wants it, the words are
`a failure to state … every matter this subdivision requires to be stated in it, **or the statement
of any such matter otherwise than as this subdivision requires it to be stated**,`. **It should be
carded, not cut here.** Note that it interacts with F3: an advisory excepted from M review needs no
misstatement limb either.

### F8 — MODERATE — title and single object. **For the memo's risk section**

**What is wrong.** Va. Const. art. IV, § 12: *"No law shall embrace more than one object, which shall
be expressed in its title."* The act's title line reads:

> *"…relating to the Virginia Retirement System; purchase of prior active duty military service
> credit by state employees; cost; pilot program; post-retirement employment; reports."*

Subdivision D 1 now compels **seven** statements about death benefits under §§ 51.1-162, 51.1-163,
51.1-165 and 51.1-165.01. **The title mentions death benefits, beneficiaries and designations
nowhere.** Six of the seven arrived at store revision 62 and the advisory is the marginal seventh, so
**this exposure is pre-existing and I am not attributing it to this cut** — but the advisory is the
first item that is not a description of the purchase's consequences but an instruction to act under
an unamended death-benefit statute, and it is the item a reader would point at.

**Concrete failure scenario / litigation surface.** A member of the General Assembly, or a party in
later litigation over a denied death benefit, argues that the act's beneficiary-designation content
was not expressed in its title. Virginia construes the clause liberally and sustains a title that
gives fair notice and does not mislead — see *Dean v. Paolicelli*, 194 Va. 219 (1952) — and I think
the Commonwealth wins. **I rate the challenge unlikely to succeed and worth disclosing anyway,
because the cure is free.**

**Direction for the fix.** Not in this cut. Add `death benefits` or `beneficiary designations` to the
title line when the title is next opened — which §7 item 129 answer (7) will require in any event if
the Director ever takes the § 51.1-162 C conforming amendment. **Editor: this one belongs in the
memo's risk section.**

### F9 — MINOR — a passive with no human actor

**What is wrong.** *"that the member **is advised** to review."* The carrier sentence's subject is
*"The Board's certification"* — a document. §6.0-65 item 1 answers that *"the operative verb belongs
to the Board's carrier sentence … so the actor, the addressee, the writing and the moment are all
already named."* That works pragmatically, and it is why I rate this minor. The residue is that the
item is, read strictly, a **statement of fact that advice was given** rather than the giving of it,
and it is the only item in a seven-item list of propositions of law that is self-verifying: it is
true because the Board states it.

**Concrete failure scenario.** Thin, which is why this is minor: a member asserts in reconsideration
that the certification recited she had been advised when no one advised her. The Board answers that
the recital is the advice. It wins. The exchange should not have been possible.

**Direction for the fix.** +4 characters, and it keeps every no-duty property:

- **FIND** (1× in §1; 3× in the document — the others are §0.0-65's and §8's record quotes, so
  scope the substitution to §1): `that the member is advised to review the member's designation`
- **Words I would use:** `that the Board advises the member to review the member's designation`

### F11 — MINOR — the issue description overstates §5.43's vocabulary claim

**What is wrong.** The issue description states: *"'Designation of beneficiary' is asserted to be
§ 51.1-162 A's own noun."* **§ 51.1-162 A does not contain the phrase "designation of beneficiary."**
Verified at source. A's actual words are *"the designated beneficiary"*, *"Each member shall
designate who is to receive a refund of accumulated contributions"*, *"The designation must be made
in a manner prescribed by the Board"*, and *"If no designation has been made."*

**§5.43 and §6.0-65 item 4 are accurate as drafted** — they claim the component vocabulary is A's,
and list the three phrases correctly; neither claims the compound appears. The overstatement is in
the issue description's restatement of them. **The compound itself is new to the act (0 → 1 in §1)
and new to § 51.1-162**, and it is nonetheless the right noun, because A does pay *"the designated
beneficiary"* and does call the act *"the designation."* **No change required to the text.** I record
it because the claim as put to me did not hold as put.

## 4. Fiscal assumption challenge

**The load-bearing assumption**, never written down because the cut carried no fiscal note:
**the advisory changes no behaviour and therefore costs nothing.**

**The test.** Suppose it works. A member who redesignates from a non-qualifying beneficiary to a
qualifying one converts, in a death-in-service case, a § 51.1-162 A lump-sum refund of accumulated
contributions into a § 51.1-162 B lifetime allowance. **B costs the trust more than A. A working
advisory therefore raises cost, not lowers it** — the direction is against the Lab's published
figures, not for them.

**The model.** `fiscal195.py` — my own file. MOO-183's published election counts; MOO-183's engine
is **not** re-run and no team file was read or modified. Exposure window from the act's own dials
(subsections E and F, and the two retirement-date definitions in subsection A). Mortality, the
non-qualifying-designee share, the switch rate and the per-case delta are **my assumptions**, each a
named constant the Modeler can move.

| | recommended, 12-mo | + option (c) | S8 @ 4.00% | S8 @ 5.00% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| elections (MOO-183) | 18.9 | 18.4 | 85.0 | 73.0 |
| expected deaths in service, central | 0.128 | 0.124 | 0.574 | 0.493 |
| expected deaths in service, upper | 0.206 | 0.200 | 0.924 | 0.794 |
| **cost caused by the advisory, central** | **$2,552** | **$2,484** | **$11,475** | **$9,855** |

**Result: the assumption survives, but by less than I expected and I will not overstate it.** The
largest cell is **$18,488** over the whole four-year pilot — about **1.4%** of the recommended row's
$1.3M and well inside the package's stated two-significant-figure resolution. **But reaching
$100,000 needs only a 5× joint error** in mortality, cohort, switch rate or the per-case delta, not
the 40× I assumed before running it. **Recommendation: no change to any published figure; the
Modeler should hold the 5× as the sensitivity of record rather than treating the effect as zero.**

**The uncomfortable symmetry, which belongs to the Director and not to me.** The same dials say the
F10 harm the advisory exists to prevent is expected **0.03 to 0.14 times over the whole pilot** — once
every seven to thirty pilots. The advice side of the trade is as small as the channel side. That is
an argument about whether the advisory should exist, which the Director has decided; **I label it as
such and do not press it.** The counter is sound: the harm when it lands is total for one family, and
the fix costs nothing.

## 6. What I checked and found sound — eleven null results, stated explicitly

- **N1 — Series grammar.** Seven items. Items 2–6 open `that `, the closing item opens `and that `,
  separators uniform. The insertion breaks nothing. **Verified by script.**
- **N2 — Referents.** Eight short-form `that section` / `that subsection` referents in the series.
  **Every one resolves inside its own item, and none crosses the insertion point.** The advisory
  itself contains no short-form referent. **Verified by script.** This is attack 4's referent
  question and it is a clean null; F4 is a reading-order point, not a referent failure.
- **N3 — No duty, no waiver, no new right.** *"Is advised to"* carries no imperative, no period and
  no consequence. The decisive internal evidence is two sentences later in the same subdivision:
  *"a written acknowledgment of the member's obligations under subsections **E, F, and G**"* — the
  subdivision enumerates the member's obligations exhaustively and the advisory is not among them.
  A member who never reviews breaks nothing and loses nothing. **Attack 2 is a null**, subject only
  to F6's argumentative residue.
- **N4 — Defined terms.** No term added, no definition touched. *"Member"*, *"designation"* and
  *"payment"* carry the meanings they already carry. `designation` 3→4, `designated` 5, unmoved.
- **N5 — Cross-references.** J 1's catch-all *"every other matter this section requires the Board to
  state in that report"* reaches D 1's *"every such failure"*; M 2's category is already named by
  D 1's failure sentence. **Nothing stranded, no conforming amendment required.** §5.43 direction 2
  confirmed independently.
- **N6 — Dillon's Rule and local duties.** Subsection L: *"Nothing in this section imposes any duty
  or cost upon a county, city, or town, upon any other political subdivision … upon a local school
  board, or upon any local retirement system."* The advisory runs to the Board and the member only.
  **No case.**
- **N7 — Compelled speech / First Amendment.** The compelled speaker is a state agency speaking in
  its own voice in its own certification. Government speech is not subject to free-speech scrutiny
  — *Pleasant Grove City v. Summum*, 555 U.S. 460 (2009); *Walker v. Texas Div., Sons of Confederate
  Veterans*, 576 U.S. 200 (2015). The employer-policy publication duty in § 51.1-142.2 B 4 e is a
  different provision and is not touched. **No case.**
- **N8 — §7 item 129 answer (7) is not enlarged.** Answer (7) is about an **operative override**: the
  act changes the legal effect of § 51.1-162 C's waiver period, so a reader of § 51.1-162 C is
  misled by what § 51.1-162 C says. **The advisory overrides nothing.** A reader of § 51.1-162 A
  learns exactly what the law is; her ignorance of the advisory costs her nothing, because the
  advisory changes no right she has. **Attack 5's answer is: no, this item does not make (7) worse.**
  The new exposure it does create is a different and smaller one — an instruction with no addressable
  machinery — and that is **F5**, not (7).
- **N9 — Sentence boundaries and ordinal references.** The insertion falls inside an existing sentence
  and contains no period. §1 carries exactly **ten** ordinal sentence references; none is to a
  sentence of subdivision D 1. No *"the preceding proposition"*, no *"the foregoing"*, no reference
  to the certification's items by count or ordinal. **Adding a seventh item strands nothing.**
  **Verified by script**, confirming §5.43 check 1.
- **N10 — Fiscal.** The zero-cost assumption survives; $2,500–$18,500 over the pilot. §4.
- **N11 — The worst legal outcome is blocked twice.** A court constructing a designation, or
  estopping the Board into paying a benefit the Code does not authorise, is barred both by
  § 2.2-4029's prohibition on the court supplying agency action and by *Segaloff*'s rule that
  estoppel does not run against a governmental entity acting in its governmental capacity.

## 7. Addendum — the conservator residual, which the record directs to this pass

**Outside the bounded scope. Offered because `bill-text-s8-item-list` asks for it by name, not
because I am reopening anything.** Item 129's residual:

> *"A conservator acting for a living member under a disability receives the subdivision D 1
> certification and is in no class in subdivision M 2. The next counsel pass is asked to say whether
> that is a defect or a non-case."*

**My answer: a non-case, for one reason.** M 2's class is a list of **parties in interest**, not of
representatives. A conservator appointed under Chapter 20 of Title 64.2 does not need to appear in
that list, because the conservator does not assert a claim of their own — they exercise **the
member's** standing, and *"a member"* is the first category M 2 names. The act's other fiduciary
references are in G 4 and are needed precisely because they concern **death** benefits, where the
member has no standing left to exercise and the fiduciary must therefore be named in their own
right.

**The residual risk is narrow and real enough to card.** M 2 opens *"A member … adversely affected by
a determination"* and then requires *"written request filed"* — if the Board reads the filing
requirement as personal to the member, a conservator's filing could be refused at intake, forcing a
petition that would plainly succeed. The one-clause cure, if the Director wants it, is to add
`or a fiduciary within the meaning of § 51.1-1012 acting for any of them` to M 2's class.

**Recommendation: card it on MOO-97, do not cut it here.** It is not in this pass's scope and the
answer above should be enough to close the residual as asked.
