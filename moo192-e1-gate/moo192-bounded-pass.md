## 1. The strongest case against, in the opposition's voice

> *This is the third try at one sentence, and the Lab is grading its own paper.*
>
> The Commonwealth is being asked to put $148,000 of general fund behind a single member's military-service purchase, and the sentence that decides who gets the money for that has now been rewritten twice under fatal findings and is going to the Division of Legislative Services having been read, in its current form, by the office that wrote it and by nobody else. Each prior version was defended in writing as sound by a competent drafter on the morning it was found fatal. The record in §6.0-61 and §6.0-62 is a record of confident drafters.
>
> And look at what the sentence does. It takes a member who has stopped working for the Commonwealth — who is not an employee, who is drawing nothing, who has filed nothing — and it directs the Board to pay her survivor a lifetime retirement allowance computed as though she had died at her desk, on creditable service she did not earn and the Commonwealth bought for her, with the statutory in-service requirement switched off by fiat. In our member's case that converts a one-time refund of her own contributions into an annuity for her husband's life worth several hundred thousand dollars in present value, on a thirty-month purchase that cost her $21,400. The General Assembly has never granted a death-in-service benefit to a separated member before. The Lab's own fiscal work has not priced this limb separately, and §2 of the bill text — the plain-English section-by-section summary that the committee staff will actually read — **does not mention §§ 51.1-162 or 51.1-165 once.** The single most consequential and most litigated sentence in the act is invisible in the act's own summary.
>
> Worse, the sentence keeps producing classes nobody meant to create and classes nobody meant to exclude. The act admits as an "eligible member" a state employee who retired early years ago on a reduced allowance and came back to covered work — § 51.1-155 B 1 contemplates her expressly — and then, by the interaction of the "qualifying retirement" definition with clause (i)'s own carve-out, makes it impossible for her purchase to ever vest on her death. She can pay her $21,400, carry a $148,000 reservation, die, and get a refund. The certification the Board hands her says the opposite. That is not a drafting typo; that is a program that takes money from a worker for a death protection it cannot deliver to her, and tells her in writing that it can.
>
> Each individual repair is small. That is the point. Three revisions in, the repairs are still small, and they are still being found by the first person who looks.

## 4. The findings in full

### F-A — serious — the returned-to-work retiree is admitted to the program and cannot be reached by it

**What the act does.** "Eligible member" requires "a member in service" who, among eight clauses, "(v) has not reached his unreduced retirement eligibility date." Clause (i) of subdivision G 4 vests the purchase on death, and its second sentence takes that away: *"Clause (i) of this subdivision does not apply where the member has retired in a retirement that is not a qualifying retirement before the date of death."* "Qualifying retirement" is defined as a service retirement under § 51.1-153 or § 51.1-155.1 A or C *"the allowance for which retirement is calculated in accordance with subdivision A 1 of § 51.1-155 and is not reduced under subdivision A 2 or A 3 of that section."*

**Failure scenario, with facts.** A state employee retires on 1 July 2027 at age 55 under § 51.1-153 B 1 with 22 years of service. She is a Plan 2 member, so her allowance is reduced under § 51.1-155 A 3. On 1 March 2029 she returns to covered state employment as an agency analyst. § 51.1-155 B 1 governs her expressly: *"if a beneficiary of a service retirement allowance under this chapter … is at any time in service as an employee in a position covered for retirement purposes … his retirement allowance shall cease while so employed,"* and *"Any member who retires and later returns to covered employment shall not be entitled to select a different retirement option for a subsequent retirement."* She is again a member in service. She satisfies every clause of "eligible member": she is a state employee; she is covered under this chapter; she is not in the hybrid program; § 51.1-142.2 B 2 permits *"any member in service"* to purchase prior active duty military service and does not bar a member who once retired; and clause (v) is satisfied **precisely because** her earlier retirement was reduced — she has not reached her unreduced retirement eligibility date. She elects, pays $21,400 for 30 months, the Board certifies a $148,000 subsidy and credits the credit. Her position is abolished. She dies before retiring again.

**Result.** Her 2027 retirement was a service retirement under § 51.1-153 whose allowance *was* reduced under § 51.1-155 A 3. It is therefore **"a retirement that is not a qualifying retirement,"** and it occurred "before the date of death." **Clause (i) does not apply.** Her purchase does not vest. Subdivision G 2 reduces the credit and refunds her payments; the reservation is released. Her husband receives nothing from the purchase. And E1's own condition fails independently, because she "has retired before the date of death."

**Why the class is not a curiosity.** It is closed under the definitions. Clause (v) of "eligible member" requires that she has *not* reached her unreduced retirement eligibility date, which for a returned retiree is only possible if her prior retirement was reduced — which is exactly what makes that retirement a non-qualifying retirement under the bill's own definition. **Every returned-to-work retiree who can qualify as an eligible member is a member for whom clause (i) can never apply.** The act admits her and cannot deliver to her.

**Authority.** § 51.1-155 B 1 (return to covered employment; allowance ceases; subsequent retirement contemplated); § 51.1-142.2 B 2 (*"any member in service"*); § 51.1-153 B 1 and § 51.1-155 A 3 (reduced early retirement for a Plan 2 member); the bill's own definitions of "eligible member" clause (v) and "qualifying retirement"; clause (i)'s second sentence.

**Answer to residual (a), and this is the part that matters.** The hardening is needed, and **the Director's proposed words would make this member's position worse, not better.** *"Has not retired under this chapter"* excludes her expressly: she did retire under this chapter. A reference to an effective date of retirement under § 51.1-153 does the same. The Director's framing — a retirement from *another* system, clause (i)'s result, predating store revision 62 — identifies the right provision and the wrong class. The defect is inside this chapter, and the test that fixes it is not *where* she retired but *whether she is retired*.

**Fix direction (one sentence; Drafting Specialist writes it).** Make both clause (i)'s second sentence and E1's condition turn on a retirement **in effect at the date of death** rather than on a retirement ever taken — e.g. a retirement "under which a retirement allowance is in payment to the member, or payable to the member, at the date of death." A member whose allowance ceased under § 51.1-155 B 1 is then not excluded, and the qualifying retiree of MOO-161 still is. Both occurrences must move together: fixing one leaves the other excluding her.

### F-B — serious — the waiver stops at § 51.1-162 and the in-service predicate that sets the refund amount is in § 51.1-124.3

**What the act does.** E1 ends: *"and for the purposes of § 51.1-162 the requirement that the member be in service shall not apply."*

**The provision it does not reach.** § 51.1-124.3 defines "accumulated contributions" and then provides: *"If a member is retired for disability from a cause which is compensable under the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act …, **dies in service prior to retirement**, or requests a refund of contributions in accordance with § 51.1-161, 'accumulated contributions' shall include all member contributions paid by the employer on behalf of the member on and after July 1, 1980, and all interest which would have accrued to these funds."* That gross-up is conditioned on dying **in service**. E1's waiver is expressly limited "for the purposes of § 51.1-162." The definition is in § 51.1-124.3.

**Failure scenario, with facts.** A Plan 1 member, hired 1986, age 59, elects and pays; her position is abolished; she dies 20 days later, not in service. Her designated beneficiary is her adult daughter. § 51.1-162 B pays nothing, because B pays only to a designee who is the member's "(i) surviving spouse, (ii) minor child, or (iii) parent(s)." § 51.1-162 A therefore pays accumulated contributions to the daughter. Because the member did not *die in service*, the second sentence of the § 51.1-124.3 definition does not operate, and the employer-paid member contributions made on her behalf from 1980 until the 2012 member-paid transition, **and all interest that would have accrued on them**, are excluded from what the daughter receives. For a long-service Plan 1 member that is the larger part of the account. The same gap bites the surviving spouse who elects, under § 51.1-162 B's last sentence, to take "the member's accumulated contributions or lump sum payment … in lieu of any other benefits under this section" — an election G 4's own release sentence names expressly, so the act contemplates the branch and underpays it.

**Why this is E1's problem and not someone else's.** The entire premise of E1 is that this member should be treated as having died in service. The act achieves that for § 51.1-162 and stops. The residue is a cash difference in a branch the act itself names.

**Authority.** § 51.1-124.3, definition of "Accumulated contributions," second sentence; § 51.1-162 A and B (verified verbatim, see §5); G 4's release sentence.

**Fix direction.** Widen the waiver by one phrase — "for the purposes of § 51.1-162 and of the definition of 'accumulated contributions' in § 51.1-124.3" — or replace the negative waiver with a deeming provision: the member "shall be deemed to have died in service for the purposes of this chapter." The deeming form also closes F-D's and F-H's edges in the same words.

### F-C — serious — the release is conditioned on a payment the act requires first

**The three provisions, in order.**

- G 4 sentence 20: the certified subsidy *"is settled … on the earliest of (a) **the date of the member's death**, (b) the effective date of a disability retirement allowance …, and (c) …"*
- Subsection N sentence 1: *"**Upon the settlement** of a certified subsidy under subdivision G 1 or G 4, the Commonwealth shall pay the amount settled to the retirement system from an appropriation made for that purpose."*
- G 4 sentence 16: where no § 51.1-162 allowance is payable, the Board *"shall release the amount settled … and shall restore it to the amount available …, **to the extent the amount settled has not been paid to the retirement system under subsection N** …"*

**Failure scenario, with facts.** Member dies 18 July 2030 with a $148,000 settled subsidy. Her designated beneficiary is her brother. Settlement occurs on 18 July 2030 by sentence 20. Subsection N's duty arises "upon the settlement," so the Commonwealth's $148,000 goes to the retirement system. The Board then determines that no allowance is payable under § 51.1-162 B, because a brother is not a spouse, minor child or parent, and § 51.1-162 A pays him her contributions. Sentence 16 now directs a release — but only "to the extent the amount settled has not been paid under subsection N," and it has been. **The release is nil. The Commonwealth has funded $148,000 of subsidy for a death benefit that no person receives, and the pilot's cap in subdivision D 2 has been consumed for it.** That is F1's economic harm, in a class E1 does not reach, through a provision that exists only because of E1.

**Why it is not already answered.** Sentence 22 redetermines the amount settled within 90 days after the Board learns of the death, which means in practice the N payment follows the redetermination rather than the death. But that is operational sequencing, not text. The act fixes N's trigger at settlement and fixes settlement at the date of death, and gives the Board no instruction to withhold the N payment until it knows whether an allowance is payable. Sentence 16's own clocks — 60 days after the election or after the determination that no allowance is payable — are not sequenced against N at all. **The outcome of a $148,000 question depends entirely on which clerk acts first.**

**Fix direction.** Sequence them. Either make N's duty arise no earlier than the later of the G 4 redetermination and the Board's determination whether a § 51.1-162 allowance is payable, or convert sentence 16's release into a repayment by the retirement system to the Commonwealth so that it works whether or not N has been performed. The second is smaller and cannot create a timing race.

### F-D — moderate — **this one is on E1's own words**

**The words.** *"where clause (i) of this subdivision applies and the member has not retired **before** the date of death."*

**Failure scenario, with facts.** Member files the § 51.1-153 notice stating 1 August 2030 as her effective date of retirement in a qualifying retirement, and elects a joint and last-survivor option under § 51.1-165 A 2 naming her husband. Her last day of service is 31 July 2030, as § 51.1-153 E requires. **She dies on 1 August 2030.** Then:

- § 51.1-165 C 1 does not apply: her death did not occur "prior to the effective date of retirement."
- § 51.1-165 C 2 does not apply, for the same reason, so **G 4's greater-of sentence, which is keyed to C 2 alone, never engages.**
- § 51.1-163 does not apply: she did not die "**after** the effective date of retirement," and § 51.1-163's refund limb is in any event excluded "unless the retirement allowance is then being paid in accordance with any of the optional benefits provided for in § 51.1-165."
- § 51.1-165 A 2 **does** apply: *"death benefits that might otherwise be provided shall not be payable upon the death of the member unless death of the member occurs prior to the effective date of retirement as set forth in subsection C."* Her death was not prior. Her husband receives the continued joint-and-survivor fraction.
- And **E1 applies**: clause (i) applies, because she died after the Board accepted the election; clause (i)'s second sentence does not exclude her, because her retirement was a qualifying retirement; and she "has not retired *before* the date of death" — she retired *on* it. So the Board **shall** determine the benefit under § 51.1-162 as on the death of a member in service.

**Result.** Her husband receives the joint-and-survivor continuation **and** a § 51.1-162 B death-in-service allowance, on the same creditable service, by two mandatory commands, with no greater-of rule between them. The subsidy has already settled under G 1 and been paid under N.

**Severity, honestly.** The class is "dies on the exact first day of the month that is her own effective date of retirement, having elected an option." Across a pilot it will probably never happen. But the act *commands* the double payment, the command is unqualified, and the Commonwealth's answer to a benefits claim should not be that the General Assembly cannot have meant it. Moderate, not serious, on frequency alone.

**Fix direction.** Three words and one clause. Make the boundary "on or before the date of death" in **both** E1's condition and clause (i)'s second sentence — they must move together or the same one-day gap reappears for the non-qualifying retirement effective on the date of death — and extend G 4's greater-of sentence from "subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165" to any optional benefit in payment under § 51.1-165 or § 51.1-165.01. Taking the boundary alone would leave her with the joint-and-survivor continuation, which is a coherent result; taking the greater-of alone is enough if the Director prefers not to touch E1's words at all. **This is the finding that, on §8's rule, re-opens MOO-188 §2.** It does not re-narrow the condition, it does not add a window, and it does not touch the retirement-status test.

### F-E — moderate — the certification states a different condition from the operative text

**The two conditions.** D 1's first death proposition: *"where a member whose election the Board has accepted **dies without having retired**, the benefit payable on the member's death is determined under subdivision G 4 and under § 51.1-162 as on the death of a member in service and not under the provisions of subdivision C 1 of § 51.1-165 or of subsection F of § 51.1-165.01 that require benefits to be paid in accordance with § 51.1-163."* E1's condition: *"has not retired before the date of death."*

**They diverge in both directions.**

- F-D's member retires on her date of death. She does **not** die "without having retired," so the certification tells her the G 4 / § 51.1-162 route does not govern her. E1 says it does. **The certification is wrong in her favour and against the act.**
- F-A's returned-to-work retiree dies without having retired *again*, so on its face the certification tells her the G 4 / § 51.1-162 route **does** govern her. Clause (i) and E1 both say it does not. **The certification is wrong against her, on the one matter that decides whether her $21,400 buys her family anything.**

**Why it bites.** D 1's own failure sentence reaches *"every matter this subdivision requires to be stated"* and makes the failure a matter for subsection M review, with the second sentence of M 2 expressly disapplied. A certification that states a required matter incorrectly is at least arguably a failure to state it. So the Board inherits a subsection M proceeding, and possibly a reliance argument, over a paraphrase that had no reason to differ from the words it paraphrases.

**Fix direction.** Do not paraphrase an operative condition in a notice provision. Either reproduce E1's words exactly in D 1's first proposition, or replace the paraphrase with a reference — "where the benefit payable on the member's death falls to be determined under subdivision G 4." This is free and it cannot drift again.

### F-F — moderate — answer to residual (b): the channel is worth one sentence, but not against E1

The Director's reasoning is right on the economics: while the member lives, review restores time to re-designate, which is the only thing worth restoring; after she dies it adds nothing and costs nothing. I am not asking for the point to be re-taken on that reasoning. **The problem is that the living member's half of it barely exists.**

D 1's failure sentence gives a person adversely affected 30 days from the date the certification is given to the member. **The defect is the absence of text.** A member who is never told that § 51.1-162 B pays only a spouse, minor child or parent cannot detect from the certification that she was not told. §6.0-62 item 6 records that the reviewer's discovery rule — *"or from the date she files written evidence of the omission, whichever is later"* — was declined as a policy setting and carded as §7 item 129 answer 3. So the only person with a usable window is a G 4 death-notice recipient, whose 30 days run from the death notice, and for whom the Director correctly says the review "adds nothing."

**My answer:** residual (b) is a non-case on its merits and should not hold E1. But the Director's justification for it depends on a remedy that is time-barred before the member could know she needed it, and that dependency should be recorded against **§7 item 129 answer 3**, not against E1. If item 129 answer 3 is decided against the discovery rule, the designation advisory's review channel should be described in the memo as a remedy for death-notice recipients only. One sentence, in the risk section, not a re-opening.

### F-G — minor — two sentences of subdivision 4 give opposite commands on F1's own facts

G 4 sentence 2: *"Subdivisions 2 and 3 **shall not apply** … if the member, after the Board accepts the election, (i) dies …"* G 4 sentence 54, inside the involuntary-separation limb: *"If the member does not so retire, **subdivision 2 shall apply**, and the Board shall make the determination required by subdivision 2 within 60 days after the earlier of (a) the later of the last day on which the member may retire in a qualifying retirement under this subdivision and the member's final qualifying retirement date, if any, and (b) the date on which the member retires in a retirement that is not a qualifying retirement."*

F1's member satisfies both triggers: her position was abolished 30 June 2030, she did not retire in a qualifying retirement within 90 days, and she died. On sentence 54 the Board must make a subdivision 2 determination around 1 November 2030. If it does, G 4 sentence 28 then bars settlement of the subsidy, and the credit is reduced, so E1's "computed on the member's creditable service, including the service credit credited under this subdivision" would deliver nothing extra. G 4 sentence 32's rescission is drafted for a member who "**thereafter** dies" — after the determination — and does not on its face reach a death that preceded it.

**Why minor and not serious.** Three things point the right way and I think they are decisive: subdivision G 2's own first sentence opens *"If the member does not retire in a qualifying retirement, the Board shall, **except as subdivision 4 provides**, …"*; sentence 2 is subdivision 4's lead rule and is an express disapplication; and applying a retirement deadline to a dead member is close to absurd. The correct outcome is clear. But G 2 sentence 6, which lists the elections for which no subdivision 2 determination is required, names subdivisions 3 and 5 and **omits the clause (i) death case**, so the drafter's own enumeration does not carry it either.

**Fix direction.** Five words in sentence 54: "If the member does not so retire **and no clause of the first sentence of this subdivision applies**, subdivision 2 shall apply …". Or add the death case to G 2 sentence 6's list.

### F-H — minor — D 1 proposition 4 states B's conditions as exhaustive and omits B's own opening limitation

D 1 proposition 4 states that § 51.1-162 B provides an allowance "**only** where no benefit is payable under subsection C of that section and **only** to a surviving spouse, minor child, or parent …". Both "only"s are accurate. But § 51.1-162 B opens *"**To the extent required by § 401(a)(37) of the Internal Revenue Code**, as amended or renumbered, and the regulations thereunder applicable to governmental plans, if a member dies in service, including a member performing active duty military service in the armed forces of the United States, …"* — a limitation the proposition does not state while presenting the subsection's conditions as closed. IRC § 401(a)(37), added by the HEART Act of 2008, requires survivor treatment where a participant dies performing qualified military service; on a strictly textual reading the lead-in qualifies the whole conditional. I do **not** think a Virginia court would read B down to military deaths — the 2009 amendments (cc. 22, 362) that added the lead-in plainly extended an existing general benefit rather than contracting it, and VRS has administered B generally for decades. **This is not a reason to hold E1**, and it is not a reason to re-open anything. It is a reason not to let the certification imply that it has told the member all of B's conditions. One clause, or delete the second "only."

## 6. The load-bearing assumption, tested

E1's cure has no fiscal model in scope and the numbers in F1's scenario are a single case, so the test here is on the **assumption**, not on a spreadsheet.

**The assumption the cure rests on:** that once the in-service requirement is waived, § 51.1-162 **B** pays. Everything E1 is for depends on that one word. B is not a general death benefit — it pays **only** to a designee who is the member's surviving spouse, minor child or parent, and only where no benefit is payable under subsection C.

**The test.** Change one fact in F1's scenario and nothing else: the member's designated beneficiary is her adult daughter rather than her husband, or she is unmarried with no minor children and no surviving parent.

**The result.** § 51.1-162 B pays nothing. § 51.1-162 A pays accumulated contributions. **E1 delivers nothing at all for that member**, and because she did not die in service the § 51.1-124.3 gross-up does not operate either (F-B), so her estate receives *less* than a member who died in service would have. The subsidy settles on the date of death, subsection N requires the Commonwealth to pay it, and G 4's release sentence cannot claw it back once N has been performed (F-C). **The Commonwealth pays the $148,000 and no one receives a benefit for it.** That is F1's harm, unchanged, for a member one designation form away from F1's own.

**Magnitude of what the cure is worth when the assumption holds — illustrative, and the inputs are mine, not F1's.** F1's scenario gives neither average final compensation nor total creditable service, so take a Plan 2 member, AFC $75,000, 30 years of prior creditable service plus the purchased 30 months. By construction of "eligible member" clause (viii), the purchase takes her to her unreduced retirement eligibility date in the month after crediting, so the § 51.1-155 A 3 early-retirement reduction is nil, and the § 51.1-162 B age-60 presumption can only help. § 51.1-155 A 1 gives roughly 1.65–1.70% × $75,000 × 32.5 ≈ **$41,000 a year** unreduced; converted to the 100% joint-and-survivor equivalent at presumed member age 60 it is on the order of **$35,000 a year for her husband's life**, against a one-time refund of her accumulated contributions in the low six figures. **F1 was correctly rated fatal, and the cure is correspondingly valuable.** It also shows why F-C matters: the same $148,000 is either buying that annuity or buying nothing, and which one is decided by a sequencing accident.

**What the Fiscal & Impact Modeler should be asked for, and it is not in this pass:** the share of the eligible population whose designated § 51.1-162 beneficiary is not a spouse, minor child or parent. That fraction is the fraction of the subsidy that F-C currently spends on nothing.
