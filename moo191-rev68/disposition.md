## 3. The seven decisions

### Decision 1 — F1 (Serious): Give the advice an object

**What counsel found.** The advisory tells the member to look and does not tell her what she is looking at. § 51.1-162 B rewards doing nothing and punishes doing the wrong thing: the member who has filed no designation is paid through branch two, in the order of precedence, while the member who has designated a non-qualifying person is paid by neither branch. The advisory urges action without saying which, so a member with no designation who reads it and acts can destroy the protection she already had. Counsel's Ms R scenario is the concrete case and the 137 characters are its but-for cause.

**My recommendation.** **Take it.** This is the only finding on which the enacted words can cause the harm they exist to prevent, and store revision 67 has already done the hard half of the work: item 4 of the series now states **both** branches of § 51.1-162 B, so the certification contains the predicate and D1 only points the member at it. The addition creates no duty, adds no defined term, invents no mechanism and states nothing that is not already in the section it cites. *"That section"* resolves to § 51.1-162, which is named earlier in the same item.

**The case against, stated because you are the one deciding.** Seventy-two more characters in an item that is already the densest in the series, and a member who is told to consider *"the effect of that designation under subsection B"* still has to go and read subsection B to find out what the effect is. The alternative — spelling the trap out in the certification — is a much larger addition and was not asked for.

### Decision 2 — F2 (Serious): The terminus of the advice

**What counsel found.** *"Before completing payment"* is the act's own phrase and it is the wrong deadline for this advice. Subdivision G 5 makes the completion of payment the last moment the **purchase** can be undone, which is why the phrase was chosen; nothing in § 51.1-162 A fixes a **designation** at that moment. A designation is revocable until the member dies. The window in which redesignating changes the § 51.1-162 outcome runs about 27 months on the act's own dials and without end for a member who does not retire, so the advice expires at the first of four legs. The recorded second reason for the deadline at §6.0-65 item 5 was not true and **was withdrawn at store revision 68**; the first reason stands.

**My recommendation.** **Take D2b.** D2a is counsel's wording verbatim and I do not recommend it: with F1's object inserted, *"under that subsection"* takes *"subsection B of that section"* as its nearest antecedent, and the power to designate is in **A**. D2b is the same policy with the referent named — a repair, not a change. **D2c is the same policy if F1 is declined**, where *"that subsection"* is safe because subsection A of § 51.1-162 is then the only subsection named in the item before the pronoun.

**The case against, stated because you are the one deciding.** The longest addition of the three live options, and it states a power the member already has under a section the act does not amend for this purpose. A reader could ask why the certification recites § 51.1-162 A's revocability but not the rest of it. Against that: the present words carry an implicature that completing payment does something to the designation, and it is false — store revision 62 was cut to stop the certification misleading her.

### Decision 3 — F3 (Serious): The channel — the question the Director reserved at MOO-188 §3.4

**What counsel found.** D 1's failure sentence reaches *"every matter this subdivision requires to be stated"*, so omitting the advisory is reviewable under subsection M and reportable under subsection J. The Director accepted that, did not except the advisory, and asked whether the channel is worth more argument than the advice is worth. **Counsel's answer is: keep the subsection J limb, drop the subsection M limb** — because M review's only possible output is a remand that cannot give the claimant anything, § 2.2-4029 barring the court from supplying the designation itself, while the J report gives the money committees the same fact at no litigation cost and with no claimant needed. Counsel also states the honest counter: an advisory excepted from M review is enforceable by nobody.

**My recommendation.** **This one is policy and I make no recommendation on whether to take it.** It reverses a decision the Director took deliberately, and the trade — an unenforceable advisory against a disproportionate litigation surface — is theirs. **On drafting, if it is taken, take D3c.** D3a is counsel's single substitution and has two defects: it says *"is not a failure for the purposes of subsection M"* in the sentence immediately after the one deeming it a failure, so the subdivision asserts both in consecutive sentences, and it turns on *"the advice this subdivision requires to be stated"*, an operative term the subdivision nowhere defines. D3b cures both but excepts *"the advice described in the next sentence"* — a cross-reference by position, which breaks if any later revision inserts a sentence between the two, and then defines the advice in a closing sentence to make the position work. **D3c carves the exception into the deeming clause itself and names the matter in the subdivision's own words, so it needs neither the position nor the definition, and it is 131 characters shorter than D3b.**

**The case against, stated because you are the one deciding.** All three forms create a two-tier failure sentence, where six of the seven matters are reviewable and the seventh is not, and a reader has to hold both tiers in mind. If the Director would rather not have the tier at all, the alternative is to decline F3 and take **D6** (decision 6), which closes the fault argument that the live channel is most likely to produce.

## 8. Drafter's note

**Structure choices.**

1. **Nothing is applied.** The commission was one edit, that edit is cut as store revision 65, and
   each of the seven findings reverses or widens a word approved verbatim at
   [MOO-188](/MOO/issues/MOO-188) §3.3 or §3.4. Choosing among them is policy; drafting them is
   not. Where I think the drafting is wrong I say so and offer the repair, and where the choice is
   yours I say that too.
2. **Candidates are grouped by decision, not by finding**, because F1, F2, F6 and F9 all substitute
   on the same FIND string and cannot be applied independently in sequence. The combined takes at
   §4 exist for that reason, not for convenience.
3. **Each passage is reproduced in full as it would read** — the certification series, or the
   failure sentence, or both where a candidate touches both — rather than as a changed fragment,
   because the test of this text is whether a reader understands the rule without the note.
4. **The whole series is given rather than the single item**, because four of the thirteen
   candidates move or re-point material between items and an item shown alone would hide that.

**Ambiguities I resolved, and how.**

1. **How to mark a delta inside an already-underlined section.** `<u>` and `~~` are the act's
   markers against the **Code**. Subdivision D 1 is inside an open `<u>` run, so using them for a
   candidate's own delta would either nest a marker or assert a deletion from the Code that is not
   happening. **Resolved:** ADDED/STRUCK brackets against the draft, the convention stated at the
   head, and the `<u>`/`</u>` balance reported for every candidate. I record this because the first
   rendering of these candidates used `<u>` and `~~` inside the run and produced markup no drafter
   would accept.
2. **D2a's *"that subsection"*.** Counsel's verbatim words are ambiguous once F1's object is
   inserted before them, because the nearest antecedent becomes subsection B and the power is in A.
   **Resolved:** D2b names the subsection and is recommended; D2a is reproduced as counsel wrote it
   so the choice is visible, not silently improved.
3. **F2 without F1.** D2a and D2b both carry F1's words inside them, so neither could be applied if
