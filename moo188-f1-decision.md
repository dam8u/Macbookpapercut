## 1. Decision 1 — store revision 61's E1 does not travel. Counsel is right.

Counsel's words: *"the subdivision should not go to DLS with E1 in its current form."* **Adopted in
full, and I want the reasoning on the record even though the text has moved, because the next drafter
needs to know why the window is forbidden and not merely why it was replaced.**

The sentence counsel reviewed waived the in-service requirement only *"where … the member dies after
the Board has received the notification of the member's effective date of retirement required by
subdivision D 1 and before that date."* Four things make that fatal rather than serious, and I agree
with each:

1. **The excluded class is created by the act's own machinery, not by the member's mistake.**
   Subdivision D 1 tells her in terms that *"nothing in this subdivision requires the member to file
   that notice any period before the effective date of retirement stated in it."* Subdivision G 4 gives
   her 90 days after an involuntary separation to retire. The act invites her to wait, and then
   withdraws the death benefit because she waited.
2. **She cannot cure it.** The triggering event is her death.
3. **The Commonwealth pays either way.** The subsidy settles *"on … the date of the member's death"* and
   subsection N obliges payment on settlement. So the public cost is incurred and the benefit it buys is
   not delivered.
4. **The loss is total, not partial.** § 51.1-162 A pays her survivor a refund of accumulated
   contributions in place of a § 51.1-162 B allowance. The subdivision exists to improve the death
   benefit and in this class it leaves it worse than the member would have had at store revisions 58
   and 60.

**That is a defect of the first order and it is not a policy setting.** A rule that takes the whole
benefit from a member who did exactly what the statute told her she could do is not a choice between
distributions; it is the act failing at the thing it is for. **No form of E1 keyed to the receipt of a
notification travels to DLS.** Two revisions have now tried to key the sentence to a filing event and
each drew a fatal finding — MOO-161's at store revision 58, F1's at store revision 61. **A third
attempt is out of bounds, and that prohibition is a standing direction and not a comment on this
revision.**

## 2. Decision 2 — the cure travels on the drafter's words, not on counsel's

**Counsel's fix direction:** *"and the member dies before the effective date of any retirement of the
member."*

**What is enacted at store revision 62:** *"and the member has not retired before the date of death."*

**I accept the substitution, and the drafter was right to make it.** The reasoning, because this is the
one place where I am declining counsel's own words on a fatal finding and the next reader is entitled to
know I did it deliberately:

**2.1 Counsel's words put the main beneficiary of the cure on an inference.** The largest part of F1's
class is the member who **never retired at all** — the separated member inside G 4's window, who has
filed no notice and has no effective date of retirement. *"Dies before the effective date of any
retirement of the member"* asks the reader to measure her death against a date that never came into
existence. A sympathetic reader supplies *"if any"*; a hostile one says a condition with no referent is
not satisfied. **I will not cure a fatal finding with a clause whose application to the core of the
class depends on which reader you get.**

**2.2 The enacted words are the act's own.** They are the negative of the carve-out clause (i) already
states in its own second sentence, which the live text carries unchanged: *"Clause (i) of this
subdivision does not apply where the member has retired in a retirement that is not a qualifying
retirement before the date of death."* **One concept, one vocabulary, two sentences that read against
each other without a glossary.** Counsel's formula would have introduced a second way of saying
*"retired"* into the same subdivision.

**2.3 The retiree carve-out counsel insisted on is carried.** This is the specific thing counsel's
handoff warned about — *"please do not re-widen without the retiree carve-out"* — so I tested it rather
than assuming it:

- A member who **retired in a qualifying retirement** and died later: *"has not retired before the date
  of death"* is false, the sentence does not reach her, and § 51.1-163 — *"Death after retirement"* —
  governs her as it governs anyone else in her position. **MOO-161's forfeiture cannot return.**
- A member who **retired in a non-qualifying retirement**: clause (i) does not apply to her at all, by
  its own second sentence, so she never reaches the waiver. **Excluded twice over.**
- A member who **has not retired**: the sentence reaches her, § 51.1-162 is applied as on the death of a
  member in service, and the in-service requirement is waived. **This is F1's class, and it is cured.**

**The three cases are exhaustive and each lands where it should.** So the widening relative to store
revision 61 — back to the scope approved at revisions 54, 58 and 60 — **satisfies counsel's warning
rather than breaching it.** The warning was against re-widening *without* the carve-out. The carve-out
is there, and it is there in clause (i) where it has been all along.

**2.4 The sentence is not re-narrowable.** Recorded so that nobody reads §1 of this document as an
invitation to find a third, cleverer window: **the condition in E1 is to remain a condition on the
member's own retirement status and not on any act of filing, notification, acceptance or Board receipt.**
That is now the second-most-constrained sentence in the act, and deliberately.

## 3. Decision 3 — what moves with E1

Counsel said F1, **M6** and **F10** *"should be read together or the cure will be partial."* Agreed, and
read together they are.

**3.1 M6 — enacted and verified.** E8's proposition about § 51.1-162 C omitted the Code's own in-service
condition, and counsel's point was that the omission *"bites in exactly the class of F1."* The live text
now reads *"that subsection C of § 51.1-162 provides a retirement allowance, **where the member dies in
service** from a cause compensable under the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act (§ 65.2-100 et
seq.)…"* — **1 occurrence in the document and 1 in §1.** **Closed.**

**One point for the next counsel pass, and it is a point about coherence and not a defect I am
asserting.** For the cured F1 member the act's own waiver makes § 51.1-162 C available to her even
though she was not in service, so a certification that states C's in-service condition without stating
the act's waiver would mislead her in the opposite direction from the one M6 identified. **It does state
the waiver** — the first proposition in the same series now reads *"where a member whose election the
Board has accepted **dies without having retired**, the benefit payable on the member's death is
determined under subdivision G 4 and under § 51.1-162 as on the death of a member in service"* (1
occurrence, §1). **So the series is accurate when read whole, which is how a certification is read.** I
record it rather than card it, and the pass is asked to confirm it.

**3.2 F10, first limb — enacted and verified.** The § 51.1-162 B proposition now carries the fact that
decides the designation: an allowance *"only to a surviving spouse, minor child, or parent **whom the
member has designated** under subsection A of that section or who is entitled in the order of precedence
that subsection B of that section states where the member has made no designation or the person
designated has predeceased the member and no other designation has been made."* **The proposition no
longer tells the member something untrue. Closed.**

**3.3 F10, second limb — the designation-review advisory. Not drafted. I approve it.**

Counsel asked for two things and got one. The missing one is *"a compelled statement advising the member
to review her designation of beneficiary before completing payment,"* drafted but not enacted at
[MOO-97](/MOO/issues/MOO-97) §7 item 129 answer (4).

**Approved, and here is the whole of my reasoning.** The member who designated a sister in 2009 and is
survived by her mother gets nothing from the purchase. **Her designation is the single fact that decides
whether the Commonwealth's subsidy buys her family anything, it is the one fact she can change for free
and in a minute, and nothing anywhere in the act tells her to look at it.** Store revision 62 stopped
the certification misleading her. It does not help her. **The cheapest intervention in the package is
also the only one that reaches the member rather than the Board, and I am not going to carry a cured
benefit to DLS with the member uninformed about the thing that decides whether she gets it.**

**One wording change to the drafted words, and the reason.** The drafted clause was *"and that the
member should review the member's designation of beneficiary…"*. **Every other item in that series is a
proposition of law introduced by *"that"*, so *"that the member should review"* reads as a statement
about what the law requires of her, which it is not.** Make it advice on its face and give it its own
place in the series:

> §1):
>
> ```
> where the member has made no designation or the person designated has predeceased the member and no other designation has been made; that subsection C of § 51.1-162
> ```
>
> **READ** (301 characters, **+137**):
>
> ```
> where the member has made no designation or the person designated has predeceased the member and no other designation has been made; that the member is advised to review the member's designation of beneficiary under subsection A of § 51.1-162 before completing payment; that subsection C of § 51.1-162
> ```

**Placement.** Immediately after the § 51.1-162 B proposition, because the designation is B's fact, and
as its own semicolon item rather than inside B's, so the propositions of law stay together and the
advice is visibly advice. **Document goes 306,404 → 306,541; §1 goes 120,888 → 121,025; `<u>` / `</u>`
balance stays 60 / 60 in §1 and 60 / 60 in the document; no figure moves, no designator is renumbered,
no section joins or leaves the act, no defined term is added, the title line does not change.** All
confirmed by applying the substitution to the returned body in a scratch copy.

**3.4 The consequence of approving it, stated rather than discovered later.** Subdivision D 1's failure
sentence reaches *"every matter this subdivision requires to be stated"* in the certification, so **a
failure to give this advice becomes reviewable under subsection M**, with the member's window running
from the certification. **I accept that, and I have thought about which way it cuts.** While the member
lives, the review restores the only thing that matters — time to re-designate — so the channel does the
work subsection M exists for. After she dies it adds nothing, because no remedy can re-designate for
her, and it costs nothing for the same reason. **I am not excepting the advisory from the failure
sentence.** If the counsel pass at §4 thinks the channel is worth more argument than the advice is worth,
it should say so and I will re-take this one point; it is one clause either way.

**3.5 The other six answers at MOO-97 §7 item 129 are not mine to answer here.** Answers (1), (2), (3),
(5), (6) and (7) are open, four of them decide who bears a loss, and **none of them is inside F1's class

## 4. Decision 4 — the condition on travel, and why there is one

**E1 does not go to DLS on my signature. It goes when one bounded counsel pass on store revision 62
returns on the new sentence.**

**The reason is the history of this one sentence and not any doubt about the drafting.**

- **Store revision 58** cured an earlier defect with a freestanding *"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply to the
  member's death."* [MOO-161](/MOO/issues/MOO-161) found that **fatal** — it reached the member who
  retired in a qualifying retirement and died years later.
- **Store revision 61** cured MOO-161 by adding the notification window. [MOO-169](/MOO/issues/MOO-169)
  found **that** fatal — F1, this issue.
- **Store revision 62** cures F1 by deleting the window and conditioning the sentence on the member's
  retirement status. **It has been reviewed by nobody.**

**Two consecutive cures of this sentence, each careful, each by a competent drafter, each producing a
fatal finding in the next pass. The base rate on this sentence is 2 for 2.** My own reading in §2 says
the three cases are exhaustive and land correctly, and I believe it. **That is exactly what the last two
drafters believed.** The thing that caught both was a bounded pass by someone whose job was to break it,
and the cost of that pass is small against the cost of carrying a third fatal finding into a DLS
drafting request.

**So: one bounded counsel pass on store revision 62, confined to the F1 cure and the provisions that
move with it.** Commissioned at §7. **It is a gate, not a courtesy.** If it returns clean, E1 travels
and this decision needs no revisiting. If it returns a finding on the new sentence, the finding comes
back to me and §2's words are re-opened — and nothing else in this document is.

**What the gate does not hold up.** The gate is on **E1 travelling to DLS**. It does not hold up the
§3.3 edit, the rest of store revision 62, the MOO-141 patch, the memo, or the package. **Nothing else in
the programme waits on this.**

## 5. Residuals — named, not cured, and not blockers

**5.1 *"Retired"* is unqualified, and the hardening is one phrase.** E1's condition is *"has not retired
before the date of death"* with no statement of what she retired **from**. On the act's own structure the
gap is closed: a non-qualifying retirement is excluded by clause (i)'s second sentence and a qualifying
retirement is excluded by E1's own condition, so the two carve-outs are exhaustive **for retirements
under this chapter**. A literal reader could ask about a retirement from somewhere else — a member who
drew a retirement from another system years ago and is now a state employee. **In that case both
exclusions fire together and she is outside clause (i) altogether, so her credit does not vest at all.**
That is clause (i)'s result and not E1's, it predates store revision 62, and counsel read clause (i) at
store revision 61 without flagging it. **I am not re-opening clause (i) inside a fatal-finding cure.**
The hardening, if the counsel pass wants it, is one phrase — *"has not retired under this chapter"* or a
reference to an effective date of retirement under § 51.1-153 — and it is put to that pass as a question,
not drafted here.

**5.2 The advisory's review channel.** §3.4. Mine, taken, and open to one re-take on the pass's view.

**5.3 Both residuals are small, both have one-clause answers, and neither is a reason to hold E1.**
Recorded so the pass has them in front of it and so neither comes back as new.
