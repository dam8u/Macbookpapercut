### §6.0-70 Ambiguities resolved in store revision 70 — and three defects recorded rather than cured

Every word enacted here is a word the Policy Director approved verbatim in the disposition at [MOO-191](/MOO/issues/MOO-191) and commissioned at [MOO-208](/MOO/issues/MOO-208). Nothing is added. Where I think something further is needed it is in this note or on a §7 card, not in the text.

1. **Why *"that the Board advises the member to review"* and not *"that the member is advised to review."*** F9, seven characters. **§6.0-65 item 1 gave a good pragmatic answer and a bad drafting answer**, and the Director has said so of their own approved words. The carrier sentence does name the Board — *"The Board shall not accept an election until the Board has, in writing, certified to the member … that …"* — so the actor was never missing from the subdivision. But the **item read alone was self-verifying**, true merely because the Board stated it, and it was the only one of seven that was; the other six state what the Code provides and are true or false independently of who writes them down. **Name the actor in every duty is my own rule and it applies to words I drafted.** *"Advises"* is preferred to *"recommends"* or *"requests"* because it is the Board's own register and imposes nothing: a member who does not take advice has not failed to do anything. Every no-duty property item 1 defends is intact, and item 1 is marked in place.
2. **Why *"before completing payment"* is kept **and** the power to re-designate is stated.** The terminus was never the defect; the defect was the inference a reader draws from it standing alone — that completing payment does something to the designation — when **nothing in § 51.1-162 A fixes a designation at that moment.** Both clauses are kept deliberately: the terminus is the prompt to act now, delivered at the one moment the member is thinking about the purchase, and the new clause removes the implicature that the window shuts then. Read together they say do it now, and know you can change it later. **On the accuracy of the new clause, which is the highest-risk proposition in the item:** *"may make or change that designation under subsection A of that section at any time before the member's death"* is a **correct reading of A and an inference from A's structure, not a quotation of an express grant.** A fixes no moment, and A's own fallback expressly contemplates a later designation — *"and another designation has not been made."* The words are tied to A so the statement follows A if A is ever amended rather than freezing a paraphrase; and A's *"in a manner prescribed by the Board"* governs the **manner**, not the **time**, so a Board procedure purporting to close the window would be contrary to A and would not make the certification false. **No amendment to § 51.1-162 A is needed and none is made.** §5.47 direction 1, rows 1 and 3.
3. **Why the item opens with the full citation and then uses *"that section"* twice inside itself.** §6.0-65 item 3's reason has lapsed with the move — the advisory is now last and the item before it names *"subsection B of § 51.1-162"*, so the short form would have resolved. The full citation is kept for a reason that item did not need: **the item now carries three propositions and shifts to *"that section"* twice inside its own span, so it must supply its own referent and not borrow a neighbour's.** The last item in a series is also the one most likely to be lifted out of it by a certification template, and an item that carries its own citation survives that. Item 3 is marked in place.
4. **Why item 7 of 7, and why it costs nothing.** F4. The Director's reasons at MOO-188 §3.3 and §6.0-65 item 2 were that the advice must be its own semicolon item and visibly advice. **At item 7 of 7 it is still its own item, still introduced by *"that"*, still the only item in a different register — and it is no longer interleaved among the six propositions of law it must not be read as part of.** What the move adds: it stops splitting item 4's forward reference to *"subsection C of that section"* from the item that restates that citation in full, and it sits beside the subsection A proposition it is actually about. **The relocation itself is zero characters, verified and not assumed** — the seven items are undesignated clauses of one sentence and nothing in the act refers to any of them by position (§5.47 direction 2, check 7). On the objection that the least legally substantive item takes the most emphatic position, the Director treats that as a feature and as part of the answer to F5: the closing position is where a reader looks for what to do next. Item 2 is marked in place, reversed as to position and not as to either reason.
5. **F3, form D3c conformed — and the defect it leaves, which is the one thing in this revision I would ask the Director to look at first. Serious.** On **form** the Director's reasons are the drafter's: D3a asserts *"is not a failure for the purposes of subsection M"* in the sentence after the one deeming it a failure; D3b cures that but buys a reference by position, *"the advice described in the next sentence"*, which breaks the day anyone inserts a sentence between them. **D3c carves the exception into the deeming clause itself and names the matter in the subdivision's own words**, so there is no undefined *"advice"* and no reference by position. **Conforming it to F9's wording is not a style choice:** unconformed it would have described *"a failure to state that the member is advised to review…"* while the subdivision required *"that the Board advises the member to review…"*, and the exception would have excepted nothing while the J limb reported nothing. One phrase now does the work in both limbs, which is why it appears exactly three times in §1.
   **What it does not do. Subdivision M 2 does not look at D 1, and three features of the act's own words settle that rather than leave it arguable.**
   - M 2's first sentence reaches *"a failure of the Board to state in a certification or notice a matter this section requires to be stated in it"* on its own terms. The advisory is a matter this subdivision requires to be stated and this subdivision is part of this section.
   - M 2's clock for a failure to state runs 30 days from the certification *"or, where the provision that requires the matter to be stated provides a longer or later period, within that period."* **M 2 therefore anticipates that a provision like D 1 will extend it** — which is exactly what D 1's bespoke clock does: 30 days from the certification for the member, 30 days from the subdivision G 4 notice for a claimant who is not the member. D 1's sentence is **conferring, not constituting**, and an exception to a conferring sentence confers less.
   - §1 carries **six** *"for the purposes of subsection M"* clauses; the other five are all of the declaratory form *"X is a determination of the Board for the purposes of subsection M"*, and **M 2's second sentence says in terms that its own reach operates *"whether or not another provision of this section expressly provides for review of it."*** The act's pattern is that these clauses restate M 2's reach rather than create it.

   **So substitution 4 withdraws D 1's extension and leaves M 2's own channel open.** The member keeps reconsideration on a 30-day clock from the certification; the claimant who is not the member loses the G 4-notice clock, **the only one a survivor could ever have used**; and M 2's second sentence, which D 1 had disapplied for the member, applies again. That is close to the opposite of the trade F3 was taken for.
   **Why it is enacted anyway, and what I did instead of curing it.** The words are the Director's approved words, the cure is a provision nobody has agreed, and **no cure inside D 1 can work** — the defect is that M 2 does not look at D 1, so the clause has to go in M 2, which this commission did not open. It is drafted at **§7 item 135** and **not enacted**. Substitution 5, the J limb, is unaffected and does the whole of what it was taken to do: subdivision J 1's catch-all — *"every other matter this section requires the Board to state in that report"* — picks it up with no conforming amendment. §5.47 direction 2, checks 1 and 2. §6.0-65 item 6 is marked in place, because its heading is no longer the law of the draft.
6. **The F3 residue, and the asymmetry it creates with the re-scoped §7 item 133.** The Director named the residue: D3c's exception names only the **first** of item 7's three propositions, so omitting the power-to-re-designate clause or the no-fault clause stays inside D 1's deeming, and the Director left that to counsel's next pass rather than splitting one item three ways. **What follows from it, and the Director's words do not reach, is this.** F7's misstatement limb at §7 item 133 is re-scoped on the Director's instruction to **the six propositions of law**. If it were ever enacted in that scope, **misstating** the re-designation clause or the no-fault clause would be unreached while **omitting** them stays reviewable — the mirror image of the gap F7 exists to close. **Scoping the limb strictly to the six propositions and no further is not draftable on the present text:** the seven items are undesignated clauses of one sentence, so there is no handle to cite, and the only clean handle would be designating them (i) through (vii), a renumbering this commission forbids and a separate edit. **So item 133's re-scoped words use the same exception phrase the enacted text now uses**, which reaches the six propositions *and* item 7's other two, and the difference between that and the Director's instruction is recorded in the item itself. **Recorded, not drafted around, as directed.**
7. **What is not drafted, and it is not silence.** **F5 is held and the acknowledgment form is not to be drafted** — the Director's direction, because it moves the advisory toward a member obligation and MOO-188 §3.3 avoided that deliberately. **No route is drafted**: § 51.1-162 A already locates the manner of designation in Board procedure, D5 would have compelled an administrative particular into a statute where it will go stale, and it would have added a matter inside D 1 so that the failure sentence reached it — enlarging, with administrative detail, exactly the channel F3 was taken to narrow. **The route question goes to the subsection J report**: if the pilot's own reports show the advice being buried, that is the evidence on which to require a route, and it will be evidence rather than a guess. Noted for the Editor's risk section. §6.0-65 item 7 is marked in place and confirmed. **F7 is not cut. Subsection E1 is not re-cut**, the **[MOO-141](/MOO/issues/MOO-141)** patch is not applied, **[MOO-176](/MOO/issues/MOO-176) revision 6 is not touched** (and see item 11), and **[MOO-97](/MOO/issues/MOO-97) §7 item 129's other answers stay open.**
8. **The title line — T2, and why both phrases and in that order.** The commission did not reach the title line; **the Director extended it expressly, as the commissioner**, and that is recorded here because an edit outside the commission as received should never rest on inference. **The ripeness is store revision 67's doing**: that revision opened the title to add *"and 51.1-162"*, the act now amends a death-benefit section on its face, and D 1 compels seven statements about the benefit payable on the member's death and about the designation of a beneficiary — while the *"relating to"* clause named none of them. Va. Const. art. IV, § 12; *Dean v. Paolicelli*, 194 Va. 219 (1952) is the authority a reader of the memo will want. **T2 over T1** because the object the act now embraces is not only the payment of a death benefit but **the member's designation of one**, which is what all seven statements are about; naming only *"death benefits"* would have given notice of six of them. **Placement:** after *"pilot program"* and before *"post-retirement employment; reports"*, following the clause's existing order — the purchase and its cost, the pilot, then the death-benefit and designation consequences, then the post-retirement employment conditions and the reports. **Style:** semicolon-separated noun phrases, lower case, no article, matching the six already there.
9. **A post-condition arithmetic I corrected rather than reported as met.** The Director required §1 at **131,066 → 131,656 (+590)** and the document at **449,196 → 449,830 (+634)**, on the stated footing that substitution 6 *"adds +44 to the document outside §1."* **It is not outside §1.** The bill's title line sits under `### Title`, and that heading is inside §1 as §1 is bounded in this document — the *"## 1. The bill"* heading to the next second-level heading. The Director's +590 is the sum of substitutions 1 to 5 measured on the §1 slice before the title line was touched, which is how the verification script reported it. **So §1's measured movement is +634, and the apply kit asserts the corrected delta and would have aborted on the stated one.** The absolute figures are spent in any event, store revision 69 having landed between the disposition and this cut; the deltas carry over. No other post-condition is affected. **A post-condition that cannot be met as stated is a defect in the post-condition, and saying so is cheaper than meeting it by redefining §1.**
10. **Why this is one revision and not six cuts.** The disposition priced every candidate against the live base and never against another candidate's output. **D1, D2b, D6 and D9 share one FIND, and D3c writes D9's FIND into the subdivision twice more**, so D9's anchor goes from 1× to 3×; in the other order F3 would have been enacted and inert in both limbs with nothing failing. **The general lesson for the next pass: price a candidate against the output of every candidate taken with it, not only against the base.** §0.0-70.
11. **A dead anchor found while verifying the scope held — [MOO-176](/MOO/issues/MOO-176) revision 6's Edit 1, and this revision did not cause it.** Edit 1's FIND ends *"…from the giving of that statement or notice is extended by three days.</u>"*; the live provision reads *"…from the giving of that statement**, notice, or certification** is extended by three days.</u>"*, widened by a revision later than MOO-176 revision 6. **0× on the base, 0× after the composite; Edits 2 and 3 are 1× before and 1× after.** The re-anchored string is at §5.47 direction 2, check 9. **I have not re-cut it**, because MOO-176 revision 6 is expressly out of scope — but it cannot be applied as written, and whoever owns it should know before they try.
