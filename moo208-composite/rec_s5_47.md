#### Direction 2 — into the words this revision enacts, from everywhere else

1. **Subdivision M 2 — ADVERSE, and serious. Substitution 4 withdraws D 1's extension of subsection M; it does not close subsection M.** M 2's first sentence gives written reconsideration to a person adversely affected *"by a failure of the Board to state in a certification or notice a matter this section requires to be stated in it."* The advisory is a matter **this subdivision** requires to be stated and D 1 is part of **this section**, so the omission is inside M 2's limb with no help from D 1 — and M 2's clock for a failure to state runs 30 days from the certification *"or, where the provision that requires the matter to be stated provides a longer or later period, within that period"*, so **M 2 anticipates that a provision like D 1 extends it.** **Net effect as enacted:** the member keeps reconsideration under M 2 on a 30-day clock from the certification; a claimant who is not the member **loses** the subdivision G 4-notice clock, the only one a survivor could ever have used; and M 2's second sentence, which D 1 had disapplied for the member, applies again. **No cure inside D 1 can fix it**, because the defect is that M 2 does not look at D 1. The clause that would work sits in M 2's first sentence and is **drafted at §7 item 135 and not enacted.** The full reasoning, including the act's five other declaratory *"for the purposes of subsection M"* clauses and M 2's own *"whether or not another provision of this section expressly provides for review of it"*, is at **§6.0-70 item 5**.
2. **Subsection J — clean, and substitution 5 works in full.** Subdivision J 1's enumeration ends *"and, to the extent not otherwise required by this subdivision, **every other matter this section requires the Board to state in that report**."* The new J limb requires the Board to state *"every failure to state that the Board advises the member to review the member's designation of beneficiary"* in that report, and J 1's catch-all picks it up. **No conforming amendment to subsection J is needed.**
3. **Subdivision G 4 — clean.** D 1's clause *"the notice required by subdivision G 4"* is unchanged in wording; only its scope narrows, by the exception. G 4 contains no reference to D 1's failure sentence, so nothing in G 4 is stranded. The narrowing is reported at check 1.
4. **Subdivision G 5 — clean.** G 5 supplies the cancellation right whose *"before completing payment"* the terminus matches. Unchanged, same event.
5. **The old advisory's words — gone, and nothing points at them.** *"The member is advised to"* now occurs **zero times in §1.** Its surviving occurrences in the document are all historical record — the §0 narratives for store revisions 65 and 68, §6.0-65 item 1, and the §8 status block for store revision 65. Those are correct as history; §6.0-65 item 1 is marked in place, and the rest are left alone, because a narrative that described its own revision accurately is not made wrong by a later one.
6. **§2, the plain-English summary — clean.** Zero occurrences of *"designation"*, *"advis"*, *"certification shall also state"* or the title line. Verified by count, not by reading.
7. **Nothing in the enacted text counts the series by position.** The seven items are undesignated clauses of one sentence and no provision refers to any of them by number. **This is why F4 cost nothing** — and it is also why §6.0-70 item 6's problem cannot be drafted around without designating the clauses (i) to (vii).
