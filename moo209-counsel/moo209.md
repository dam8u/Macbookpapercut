## Findings

### F1 — SERIOUS — Edit 164-7, §3.7. The exception reaches the 15-day duty on the application and not R-6's 15-day duty to give notice of the date, so trigger two's class gets a new retirement date by operation of law, no notice, and no filing vehicle

The saving sentence blocks the deeming from determining *"the date by which the Board is required …
to give a notice or a decision."* The one express exception names only M 2's duty to act upon an
application. So R-6 — *"where the Board does not do so it shall give the member written notice of
that date no later than 15 days after it acts upon the request"* — is **not** reached, and no notice
duty ever falls due in the deemed case.

**Worked case.** Member files a request under the definition on 1 July. The definition allows 30
days; the Board does nothing. Edit 164-4's R-5 floor moves her final qualifying retirement date to
1 September by operation of law. Edit 164-7 deems the Board to have acted on 1 August — so every
period keyed to the act now has a date. But:

- R-6's notice-of-date duty is expressly excluded by the saving, so **no notice is due**;
- D 1's lateness sentence is triggered by the Board *giving* a statement or notice *"later than the
  time required by that definition"* — nothing is given, so **no extension and no effective-date
  permission**;
- **Edit 164-8's new vehicle is triggered by the Board** ***giving*** **a notice** — nothing is
  given, so **the vehicle does not fire**.

She has a later retirement date she cannot learn and cannot file against. This is precisely the
§13.5 defect — a permission with no vehicle — reappearing inside the case Edit 164-7 was promoted to
cure, and it is not in §3.7's statement of what is left open.

**Authority / evidence:** R-6 and the saving sentence as applied; D 1's lateness sentence; Edit
164-8's trigger. All four quoted strings resolve exactly once in `applied209.md` (`check209.py`, F1
block and F2 block).

**Smallest cure:** extend the exception to name R-6's duty as well — one clause, no new number, the
section's own words. That makes the Board's silence a breach of a notice duty, which J 1 expressly
reports (*"failure … to give a notice"*), and when the notice is eventually given it is late, so D 1's
lateness sentence and Edit 164-8 both fire. **It does not cure the Board that never gives it at all**;
for that, D 1's lateness trigger would have to key to the expiry of the time required rather than to
the giving, which is a larger change and I am not drafting it.

### F2 — SERIOUS — Edit 164-8, §3.8. The premise does need re-checking: § 51.1-153 is **not** silent, and the edit's unqualified *"satisfies § 51.1-153"* strips a qualification D 1 carries twice

You asked whether the premise read on 12 October needs re-checking. **Half of it does.** D 1 has no
power to amend, replace or withdraw a filed notice — confirmed. But § 51.1-153 is not silent on what
a replacement notice has to do. **Subsection E:** *"The effective date of retirement shall be after
the last day of service of the member, but shall not be more than 90 days prior to the filing of the
notice of retirement."* (read at source, law.lis.virginia.gov, 12 October 2026).

D 1 itself defers to that twice — its retroactivity sentence (*"payable retroactively to that date,
**subject to subsection E of § 51.1-153**"*) and clause (ii) of the statement-contents sentence
(*"an earlier effective date … to which the member is otherwise entitled, **subject to subsection E
of § 51.1-153**"*). Edit 164-8 says the replacement notice *"satisfies § 51.1-153"* **flat**.

**Worked case.** Statement given 1 March; window closes 31 March; member does not file because her
request is pending. On 1 December the Board gives a late notice of a date. Her last day of service
was 30 June and she wants 1 July — a date to which clause (ii) says she is otherwise entitled.
1 July is more than 90 days before 1 December, so **subsection E bars it**. Edit 164-8 either
overrides subsection E by implication — an unflagged amendment to Chapter 1's retroactivity cap that
nothing in the cut discloses — or is read down, in which case *"satisfies § 51.1-153"* is false for
the one date she is most likely to want.

**Smallest cure: delete the three words `, satisfies § 51.1-153,` (−22 characters).** They are
surplusage. Edit 164-8 already deems the replacement *"filed within the period allowed by this
subdivision"*, and D 1's existing sentence — *"A notice filed within the period allowed by this
subdivision satisfies § 51.1-153 …"* — then does the work **with** the subsection E qualification
attached. The edit gets shorter and more correct at the same time.

### F3 — SERIOUS — Edit 164-8, §3.8. *"[S]upersedes any notice previously filed"* has no temporal limit, so it purports to unmake a retirement that has already taken effect

You asked whether supersession leaves the Board's duties on the superseded notice cleanly
discharged. **No.** Nothing confines the power to a notice whose stated effective date has not
passed, and nothing says what happens to allowances already paid under the superseded notice.

**Worked case.** Statement 1 March; member files 20 March stating 1 July; she retires 1 July and is
paid July to November. On 1 November the Board gives a late notice of a later approved date of
1 February. On 15 November she files a replacement stating 1 February, and it *"supersedes any notice
previously filed."* Her 1 July retirement is now unmade by a filing; five months of allowance were
paid under a notice that no longer exists; the qualifying retirement that settled her election under
G 1 is undone, which puts her into a G 2 determination with its reduction and refund machinery. The
section provides for none of this.

Two smaller defects in the same eleven words. *"[A]ny notice previously filed"* is unbounded **as to
kind** — D 1 uses *"notice"* for the Board's own documents (*"any notice of a later approved date"*)
— and **as to filer**; nothing says *of retirement* or *by the member*.

**Smallest cure:** confine the power and name the instrument: *"supersedes any notice of retirement
previously filed by the member, except that no notice may be filed under this sentence after the
member's retirement has taken effect."* The trade is honest and should be stated: the member whose
retirement has already taken effect gets nothing from this vehicle, and pretending otherwise buys a
payment problem rather than a remedy.

### F4 — SERIOUS — Edit 164-8, §3.8. The new right has no disclosure duty, against D 1's own practice of disclosing every lateness consequence, and clause (ii) now understates the member's options

The sentence immediately after the insertion requires the Board to state, in the statement and in
every notice of a later approved date, **(i)** the extension, **(ii)** the effective-date permission
and **(iii)** the three-day mailing extension. Edit 164-8 adds a **fourth** consequence of exactly
the same family — a 30-day window to file or replace the § 51.1-153 notice — and adds **no clause
(iv)**. Counted: that sentence contains (i), (ii), (iii) and no (iv).

**Worked case.** Board gives a late notice of a later approved date on 1 November, stating
(i), (ii) and (iii) exactly as required. The member, who filed her notice of retirement in March,
reads that she *"may state"* a later effective date, has no vehicle disclosed to her, and lets the
30 days run. The vehicle expires unused, and the Board has complied with every duty in the
subdivision. A 30-day window that the only document she receives does not mention is not a remedy.

A second limb, pre-existing but widened by this edit: the disclosure sentence reaches *"the written
statement … and … every notice of a later approved date"* — **not** the third document, *"any notice
of a date required by that definition by reason of the Board's action upon a request"* (Edit 164-5's
descriptor), which is one of the two documents Edit 164-8's trigger does reach.

**Smallest cure:** a clause (iv) in the existing sentence reciting the new window, and extend that
sentence's list of documents to the third notice.

### F5 — SERIOUS — §16.2, §2.2 and §9.3. The re-cut derivation of the 48-day cap is still wrong, and the verifier counts a true string under a false label

§16.2 fixed the mailing half and left the load-bearing half. Three problems.

1. **The 15 belongs to a different document.** §2.2 says *"[t]he 15 is the definition's own notice
   backstop."* R-6's 15-day duty governs a notice of the member's **final qualifying retirement
   date**, and only *"where the Board does not [state that date] in its decision."* Edit 164-1's
   endpoint runs 30 days from *"written notice of **its decision** upon the request"* — a different
   document.
2. **No provision of the section requires that document by any date.** *"[N]otice of its decision"*
   resolves **0 times in enacted §1 at store revision 68** and 1 time only because Edit 164-1 adds
   it. For a request under the definition the governing duty is *"written notice of its determination
   and of the reasons for it, no later than 30 days after the request is filed"* — the same deadline
   as the act. For a request for reconsideration there is **no deadline at all**.
3. **So *"nothing reaches past 48"* is false.** Definition request, Board acts on day 1 of the 30-day
   window and gives notice of its determination on day 30 — every duty met — and mails it: the
   uncapped notice-keyed endpoint is **act + 29 + 30 + 3 = act + 62**. Reconsideration request: no
   duty fixes the giving, so the uncapped endpoint is **unbounded**.

**Consequence.** 48 is a **chosen outer limit**, not a restatement of existing periods, and §9.3's
*"the cap is not invented: every number in it is already in the section"* does not hold. The +18
movement figure is unaffected — it is 48 − 30 and follows from the cap itself — which is why this is
a record defect rather than a text defect. But MOO-204 is being asked to price *"the remaining 60
days"* against a framing that says the cap merely restates the section's arithmetic. It does not.

**The script is wrong in the way you said you would rather hear about.**
`verify_r68_v6.py` asserts `the definition's 15-day notice backstop, which supplies the 15 in
48 = 15 + 3 + 30` at count 1. The string is there; the proposition in the label is false, and the
check cannot detect that because it counts a string.

**Smallest cure:** restate §16.2 / §2.2 / §9.3 as *48 is a chosen outer limit; the periods it
displaces are act + 62 for a request under the definition and unbounded for a request for
reconsideration*, and relabel that verifier line, or replace it with a check on the duty that
governs the notice the endpoint actually keys to — of which there is none, which is the finding.

### F6 — MODERATE — Edit 164-12, §3.11. *"Notwithstanding the definition in subdivision 4"* is surplusage carrying a negative implication, because G 4's definition is already expressly confined to G 4

You asked whether it is enough to stop a court harmonising. **It is more than enough, and that is
the problem.** G 4's definition opens *"**For the purposes of this subdivision** an application for a
disability retirement allowance is pending …"* — counted, once, unamended in the applied text. It
never travelled into G 2. There is nothing to notwithstand.

The premise at §4.2 — *"subsection G would define 'pending' for a disability application twice,
differently, in adjacent subdivisions"* and *"the natural judicial move on a word defined twice in
one subsection is to harmonise"* — is not made out, because both definitions are expressly confined
to their own subdivision. A word confined twice is not a word defined twice.

**Worked consequence.** A litigant reads the notwithstanding as the drafter's acknowledgment that,
but for it, G 4's *"for the purposes of this subdivision"* **would** have reached G 2 — and then
argues the same about any other confinement clause in the section that has no notwithstanding beside
it. **Enacted §1 carries seven clauses of that family** (four *"purposes of this subdivision"*, three
*"purposes of this subsection"*), counted. The clause buys presentational comfort in G 2 at the cost
of weakening six confinement clauses elsewhere.

**Smallest cure:** delete `Notwithstanding the definition in subdivision 4, and` (−50 characters),
leaving *"For the purposes of this subdivision, such an application is pending from …"*. If the
divergence must be visible, the place for it is the §0 record and the designation-review
read-through — which §3.11 already drafts — not the operative text.

### F7 — MODERATE — §3.7 claim 2 and §16.1 question 9. *"[T]he identical date R-5's floor keys to"* is not accurate: same triggering event, different date, and a wider class

Two provisions, two dates:

- R-5's floor: *"the first day of the second month following the month in which that period expired."*
- Edit 164-7's deemed act: *"the day after the expiration of that period."*

**Worked divergence.** Request under the definition filed 1 July; the 30 days expire 31 July. R-5's
floor is **1 September**. The deemed act is **1 August**, which falls in the next month, so R-3 —
*"the first day of the second month following the month in which the Board last acts upon a
request"*, which the deeming reaches expressly through *"to last act upon it"* — gives **1 October**.
One month apart. No harm follows, because R-5 bites only *"where the date that would otherwise apply
… is earlier"* and the member therefore takes the later; but the two provisions do not *"agree"*, and
the resolution is the floor mechanism, not agreement.

**And the classes differ.** R-5 reaches *"a request the filing of which tolled the running of the
member's final qualifying retirement date under subsection M"* — which includes a **request for
reconsideration**. Trigger two reaches *"a request under the definition … in subsection A"* only.
R-5 is wider. Both counted.

What is identical is the **formula** (nine words, the census counts it at two of the five sites) and
the **triggering event** (the expiry of the allowed period). Saying so is enough to carry the point
§16.1 needs — that trigger two invents no bound. **Smallest cure:** restate both passages as *the
same triggering event and the same formula*, and drop *"the identical date"* and *"two provisions
about one date now agree."* This is the §16.2 defect in a second place: the figure is right and the
sentence explaining it is wrong.

### F8 — MODERATE — §7.3's re-run. Subsection C's report line cannot reach an M 2 duty, J 1's does not say what it is quoted as saying, and the conclusion survives on J 1 alone

You asked me to press the *no new duty* conclusion. **The conclusion holds. Its stated basis does
not.** §7.3 says both provisions report failures *"to make a determination, to give a notice, or to
make a payment within a period required by this section."* That exact string resolves **once** in
enacted §1 — and it is **M 2's own** standing sentence, not either report line.

- **J 1** reads *"every failure of the Board to make a determination, **or to give a notice**, within
  a period required by this section, stated separately for each provision."* No payment limb. The
  quotation attributed to it is not its words.
- **Subsection C** reads *"every failure to make a determination, to state a matter **this subsection**
  requires it to state, or to give a notice, **required by this subsection** within the period **this
  subsection** allows for it."* It is confined to subsection C's own duties and periods. **It cannot
  report a failure of an M 2 duty at all.**

So *"reportable under J 1 and subsection C by the words those provisions already have"* is wrong as
to subsection C. On J 1 it holds only if a failure *to act upon an application* is a failure *to make
a determination* — and M 2's expansive list (*"placement on a waiting list … refusal to accept an
election … is a determination of the Board"*) cannot supply that, being confined *"for the purposes
of this subsection."* It is the right reading on ordinary meaning, and note that the bill's own
practice elsewhere is to attach an express report line to each duty (five instances counted of
*"shall state in the report required by subsection J every failure to …"*), which M 2's 15-day duty
does not have.

**Smallest cure:** in §7.3, drop subsection C from the claim and quote J 1's actual words. If the
Director wants the reporting route beyond argument, the J 1 catch-all would take *"or to act upon an
application"* — but that is an amendment this cut declined, and I am not recommending it over the
ordinary-meaning reading.

### F9 — MODERATE — the eleven plus MOO-141 Edit 1. Two savings, asymmetric, and two deeming sentences carrying the identical 44-word confinement clause with no cross-reference between them

On the MOO-141 patch as this kit models it, Edit 1's saving carries **no** exception and Edit 164-7's
carries one. Both are otherwise the same 256 characters (see N1). The result:

- **No-act case** (164-7): the 15-day duty on the application **is** dated by the deeming.
- **Two-act case** (Edit 1, token act day 7, decision day 85): Edit 1's saving says the deeming does
  not determine *"the date by which the Board is required … to make a determination,"* so the 15-day
  duty on the application arguably has **no determinate commencement** — the ambiguity Edit 1 exists
  to kill, re-opened for that one duty.

The Board that acts twice is left with a looser rule than the Board that never acts. And the section
would carry **two** deeming sentences with the identical confinement clause, in two subsections,
one answering *which* act and one answering *what if none*, with **neither cross-referring to the
other** and nothing on the face of either saying which question it answers.

**Decision 1 is honoured in letter and not in effect.** §16.6's test — *"no existing occurrence is
amended or changed in meaning"* — cannot detect this, because it counts occurrences and the problem
is a rule. (Strictly, *"changed in meaning"* is also too strong: the deeming's whole purpose is to
change what existing occurrences mean in the deemed case. The defensible form is *no occurrence is
re-worded, and none acquires a different date where the section already fixed one.*)

**Routing, not a cure here:** this is MOO-141's saving to re-cut, and a re-cut carrying 164-7's
exception appears to be in flight in the shared workspace (uncommitted, `moo141-rev68-recut/`). What
belongs to MOO-164 is the record: the two deemings should land as one joint decision with an express
cross-reference, not as two independent patches.

### F10 — MINOR — Edit 164-12, §3.11. *"[G]rants or denies"* has no anchor in the section or in the Code provision that governs the application

F10's collision is cleanly avoided — see N8. But *"grants"* and *"denies"* each resolve **0 times in
enacted §1** before this edit, and § 51.1-156 does not use them: the Medical Board *"shall certify …
that the member should be retired"* (read at source, 12 October 2026). The section's own words for
the same event, in the adjacent subdivision, are *"the date on which the Board **first acts upon
it**"* and *"**approved for** a disability retirement allowance under this chapter."*

**Worked case.** The Board neither grants nor denies: it defers pending further medical evidence, or
the application is withdrawn, or it is closed administratively. Under G 2 as amended the application
is **pending indefinitely**, so the carve-out validates every allowance paid for as long as that
lasts; under G 4's words it would have ended at the first act. §4.2's *"nobody loses"* holds — I
checked the limb it rests on (N9) and the Commonwealth bears it — but the amount between the first
act and a disposal that never comes is unbounded and unpriced.

**Smallest cure:** either use the section's own verb (*"until the date on which the Board first acts
upon it"* would collapse the divergence and defeat the purpose, so not that) or close the open end:
*"until the date on which the Board grants or denies it or the application is otherwise disposed
of."*

## Question 2, answered directly — do not convert 164-7's two references

**Keep them.** *"[T]he preceding sentence"* is the bill's own instrument: **18 occurrences in enacted
§1 at store revision 68**, plus one *"the two preceding sentences"*, and **zero** cross-references by
content paraphrase. Converting 164-7's two would make it the only provision in the section that
cross-refers by paraphrase, against §9.6's own stated principle.

The two instruments are different risk classes and the planned conversion of D 1's ordinal does not
imply converting these. *"[T]he second sentence of subdivision M 2"* is an **absolute ordinal into
another subdivision** and breaks silently on any insertion before M 2's second sentence — six
sentences' worth of exposure. *"[T]he preceding sentence"* is **relative and adjacent** and breaks
only on an insertion between two named sentences. §16.5 already routes the trap; that is the right
disposition. The one thing I would add is a line in M 2's §7 record stating the adjacency
requirement in the words a future drafter will search for, because the verifier can only protect the
base it was written against.
