## 1. Q1 — candidate 164-15 is taken as drafted, with the 90

**The board took it with the bound, and I would have.** 164-8 is the first provision in this section
to attach a **forfeiture** to a notice the member may never receive, and under subsection A's giving
rule the notice is *given* when the Board **mails** it. Clause (iv) — the disclosure written to warn
her — is printed in the document that did not arrive.

**On the 90.** The drafter declined to choose it alone after MOO-209's F5, and that was the right

over 80, not a reason to call it a restatement.** It must be described that way in the document, in
the memo and in the Editor's risk note. **No figure in this cut may be described as already in the
section unless the verifier counts it there.**

**Conformance owed, and it is counted rather than asserted.** 164-15 puts the non-receipt limb into

whole reason the board answer says "in the same cut."* **Clause (iv) takes the same two limbs, in the

## 2. Q2 — candidate 164-17 is taken into this cut

**Take it here, not in a new issue.** The written statement's disclosure duty is the one Edit 164-13
just enlarged; splitting a duty from its remedy across two passes is how one of them gets lost.

**I considered confining it to the disclosure sentence's own clauses (i)–(iv) and rejected that, and
the reason is worth recording because it is the kind of thing a reviewer will press.** Confined, the
bill would make a failure to disclose an **ancillary** right reviewable while a failure to state **the
two dates** — the operative content, and the content the member's filing clock runs from — stayed
reviewable nowhere. **That inversion is worse than either extreme.** 164-17 as drafted is confined to
*"every matter **this subdivision** requires to be stated,"* which is D 1's own content and not the
section's, so it is bounded on its face.

**One thing it does that nobody has said out loud: it adds a seventh site to Decision 2.** Its review
period runs *"no later than 30 days after the date on which the written statement or notice is **given**
to the member"* — a period within which a **person** must act, keyed to **notice**. That is Decision 2's
rule, applied without being cited. It belongs in §2.1's list of sites, which is now the strongest
single argument in this document and was built almost entirely out of other people's revisions.

**What I am not pretending.** 164-17 imports subsection M's review machinery into a document that has
never had it, and that is a widening of the bill's remedial footprint. It is justified because this
cut enlarged the duty, and because counsel's S4 shows the *"or else"* this document twice claimed —
subsection J's report — **is not in subsection J, counted 0**. The withdrawal of that claim is the
reason a route is needed at all.

## 4. R1 — MOO-141's F7 request is granted, and the repair is not the insertion

MOO-141 revision 9 asks for one thing: give 164-7's saving the same three duty families, byte for
byte, so the two saving bodies stay identical. **Granted.**

**I have ruled on the objection MOO-141 invited rather than leaving it open.** The three families are
**inoperative in 164-7** — its two classes cannot produce a reversal or modification of a D 2 or D 3
determination. Surplusage in an **operative** provision invites the argument that it does something;
surplusage in a **limitation** cannot, because a limitation covering an empty class is inert. Set
against that, two differently-worded savings in one section is the *Forst* argument in its plainest
form, and it has already bitten this bill twice — MOO-187's F19 and MOO-214's S3 are the same shape of
error. **Byte-identity is worth two inert phrases. Take the families.**

**The part that is not in MOO-141's request, and would have been missed.** The repair is **not** an
insertion. Inserting the families into 164-7's body does **not** reproduce MOO-141's, because the
series' *"or"* moves off *"to give a notice or a decision"* and onto the new last item. **Counted both
ways:** insertion alone → not equal; insertion **plus the series repair** → **byte-identical to
MOO-141's 367-character body.** +111 characters, of which the series repair is **−3**.

**That is the fourth time in this bill that adding to a series has required moving its *"or,"*** and
the third time somebody else's revision had already done the identical repair elsewhere. It belongs in
the drafting conventions, not in a finding each time.

defect the withdrawal was for.** MOO-141 §3.4 withdrew requirement 1 because a signpost saying Edit 1's

**The replacement, drafted to the four requirements and counted against each of them:**

> *"A date on which the Board is deemed by this subsection to act upon a request is a date on which the

> upon a request, and to last act upon it, on the date on which the Board first acts upon it."*

- **Requirement 1 — characterises neither scope.** *"Applies where"* occurs **0** times.
- **Requirement 2 — it is exactly the one fact that makes them cumulative**, and on MOO-141 §3.2 it

- **Requirement 3 — moves no date and adds no exception.** *"No later than," "days," "month," "date

- **Requirement 4** is not engaged: it needed no priority rule.
- It names the other deeming **by content, not by citation** — *"subsection A"* occurs **0** times — so

## 7. R4 — F4's residue is cured in 164-7, in Edit 1's own policy

MOO-141 §3.2 routed it here because it is inside 164-7's own words: 164-7 deems the Board *"to act upon
the request, and to **last** act upon it"* on the deemed date, and **a real act can follow a deemed last
act** — the backlog case, denial by operation of law on 6 May, the Board actually acting on 20 June.

**Where both patches land, Edit 1 answers it:** a deemed act is an act for Edit 1's purposes, Edit 1
selects the date the Board **first** acts, and on MOO-141 §3.2 the deemed date necessarily precedes any
real act — so last collapses into first and the two agree by construction. **Where this cut lands
alone, nothing answers it**, and this cut's standard is that each edit is operative applied alone.

> *"A subsequent act of the Board upon the request does not affect the date on which the Board is deemed
> by this subsection to act, or to last act, upon it."*

**+153 characters.** It is **Edit 1's own rule in miniature** — the earliest act controls — so it adds
nothing where Edit 1 is present and supplies the same answer where it is not. It speaks only to the
**deemed date**; it does not touch the validity or the effect of the later real act, and it must not be
drafted so that it could. **Test that and tell me if the words do more than I think they do.**

**Net on 164-7: +43** over revision 8 — **+111** saving, **−221** cross-reference, **+153** residue.
