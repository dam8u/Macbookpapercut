## 1. Question 1 — card (c): **(c-2)**, and the item-46 question does not need to be put

**(c-2), as redrafted at §4.2 on F10.** The reasoning I accept is not the one I expected to accept.
I asked for this to come to me because a one-word change was going to reprice an enacted board
decision, and that is still true of (c-1): it ends item 46's carve-out at the Board's **first action**
on a disability application — an acknowledgment, a records request — and G 2's third sentence then bars
any allowance until the determination takes effect, bounded at 120 days after the determination date
and unbounded in the late-notice case. **Item 46 was enacted on the ground that the no-recovery limb
absorbs any overpayment, so the Commonwealth bears the cost either way and paying her is strictly
better. A clarifying patch may not take part of that back as a side effect of defining a word.**

**But counsel's F9 is the reason (c-1) is not merely impolitic — it is defective.** (c-1) narrows
**both** of G 2's uses of *"pending"*, and the second is the **validating** limb. Narrowed to
first-action pendency, an allowance paid after the Board's first action is no longer paid *"while such
an application was pending,"* so the notwithstanding does not reach it, and G 2's self-correction
clause is attached to the floor sentence and not to this one. On the worked case — application day 0,
acknowledgment day 5, allowance paid day 40, determination effective day 90 — a litigant on either
side can argue the determination is of no effect **or** the payment was unlawful. **That is a worse
outcome than the repricing, and it is produced by one word.**

**I am not putting an item-46 question to the board, and I want the reason on the record rather than
left as an omission.** The instruction in this issue's description was that card (c) comes to me and I
take the item-46 question to the board. **(c-2) confirms item 46 exactly as the board enacted it and
costs nobody anything against today's ordinary-meaning reading.** There is no decision left for the
board to take: a board card that reads *"do you still want the thing you decided?"* spends the board's
attention and invites a reopening nobody asked for. **The question was contingent on (c-1) and (c-1)
is declined.**

**One thing the board must be *told*, not asked.** (c-2) leaves subsection G defining *"pending"* for
a disability application **twice, differently, in adjacent subdivisions** — G 4 from filing to first
action, G 2 from filing to grant or denial. That is deliberate and operationally coherent: the Board
may **make** the G 2 determination once it first acts, and must keep **paying** until it grants or
denies. *"Notwithstanding the definition in subdivision 4"* puts the divergence on the face of the
text. **Carry it into the designation-review read-through as a stated divergence**, so that nobody
later harmonises it as an error. It is not a gate.

## 2. Question 2 — item 130's residual: **route (c)**

**Adopt candidate 164-7 as revised at revision 5, carrying F19's limb (B) and the verbatim saving
clause with the express exception for the 15-day duty.**

I asked for all three limbs in front of this issue before anything was drafted, and that was the right
order: the answer that reaches one limb and the answer that reaches three are different answers, and
route (b) would have spent the only date movement on the table to buy one.

**What settled it was not the trade — it was §14.2.** My open question was whether subsection A's
late-Board backstop already protects limb 1, which would have made route (a) defensible. **It does
not, and the priority fight over the *"notwithstanding"* is immaterial.** The backstop's trigger is a
one-way comparison — it engages only where the otherwise-applicable date is *"earlier than"* the
computed day — and its consequent **raises an early date to a fixed day**. It contains no *"in no
event later than."* Limb 1's defect runs the other way: unbounded tolling pushes the date **later**
without limit. **A floor cannot bound a date that is too late, on either reading of its scope. Route
(a) leaves all three limbs open, not two.**

**So limb 3 decides it.** M 2's duty to act upon the application *"no later than 15 days after it acts
upon the request"* is a duty with an actor, an object and a deadline, and **no date on which the
deadline falls** where the Board never acts. *Who, what, by when, or else* — it fails *by when* on its
own text. **That is a defect, not a trade, and defects get cured.**

**I accept the drafting reason 164-7 reaches limb 3 and I want it recorded, because a careless cure
would miss it.** The 15 days is a period *measured from* the Board's acting, not one that begins or
ends upon it. 164-7's confinement clause reaches periods that *"begin or end upon, **or are measured
from**, the Board's acting."* **The third form is why limb 3 is in.** And §14.1's fact that the D 4 /
D 5 retention and the 15-day duty are **one sentence**, 112 characters apart, is a better reason for
ruling route (b) out than the one I gave: route (b) amends **clause (ii) of the tolling sentence**, and
both limbs live in the sentence after it. **No amendment confined to clause (ii) reaches either, on any
reading.**

**My F19 correction is accepted and I note what it cost.** 164-7 as first drafted carried one of
MOO-141 Edit 1's two trigger limbs, and the missing one — *"where a date is fixed by reference to the
month in which the Board acts, or last acts"* — is the only limb that reaches the definition's
**reset**. **As drafted, the candidate I was being asked to adopt would have ended her tolling and
removed her reset in the same move, and the 12-month cap would then bind with no reset at all.** The
claim that it was lifted *"word for word"* was made to me twice and was not true. **The withdrawal is
accepted; the lesson is that *"verbatim"* is a counted claim like any other, and it should be asserted
in the verifier, not in prose.**

**The cost of being wrong, which is why I am comfortable:** adopted and wrong, tolling is bounded at
denial + 48 by Edit 164-1's existing cap and the retention at denial + 120; she keeps the deemed
denial, which store revision 63 makes a case decision, and her judicial review with it; G 2's arrears
sentence still pays in full. **Bounded, computable, nothing forfeited.** Declined and wrong: tolling
without end, clause (ii) never binding, a queue position held without end at third-party cost, and no
decision ever falling due on her application.

## 6. Question 6 — MOO-97's route (b): **declined**

**Declined, as a decision and not by implication.** I held it rather than declining it so that this
issue could be decided with all the limbs visible; they now are.

**The ground is incidence, and it is the ground I warned about.** Subdivision G 4's *"a failure of the
Board to act upon such a request … shall not extend that aggregate"* caps **the Board's own clocks**,
so those words make the Board bear its own delay. M 2 clause (ii)'s aggregate caps **the member's
tolling**, so the identical words make the **member** bear it. **The form is conserved and the effect
reverses.** Route (b) is therefore a policy choice, it must never be written up as a conforming
amendment, and the Drafting Specialist is right that they would not sign it as one.

**And route (b) buys less than the table suggested.** §14.1 establishes that limbs 2 and 3 are in the
sentence **after** clause (ii), so no amendment confined to clause (ii) reaches them. Route (b) buys
limb 1, spends the only date movement on the table, and leaves the priced limb open.

**(b) + (c) is also declined.** It is the only combination that bounds tolling where no denial date is
ever fixed, and I accept that as a real difference in reach. **It is not worth what it costs:** clause
(ii) would then be bounded twice, by two instruments for one concept, with the second drafted as an
exception to the first. **And the case it uniquely covers is the case where unbounded tolling favours
the member, with Edit 164-4 already protecting her floor without any filing by anyone.** Buying a
second instrument to take time away from the member in the only case route (c) misses is the wrong
trade.

## 9. Question 9 — candidate 164-7-B: **take it.** The reason, and the cost of being wrong

**This was left to me without a recommendation, and it is right that it was: it is a trade and not a
defect. I am taking it.**

**F22 is the finding that decides it, and it corrects the framing and not a cell.** Revision 4
described the uncured case as the member's **choice** — *"may is a power, not a duty."* For a request
**under the definition** of *"final qualifying retirement date"* that is wrong. M 2 tolls on **two**
classes of request; store revision 63's expiry-notice and deemed-denial machinery opens *"Where the
Board does not act upon **a request for reconsideration**"* and is confined to that class. **For a
definition request there is no notice she can file, no denial date, and nothing for 164-7 to attach
to. There is no row she can be in.** A gap that is elective can be carded. **A gap that is structural
is a different thing, and this document was right to insist on the distinction.**

**Why I take it, against the reading that unbounded tolling favours her.** That reading treats *later*
as *better*, and for this provision it is not. The tolled thing is her **final qualifying retirement
date** — the outer date by which she must retire to qualify. Tolled without end, that date is not
later; it is **indeterminate**. **She cannot plan a retirement against a date that never fixes, and
she cannot be told what it is, because no provision computes it.** Every period within which she must
act is tolled with it, so she also cannot be in default — and cannot bring the matter to an end.
**Indefinite is not generous. It is unusable.**

**And 164-7-B does not invent the bound; it conforms one.** Its second trigger fixes the deemed act at
*"the day after the expiration of that period"* — **the identical date Edit 164-4 already keys R-5's
floor to**. R-5 keys to the expiry of the allowed period whether or not anyone files anything. So
without 164-7-B the definition's own floor is determinate and M 2's tolling of the same date is not.
**164-7-B makes two provisions about one date agree. That is the character of the whole cut, and it is
why I can take it without reopening Decision 1: it defines no verb and it touches none of the 21
occurrences.**

**The cost of being wrong, stated plainly, because it runs against the member in one class.** Taken
and wrong: a member who asked to be allowed to retire **later**, whose request the Board never acted
on at all, has her tolling bounded at the expiry of the allowed period + 30, capped at + 48 by Edit
164-1. **She loses time she would have had under an unbounded reading, in the case where the Board is
wholly at fault.** Not taken and wrong: she has an indeterminate qualifying retirement date, no
instrument to fix it, no deadline she can breach and therefore nothing to litigate.

**One asymmetry I am carding rather than curing, and the Drafting Specialist should not draft it
here.** 164-7-B gives the definition-request class a deemed **act**; the reconsideration class gets a
deemed **denial**, which store revision 63 makes a case decision and therefore a route into court.
**So after 164-7-B the definition-request class has a determinate date and still no decision to
challenge.** Closing that means parallel expiry-notice machinery for definition requests, which is
MOO-42's §7 and the board's to want. **Record it with question 5's item. Do not draft it into this
cut.**

### 12.2 Two new facts in subsection A, and one of them touches Edit 164-1's arithmetic

**Store revision 68 added an express giving rule:** a statement, notice or certification required of
the Board *"is given on the date the Board mails it … or transmits it electronically to an address
that person has provided to the Board for that purpose, except that an electronic transmission
completed after 5:00 p.m. is given on the following day."*

**Edit 164-1's 48-day cap still binds, and its derivation has changed.** The cap was derived as
`48 = 15 (the notice backstop) + 3 (subsection A's mailing extension) + 30`. The three-day extension
is now available **only where the Board mails**, and electronic transmission instead carries the
5:00 p.m. rule, which shifts the *given* date and cannot push it past the fifteenth day. **So mailing
remains the worst case and 48 remains the outer bound — but §3.1 must stop deriving it from an
unconditional extension.** The figure is right; the sentence explaining it is now wrong.

**And Decision 2 gains a sixth site, not a fifth.** The new giving rule is keyed to **certifications**
as well as statements and notices, which is the rule §2.1 argues the bill already applies. **§2.1's
