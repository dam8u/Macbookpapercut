**What was decided and by whom.** Interaction `ff469794` on this issue, answered by the board at 17:38 UTC on 12 October 2026: **option (d)**, the member-triggered denial — against the carded (b), which was this item's own recommendation until the addendum of the same day. Occasioned by the Policy Director's comment of 17:28 making §7 item 124 a first-class blocker edge on [MOO-141](/MOO/issues/MOO-141), its **gate 2**.

**The residual it closes.** Store revision 59 enacted §7 item 88's option (d) together with the answer to item 110, and the two in combination closed both escapes: M 2's last sentence disapplies Articles 3 (§ 2.2-4018 et seq.) and 4 (§ 2.2-4024) of the Administrative Process Act, and M 3 disapplies the deemed-in-favour sentences of § 2.2-4021 B and C. So a reconsideration the Board simply never completed produced **no case decision at all**, and § 2.2-4026 A gives the court action only to a party aggrieved by one. **The member had no route into court, by two express exclusions rather than by argument.** Each of those three provisions was re-counted in §1 of the live body before any substitution: one occurrence each.

**The enacted words**, inserted immediately after M 2's sentence *"The Board shall act upon a request for reconsideration within 90 days."* — one occurrence in §1, asserted before the substitution. The whole of § 51.1-142.4 is new language and subdivision M 2 is a single underlined paragraph, so the insertion carries no markup of its own:

> <u>Where the Board does not act upon a request for reconsideration within the 90 days allowed by this subsection, the person who filed the request may file with the Board written notice that the period has expired. Where the Board does not act upon the request within 30 days after that notice is filed, the request is denied on the first day after the expiration of those 30 days, and that denial constitutes the final determination of the Board upon the reconsideration and a case decision as defined in § 2.2-4001 for the purposes of this subsection.</u>

**Three repairs are built into the enacted words, and two of them are against this document's own card.**

1. **The denial is characterised as a case decision expressly, because the carded sentence did not reach the sentence it relied on.** Item 124 said the cure *"uses the section's own vocabulary … so it needs no further words to become a case decision."* That was wrong. M 2's existing sentence is keyed twice to events a deemed denial does not produce — *"**Upon completion** of a reconsideration under this subsection, the final determination **issued by** the Board shall constitute a case decision"* — and nothing is completed and nothing is issued. Unrepaired, the cure would have given the member a dated denial and still no route into court, which is the whole of what it exists to supply. **The express characterisation is safe here in a way it was not at item 88**, where it was refused because calling Board inaction a case decision opens § 2.2-4021's statutory default against limitations subdivision D 2 calls self-executing: **M 2's last sentence and M 3 now close that default by express text.** Nothing in the enacted words is deemed in the member's favour.
2. **The denial falls on the first day after the 90 days expire, which is the Policy Director's *"91st day"* and not the carded *"last day of that period."*** On the carded words the denial fell on day 90, a day on which the Board may still lawfully act, so the section would have carried a denial and a live power to act on one date and two inconsistent determinations could have existed on the same day. Cost of the correction: one day.
3. **The silence is not cured and is disclosed rather than netted away.** A denial by operation of this sentence carries no writing, no reasons and no notice, where a **completed** reconsideration is *"written reconsideration by the Board stating the reasons for its decision"* and M 2 keys the member's own 30-day window to *"after the notice of the determination is given."* Option (d) answers it as far as drafting can — **the member creates the date, so she knows it** — and that is the whole reason the board's answer is the better one. What remains unverified is whether an Article 5 period could run from a silent decision at all: **§ 2.2-4026 does not state the period** (subsection A commits it to the Rules of the Supreme Court of Virginia; subsection C fixes an appeal date only for regulations, verified at source on 12 October 2026), **and Rule 2A:2 has not been retrieved — one source returned 403 and one host is blocked.** [MOO-186](/MOO/issues/MOO-186) asks Statutory Researcher. It does not bear on the enacted words, because on option (d) the date is the member's own and she has notice of it by construction.

**What I did not decide, and it is new §7 item 130.** The enacted sentence does **not** deem the denial to be the Board *"acting upon the request"*, and I will not make it do so by drafting, because that verb is the subject of [MOO-164](/MOO/issues/MOO-164). The consequence is stated in §5.42 and is real: on the text as it now stands a Board that never acts leaves **the member's tolling** and **the subdivision D 4 / D 5 filing-date retention** running without end, because both endpoints are the Board's act and M 2 clause (ii) expressly excludes from its 120-day aggregate *"any period after the expiration of the 90 days allowed by this subsection and before the Board acts upon the request."* **Store revision 62 enacted the cure for the mirror-image exposure in subdivision G 4** — *"a failure of the Board to act upon such a request within the period allowed to it for acting upon the request shall not extend that aggregate"* — so the precedent for what M 2 clause (ii) could say is already inside this bill. It is [MOO-97](/MOO/issues/MOO-97)'s uncapped-tolling residual and MOO-164's verb, and this revision neither cures nor worsens it.

### 0.0-62 Store revision 62 — [MOO-169](/MOO/issues/MOO-169)'s counsel pass disposed and the Policy Director's seven answers of 12 October 2026 enacted. Twenty-one substitutions in five provisions of § 51.1-142.4

**Cut and applied on [MOO-97](/MOO/issues/MOO-97). **The fatal finding, cured by taking the window out again.** [MOO-169](/MOO/issues/MOO-169) F1 held that store revision 61's confinement of the death-benefit sentence to the notification window in subdivision D 1 *moved* MOO-161 F1's injury rather than curing it: a member who is out of service after an involuntary separation, whom subdivision D 1 expressly tells she need not file her § 51.1-153 notice early, and who dies before filing, fell outside the window — so the in-service waiver never reached her, § 51.1-162 B was unavailable, and § 51.1-162 A paid her survivor a refund of contributions while the Commonwealth settled and paid the certified subsidy on the date of her death. **The window is deleted and the sentence is conditioned instead on the member not having retired before the date of death**, which is the carve-out clause (i) already uses in its own second sentence. That restores the scope of answer **(f)** as the Director approved it on 12 October 2026 and as it stood at store revisions 54, 58 and 60; **the confinement was this drafter's, not the Director's**, and §6.0-62 item 1 says so. **F2 is cured by the same deletion** — there is no longer a cross-reference to a notification *"required by subdivision D 1"* for the Board to read narrowly.

**Three statements of law the act compels the Board to make before it may take a member's money were wrong, and are now right.** § 51.1-165 **C 2** does not provide a greater-of at large: it pays in accordance with § 51.1-163 and switches to § 51.1-162 B **only where the contingent annuitant and the § 51.1-162 B payee are the same person or persons**, and the certification's invented words *"each payable to the person entitled to it under the provision under which it is determined"* described a mechanism the Code does not have (F9). § 51.1-162 **B** does not pay on survival alone: it pays a **designated** spouse, minor child or parent, or, absent a designation, in the order of precedence it states — so a member who designated her sister in 2009 and is survived by her mother gets nothing from the purchase, and the certification told her otherwise (F10). § 51.1-162 **C** requires death **in service** (M6). § 51.1-162 **A**'s payee rule is now stated too (m3), and the displacement of § 51.1-165 C 1 and § 51.1-165.01 F is described as what it is — a displacement of their routing to § 51.1-163 only, leaving their first sentences, which void the member's option election, in force (M5).

**The remedy for an incomplete certification now works for the person most affected by it.** It reached only the six propositions, not the certification's earlier content sentence; it ran the member's clock from a death notice she will never receive; it named a category — *"a failure of the Board"* — that subdivision M 2 does not contain; and it collided with M 2's second sentence, which deems a certification a determination and had already closed her 30 days. **All four are cured in one substitution** (F5, m2): every matter the subdivision requires, the M 2 category named exactly, the member's period run from the certification she actually received, the death-case person's period left where it was, and M 2's second sentence expressly disapplied to such a failure. M 2's own third window — *"within the period provided by the provision that requires the matter to be stated"* — was **empty everywhere but subdivision D 1**, and now carries the chapter's own 30 days with a longer-period rider (F6).

**The reconsideration class is widened, with the boundary store revision 61 was reaching for.** Standing turned on the Board having already decided against the claimant, so the Board could defeat review by never deciding — and because M 2 makes the case decision the product of reconsideration, exclusion from reconsideration foreclosed the circuit court as well (F3). The same narrowing had stripped the **court-appointed conservator of a minor beneficiary** whom the same revision had made the addressee of the death notice (F4). Both of M 2's class lists now name *"person who **has** claimed any benefit payable on the death of a member"* — with no adverse-determination gate — and *"person to whom this section requires a notice to be given **in respect of a benefit payable on the death of a member**."* **This is not a reversal of store revision 61's reasoning**: what was wrong with the open formula was that it had no subject-matter boundary and so reached the public through subsection K; the new words carry one. §6.0-62 item 3.

**The $3,000,000 bar is made arithmetically honest in two of the three places the pass attacked.** A reinstated settled amount is now **charged against the limitations in subdivision D 2** notwithstanding that the amount available is thereby exceeded, so commitments can no longer stand at $3,148,000 under a bar subdivision D 2 calls self-executing (F8); the release carries a *"to the extent it has not already been released and not reinstated"* rider, so it cannot be required twice of the same amount (M3); and the stranded words *"together with any contingency amount released with it"*, which pointed at a release that never carries a contingency amount, are deleted (M2). **The third place is not cured and is the Director's**: subsection N fixes no time for payment while subdivision G 4 settles the subsidy on the date of death, so whether a subsidy that buys no benefit comes back to the cap is an unregulated administrative choice (F7, §7 item 129 answer 1).

**The conflict with § 51.1-165 C 2 is resolved by preserving C 2, not by displacing it.** C 2 is a *saving* provision: it gives a spouse, minor child or parent named under a joint and last-survivor option the **greater** of § 51.1-163 and § 51.1-162 B. Displacing it would have taken an existing right from a named class to make the act tidier. The enacted sentence instead pays the greater of the two, so no member is worse off than under present law. **The displacement alternative is drafted at §7 item 122 and is not enacted**, because it decides who bears a loss.

**Three more cures are about words that were not true.** The certification compelled the Board to state four propositions of law and **three were wrong**: all of subsection C of § 51.1-165 displaced when one subdivision is (F6(a)); § 51.1-162 B's relation test stated as its only condition when it is also conditioned on § 51.1-162 C (F6(b)); and *"in every other case subsection A"* when a death compensable under the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act is governed by § 51.1-162 **C** (F6(c)). The Board may not be put under a statutory duty to misstate the law to a member about to file.

**And one premise of my own was wrong.** The issue reasoned that D 1's day-for-day extension carried the consequence of an incomplete certification. It does not — that extension attaches to the statement of qualifying retirement dates required by the definition in subsection A, not to the Board's certification (F7). The duty had **no** consequence at all. It now has the two the section uses everywhere else: a determination for the purposes of subsection M, and a line in the subsection J report.

**What this revision does not do.** **F5 — the unreduced-retirement-eligibility bar in the second limb of subsection E — is not closed**, and it is the ERF residual this issue was opened on. The Commonwealth prices the subsidy on an **unreduced** retirement and subsection E's second limb may leave the survivor the **reduced** allowance. Closing it costs money and the figure is Fiscal's, so it is drafted two ways at §7 item 122 and enacted neither way. **F9, F10, F12's alternative, F13(c) and (e), and F15's payment side are likewise drafted and not enacted**, each for the same reason: each moves a loss between the survivor, the trust fund, the General Fund and the employing agency.

<u>"Election" means a qualifying military service election as defined in subdivision B 4 of § 51.1-142.2.</u>

<u>"Eligible member" means a member in service who (i) is a state employee, (ii) is covered under the retirement plan established by this chapter, whether or not the member is a person described in clause (i) or (ii) of subdivision A 1 of § 51.1-155, (iii) does not participate in the hybrid retirement program established by § 51.1-169, (iv) is eligible to purchase service credit for prior active duty military service under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2, (v) has not reached his unreduced retirement eligibility date, determined without the service credit to be purchased, on the date the member files a complete application, taking account of all creditable service for which the member is entitled to credit as of that date, (vi) would, if the member's maximum purchasable amount determined as of the date the member files that application were credited to the member on that date, reach his unreduced retirement eligibility date on or before the first day of the month following the month in which the member files that application, (vii) would, if the least of the member's maximum purchasable amount and the amount specified in clause (ii) of subsection C, each determined as of the date the member files that application, or, where no amount is specified in clause (ii) of subsection C, the member's maximum purchasable amount determined as of that date, were credited to the member on that date, reach his unreduced retirement eligibility date at least one month earlier than the date on which the member would reach his unreduced retirement eligibility date determined without that service credit, and (viii) would reach his unreduced retirement eligibility date, determined with the service credit to be purchased, on or before the first day of the month following the month in which that service credit is credited to the member. For the purposes of clause (vii) of this definition, a date is at least one month earlier than a second date if it is on or before the day, of the calendar month preceding the month in which that second date falls, that corresponds in number to the day of that second date, or, where that preceding month has no corresponding day, on or before the first day of the month in which that second date falls. "Eligible member" does not include a member of a retirement system established under Chapter 2 (§ 51.1-200 et seq.), Chapter 2.1 (§ 51.1-211 et seq.), or Chapter 3 (§ 51.1-300 et seq.), a teacher, an employee of a political subdivision, the Governor, the Lieutenant Governor, the Attorney General, a member of the General Assembly, or any person whose retirement allowance would be payable under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-155.</u>

<u>"Final qualifying retirement date" means the date that is 12 months after the member's earliest qualifying retirement date, except that the Board shall approve a later date where the Board's own processing requirements, or any other cause not attributable to the member, make the member's retirement on or before the date that would otherwise apply under this definition impracticable. A later date approved under this definition shall be the first day of a month and shall be not more than three months after the date determined under the first sentence of this definition. The Board shall approve a later date on its own initiative or upon the written request of the member, which the member may file at any time before the date that would otherwise apply under this definition or before any later date approved under this definition; the Board shall act upon such a request, and shall give the member written notice of its determination and of the reasons for it, no later than 30 days after the request is filed; and the member may seek review of the determination under subsection M. The Board shall determine the member's earliest qualifying retirement date and final qualifying retirement date, and shall state both of them and the reasons for any later date approved under this definition, in writing to the member no later than 15 days after the Board credits to the member the service credit purchased under the member's election, and shall give the member written notice of any later date approved after that statement no later than 15 days after the approval. Where the member's final qualifying retirement date as extended under subdivision D 1 or as tolled under subsection M is not the first day of a month, the member's final qualifying retirement date is the first day of the next month. In no event shall a member's final qualifying retirement date, as extended under subdivision D 1 or as tolled under subsection M, be later than 12 months after the date that applies under the first sentence of this definition or, where a later date has been approved under this definition, after that later date, except that this sentence does not apply to an extension under subdivision D 1 by reason of a statement or notice given later than the time required by this definition, and the aggregate of all extensions so excluded shall not exceed 12 months. Where the running of a period has been tolled under subsection M and the member is prevented by the preceding sentence from retiring in a qualifying retirement after that tolling ends, the member's final qualifying retirement date is, notwithstanding the preceding sentence, the first day of the second month following the month in which the Board last acts upon a request the filing of which tolled that period, and in no event later than 12 months after the latest date the preceding sentence permits. Notwithstanding the two preceding sentences, where the date that would otherwise apply under this definition is earlier than the first day of the second month following the month in which the Board gives the statement required by this definition, the member's final qualifying retirement date is that first day. Where the Board does not act upon a request the filing of which tolled the running of the member's final qualifying retirement date under subsection M within the period this definition allows for acting upon it, and the date that would otherwise apply under this definition is earlier than the first day of the second month following the month in which that period expired, the member's final qualifying retirement date is, notwithstanding any other provision of this definition, that first day. Where the Board's action upon a request determines a member's final qualifying retirement date under this definition, the Board shall state that date in its decision upon the request, and where the Board does not do so it shall give the member written notice of that date no later than 15 days after it acts upon the request. Where the date that would otherwise apply under this definition is earlier than the first day of the second month following the month in which the Board gives a decision or notice required by the preceding sentence, the member's final qualifying retirement date is, notwithstanding any other provision of this definition, that first day.</u>

<u>"Involuntary separation" means an involuntary separation as defined in subsection B of § 2.2-3200. "Involuntary separation" does not include a separation for reasons related to the job performance or misconduct of the member or a voluntary resignation. The date of an involuntary separation is the date on which the member's employment terminates or, where the member is placed on leave without pay-layoff or equivalent status, the date of that placement, or, in the case of any other involuntary separation, the date on which the member ceases to occupy the position from which the member is separated.</u>

<u>"Maximum purchasable amount" means, with respect to a member and a date, the least of (a) the amount of service credit the member is eligible to purchase under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2 on that date, (b) the amount of service credit specified in clause (i) of subsection C on that date, and (c) the amount of service credit specified in clause (iii) of subsection C on that date. Clause (ii) of subsection C does not limit the maximum purchasable amount.</u>

<u>"Program" means the pilot program established by this section.</u>

<u>"Qualifying retirement" means a service retirement under § 51.1-153, or under subsection A or C of § 51.1-155.1, with an effective date of retirement that is the first day of a month, is no earlier than the member's earliest qualifying retirement date, and is no later than the member's final qualifying retirement date, the allowance for which retirement is calculated in accordance with subdivision A 1 of § 51.1-155 and is not reduced under subdivision A 2 or A 3 of that section, in each case determined as though no option had been elected under § 51.1-165 or § 51.1-165.01 and no lump-sum distribution had been paid under § 51.1-165.01. Where the Board's processing requirements delay the commencement of payment of the member's retirement allowance beyond the effective date of retirement, the allowance shall be payable retroactively to that effective date.</u>

<u>"Standard cost" means standard cost as defined in subdivision B 4 a of § 51.1-142.2. A reference in this section to the standard cost of an amount of service credit is a reference to the aggregate of the standard cost for each year or portion thereof of that service credit.</u>

<u>"State agency" means any agency, authority, board, bureau, commission, department, or institution of the Commonwealth, including a public institution of higher education and an agency of the legislative or judicial branch.</u>

<u>"Unreduced retirement eligibility date" means the earliest date on which the member may retire under § 51.1-153, or under subsection A or C of § 51.1-155.1, and receive a service retirement allowance that is calculated in accordance with subdivision A 1 of § 51.1-155 and is not reduced under subdivision A 2 or A 3 of that section, in each case determined as though no option had been elected under § 51.1-165 or § 51.1-165.01 and no lump-sum distribution had been paid under § 51.1-165.01. For the purposes of this section the member may retire under subsection A or C of § 51.1-155.1 only where the member has incurred an involuntary separation, or the member's office has been lawfully abolished, as the case may be, on or before the date as of which the member's unreduced retirement eligibility date is determined or, where this sentence is applied otherwise than in the determination of the member's unreduced retirement eligibility date, on or before the member's effective date of retirement. Where this section does not state a date as of which the member's unreduced retirement eligibility date is determined, that date is, for the purposes of the preceding sentence, the date on which the Board makes the certification, recertification, determination, statement, or other decision in which that date is applied.</u>

<u>For the purposes of this section and of subdivision B 4 f of § 51.1-142.2, a written statement or notice required of the Board is given on the date the Board mails it to the person to whom it is required to be given or transmits it electronically to an address that person has provided to the Board for that purpose, except that an electronic transmission completed after 5:00 p.m. is given on the following day. For the purposes of this section and of subdivision B 4 f of § 51.1-142.2, where the Board mails a written statement or notice, every period within which a person may or must act that runs from the giving of that statement or notice is extended by three days.</u>

<u>**B.** The Program is established. An eligible member may purchase service credit for prior active duty military service under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2 at the cost determined under subdivision B 4 of § 51.1-142.2 if, and only if, the Board has accepted the member's election under subsection D. For the avoidance of doubt, service credit purchased under an election is prior service purchased under § 51.1-142.2, is creditable service as defined in § 51.1-124.3, and shall be treated for all purposes of this chapter in the same manner as other service credit purchased under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2. Participation in the Program is voluntary, and no employer shall require, request, or induce any employee to make an election.</u>

<u>**C.** An eligible member may purchase under an election no more service credit than the least of (i) four years, less any service credit previously purchased by the member under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2; (ii) the number of months of creditable service the member needs in order to have reached his unreduced retirement eligibility date as of the filing date of the member's complete application, taking account of all creditable service for which the member is entitled to credit as of that date, any fraction of a month in the number so determined being counted as a whole month; and (iii) the number of years of creditable service the member has earned as a state employee. The Board shall determine each of these amounts no later than 30 days after the member's application is completed and shall state them, the filing date of the member's complete application, and the completion date, in the certification required by subsection D. The Board shall not accept an election for which the amount determined under clause (ii) of this subsection is zero. Where the amount determined under clause (ii) of this subsection is zero, the Board shall give the member written notice of that determination, of the reasons for it, of the member's rights under subsection B of § 51.1-142.2, and of the standard cost, determined as of the date of the notice, of the service credit, if any, the member is eligible to purchase under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2 as of that date, no later than 30 days after the member's application is completed, and the member may seek review of the determination under subsection M. Where no number of months of creditable service would cause the member to have reached his unreduced retirement eligibility date as of the filing date of the member's complete application, clause (ii) of this subsection does not limit the service credit the member may purchase, and the Board shall, in place of determining and stating an amount under that clause, determine, no later than 30 days after the member's application is completed, that no amount is specified in that clause and shall state that determination in the certification required by subsection D. A determination that no amount is specified in clause (ii) of this subsection is not a determination that the amount under that clause is zero. Where no amount is specified in clause (ii) of this subsection, the amount determined under that clause is not zero for the purposes of this subsection. The Board shall state in the report required by subsection J every failure to make a determination, to state a matter this subsection requires it to state, or to give a notice, required by this subsection within the period this subsection allows for it. Where the Board does not give the notice required by this subsection within that period, the member may seek review under subsection M of the Board's failure to give it at any time before the Board gives it, and the period within which the member may seek review of a determination under this subsection runs from the date the Board gives that notice.</u>

<u>**D. 1.** The Board shall not accept an election until the Board has, in writing, certified to the member and reserved the certified subsidy for that election under this subsection. The Board shall determine the certified subsidy for an election no later than 30 days after the member's application is completed. The Board shall give the certification required by this subsection no later than 15 days after the confirmation required by subdivision 3 is obtained. The Board's certification shall state the matters required by subdivision B 4 c of § 51.1-142.2, the filing date of the member's complete application, the amount of service credit to be purchased, the certified subsidy, the valuation date, the completion date by which the member must complete payment, the member's anticipated earliest qualifying retirement date and anticipated final qualifying retirement date, the member's unreduced retirement eligibility date determined with the service credit to be purchased as certified, the member's right to cancel the election under subdivision G 5 at any time before completing payment, and the member's obligations under subsections E, F, and G. The Board shall not accept an election until the member has filed with the Board a written acknowledgment of the member's obligations under subsections E, F, and G. The Board's certification shall also state that, where a member whose election the Board has accepted dies without having retired, the benefit payable on the member's death is determined under subdivision G 4 and under § 51.1-162 as on the death of a member in service and not under the provisions of subdivision C 1 of § 51.1-165 or of subsection F of § 51.1-165.01 that require benefits to be paid in accordance with § 51.1-163; that where the member dies while in service and subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165 otherwise applies, that subdivision provides for the payment of benefits in accordance with § 51.1-163, except that where the contingent annuitant or annuitants under the option elected are the same person or persons who would receive a monthly benefit under subsection B of § 51.1-162 and that monthly benefit would be greater, that subdivision provides for the payment of benefits in accordance with that subsection, and that the Board shall pay the benefit that subdivision provides or the benefit determined under subdivision G 4, whichever is greater; that no other option elected under § 51.1-165 or § 51.1-165.01 governs that benefit; that subsection B of § 51.1-162 provides a retirement allowance on the death of a member in service only where no benefit is payable under subsection C of that section and only to a surviving spouse, minor child, or parent whom the member has designated under subsection A of that section or who is entitled in the order of precedence that subsection B of that section states where the member has made no designation or the person designated has predeceased the member and no other designation has been made; that subsection C of § 51.1-162 provides a retirement allowance, where the member dies in service from a cause compensable under the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act (§ 65.2-100 et seq.), to the member's surviving spouse, minor children, or parents as that subsection provides; and that where no benefit is payable under subsection B of § 51.1-162, subsection A of that section provides for the payment of the member's accumulated contributions to the person the member has designated or, where the member has made no designation, to a surviving relative in the order of precedence that section states. A failure to state in the Board's certification every matter this subdivision requires to be stated in it is a failure of the Board to state in a certification a matter this section requires to be stated in it for the purposes of subsection M; a person adversely affected by it may seek review under subsection M no later than 30 days after the date on which the certification is given to the member or, where that person is not the member, no later than 30 days after the date on which the notice required by subdivision G 4 is given to that person; the second sentence of subdivision M 2 does not apply to such a failure; and the Board shall state every such failure in the report required by subsection J. The member shall file the notice of retirement required by § 51.1-153, stating as the effective date of retirement a date on which the member will retire in a qualifying retirement, no later than 30 days after the date on which the Board gives the member the written statement of the member's earliest qualifying retirement date and final qualifying retirement date first required by the definition of "final qualifying retirement date" in subsection A. Nothing in this subdivision requires the member to file that notice any period before the effective date of retirement stated in it. A notice filed within the period allowed by this subdivision satisfies § 51.1-153 notwithstanding that it is filed after the effective date of retirement stated in it; and where such a notice is filed after that effective date, the member's retirement shall take effect on that date and the retirement allowance shall be payable retroactively to that date, subject to subsection E of § 51.1-153 and provided that the effective date stated is on or after the day following the member's last day of service. The Board shall state the period allowed by this subdivision in the written statement required by that definition. Where the Board gives that written statement, any notice of a later approved date required by that definition, or any notice of a date required by that definition upon the Board's action upon a request, later than the time required by that definition, the member's final qualifying retirement date is extended by the number of days by which the statement or notice is late, the member may state an effective date of retirement that is no earlier than the first day of the month following the month in which the Board gives the statement or notice, and the Board shall state every such delay in the report required by subsection J. The Board shall state, in the written statement required by that definition and in every notice of a later approved date required by that definition, (i) that where the Board gives the statement or notice later than the time required by that definition the member's final qualifying retirement date is extended by the number of days by which the statement or notice is late, that the date so extended is subject to the limits stated in that definition, and that where the date so extended is not the first day of a month it is the first day of the next month; (ii) that the member may state as the effective date of retirement a date no earlier than the first day of the month following the month in which the Board gives the statement or notice, and that nothing in this subdivision prevents the member from stating an earlier effective date of retirement to which the member is otherwise entitled, subject to subsection E of § 51.1-153; and (iii) that where the Board mails the statement or notice, every period that runs from the giving of it is extended by three days.</u>

<u>2. The Board shall not accept an election if the certified subsidy for that election, together with the contingency amount required by subdivision 3 for that election and together with all certified subsidies and all contingency amounts previously reserved under this subsection and not released, would exceed $3,000,000. A certified subsidy that has been settled shall not be released, except to the extent required by subdivision G 1 or G 4, and the amount settled shall remain charged against the limitation in the first sentence of this subdivision. The Board shall not accept an election for which the certified subsidy exceeds $170,000. Where the certified subsidy determined for an election exceeds $170,000, the Board shall give the applicant written notice of the Board's determination that it may not accept the election, of the reasons for that determination, of the certified subsidy determined for the election, of the applicant's rights under subsection B of § 51.1-142.2, and of the standard cost, determined as of the date of the notice, of the service credit, if any, the applicant is eligible to purchase under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2 as of that date, no later than 30 days after the applicant's application is completed, and the applicant may seek review of the determination under subsection M. The limitations in this subdivision are self-executing and require no further act of the General Assembly, the Board, or any other person. A certification that the certified subsidy for an election is zero or less than zero shall not increase the amount available to be reserved for any other election.</u>

<u>3. Before reserving a certified subsidy the Board shall obtain the written confirmation of the Director of the Department of Planning and Budget, or his designee, that funds appropriated and legally available for the purposes of this section are sufficient to fund the reservation. The Board shall file the request required by this subdivision no later than 15 days after the member's application is completed. The Director or his designee shall act upon the Board's request within 30 days after the request is filed. If the Director or his designee does not act within that period, the Board shall give written notice of that fact, and of the date of the Board's request, to the member and to the Chairmen of the House Committee on Appropriations and the Senate Committee on Finance and Appropriations no later than 15 days after that period expires, and shall state every such lapse in the report required by subsection J. An application for which a request under this subdivision is pending retains its filing date for the purposes of subdivisions 4 and 5, and the Board shall act upon the application no later than 15 days after the confirmation is obtained. Where a request under this subdivision has been pending for 90 days, the Board shall give the applicant written notice of that fact, of the applicant's rights under subsection B of § 51.1-142.2, and of the standard cost, determined as of the date of the notice, of the service credit, if any, the applicant is eligible to purchase under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2 as of that date. If the Director or his designee determines that funds appropriated and legally available are not sufficient to fund the reservation, the Board shall place the application on a waiting list in the order determined under subdivision 4, shall give the applicant the notice required by subdivision 5, shall give written notice of the determination and of the reasons stated for it to the Chairmen of the House Committee on Appropriations and the Senate Committee on Finance and Appropriations no later than 15 days after the determination, shall state every such determination in the report required by subsection J, and shall request a further confirmation under this subdivision no less often than annually and no later than 30 days after the general appropriation act making appropriations for that fiscal year becomes law, until the application is accepted, the applicant withdraws it, or this section expires. A placement on the waiting list under this subdivision is a determination of the Board for the purposes of subsection M. The Board shall also reserve, at the time it reserves each certified subsidy, a contingency amount equal to `[20]` percent of that certified subsidy, to fund any recertification required by subdivision 6. A contingency amount reserved under this subdivision is charged against the limitation in subdivision 2, and the Board shall release it when the certified subsidy is settled under subdivision G 1 or G 4 or the reservation is released under subdivision 7. A reservation shall remain in effect until the certified subsidy is settled under subdivision G 1 or G 4 or the reservation is released under subdivision 7. The Board shall obtain a further written confirmation under this subdivision for each fiscal year in which any reservation remains in effect; until that confirmation is obtained the Board shall accept no further election, but the absence of the confirmation shall not release any reservation, shall not affect any election the Board has accepted, and shall not affect any service credit the Board has credited.</u>

<u>4. The Board shall accept elections in the order in which eligible members file complete applications. The Board shall give written notice to each applicant, within 30 days after the application is filed, stating whether the application is complete and, if it is not, identifying every item that is missing; the applicant shall have 60 days from that notice to supply the missing items, and an application completed within that period retains the filing date of the original application for all purposes of this section, including the determination of eligibility required by this subdivision. Where the Board does not give the notice required by this subdivision within 30 days after the application is filed, the applicant shall have 60 days from the date the Board gives the notice to supply the missing items, an application completed within that period or before the Board gives the notice retains the filing date of the original application for all purposes of this section, and the Board shall state every such delay in the report required by subsection J. Where two or more complete applications bear the same filing date and the amount available to be reserved is insufficient for all of them, the Board shall determine the order among them by lot. The Board shall determine whether an applicant is an eligible member as of the filing date of the applicant's complete application and shall give the applicant written notice of the determination no later than 30 days after the application is completed. Where the Board determines that an applicant is not an eligible member, the notice shall state the reasons for the determination, shall state the applicant's rights under subsection B of § 51.1-142.2 and the standard cost, determined as of the date of the notice, of the service credit, if any, the applicant is eligible to purchase under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2 as of that date, and shall state that the applicant may file a further application; and the applicant may seek review of the determination under subsection M. The Board shall accept applications through at least two channels, one of which shall not require the use of an electronic system, and shall publish on its website the amount remaining available to be reserved, updated no less often than monthly. An applicant may withdraw an application at any time before the Board accepts the election by giving the Board written notice of the withdrawal, and the withdrawal is effective on the date the Board receives the notice.</u>

<u>5. When the amount available to be reserved is insufficient for the certified subsidy of a complete application, the Board shall place the application on a waiting list in the order determined under subdivision 4, shall give the applicant written notice of that fact and of the reasons for it, and shall accept the election if and when sufficient amounts become available before the expiration of this section. The Board shall not accept an election, and no member shall acquire any right to purchase service credit at the cost determined under subdivision B 4 of § 51.1-142.2, before the Board has reserved the certified subsidy. An applicant who is denied or wait-listed retains every right to purchase service credit under subsection B of § 51.1-142.2 at the standard cost, and the Board shall state that right, and the standard cost, determined as of the date of the notice, of the service credit, if any, the applicant is eligible to purchase under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2 as of that date, in the notice required by subdivision 4 or by this subdivision, as the case may be. No later than 30 days before this section expires, the Board shall give written notice to each applicant then remaining on the waiting list stating that the application will not be accepted, stating the applicant's rights under subsection B of § 51.1-142.2, and stating the standard cost, determined as of the date of the notice, of the service credit, if any, the applicant is eligible to purchase under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2 as of that date.</u>

<u>6. If, before the member completes payment, the member's creditable compensation, creditable service, or anticipated effective date of retirement changes, or is corrected, such that the certified subsidy would increase or such that the service credit the member may purchase under subsection C would change, or if the member requests a redetermination under subdivision B 4 c of § 51.1-142.2, the Board shall recertify the certified subsidy and the service credit to be purchased and shall reserve any increase in the certified subsidy, in each case before the member completes payment. If the amount available to be reserved, including the contingency amount reserved under subdivision 3, is insufficient to fund the increase, the Board shall so notify the member in writing within 30 days and the member may (i) reduce the amount of service credit to be purchased, subject to subsection C, (ii) pay the amount of the increase, or (iii) cancel the election under subdivision G 5. The Board shall give the member written notice of every recertification under this subdivision, of the amounts as recertified, and of the reasons for the recertification, no later than 30 days after making it, and the member may seek review of the recertification under subsection M. Where a recertification under this subdivision reduces the amount of service credit the member may purchase under clause (ii) of subsection C to zero, the member's election is cancelled, the Board shall refund every amount paid under the election, shall release the reservation and the contingency amount reserved for it, and shall give the member written notice of the cancellation, of the reasons for it, of the member's rights under subsection B of § 51.1-142.2, and of the standard cost, determined as of the date of the notice, of the service credit, if any, the member is eligible to purchase under subdivision B 2 of § 51.1-142.2 as of that date, no later than 30 days after making the recertification, and the member may seek review of the cancellation under subsection M. Where a recertification under this subdivision reduces the amount of service credit the member may purchase but not to zero, the service credit to be purchased is reduced to the amount the member may purchase as recertified, and the Board shall refund any amount paid that exceeds the amount payable as so recertified. Where the member reduces the amount of service credit to be purchased under clause (i) of this subdivision or under clause (i) of subdivision B 4 f of § 51.1-142.2, the service credit to be purchased is reduced to the amount to which the member reduces it, the Board shall recertify the certified subsidy and the amount payable to reflect the reduction, and the Board shall refund any amount paid that exceeds the amount payable as so recertified. The Board shall pay a refund required by this subdivision to the member and to any employer that paid on the member's behalf, in proportion to the amounts each paid, within 60 days after the recertification, except that to the extent a refund may not be paid without adverse federal tax consequence the Board shall apply that amount to the purchase of additional service credit for the member at the standard cost and shall notify the member in writing of the amount so applied. An error by the Board in a certification shall not create any obligation of the member to pay any amount beyond the amount certified, and the Board shall correct the error and reserve any resulting increase from amounts available under subdivision 2.</u>

<u>7. A reservation expires, and the Board shall release the reserved amount, if the member has not completed payment by the completion date stated in the certification, if the member cancels the election, if the member fails to retire as required by subsection F, if the member's election is cancelled under subdivision 6, or, to the extent of any reduction, if the member reduces the service credit to be purchased under subdivision 6 or if a recertification under subdivision 6 reduces the certified subsidy. A reservation shall not be released under this subdivision by reason of the member's failure to retire as required by subsection F, or by reason of the member's failure to complete payment by the completion date, where the service credit purchased under the election vests under subdivision G 4; and a reservation shall not be released under this subdivision by reason of the member's failure to complete payment by the completion date previously stated where the member has made an election under clause (ii) of subdivision B 4 f of § 51.1-142.2 or where the period allowed by that subdivision for making such an election has not expired. The Board shall give the member written notice of the expiration and of the reasons for it. The Board shall release a reserved certified subsidy and its contingency amount upon giving the notice required by subsection C or by subdivision D 2.</u>

<u>**E.** The member shall complete payment of the amount determined under subdivision B 4 of § 51.1-142.2 by the completion date stated in the Board's certification, which shall be no later than 12 months after the date the Board accepts the election. Payment may be made by any method authorized by subsection F of § 51.1-142.2 and by the Board's procedures, provided that the aggregate of all payments shall equal the amount stated in the certification and no additional amount shall be charged by reason of the method or the timing of payment. The Board shall credit the purchased service credit to the member only upon receipt of payment in full, and shall do so within 30 days after receipt of payment in full. The member may request in writing, before or together with the final payment, that the Board credit the service credit as of the last day of a month stated in the request that is not later than 30 days after the date on which the member expects to make the final payment; the Board shall credit the service credit as of the date so stated if payment in full is received on or before that date, and in every other case shall credit it within 30 days after receipt of payment in full. In every case the Board shall give the member written notice of the date as of which it credits the service credit no later than five days after it credits it. No service credit purchased under an election shall be credited before payment in full is received, except as subdivision G 4 provides in the case of the member's death, of the member's approval for a disability retirement allowance, or of an error of the Board, and, except for the purposes of the definition of "eligible member" in subsection A, no member shall be treated as having reached his unreduced retirement eligibility date by reason of an election before payment in full is received.</u>

<u>**F.** A member whose election the Board has accepted shall retire in a qualifying retirement on or before the member's final qualifying retirement date. ## 2. Section-by-section summary in plain English

**For the Editor, in three sentences.** The bill lets a Virginia state employee who is a veteran buy
up to four years of prior active-duty military service at a price based on what that service would
have cost had it been credited when the employee was first hired — the plan's published normal cost
rate applied to the employee's own first three years of state pay, plus 6 percent interest to a
valuation date — instead of the much higher "actuarial equivalent cost" the employee pays today for
missing a 24-month deadline that ran out years ago. Only employees who need the purchased months to
reach an unreduced retirement — and who are already within reach of one on the day they apply, on the
service they actually have — may buy at that price, they must actually retire within a 12-month window
that opens when the purchase is credited, and the Virginia Retirement System may not accept an
election at all unless it has first certified and set aside the Commonwealth's share of the cost
within a $3 million aggregate ceiling and a per-member ceiling. The program runs for four years, applies only to state agency
employees — no locality, school division or teacher is touched — and nothing in it creates any debt
a member can be billed for after a purchase.

| Provision | What it does |
|---|---|
| **§ 51.1-142.2 B 2** (one new sentence) | Points the military-service price at the new subdivision 4 for members accepted into the pilot. Leaves the four-year maximum, the "not dishonorable" condition and everything else in B 2 alone. |
| **§ 51.1-142.2 B 3** (one inserted phrase) | Carves the pilot out of the approximate-normal-cost sentence, so a member inside a 24-month window who elects does not face two live prices. Neither sentence changes for the eight categories in B 1 or for anyone outside the pilot. |
| **§ 51.1-142.2 B 4 a** | Defines six terms (the sixth, **"limitation year," is new in revision 12** and points at § 415 and its regulations, because the § 415 deferral in B 4 f runs on that unit): "initial average compensation" (the average of the member's own first 36 months of state pay, with a stated tie-breaker when VRS records are incomplete), "statutory interest" (6 percent, compounded annually), "total normal cost rate" (the rate VRS publishes and already uses for approximate normal cost, **with the July 1, 2026 figures stated in the text** — 12.50 percent Plan 1, 10.74 percent Plan 2), "standard cost" (what the member would pay if the pilot did not exist), and "qualifying military service election." |
| **§ 51.1-142.2 B 4 b** | The price. Normal cost rate × first-36-month average pay × years bought, plus 6 percent compound interest from the end of that 36-month period to a single valuation date — **or** the standard cost, whichever is lower. Interest stops at the valuation date, so the certified amount is a fixed sum no matter how the member pays. No member can be made worse off than today. |
| **§ 51.1-142.2 B 4 c** | VRS must show its work: both prices, the valuation date, the rate used and the assumptions behind the standard cost. The member gets one right to demand a re-quote before paying, and pays the lowest of all four figures. |
| **§ 51.1-142.2 B 4 d** | The 24-month window does not apply to a pilot purchase. This is the reform: the price stops depending on how long ago the member was hired. |
| **§ 51.1-142.2 B 4 e** | The member pays at least 5 percent of the price from money the employer did not give them for that purpose; an agency may pay the rest only after filing a written policy covering all of its **eligible** employees, and the policy may cap the agency's own annual exposure. |
| **§ 51.1-142.2 B 4 f** | VRS must apply the federal § 415 limits before accepting any payment, and nothing in the bill touches USERRA rights or the existing free military-leave credit. **Revision 12:** if the limit blocks part of a payment, the member is told within 30 days and may buy fewer months, **defer the blocked amount to the next limitation year** with the payment deadline extended and the reservation held, or cancel — so a federal limit nobody controls cannot cost the member the election and the price. The determination is reviewable, and it is expressly not treated as VRS error. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 A** | Definitions, including "certified subsidy" — the Commonwealth's share, measured as the increase in the plan's liability caused by the election, counting both the price discount and the earlier retirement, minus what the member and employer pay, with a floor equal to the price discount, **reserved on the assumption that the member retires on the first date they may and then redetermined against the date they actually retire**. Also defines "unreduced retirement eligibility date," which is the test every eligibility and quantity rule now turns on; **"earliest qualifying retirement date" and "final qualifying retirement date," which are the two ends of the retirement window — the **12 months** in the second of those sets how long a member has to go, chosen by the Director's direction of 12 October 2026 and no longer bracketed**; "involuntary separation," by reference to the Workforce Transition Act; and "state agency." Eligibility expressly covers **both benefit tiers** (VRS Plan 1 and Plan 2), stated by reference to the Code's own discriminator in § 51.1-155 A 1 rather than by the administrative plan names; hybrid program participants remain out. **Revisions 16 and 17 complete the admission test.** "Eligible member" now has **two** date legs rather than one: the employee must be within reach of an unreduced retirement **on the day the application is filed** (clause (vi)), *and* the months actually bought must get them there when the service is credited (clause (vii)). Without the first leg, an employee up to about 13 months short could qualify simply by taking the full 12 months to pay — a different and larger group than every costed figure in the package describes. Revision 17 then bounds the first leg by **the military service the employee actually has** and by the four-year and state-service limits, so no one can be admitted on a purchase they could never make and left to fail at crediting; and it says in terms that the *minimum-necessary* trim in subsection C does **not** bound that test, which is what keeps the program from admitting nobody at all. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 B** | Establishes the program; confirms purchased credit is ordinary creditable service; makes participation voluntary and forbids an employer from pushing an employee into it. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 C** | Quantity limit: no more than the months actually needed to reach unreduced retirement, measured as of one stated completion date, capped by the existing four years and by the member's own years of state service. Paying early requires a re-quote. **Store revision 59, on the Director's direction of 12 October 2026 (§7 item 109), extends the subsection's own reporting duty to a failure to *state* a matter the subsection requires the Board to state** — previously it reached only a failure to determine or to notify, so the one duty edit 1 created had no *"or else."* |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 D** | The cap as an admission rule. VRS must certify the price and the Commonwealth's share, get written confirmation from DPB (or a designee, within 30 days) that money is legally available, and reserve the money **before** accepting an election. Aggregate ceiling $3,000,000, per-member ceiling **$170,000, set by the Policy Director's direction of 11 October 2026 and no longer bracketed (§0.0000000)** — and note that the sentence **refuses** an election above the ceiling rather than trimming it (§0.0000000c); first-come order with a 60-day cure period that keeps the original filing date, a lot-draw for same-day ties, a non-electronic filing channel, a published running balance, wait-listing instead of rejection with written notice on expiry, a fixed **`[20]` percent** contingency that is **charged against the $3,000,000**, recertification on **any** increase, and release of money when a reservation lapses. Reservations do not survive past the fiscal year following the year they were made without a fresh DPB confirmation. A zero-cost certification cannot create headroom for someone else. **Revision 3:** a reservation now lasts until the election settles or is released, and the annual DPB confirmation is a brake on *new* admissions rather than something that can strand a member who has already paid. The member signs an acknowledgment of obligations at acceptance. **Revision 12 fixes the notice deadline, which could not be met as drafted:** the member files the formal § 51.1-153 notice of retirement on the later of 30 days before the date they choose and **30 days after VRS tells them the window** — and because § 51.1-153 E allows a notice up to 90 days after the effective date, a member whose window opens almost immediately can still file and is paid retroactively. **Revision 12 also puts a consequence behind DPB's 30 days:** if the Director does not act, VRS must notify the member and the money-committee chairmen within 15 days, report every lapse, hold the application's filing date, and act within 15 days of the confirmation when it comes. **Revision 5:** the contingency is counted against the $3,000,000, so the cap is a cap, and it rises to `[20]` percent because 10 percent failed the 90th percentile of recertification increases. (Revision 5 also set the per-member ceiling at `[$100,000]`; **that figure is superseded by the $170,000 above** and is recorded only as the revision history.) **Store revision 59 puts two more clocks in, on the Director's direction of 12 October 2026 (§7 item 108):** VRS must **file** the DPB request within 15 days after the application is completed, and must **give** the certification within 15 days after the confirmation is obtained. Before them the Board could meet every period the section stated and still leave an eligible member waiting for ever, because subdivision D 1 bars acceptance until the certification issues and nothing required the certification. The worst case now ends in the existing 90-day applicant notice at about **day 105** instead of never. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 E** | Payment within 12 months and in the certified amount; VRS credits the service only when payment is complete, and within 30 days of completion. Nobody is treated as retirement-eligible on a half-paid purchase. **Revision 12:** the member may ask VRS to credit the service **as of the last day of a stated month**, and VRS must confirm the date it credits within five days. That matters because § 51.1-153 E requires a retirement to take effect *after* the member's last day of service, and without a fixed crediting date the member would have to resign against a date they could not yet know. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 F** | The retirement condition: the member must retire on the first of some month in the window that opens the month after the credit is posted and closes **12 months** later, with up to three further months where VRS's own processing — or **any other cause not the member's fault** — makes retirement by the deadline impracticable. **Revision 12 turns that extension from a discretion into a duty**: VRS *shall* approve it, the member may ask for it in writing at any time before the deadline, VRS must answer within 30 days with reasons, and the refusal is reviewable. VRS must tell the member both dates in writing within 15 days of posting the credit — i.e. before the member resigns — and must pay retroactively if its own processing delays commencement. States in terms that failure to retire creates **no** debt and that nothing may be collected or withheld. **Revision 21 closes the one place where that promise was not kept** (pass 4's F4 and [MOO-65](/MOO/issues/MOO-65)'s F-2, found independently and both called fatal): VRS may now make the G 2 determination as soon as the member retires in a non-qualifying retirement rather than only after the deadline has passed, the member's first allowance payment must carry every amount that would have been payable from the effective date of retirement, and subsection F says in terms that the deferral is not a withholding and reduces nothing. A member who retires early in her window is no longer left up to roughly thirteen months without a retirement cheque. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 G** | Settlement at retirement, and what happens if it does not happen. If the member retires, the subsidy settles and the credit vests, and within 90 days VRS **redetermines the subsidy against the date the member actually retired** — capped at the amount reserved, with the difference released to fund other elections, and with no consequence of any kind for the member, the member's beneficiaries or the employer. If the member chooses not to retire, VRS re-values the payments at ordinary prices, keeps the member's credit at that reduced amount, refunds the excess within 60 days and releases the reservation — with 30 days' written notice, reasons and a review right, and **before any allowance is ever paid**, so no overpayment can arise. Partial payment buys pro-rata credit at ordinary prices. **Revision 10 — the middle treatment.** Death, disability retirement and VRS's own error are carved out: the credit vests at the pilot price, and a survivor is paid as though the member had retired. **Involuntary separation is no longer carved out.** A member who is involuntarily separated keeps the pilot price only by retiring in a qualifying retirement within 90 days of the separation; otherwise the ordinary re-valuation applies — the member's own payments buy credit at ordinary prices, the excess is refunded, and nothing is billed to anyone. VRS must tell the member the date within 15 days of learning of the separation. The 12-month rehire rule is deleted, because there is no longer a vesting carve-out for it to police. **Revision 12 closes the hole in that carve-out.** The credit vested on death, disability or VRS error but nothing *settled*, because settlement keyed only to a retirement inside the window — so the Commonwealth's appropriation never paid and the pension trust was left carrying the subsidy. G 4 now settles on the earliest of death, the start of a disability allowance, and the member's actual retirement, runs the same redetermination as a normal settlement, and repeats the no-debt guarantee for survivors and beneficiaries. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 H** | One election per member — but the bar bites only on a member who actually got credit or settled, so a member whose application expired on the waiting list or whose election was cancelled by VRS error is not locked out. Every existing purchase right is preserved. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 I** | Closes the contractor route: a retiree who comes back within 12 months doing substantially the same work — measured against the Employee Work Profile for the job they left — is treated as re-employed, so the allowance stops under § 51.1-155 B 1. The agency reports within 30 days, and VRS must give notice, 30 days to respond and a written determination **before** stopping any payment, which stops prospectively only. No service accrues and no contributions are owed for the period. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 J** | Reporting. VRS reports every December 1, during the pilot and then until every election has been settled, cancelled or determined — a tail tied to the facts rather than to a fixed three years, because at a long retirement window the last determinations would otherwise fall after the last report — on applications, admissions, denials, wait-lists, money reserved, settled and paid, who actually retired, and the variance between its certified subsidies and realised actuarial cost. DHRM and DOA supply replacement-cost payroll and contract data to **DPB**, and **DPB** — not the Board — writes the December 1, 2032 evaluation. **Store revision 59 gives the post-terminal further report an independent trigger** (§7 item 113): a matter arising after the last annual report requires a further report even where no rescission under subdivision G 4 occurs, the rescission having been the only trigger before. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 K** | The kill switch. If the General Assembly declines to appropriate what the Board certifies it needs, VRS stops accepting **new** elections — and elections already accepted, money already reserved and credit already posted are untouched. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 L** | Says in the text that no locality, political subdivision, school board or local retirement system bears any duty or cost, and that no teacher or local employee may elect. |
| **§ 51.1-142.4 M** | Delegation boundary. ## 4. What could not be drafted without the MOO-39 answer

MOO-39 asks whether a Plan 1 member's early-retirement reduction is measured back from age 65 or
from the date the member *would have* reached 30 years of creditable service. It is worth 32 percent
of the S9 score. **S8 as drafted above does not depend on the answer, and nothing in §1 is held for
it.** Three items are:

1. **The S9 reduction-waiver provisions are not drafted.** S9 buys off the early-retirement
   reduction instead of selling service credit. Its operative sentence has to say what is being
   waived, measured how — and that is precisely the open question. **Conditional on MOO-39:** if the
   answer is "projected," an S9 provision can be drafted as a purchased waiver of the reduction
   computed under § 51.1-155 with the unreduced date determined as though service had continued; if
   the answer is "frozen," the waiver is worth roughly 35 percent more and the price, the cap and the
   per-member ceiling all have to be reset before any language is useful.
2. **MOO-39's owner should be pointed at § 51.1-155, not § 51.1-153.** This is MOO-44's finding
   outside its assignment and it is correct, and I can now confirm it from the live text I retrieved
   for the conforming amendment. § 51.1-155 A 3 states the measure as *"the first date on which the
   sum of his then attained age plus his then creditable service **would have been equal to 90 or
   more had he remained in service until such date**"* and A 2 as *"the first date on which he
   **would have completed** a total of 30 years of creditable service."* **That is the projected
   reading, expressly so in A 3.** The Code is not silent on this question. Routing to Policy
   Director rather than acting on it, because MOO-39 is not mine.
3. **The per-member subsidy ceiling cannot be carried across to S9.** The bracketed figure, whatever
   MOO-48 returns, will be derived from the S8 run, on this definition, at a chosen window length. A
   single ceiling across two designs needs both priced on the same basis.

Note also that the eligibility test in § 51.1-142.4 A and the quantity limit in subsection C now
turn on the defined term "unreduced retirement eligibility date," which **as revised in revision 7**
cites § 51.1-155 **A 1, A 2 and A 3** by name — the positive limb routes to the normal-retirement
computation in A 1 and the negative limb excludes the two reduction provisions. That is a strict
improvement for MOO-39 purposes, and revision 7 makes it a larger one: the draft no longer pretends
§ 51.1-153 settles the reduction question, and any future S9 language has one defined term to attach
to whose both limbs are explicit. **For MOO-39 specifically** — whether a Plan 1 member's reduction
is measured back from age 65 or from the projected 30-year date — revision 7 changes nothing. The
definition asks whether an allowance **is** reduced under A 2 or A 3, not by how much or measured from
when, so the answer to MOO-39 moves the S9 price and not this bill's admission test.

### §6.0-63 Ambiguities resolved in store revision 63

1. **Why *"the person who filed the request"* and not *"the member."*** M 2's class of persons who may seek reconsideration is seven categories wide — member, retiree, beneficiary, applicant, personal representative of a deceased member's estate, person who claimed a death benefit and was refused, and employer that paid on a member's behalf. A notice right keyed to *"the member"* would have given the estate, the refused death-benefit claimant and the paying employer a reconsideration they could not convert into a decision. The enacted words follow the filing, which is the only fact common to all seven.
2. **Why the denial is made a case decision in terms rather than by relying on the completion sentence.** §0.0-63 repair 1. 3. **Why *"first day after the expiration of those 30 days"* and not *"the last day."*** §0.0-63 repair 2: a denial on the last day of a period in which the Board may still lawfully act puts two inconsistent determinations on one date.
4. **Why the 30 days are not tolled, extended or qualified.** The member's 30-day notice period under the new sentence runs from her own filing, so there is nothing for the Board to delay. Subsection M 2's tolling operates on *"any period within which the member or applicant must act"* — and the new period is one in which the **Board** must act, so the tolling provision does not reach it and no carve-out is needed.
5. **Why the denial is not deemed an act of the Board.** §0.0-63, last paragraph, and §7 item 130. Deeming it an act would have ended the member's tolling and her D 4 / D 5 filing-date retention as a by-product — two endpoints that belong to [MOO-164](/MOO/issues/MOO-164) and card (k) and that nobody has decided. **A cure for a residual must not move a priced endpoint in passing.**
6. **What the board's answer costs, stated and not netted away.** Up to 30 further days before the member may go to court, and a member who never files the notice never gets a decision. That is the same bargain § 2.2-4021 B strikes against every party before every agency in the Commonwealth, which is why option (d) was modelled on it — but it is a cost, and it falls on the member who does not know she has a right to assert. 2. **Item 117(b) anchors on clause (ii) alone and not on the whole of subsection H.** § 51.1-156 H's mandatory limb has two conditions: (i) the member *"would otherwise qualify for disability retirement but for failing to comply"*, and (ii) the Medical Board's certification of three facts. **Clause (i) is supplied by the approval itself** — the trigger operates only where the member *"is thereafter approved for a disability retirement allowance"*, and a member who is approved has otherwise qualified. Anchoring on both would have made the Board prove a condition its own approval had already decided. **This is the point of the finding:** the enacted trigger is a **fact the Board can see in its file**, not a legal conclusion about what the Board *"was required"* to do.

3. **No express subsection J report line was drafted for item 103's determination, and no fallback was drafted for pass 17's M-9 — and the second of those is a decision, not an omission.** J 1 already reports *"every failure of the Board to make a determination, or to give a notice, within a period required by this section, stated separately for each provision"*, so an express line would be surplusage. **M-9 is CLOSED and needs no text.** It was held to item 103 on the ground that the redetermination clock has no fallback where the Board never makes the error determination. With item 103 enacted the gap is **self-consistent and remediable**: the clock sentence opens *"Where the Board rescinds a determination"*, and the rescission cannot occur without the determination, so a fallback date would start a clock that never runs. What the member needed was a **remedy for the absent determination**, and item 103 gives her one — a 30-day period, a notice duty, review under subsection M 2's failure limb, judicial review under Article 5, and a line in the J report. **Drafting a deemed determination instead would have been a policy choice nobody made.**

4. **Item 120(b) is enacted as a conjunctive duty, not as an exception.** The carded repair was *"the same words R2 enacted in the rescission branch"* — an *"except that where the member has died"* clause. **In the death branch the member has always died**, so the condition is always true and the exception form is nonsense. Enacted: *"and shall pay the whole of that refund to the member's estate … and shall apply no amount of it to the purchase of service credit."* The form also cures **pass 18's M18-3** as a by-product: as an exception restating the verb without a period, the death refund could be read to carry away the main clause's **60-day deadline**; as a conjunctive duty it cannot, and the estate keeps the deadline.

**Five further choices, none of which changes which member passes either definitional test.**

5. **Item 114's amendment leaves the two existing *notwithstanding* clauses standing.** They become surplusage. Deleting them would be tidier and would re-open the argument the amendment exists to foreclose — that the bar was never meant to catch these cases at all — and store revision 52 legislated twice on the strict historical-fact reading. **Surplusage is the cheaper of the two risks.** The record states it so a reader does not take the redundancy for a drafting slip.

6. **Item 114's *"in full"* is confined to the whole-reservation release and that is what makes it operative.** D 7 releases *"to the extent of any reduction"* on a D 6 recertification and releases the whole reserved amount in its other five limbs. Had the amendment read *"released in accordance with subdivision D 7 and has not been reinstated"* it would have cured nothing for the D 6 member, because her partial release is never reinstated — it was correct.

7. **Item 118(b)'s long-stop is the last sentence of J 1 and reads on *"report under this subdivision."*** The card attached it to limb (a) of the first sentence. Placed there it would not have reached the **third** sentence's further report, which has its own trigger, and the duty would still have been interminable on the facts item 118 describes. As the closing sentence it bounds **every** report J 1 requires, which is what the card's stated purpose — *"makes the duty terminable whatever happens"* — asks for. It is confined to *"this subdivision"*, so J 2 and J 3 are untouched.

8. **Item 103's route is confined to the period after a determination under subdivision 2 or 3 has taken effect.** Before that moment the Board is already under a **60-day** duty to determine whether clause (iii) applies, with a 15-day notice duty, a J report line and review under subsection M; an unconditioned member-filing route would have put a **second clock on one determination**, which is pass 18's M18-9. 9. **Item 120(a) is enacted although pass 18 shows its stated premise is false, and the part that would actually fix the problem is carded.** The card's ground was that *"G 4 credits the whole purchase on clauses (ii) and (iii) too."* It does not: G 4's crediting duties are confined to **death** and to the **rescission** branch, and clause (ii) switches off G 3's partial-crediting duty along with subdivisions 2 and 3. So for a member approved for a disability retirement allowance **before** completing payment, with no prior determination, there is **no crediting duty to lift a bar in front of**. The amendment is still right on its own terms — E's death-only exception conflicted on its face with G 4's unqualified *"Subsection E does not prevent the crediting of service credit required by this subdivision"* — and it is enacted on that narrower ground. **The crediting duty is §7 item 126**, drafted in full, enacted nowhere, and recommended: it decides what a partial payer is credited with and what she is refunded, which is a money question and the Director's.
