### §6.0-73 Ambiguities resolved in store revision 73 — with two departures from the approved strings, two adverse findings recorded rather than cured, and one correction to the pass

1. **Why S3's inserted clause carries no conjunction, and why that is not a change of policy.** The approved string opened *", and that that designation determines…"*. Item 7 is a series of three *"that"* clauses and its single *"and"* belongs to the last of them. A clause inserted in front of the last clause and bringing its own *"and"* gives **"A, B, and C, and D"** — the defect **substitution 3 of the composite spent four characters to avoid in the outer series one revision earlier, in the same sentence**. The choice was between inserting without the conjunction (one edit, **+123**) and inserting with it and striking the later one (two edits, the same net figure, touching the no-fault clause that M4 also edits). **The one-edit form wins on footprint.** The approved form is recovered by adding the word *"and"*, and the Director should say so if the literal string matters more than the series.
2. **Why M3 names the report instead of saying *"that report"*.** The approved string ended *"to state a matter in that report"*. In enactment clause 3 clause (iii) the only antecedent is *"the last report and evaluation required by that subsection"*, so the approved words would have continued past the expiry only those provisions requiring a statement in the **last** report. The clause continues the **duties** until the last report has been submitted; it does not continue provisions for the last report only. **The act's own standing phrase — *"the report required by subsection J"*, 14 times before this cut and 15 after — is the referent, and naming it costs 24 characters.** The alternative, *"a report required by that subsection"*, would have introduced a second phrase for one concept.
3. **ADVERSE, and the Director should read it before anything else: S1 narrows subsection M's channel and does not close it.** Subsection M 2's second sentence deems *"Every determination, **certification**, redetermination, …"* a determination of the Board for the purposes of the subsection, *"whether or not another provision of this section expressly provides for review of it."* **S1 excepts the advice failure from the failure-to-state limb and leaves the determination limb untouched**, so the same grievance can be brought by a member *"adversely affected by a determination of the Board under this section."* The route is demonstrated by the act's own words and by a clause this drafter wrote: **subdivision D 1 disapplies M 2's second sentence for the member, which it would have no reason to do unless the determination limb were otherwise available**, and it disapplies it only *"to such a failure"* — which after F3 and S1 no longer includes the advice failure. **The least generous reading is therefore that the one failure the Director has twice decided should not be reviewable is the one failure for which the deeming sentence is left in force.** The better reading is that a specific exception controls a general deeming; the text does not say it, and **one sentence in M 2 would**. Form **M-c, +216**, drafted at §7 item 137, resolving 1× on the cut text, **and enacted nowhere** — because the words are the Director's to approve and because subsection M is open only so far as the Director has opened it.
4. **ADVERSE: S5 does not reach the subdivision G 4 notice, which is the one document a survivor ever receives.** S5 freezes *"a statement **this subdivision** requires"*. **Subdivision G 4 requires three statements of the effect of provisions of § 51.1-162** in the death notice — the subsection C right to waive the allowance, the period that subsection states for the waiver, and the subsection B right to elect the accumulated contributions — and S5 reaches none of them. The defect S5 cures therefore survives in the worse place. **One sentence cures both**: S5's own sentence generalised from the subdivision to the section, with the date supplied by the act's existing compound *"that statement, notice, or certification"* from subsection A, and the final words changed from *"satisfies this subdivision"* to *"satisfies the provision of this section that requires it"*. Form **S5-w, +48 over S5 as enacted**, at §7 item 138, **not enacted.** The alternative — a parallel sentence in G 4 — would put two constructions of one concept in two places, which is the thing the chapter's vocabulary rule forbids.
5. **Why S5 freezes the four Virginia sections and leaves the Internal Revenue Code reference dynamic.** §1 says *"as amended or renumbered"* twice, both times of § 401(a)(37) of the Internal Revenue Code, and S5 deliberately does not use that formula. **The two point in opposite directions.** The Code reference is a live **condition on a duty** — the Board must state the subsection B proposition only *"to the extent required by"* § 401(a)(37) — so it must follow amendments, or the duty would be frozen to a repealed federal rule. A compelled **description of Virginia law** must not move after it has been given, or the Board is in breach of a statement it made correctly. The asymmetry is the point, and S5's *"as it reads on the date the certification is given"* is new to the act for that reason.
6. **Why M4 widens one of three *"nothing in this subdivision"* clauses and leaves two.** Subdivision D 1 carries three. M4 widens the **no-fault clause**, because the thing it has to foreclose — a procedure adopted under subdivision M 1 treating a member's non-review as a fault — is not in the subdivision at all. The other two concern the **notice of retirement**, which is a duty this subdivision alone creates, and widening them would make statements about provisions that do not exist. The lower-case count moves **3 → 2** and the capitalised *"Nothing in this section"* formula stays at **3**, M4's clause being mid-sentence.
7. **CORRECTION to the [MOO-211](/MOO/issues/MOO-211) pass, C1: M4's recorded description does not hold.** That kit recorded that M4 *"widens the first limb only; the second limb ('or affects any benefit…') stays keyed to the subdivision."* **The clause has one subject governing both verbs** — *"nothing in this subdivision **requires** … or **affects** …"* — so replacing the subject widens both. The consequence is harmless and arguably better, because **no provision of § 51.1-142.4 conditions any benefit on the member's having reviewed the designation**, so the wider limb forecloses an argument nobody should be able to make. **But the words were approved on a description of them that was wrong, and the words are enacted while the description is corrected.**
8. **Recorded and not cured: §2 has never described the advisory.** The cross-reference sweep's check 6 found that §2's plain-English summary contains *"designation"* **0 times**, *"beneficiary"* **0**, *"advis-"* **0** and *"failure to state"* **0** in 22,876 characters. **So four revisions have built a provision the summary does not mention** — store revision 65 created it, 70 re-sited and re-worded it, 73 limits it twice and excepts it in subsection M. That is a gap in the summary and not a defect in the bill; the Editor owns §2 and nobody has commissioned it. **It is recorded here so that it is not discovered by a reader of the memo.**
9. **Why the Director's confirmation on scope135 needed no drafting.** The Director confirmed that the exception names **the advice and only the advice**. Nothing was cut for it: item 7's two propositions of law keep subsection M review, which is the state the composite already enacted. **A confirmation that the text is right is discharged by leaving the text alone**, and the item is closed on that footing rather than on a substitution.
10. **Why the retention rotation moved both halves and not only the half that was forced.** §5's preamble named §5.43 and §5.44 as the next to move; §6's named §6.0-63 and §6.0-65. The **§5 half alone** would have cleared the record layer with roughly nine thousand characters to spare, which is the margin store revision 71 inherited and could not write in. **Store revision 70 moved one half and store revision 71 then had to move the other under pressure**, so this revision moves both halves of a rotation that two paragraphs had already named. **The structural answer remains the one recorded at store revision 70: §5 and §6 should hold the current entry and one predecessor and nothing else.**
