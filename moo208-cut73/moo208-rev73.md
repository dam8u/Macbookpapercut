**Two of the approved strings carried mechanical defects on their face. Neither departure adds, removes or qualifies a proposition, and each is reversible with one word.**

**S3, −4.** The approved insert opened *", and that that designation determines…"*. Item 7 is a series of three *"that"* clauses whose single *"and"* belongs to the last — *"…that the member may make or change…, and that nothing in this subdivision requires…"* — so a clause inserted in front of the last and bringing its own *"and"* gives **"A, B, and C, and D"**. That is the defect **substitution 3 of the composite spent four characters to avoid in the outer series**, one revision earlier and in the same sentence. The inserted clause therefore takes no conjunction. **Say the word and it goes back to +127.**

**M3, +24.** The approved insert ended *"…to state a matter in that report,"*. In enactment clause 3 clause (iii) the **only** antecedent for *"that report"* is *"the last report and evaluation required by that subsection"* — the clause names no other report — so the approved words would have carried past the expiry only those provisions requiring a statement in the **last** report. The referent is named in the act's own standing phrase, *"the report required by subsection J"*, **14× in §1 before this cut and 15× after**. This is the rule against cross-referencing by paraphrase, applied to a paraphrase.

## 4. The other enactment clauses, and what this revision did to them

- **Enactment 1** — the operative amendments and the new section.

**A note on S2's drafting, which is an ambiguity resolved rather than a choice made.** Clause (v) opens *"every duty of the Board to determine and to give notice under § 51.1-142.4 in respect of an application filed before that date"* and then continues subsection M *"as it applies to **such a** determination or to a failure to make **such a** determination or to give **such a** notice."* S2's limb deliberately carries **no *"such"***: a certification exists only on an application, and an application can only have been filed before the expiry, so the narrowing would have added words without narrowing anything — and a *"such"* with no antecedent of its own is how a limb gets argued out of existence.

## 6. Two adverse findings on the cut text, drafted and enacted nowhere

**Neither is among the six you approved. Both are yours to decide, and I have added nothing to the bill.**

### 6.1 The cure you approved for F3 narrows the subsection M channel and does not close it — form M-c, +216, §7 item 137

S1 excepts the advice failure from subsection M 2's **failure-to-state limb**. **M 2's second sentence independently deems a *certification* a determination of the Board**, by name:

> *"Every determination, **certification**, redetermination, recertification, reduction, cancellation, rescission, restoration, crediting of service credit, placement on a waiting list, expiration of a reservation, denial of an election, and refusal to accept an election under this section is a determination of the Board for the purposes of this subsection, whether or not another provision of this section expressly provides for review of it."*

So a member whose certification omits the advice may still seek reconsideration as a person *"adversely affected by a determination of the Board under this section"*, on the determination clock.

**The proof that this is a route and not a reading is a clause I wrote.** Subdivision D 1 disapplies *"the second sentence of subdivision M 2"* for the member — **a disapplication that exists for no other reason than that the determination limb is otherwise available** — and it disapplies it only *"to such a failure"*, which after F3 and S1 no longer includes the advice failure. **The one failure you have twice decided should not be reviewable is the one failure for which the deeming sentence is left in force.** The contrary reading is respectable — a specific exception controls a general deeming — but the text does not say so.

**The cure.** One sentence in M 2 after the deeming sentence: *"A certification is not a determination of the Board for the purposes of this subsection by reason only of a failure to state in it that the Board advises the member to review the member's designation of beneficiary."* **+216, no markup, no conforming amendment anywhere**, FIND 154 characters resolving 1× in the live §1. **My recommendation: take it.** It is the same finding as F3, in the only provision that can close it, and the composite's record should not sit with the channel described as closed.

### 6.2 The saving clause you approved does not reach the subdivision G 4 death notice — form S5-w, +48, §7 item 138

S5 freezes *"a statement **this subdivision** requires"*, and subdivision D 1 is not the only provision that compels a description of § 51.1-162. **Subdivision G 4 compels three, in the notice the Board gives on the member's death:** the subsection C right to waive the allowance, **the period that subsection states for the waiver**, and the subsection B right to elect the accumulated contributions. **None is frozen.** If § 51.1-162 C were amended to change the waiver period, the Board would be compelled to state the new period with no saving clause — and the reader of that notice is **a survivor acting on a deadline**, not a member with years in hand. The defect S5 cures survives in the worse place, which is also the **only** document a survivor ever receives.

**The cure replaces S5's own sentence rather than adding one**: the subdivision becomes the section, the date is supplied by **the act's own compound *"that statement, notice, or certification"*** which subsection A already carries, and the satisfaction clause follows. **+48 over S5 as enacted, no markup, no conforming amendment.** **My recommendation: take it.** Three characters of policy and forty-eight of drafting, in the provision whose failure a survivor cannot appeal.
