## 0. The class, restated more narrowly than the referral states it

The referral says the discretionary-waiver member "is reached by **neither** limb of the cured rescission trigger." That is true of the two *cured* limbs and is **not a sufficient statement of the class**, because the rescission sentence has a **third, pre-existing limb** that the referral does not mention. Verbatim from the live text, the sentence reaches a member thereafter approved for a disability retirement allowance:

> "…**upon an application filed no later than the date on which the determination took effect**, or upon an application the filing of which did not require a waiver of a ninety-day notification period set forth in subsection A or B of § 51.1-156, or upon an application as to which the Medical Board has made a certification described in clause (ii) of subsection H of § 51.1-156…"

(count 1 each for all three limbs; see §3.)

The first limb — the team's **class 1** in the item-117 taxonomy at `moo208-cut73/out-items-73.md` line 2603 — rescues **any** member whose application was filed on or before the determination's effective date, **including a discretionary-waiver member**. The register has always had this right; the referral's framing of the class does not. The surviving class is therefore **five conditions deep**, not two:

**Class 3, stated completely.** A member who
1. elected under § 51.1-142.4 and whose election the Board accepted;
2. did not retire in a qualifying retirement, so that a determination under subdivision G 2 or G 3 was made **and took effect**;
3. filed her application for a disability retirement allowance **after** the date on which that determination took effect;
4. filed it more than ninety days after termination of service (and was not in service when she filed), so that a waiver of the § 51.1-156 A or B notification period **was required**; and
5. obtained that waiver from the Board **for good cause shown under the first sentence of § 51.1-156 H**, no certification described in clause (ii) of subsection H having been made by the Medical Board.

**This matters for the memo.** Any risk-section sentence drafted from the referral's two-limb framing will overstate the class — it will read as though every discretionary-waiver member is excluded, when the great majority of them (everyone who filed before the determination took effect) is rescued by limb 1. **Marked for the Editor: M-1.**

## 1. The strongest case against, in the opposition's voice

*Written as its best advocate would write it. This is a steelman, not my conclusion.*

> Virginia is about to enact a statute under which a disabled career state employee loses a six-figure retirement benefit because of a procedural choice her own government made about her, and which she had no power to influence, review, or even be told about.
>
> Take the Commonwealth at its word about what this bill does. A state employee buys back her active-duty military service under a programme the General Assembly created to reward military service. The Commonwealth subsidises that purchase — on the Lab's own measured figures, an average of **$106,253** a head, up to a statutory **$170,000**. She pays her share. Then she becomes catastrophically ill and cannot reach the retirement date the programme requires. The Board reduces her credit, takes back the subsidy, and refunds her a prorated sliver.
>
> The General Assembly saw exactly this case coming and wrote a rescue. If she is later approved for disability retirement, the Board must restore everything. But the rescue is rationed, and here is how it is rationed. If the Medical Board happens to have signed a certificate under clause (ii) of § 51.1-156 H, she is restored. If it did not, she is not. And whether it did is not a fact about her, her service, her disability, or her diligence. Section 51.1-156 H says in terms that the Medical Board acts **"solely in its own discretion."** She cannot apply for that certificate. She cannot appeal its absence. The retirement Board that holds her $106,000 cannot be compelled to seek it — and has every reason not to, because this programme runs under a hard $3,000,000 cap, every restoration is charged against that cap **"notwithstanding that the amount available to be reserved is thereby exceeded,"** and the Board administers a waiting list of other members who want the money. The cheapest thing the Board can possibly do is grant her waiver quickly on general good cause, so that the clause (ii) question never arises, and then tell her the statute does not reach her.
>
> So the Commonwealth's position reduces to this: two members, identically disabled, identically late, identically blameless, are treated differently — one keeps $106,000 and one does not — and the sorting criterion is an unreviewable internal decision by a state medical panel about whether to put pen to paper. That is not a legislative classification entitled to deference under *FCC v. Beach Communications*. It is the government **"intentionally treated differently from others similarly situated"** with **"no rational basis for the difference in treatment"** — *Village of Willowbrook v. Olech*, 528 U.S. 562, 564 (2000) — and the number of people in the class is, the Court said there, immaterial.
>
> And the Commonwealth cannot plead administrative necessity, because it already conceded the point three times. It rescinds for the member who filed early. It rescinds for the member who filed on time. It rescinds for the member with a clause (ii) certificate. Each of those rescissions blows through the cap by express legislative command. Having written **"notwithstanding that the amount available to be reserved is thereby exceeded"** into the bill twice, the Commonwealth is in no position to say that a fourth category would make the cap unadministrable. What it actually means is that it would rather not pay. Under *Olech* that is not enough, and under Article X, § 11 of the Constitution of Virginia — a retirement system **"administered solely in the interests of the members and beneficiaries thereof"** — it is close to an admission.
>
> There is no finding in the record of why class 3 fares worse than class 2. The Lab's own register says so: the exclusion is recorded as **"a policy choice, not a drafting defect."** A policy choice is exactly what rational-basis review asks the Commonwealth to defend, and the Commonwealth has not written down a reason.

**Where that brief is strongest:** the sorting criterion is an agency act, not a member characteristic; and the cap-breach concession undercuts the administrability defence. Those two points are real and I have turned them into findings S-1 and S-2.

**Where it is weakest:** it needs *Olech* to survive *Engquist v. Oregon Department of Agriculture*, 553 U.S. 591 (2008), which held the class-of-one theory inapplicable to state action that "by [its] nature involve[s] discretionary decisionmaking based on a vast array of subjective, individualized assessments" — which is a precise description of a good-cause disability waiver. It also has to get past the member's written acknowledgment of her obligations under subsections E, F and G, filed before the Board accepted her election. And Article X, § 11 ends with "**The retirement system shall be subject to restrictions, terms, and conditions as may be prescribed by the General Assembly**," which is a complete answer to the Article X limb.

## 2. The Director's five questions, answered

### Q1 — The affected classes

The item-117 taxonomy, confirmed against the live text. All four classes presuppose that a G 2 or G 3 determination has **taken effect** and the member is **thereafter** approved for a disability retirement allowance.

| | Class | Reached by | Live? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Application filed on or before the determination's effective date | limb 1, pre-existing | **rescission** |
| **2** | Filed after; § 51.1-156 H's **second sentence required** the waiver, the Medical Board having certified under clause (ii) | limb 3 (cure 2) | **rescission** |
| **4** | Filed after, but inside § 51.1-156 A or B's own ninety days — or while still in service, for whom § 51.1-156 A sets no period at all — so no waiver was required | limb 2 (cure 1) | **rescission** |
| **3** | Filed after; waiver **required** and granted by the Board **for good cause shown under H's first sentence**, no clause (ii) certification made | **nothing** | **excluded** |

**A fifth class nobody has named, and it is the right comparator.** The **pre-determination** disability member — approved *before* any G 2 or G 3 determination takes effect — is protected by G 4's **first sentence** ("Subdivisions 2 and 3 shall not apply…"), which carries **no waiver condition of any kind**, reinforced by two further protections in the same subdivision: the Board "shall not make a determination under subdivision 2 … while an application of the member for a disability retirement allowance under this chapter is pending, and a determination made in contravention of this sentence is of no effect," and the G 2 clocks do not run while such an application is pending (subject to a 120-day aggregate for second and later applications).

**Consequence, and it is the analytical core of this review.** A discretionary-waiver member who is approved *before* the determination takes effect is treated **identically** to the timely filer and the clause (ii) filer. The distinction the Director asks about therefore exists **only inside the post-determination rescission window** — and only for a member who, having already had her credit cut, then files late and is waived discretionarily. The classification is not "the bill treats discretionary waivers worse." It is "the bill does not reopen a *final* determination for a discretionary waiver." That reframing is worth a great deal to the Commonwealth and the memo should carry it.

### Q2 — The distinction in statutory treatment

What class 3 loses, and what classes 1, 2 and 4 keep:

- **Classes 1, 2, 4:** the Board "shall rescind the determination and shall restore or credit service credit as this subdivision provides, no later than 60 days" after approval; the certified subsidy "is settled under this subdivision notwithstanding that the rescinded determination had taken effect, and notwithstanding that the reservation for the election has been released"; purchased credit vests at the **subdivision B 4 subsidised cost**; the Commonwealth pays the settled amount to the retirement system under subsection N.
- **Class 3:** the G 2 determination stands. Her credit stays reduced to "the largest number of whole months of service credit that **the member's payments** would have purchased at the **standard cost**." She receives a refund of the excess — but only her **proportionate share** of it, "in proportion to the amounts each paid" as between her and any state agency that paid on her behalf. The subsidy is not paid.

**Quantum.** On the authorised figure set (MOO-199, 6.00% statutory interest, 12-month rung, four-year pilot from 1 July 2029): mean certified subsidy per participant **$106,253** nominal, against the **$170,000** per-election cap. That is the size of the dollar differential, and it is substantial for an individual. It is **not** a forfeiture — she keeps credit bought at standard cost and gets a refund — which materially reduces the constitutional stakes. The differential also has a **service-credit** dimension, and its size is unsettled: see **M-2**, where two available readings of G 2 clause (i) are four months of retained creditable service apart.

### Q3 — The applicable level of constitutional scrutiny

**Rational basis, on both the federal and the Virginia route. I found no credible argument for heightened scrutiny and I looked for one.**

- **No suspect or quasi-suspect class.** Disability classifications draw rational-basis review: *City of Cleburne v. Living Center*, 473 U.S. 432, 442–47 (1985); *Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett*, 531 U.S. 356, 366–68 (2001). Age likewise, in a public-retirement case: *Massachusetts Board of Retirement v. Murgia*, 427 U.S. 307, 312–14 (1976).
- **No fundamental right.** The interest is a conditional subsidy toward an optional purchase of service credit — economic and social-welfare legislation. *Dandridge v. Williams*, 397 U.S. 471, 485 (1970); *FCC v. Beach Communications*, 508 U.S. 307, 313–15 (1993) (classification sustained "if there is any reasonably conceivable state of facts that could provide a rational basis"; the legislature "need not articulate its reasons").
- **The actual classification is by filing posture, not by disability.** Every class here is disabled and approved. They are sorted by *when they filed* and *how the waiver came about*. A classification on procedural-compliance timing in a benefits scheme is the paradigm case for deference.
- **Virginia adds nothing.** Article I, § 11's anti-discrimination clause is **enumerated** — "religious conviction, race, color, sex, or national origin" (verified on `law.lis.virginia.gov`, 12 October 2026). Disability and filing timing are not on that list. The Supreme Court of Virginia applies rational basis to economic classifications under its due process, equal protection and special-legislation provisions alike: *Willis v. Mullett*, 263 Va. 653 (2002) (legislation is sustained "if the legislature could reasonably have concluded that the challenged classification would promote a legitimate state purpose").
- **Article IV, § 14 special legislation is not engaged.** This is a general law classifying by conduct, not by named person, place, or closed group.
- **Article X, § 11 cuts for the Commonwealth, not against.** The clause the opposition would plead — funds "invested and administered solely in the interests of the members and beneficiaries thereof" — governs the **trust funds**, and the subsidy at issue is **not** trust money: subsection N pays it to the retirement system "from an appropriation made for that purpose." Refusing it costs the trust fund nothing. And the section closes: "The retirement system shall be subject to restrictions, terms, and conditions as may be prescribed by the General Assembly." Verified verbatim, 12 October 2026.
- **The real risk is not class-based.** It is *Olech*. A class of one does not need a suspect class or a fundamental right; it needs intentional differential treatment of a similarly situated person with no rational basis. That is why S-1 is my top finding and why the scrutiny label is not where this case is won or lost. Against it the Commonwealth has *Engquist*, 553 U.S. at 603–04, and *Engquist* is a strong fit on these facts, because the waiver decision is exactly the kind of "subjective, individualized assessment" the Court held unsuited to the theory. I note the honest limit: *Engquist* arose in public employment, where the government acts "as proprietor," and its reach into discretionary benefits administration is not uniformly settled. The VRS relationship is a benefits relationship with current and former employees, which is closer to *Engquist* than to the zoning and utility-connection cases.

### Q4 — The government's justification

Three, in descending strength. Only the first is strong, and the Commonwealth does not presently have it in writing.

1. **Finality bounded by objectively verifiable facts, so that a hard cap stays administrable.** The programme runs on a **$3,000,000** aggregate reservation limit and a **$170,000** per-election limit. When a G 2 determination takes effect the reservation is released and the money returns to the pool, where it can be committed to other members from the waiting list. Each rescission limb therefore **reinstates an amount over the cap by express command** — "charged against the limitations in subdivision D 2 notwithstanding that the amount available to be reserved is thereby exceeded" (count 2 in the live text). The Commonwealth may rationally confine that programmed breach to limbs whose trigger is a **verifiable historical fact**: a filing date (limbs 1 and 2) or a certificate on the file (limb 3). "Good cause shown" is, by design, not such a fact — § 51.1-156 H's first sentence supplies no standard for it beyond the second sentence's safe harbour. **This is the one justification that would carry a rational-basis case, and it is a good one.**
2. **Avoiding a standardless reopening power — a nondelegation point running the other way.** Folding class 3 in would mean the same Board that made the G 2 determination could reopen it, and breach its own cap, whenever it chose to find good cause, on a standard the Code does not define. The exclusion of class 3 is what keeps the cap breach tied to rules rather than to grace. A court is likelier to be troubled by the alternative than by the line as drawn.
3. **Notice and consent.** The Board may not accept an election until the member has filed "a written acknowledgment of the member's obligations under subsections E, F, and G," and subdivision G requires the certification to state the G 4 consequences. She knew the terms. Weak as an answer to an equal-protection claim — consent does not cure an irrational classification — but it defeats any Contract Clause or vested-rights framing (U.S. Const. art. I, § 10; Va. Const. art. I, § 11), since the condition is prospective and disclosed before acceptance.

**The gap.** Justification 1 is not stated anywhere in the record as the reason for the class-3 line. The register records the exclusion as "a policy choice, not a drafting defect," and item 117 says in terms that "there is no reason in the record" why the classes fare differently. Under *Beach Communications* the Commonwealth does not need to have articulated a reason — but a bill that carries the reason in a drafter's note or a patron's statement is a materially better defendant than one that does not. **Marked for the Editor: M-3.**

### Q5 — Material litigation risk?

**My assessment: a real but low risk, and — this is the point the Director should take from this review — the risk is materially lower than the inversion's was, for a reason that has nothing to do with the merits.** Four things changed when the inversion was cured.

1. **The sympathetic plaintiff is gone.** The inversion's plaintiff was the member who *complied with the Code's own deadline* and was treated worse than one who missed it by eight months. That is a self-proving case: no judge needs a brief to see it. Class 3's plaintiff missed a statutory deadline and was excused as a matter of grace. She is a far worse vehicle.
2. **The class shrank to a fraction of a person.** See §4: expected class size over the four-year pilot is **0.04 members** central, **0.48** in a deliberately pessimistic case. A classification nobody is standing in is rarely litigated.
3. **Standing and venue are narrow but real.** The route is **not** a direct constitutional action in the first instance. It is: request reconsideration under subdivision M 2 — and M 2's catch-all reaches her, both because "rescission" and "reduction" are expressly determinations for M 2 purposes "whether or not another provision of this section expressly provides for review of it," and because she may proceed on a "failure of the Board to make a determination … within a period required by this section," which she may file "at any time before the Board makes the determination," so the 30-day clock on the original G 2 notice does not shut her out. The Board's decision on reconsideration "shall constitute a case decision as defined in § 2.2-4001." Then Article 5 of the Administrative Process Act, Va. Code § 2.2-4026: notice of appeal with the agency secretary within 30 days of service of the final order (Rule 2A:2), petition for appeal in the circuit court named within 30 days more (Rule 2A:4) — the path the Lab established on MOO-186. The equal-protection claim would be pleaded there, and/or as a 42 U.S.C. § 1983 claim in the Eastern District of Virginia, Richmond Division. She will plead *Olech* first and class-based equal protection second.
4. **Constitutional avoidance is unavailable, which is a mixed blessing.** Because the saving sentence resolves the text expressly (see §5, null result N-1), a court cannot duck the question by construing G 4's first sentence to control. It must decide it. That makes the question unavoidable but also confines it: there is **one** question, and it is decided under rational basis, where the Commonwealth has justification 1.

**Bottom line, with both halves travelling together as MOO-160 C16 binding 1 requires.** The Lab's Red Team assesses that **the Commonwealth would probably prevail** on a challenge to the class-3 exclusion. That is a litigation assessment of the Lab's Red Team, not legal advice; counsel has not cleared the proposal, and that caveat travels with the assessment wherever it is quoted. At the same time, **the classification is not unproblematic, it is not clearly constitutional, and it would not certainly survive** — the *Olech* route described in S-1 is a genuine one, and it would be much weaker if S-1 were fixed.

**And it is nearly free to fix.** That is the recommendation this review ends on. See §4.

## 4. Fiscal assumption challenge

**The load-bearing assumption.** That the residual class is de minimis, so excluding it costs the Commonwealth nothing it would notice and exposes it to nothing it should fear. Nobody in the record has stated this assumption or tested it, and **the fiscal model has no disability channel at all** — its own caveat says the figures exclude "life, survivor loading, disability or death-in-service, which **understates** cost." **No figure anywhere in the record prices any of G 4's four rescission limbs.** This section is the first quantification of that channel.

**The test.** `moo218-counsel/class3_exposure.py` — my own working file. It modifies **no** team file and does **not** rerun the MOO-183/MOO-199 engine (which has no disability dial to turn). It reads the authorised export `moo199-authorised-export/out_moo199_model_data.json` read-only and layers a five-step decomposition over the measured participant count. Every probability in it is **mine and labelled as mine**; none is measured.

Measured inputs: participants central **18.3965**, high **30.6608**; certified reserved nominal central **$1,954,680**; cap utilisation at $3,000,000 central **0.7819**, high **1.3031**. Derived: mean certified subsidy **$106,253** (62.5% of the per-election cap); central headroom **$654,384**; high **overshoot $909,360**.

Decomposition — p2 does not reach a qualifying retirement (so a determination is made); p3 disability approval lands *after* it takes effect, conditional on p2, which selects **for** ill health and so sits far above the VRS base rate; p4 filed more than ninety days after termination, so a waiver is required; p5 waived discretionarily under H's **first** sentence with no clause (ii) certification:

| Scenario | n | p2 | p3 | p4 | p5 | Expected class-3 members, 4-year pilot | Value of the exclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Central | 18.40 | .15 | .15 | .20 | .50 | **0.0414** | **$4,398** (0.225% of certified reservations) |
| Pessimistic | 30.66 | .25 | .30 | .30 | .70 | **0.4829** | **$51,310**; **$82,094** if every case prices at the $170,000 cap |
| Absurd ceiling (every participant) | 30.66 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 30.66 | $3,257,800 — stated only to bound the arithmetic |

**The result, and it reverses the premise.** The assumption that the class is de minimis **holds** — and that is bad news for the exclusion, not good news. Because the class is tiny, **excluding it saves the Commonwealth roughly four thousand dollars, central, against a programme reserving $1.95m.** The fiscal case for the exclusion is not load-bearing; it is rounding error.

**The alternate number, stated as my role requires.** If the Director folds class 3 into the rescission trigger:

- **Central: +$4,398.** Absorbed inside the $654,384 of existing cap headroom roughly 149 times over.
- **Pessimistic: +$51,310, at most +$82,094.** Still inside existing central headroom.
- **At high take-up it changes nothing structurally**, because the cap is already breached by design at 130.3% ($909,360 over), and the rescission limbs already carry the express over-cap command. A fourth limb adds **no new mechanism** — only a fourth occasion for one that is already in the bill twice.

**What this does to justification 1.** It does not destroy it — administrability is about *predictability of the standard*, not about dollars, and "good cause shown" is genuinely unbounded in a way a filing date is not. But it means the Director's decision now rests **entirely** on the standards rationale and **not at all** on cost. If the memo defends the exclusion on cost, the defence is false on the Lab's own figures. **Marked for the Editor: M-5.**

**Referred to Fiscal & Impact Modeler:** the model has no disability channel, so the whole G 4 rescission branch — classes 1, 2 and 4 as well as 3 — is unpriced, and every rescission is a programmed cap breach. At high take-up the cap is already at 130.3% before any of it is counted. That is a gap in the fiscal record, not a finding against this bill's text, and it is **M-6**.

## 5. Draft text defects, clause by clause

Re-read in full from the live text. Who, what, by when, or else; and is every term defined.

**N-1 — NULL RESULT. The first-sentence contradiction is cured, and I checked specifically because I expected to find it.** G 4's first sentence says subdivisions 2 and 3 "shall not apply" to a member approved for disability, with no timing condition. That is in apparent tension with letting a G 2 determination stand against class 3 — item 91's "the text therefore says both that subdivision 2 never applied to her and that its determination stands." **It is expressly resolved**, count 1: "A determination under subdivision 2 or 3 that has taken effect is **not affected** by an approval of the member for a disability retirement allowance upon an application filed after the date on which the determination took effect, **except as this subdivision provides for the rescission** of the determination." Specific over general, and the exception is named. **The plaintiff's statutory-construction route is closed and I would not plead it.** This is also why constitutional avoidance is unavailable (Q5, point 4).

**N-2 — NULL RESULT. Cure 1's cross-reference is correct and its scope is right.** "a ninety-day notification period set forth in subsection A or B of § 51.1-156" tracks § 51.1-156 H's **own** label for those periods ("the ninety-day notification periods set forth in subsections A and B"), so there is no undefined term and no stranded reference. It correctly does **not** reach § 51.1-156 **D**'s separate ninety-day requirement (effective date no more than ninety days before filing, waivable for good cause), which is not "set forth in subsection A or B." A member who needed only a D waiver therefore remains inside limb 2 and is rescued. That is the right result and I confirm the drafting achieves it.

**N-3 — NULL RESULT. Cure 1 is wider than the carded words and the widening is correct.** Framing it on whether a waiver *was required*, rather than on filing "within a period set forth in subsection A or B," reaches the member whose final qualifying retirement date passed **while she was still in service** — for whom § 51.1-156 A sets no period at all, so no waiver could be required. The carded form would have missed her. Confirmed against § 51.1-156 A's "Any member in service **or** within ninety days after termination of service."

**N-4 — NULL RESULT. Cure 2's cross-reference resolves.** "clause (ii) of subsection H of § 51.1-156" is the Medical Board certification clause of H's second sentence, and it is distinct from the general certification every disability retirement needs under § 51.1-156 **E**. No collision, no ambiguity as to which certification is meant.

**N-5 — NULL RESULT. Class 3 is not without a procedural route.** I checked for a duty with no "or else." Subdivision M 2's catch-all reaches her on two independent footings (see Q5, point 3), the Board's decision is a case decision under § 2.2-4001, and Article 5 judicial review follows. She can get to a court. Her problem is the **merits** — the trigger is keyed to a certification she cannot compel — which is S-1, not a procedural gap.

**N-6 — NULL RESULT. No ex post facto, Contract Clause, or takings problem on this class.** The programme is prospective; the G 4 terms are disclosed in the Board's certification; the member files a written acknowledgment of her obligations under subsections E, F and G before acceptance; and G 2 **refunds** rather than forfeits. There is no vested interest in a subsidy that has not settled. I looked for a takings framing and there is none worth pleading.

**S-1 — SERIOUS. The trigger is keyed to an agency *act* nobody can compel, not to the *facts* that compel the act.**
Cure 2 reaches an application "as to which the Medical Board **has made** a certification described in clause (ii)." Section 51.1-156 H (ii) says the Medical Board acts "**solely in its own discretion**." So:
- the member cannot apply for the certification and cannot appeal its absence;
- the retirement Board cannot compel the Medical Board to make it; and
- **nothing in § 51.1-156 or in the bill occasions a clause (ii) certification once the waiver has already been granted.** H's second sentence operates on whether the Board "shall waive"; once the period *is* waived, the clause (ii) question is moot and the Medical Board has no reason to reach it.

**The compliance-arbitrage path, which is the Board's and not a regulated party's.** The cheapest way for the Board to avoid a cap-breaching restoration is to grant the waiver **promptly and generously on general good cause**, so that the clause (ii) question never arises. The member gets her disability retirement — she is not harmed in the disability system at all — and loses $106,253 on average because of the route her waiver travelled. The incentive is real and it is not fiscal self-dealing: the subsidy comes from a general appropriation under subsection N, not from trust funds, so the Board gains nothing fiduciary. What it gains is **cap capacity for the waiting list** — every restoration is charged over the cap and crowds out other members' elections. The pressure is member-against-member, which is precisely the kind of pressure a statute should not leave to unguided discretion.

**Concrete failure scenario, with facts.** A corrections officer, 31 years of service, elects in August 2030 to buy 24 months of active-duty credit; certified subsidy $118,000; she and her agency complete payment. Her final qualifying retirement date is 1 March 2033. In January 2033 she is diagnosed with early-onset ALS and stops work on 15 February 2033; she **knows** her diagnosis. She misses 1 March. The Board makes its G 2 determination on 10 April 2033, notices it on 12 April, and it takes effect 15 May 2033 — reducing her 24 months to the 9 months her own payments buy at standard cost and refunding her share of the excess. Her condition deteriorates; her agency does not file on her behalf (§ 51.1-156 F says the appointing authority "may," not "shall"); she files her disability notification on **3 August 2033**, 169 days after termination. The Board waives the ninety-day period on 20 August for good cause shown under H's **first** sentence — she was hospitalised, the record is unambiguous, and the Board grants it without troubling the Medical Board on the H (ii) question, which it would lose anyway because she had **knowledge** of her disability within ninety days, so H (ii)(b) fails on its face. She is approved for disability retirement on 1 October 2033. **Result:** limb 1 fails (filed 3 August, after 15 May); limb 2 fails (a waiver was required); limb 3 fails (no clause (ii) certification). **She loses $118,000 because the Board was kind to her quickly, and she had no way to ask for the certificate that would have saved her.** Had she filed on 10 May instead of 3 August — five days before the determination took effect, with her condition no different — limb 1 would have restored the whole of it.

**Authority.** § 51.1-156 H (ii) ("solely in its own discretion") — verified, count 1. *Village of Willowbrook v. Olech*, 528 U.S. 562, 564 (2000). The counter is *Engquist*, 553 U.S. at 603–04, and it is a good counter — which is why this is serious and not fatal.

**Fix direction, one sentence, for Drafting Specialist.** Key the limb to the **facts** rather than to the certificate: reach an application "as to which the Medical Board has made a certification described in clause (ii) of subsection H of § 51.1-156, **or as to which the Board finds that the matters described in subdivisions (a), (b) and (c) of that clause existed**." That preserves the enacted language untouched (ruling 4 and ruling 7 respected — nothing is reopened, a disjunct is added), removes the agency's power to sort members by procedural route, gives the member something she can prove, and keeps the trigger objective. It costs, on my §4 arithmetic, a fraction of the $4,398 central figure, because it only reaches the subset of class 3 who could have satisfied H (ii) on the facts.

**S-2 — SERIOUS. The administrability justification is never stated, and the record affirmatively says there is no reason.**
Justification 1 is strong and nothing in the bill, the drafter's notes, or the register asserts it. The register records the exclusion as "a policy choice, not a drafting defect," and item 117 says in terms that "there is **no reason in the record** why class 4 fares worse than class 2" — the same silence now attaches to class 3.

**Failure scenario.** Class-3 plaintiff moves for summary judgment in the Circuit Court on an Article 5 petition, attaching the Lab's own published materials, and argues that the Commonwealth has never articulated any distinction between class 2 and class 3 beyond unwillingness to pay. The Commonwealth's counsel must then invent the finality-and-administrability rationale at the brief-writing stage, with no legislative or administrative record behind it. *Beach Communications*, 508 U.S. at 315, says the Commonwealth may do exactly that — the legislature "need not articulate its reasons" and the burden is on the challenger to negative every conceivable basis. So the Commonwealth very likely still wins. But the posture is avoidable and it is the difference between a summary dismissal and a contested merits ruling with a written opinion about a VRS classification.

**Authority.** *FCC v. Beach Communications*, 508 U.S. 307, 313–15 (1993); *Willis v. Mullett*, 263 Va. 653 (2002).

**Fix direction.** State justification 1 once, in a drafter's note or in the memo's risk section, in the terms of §2 Q4 point 1: the rescission limbs breach the aggregate cap by express command, so they are confined to triggers that rest on a verifiable historical fact. This is free and it is the highest-value item in this review after S-1.

**M-1 — MODERATE. The referral's two-limb framing of the class is incomplete and would overstate the class in any memo drafted from it.** See §0 and citation check 10. **For the Editor.** Any risk-section sentence must carry condition 3 — that the application was filed *after* the determination took effect — or it will describe a class several times larger than the one that exists, and will be wrong on the face of the bill. Failure scenario: a memo sentence reading "members whose ninety-day waiver was granted as a matter of discretion are excluded from the carve-out" is **false**; the discretionary-waiver member who filed before the determination took effect is restored in full by limb 1, and the one approved before any determination takes effect never loses anything at all.

**M-2 — MODERATE. "The member's payments" versus "the amounts paid" — the reduction base and the refund base do not match, and the size of the class-3 member's retained position turns on which reading governs.**
G 2 clause (i) reduces credit to what "**the member's payments**" would have bought at standard cost. G 2 clause (ii) refunds the excess of "**the amounts paid**," split "in proportion to the amounts each paid" between the member and any state agency that paid on her behalf. Read naturally — and the drafter's own contrast between the two phrases confirms the reading — an agency contribution to the purchase price **buys her nothing** under clause (i) but **is counted** in the clause (ii) refund base.

**Worked example** (arithmetic run; standard cost $5,000 a month; member pays $60,000, her agency pays $20,000 on her behalf; the certified subsidy is a separate Commonwealth payment that under subsection N never settles in a G 2 case):

| | Credit retained | Excess refunded | To member | To agency | Member's net cost per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Harsher reading** — clause (i) counts only her own payments | **12 months** | $20,000 | $15,000 | $5,000 | $3,750 |
| **Milder reading** — "the member's payments" includes amounts paid on her behalf | **16 months** | $0 | — | — | $3,750 |

**What the arithmetic actually shows, which is not what I first supposed.** The member's cost **per month of retained credit is identical** on both readings — $3,750 — because the refund share she receives under the harsher reading is drawn partly from the agency's money. So the mismatch does **not** make her dollar loss larger. What it changes is **four months of creditable service**: she retains 12 months rather than 16. And the party that actually bears the mismatch is the **state agency**, which under the harsher reading nets $15,000 spent for **zero** additional months of credit for the member it paid for.

**Why it is in scope.** It is not a class-3 point — it hits every G 2 member identically — but the Director's question 2 asks what the bill does differently to each class, and class 3's **retained** position cannot be stated without resolving the reading. Against classes 1, 2 and 4, who keep the whole purchase at subsidised cost, the class-3 member retains either 12 or 16 of her 24 months. **The differential in creditable service is one third larger under the harsher reading.** Not a reopening of C16 and not a defect in the cured language.

**Fix direction.** Define the term once, either way, or conform the two phrases. **For Drafting Specialist and for Fiscal & Impact Modeler**, who should know which reading the figures assume.

**M-3 — MODERATE. No stated reason for the class-3 line in any artifact.** The reportable half of S-2, carried separately because it is the Editor's action, not the drafter's. **For the Editor:** this must appear in the memo's risk section.

**M-4 — MODERATE. The Commonwealth's best answer to *Olech* rests on authority I could not confirm for this circuit.** *Engquist* was a public-employment case. Its extension to discretionary benefits administration is not uniformly settled and I found no Fourth Circuit decision resolving it. Failure scenario: the memo states flatly that class-of-one claims are unavailable here; the Lab is asked for the Fourth Circuit case; there is none in the record, and the memo's risk section is weaker than the one that had simply said the defence is strong but not settled. **For Statutory Researcher** (pull Fourth Circuit treatment of *Engquist* outside employment) and **for the Editor** (until then, state the defence as strong and not settled, never as settled).

**M-5 — MODERATE. Do not defend the exclusion on cost.** On the Lab's own authorised figures the exclusion saves about **$4,398** central. A memo sentence implying the class-3 line protects the cap or the appropriation is false on the Lab's own numbers and would be the easiest thing in the package for an opponent to falsify. **For the Editor.** Defend it on standards and finality or not at all.

**m-1 — MINOR. Cure 2 is slightly wider than "the mandatory-waiver class," and that is harmless.** It reaches any application "as to which the Medical Board has made a certification described in clause (ii)," whether or not the Board's waiver actually rested on it — so a member who received a discretionary waiver *and* happens to have a clause (ii) certification on file is restored. That over-inclusion runs in the member's favour and I would not change it. Recorded so that nobody later mistakes it for a defect.

**m-2 — MINOR. The 120-day aggregate tolling cap can be reached by a member in good faith.** Periods in G 2 and G 4 do not run while a disability application is pending, but the aggregate of non-running attributable to reconsideration requests and to **second and later** disability applications is capped at 120 days. A member who files, is refused, obtains medical evidence, and refiles can exhaust the aggregate and watch the determination take effect under her — pushing her out of limb 1 and into class 3 through no fault of her own. This is the mechanism that **populates** class 3 rather than a defect in the class-3 line, and it was a deliberate choice on item 118. Recorded as a contributor to p3 in §4.

**m-3 — MINOR. Section 51.1-156 F's permissive appointing-authority filing is a quiet contributor.** Where a member is "physically or mentally unable to submit written notification," her appointing authority "**may**" submit on her behalf — permissive, with no duty, no deadline, and no consequence for inaction. The most sympathetic route into class 3 runs straight through it: the member who could not file and whose employer did not. Amending § 51.1-156 F is outside this bill's four amended sections and I am **not** recommending it; recorded because the opposition will use it, and the Lab should not be surprised by it.

## 6. What I checked and found sound — explicitly

- **The C16 cure itself.** Both cured limbs are live at the asserted counts, the counterfactual trigger is absent from the whole of §1, the cross-references resolve, and cure 1's "did not require a waiver" form is correctly wider than the carded words. **I identify no remaining defect in the corrected language**, and ruling 7 is therefore satisfied: nothing here reopens it. **S-1 adds a disjunct; it does not alter an enacted word.**
- **The first-sentence contradiction (N-1).** Cured by the saving sentence. I went looking for it specifically and it is not there.
- **Internal coherence of G 4's rescission branch.** Four limbs plus death plus Board error; each has an actor, an act, a 60-day deadline, and an M 2 review route. The over-cap command and the reinstatement rule are both present, so a rescission cannot fail for want of headroom.
- **The procedural route for class 3 (N-5).** She has reconsideration under M 2 and Article 5 judicial review. No duty here lacks an "or else."
- **Scrutiny level.** Rational basis on both routes. I looked hard for a heightened-scrutiny hook — Article I, § 11's enumerated grounds, Article IV, § 14 special legislation, Article X, § 11, a fundamental-right framing — and there is none.
- **Article X, § 11.** Checked as a possible independent Virginia hook on the theory that a retirement system must be administered in members' interests. **It runs for the Commonwealth, not against**: the duty attaches to trust funds, the subsidy is appropriated money under subsection N, and the section's closing sentence reserves to the General Assembly the power to prescribe terms and conditions.
- **Contract, ex post facto, takings, vested rights (N-6).** Nothing to plead. Prospective programme, disclosed terms, written acknowledgment before acceptance, refund rather than forfeiture.
- **Dillon's Rule and preemption.** Checked and **not engaged**. No local power, duty, or displaced ordinance anywhere in the provision; no federal scheme occupying the field. The only federal touch is § 401(a)(37) of the Internal Revenue Code, which the bill accommodates rather than contradicts.
- **The team's class taxonomy.** Item 117's four classes match the live text limb for limb. The register is right; only the referral's restatement is incomplete.
- **Severity discipline.** Nothing is rated fatal. S-1 is a real design defect with a real failure scenario and an expected frequency under one twentieth of one member over four years; calling that fatal would cost this review its usefulness.
