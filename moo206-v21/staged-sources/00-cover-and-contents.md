## What this package asks for

One change to one sentence of the Code of Virginia, confined to military service.

Today a Virginia state employee who served on active duty before being hired may buy up to four
years of that service toward VRS retirement. The price depends on **when the member was hired**, not
on the service bought. Inside the first 24 months of state employment the price is approximate
normal cost. One day later it becomes actuarial-equivalent cost — about three times as much for a
long-service member.

The bill replaces that calendar test, for military service only, with a price measured off the
member's own early-career earnings plus statutory interest — and removes the 24-month deadline. The
Commonwealth's exposure is fixed by an appropriation that the bill will not let VRS exceed.

::: key
A veteran hired at 24 pays approximate normal cost for four years of military service. An otherwise
identical veteran hired at 34 — after a full enlistment — pays roughly three times as much for the
same four years. Nothing about the service differs. Only the hire date does.
:::

## The four things worth knowing before you read further

**1. The ask is an appropriation, not an open-ended liability.** The bill admits a member only while
VRS certifies that enough of a capped subsidy reservation remains to cover that member's estimated
cost. When the reservation is exhausted, further applicants are wait-listed — and keep every right
they have under current law today. The Commonwealth's maximum exposure is the number written in the
enactment clause: **$3.0 million over the four-year pilot**. The estimated net cost to the trust is
**$1.3 million** in present value, range **$0.66M to $2.2M**, or **$1.7 million** with the one-time
$375,000 of VRS systems work. **That rung prices every member retiring on the last permissible day,
so the exposure is one-sided dearer: +7.5% on a uniform spread of retirement dates and +16.3% if all
retire at the earliest.** The **per-election** figure is a mean certified subsidy of **$106,253** as
the cap clause certifies it, across **18.4 elections (range 9.2–30.7)** — **a count that is an upper
bound**, because clause (viii) of "eligible member" is in the live draft and in no committed model
run and can only refuse more. On modelled demand the appropriation is **39% / 78% / 130%** committed
at low, central and high take-up with the `[20]`% contingency inside it — modelled demand against the
cap, not observed utilisation.

**2. The interest rate sets the member's price; the retirement deadline sets the Commonwealth's
cost.** We recommend **6.00% per annum, compounded annually, written as a fixed number** —
Virginia's own legal rate of interest under Va. Code § 6.2-301 — and the draft charges the
**lesser** of that price and the current-law price, so no member can ever be worse off. But the
rate is not the cost control. This design costs money because members retire earlier than VRS
expects them to, and the member's payment does not reach that. The levers that do are the
retirement deadline, the completion requirement and the cohort. **The deadline is still open and it
is worth 32% of the cost** — see document 03 §8.

**3. We do not claim the bill pays for itself, and the package contains no savings claim.** The
workforce value of an earlier, orderly retirement is real, it is quantified in document 04, and at
central assumptions it does **not** cover the pension cost. The independent-auditor record on state
early-retirement incentives is bad enough that any savings claim would lose the argument it started.
We present the workforce effect as a measured, bounded exchange with its frictions shown.

**4. The package carries its own adversarial review.** Document 07 is a risk register produced by
reviewers whose assignment was to defeat this proposal. They found no fatal legal defect and a
number of serious items, all listed with their current status — including the ones still open. They are in the package because a committee will find them anyway, and it is better that they
arrive with our answer attached.

## Open items, stated up front

| Item | Status | Who closes it |
|---|---|---|
| The retirement deadline in the bill | **Open, and worth 32% of the modelled cost to the trust at a constant participant count.** The draft requires retirement on the first of the month after the credit posts, which is the most expensive setting on the priced ladder. A 12- or 24-month deadline costs less on that ladder and changes nothing the veteran pays. **This is a modelled conditional result, not a demonstrated 32% reduction in programme expenditure or in the appropriation:** the ladder holds participation constant and no source produces a participant count per deadline setting. | Patron and policy lead |
