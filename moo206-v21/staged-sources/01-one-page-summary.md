> **Reprice military-service purchase off the service instead of the calendar — total normal cost on the member's first 36 months of state pay plus 6.00% statutory interest, no election window, state agency employees only — and appropriate $3.0 million as a hard cap on the Commonwealth's share for a four-year pilot beginning 1 July 2029.**

## The problem

- **The price depends on when the member was hired, not on the service bought.** Inside the first
  **24 months** of state employment military service costs approximate normal cost; one day later it
  costs actuarial-equivalent cost.
- **For a long-service member that is about three times as much** for the identical four years. On
  one modelled profile — Plan 1, age 55, 27 years, $91,700 salary, buying three years — it is
  **$116,622 instead of $47,705**.
- **The clock starts before the member has met a VRS counsellor.** A veteran who enlisted at 18,
  served a full term and joined the Commonwealth at 34 is already outside the window.
- **Of the 20 states where we verified the price basis, Virginia is the only one that withdraws its
  preferential price on a calendar measured from hire.**

## What the bill does

- **Replaces the calendar test with a price measured off the service.** The member pays the **total
  normal cost** of their plan on their **first 36 months** of creditable compensation, plus **6.00%
  statutory interest** — Virginia's own legal rate, § 6.2-301 — to the date of payment. The basis is
  Pennsylvania's, 71 Pa.C.S. § 5505(b).
- **Leaves the member paying more than VRS's own stated cost** of a year of service credit:
  **1.42× approximate normal cost**, and **43% of the current-law price**, on the unreduced subdivision A 1 cohort, reached through § 51.1-153 A, B 2 or B 3.
- **Caps the Commonwealth's share in the enactment clause** at **$3.0 million** of certified
  subsidy, self-executing: VRS certifies an available reservation before accepting an election and
  wait-lists beyond it, and a wait-listed applicant keeps every right current law gives them today.

## Cost

**The pilot costs the trust $1.3 million in present value — range $0.66M to $2.2M — or $1.7 million
with one-time systems work.** That rung assumes every member retires on the last day the bill allows,
so it is the cheap end: **+7.5%** if retirement dates spread evenly, **+16.3%** if all retire at the
earliest. **18.4 elections** over the four years, range **9.2 – 30.7** — an **upper bound**, because a
further eligibility screen in the live draft is in no model run — each certifying a mean **$106,253**
of subsidy, median $106,405 and 90th percentile $152,558, as the cap clause certifies it. On modelled
demand that commits **39% / 78% / 130%** of the $3.0 million at low, central and high take-up with
the contingency inside it — so **at the central estimate the appropriation funds every election the
bill admits, and at the top of the range it binds and rations.** Those are modelled demand against
the cap, not observed utilisation. Add up to **$375,000** of one-time VRS systems work in FY2029 —
the top of VRS's published range, and its direction of error is that it understates. **No benefit
cost falls before FY2031.**

## Two of the bill's own limits refuse members before the money does

- **The statute refuses an election that buys nothing** — a purchase that would not advance the
  member's unreduced eligibility date: **1.0 of 20.65** modelled members. The bill's older
  zero-month bar refuses none on top of that, because everyone it would catch is already caught.
- **A $170,000 per-member ceiling** refuses the most expensive **4.74%**, about **1.2 of 20.65**.
  *A refusal is not a trim:* the refused member's whole **$183,577** of subsidy goes against
  **$9,147** of excess, a factor of twenty. The bill still brackets `[$100,000]`; $170,000 is
  directed and not yet in the text.

## The strongest objection, and the answer

**"You are selling service credit below its actuarial value."** That is correct and the bill does
not deny it. What the bill does is make the gap **bounded, certified and appropriated** — and
current law already sells the same service below actuarial cost to anyone inside the 24-month
window. **We do not claim the bill pays for itself:** on central assumptions the earlier
retirement's payroll value does not cover the pension cost, and the pilot is built to measure that
exchange and report it.
