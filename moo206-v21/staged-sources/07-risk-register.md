> normal cost** of a year of VRS service credit and **43% of the current-law price**, so the veteran
> keeps most of the benefit and the Commonwealth is not selling service credit below the cost VRS
> itself states. On the authorised basis the pilot is **18.4 elections over four years, range 9.2 to
> 30.7** — measured on § 51.1-142.4 C as option (c) with the no-advance bar of clause (vii) in the
> unreduced subdivision A 1 cohort, reached through § 51.1-153 A, B 2 or B 3. **That count is an
> upper bound, not a measurement of the current text:** clause (viii) of
> "eligible member" is in the live draft and in no committed model run, and it is a further screen,
> so it can only refuse more. Each election certifies a mean **$106,253** of subsidy as
> § 51.1-142.4 D 2 certifies it — median $106,405, 90th percentile $152,558 — which is the quantity
> D 2 caps. **The trust's own net cost per election is a different and smaller quantity, $71,813 in
> present value on the authorised 12-month rung**, because D 2 certifies the reservation on the
> earliest qualifying retirement date and the retirement window does not move it. **Two of the
> bill's own limits turn members away before money does:** § 51.1-142.4 C refuses any election that
> would not advance the member's unreduced retirement eligibility date, and a **$170,000** per-member
> ceiling refuses the most expensive 4.74% of that cohort — and the bill still brackets `[$100,000]`,
> so the ceiling figure is directed but not yet in the text. On modelled demand, at the central
> estimate the $3,000,000 funds every election the bill admits, and at the top of the range the
> appropriation binds and rations. **The pilot's estimated net cost to the trust is $1.3 million in
> present value, range $0.66M to $2.2M — about $71,813 per participating member** — and with the
> one-time $375,000 implementation allowance the headline cost to the Commonwealth is **$1.7
> million**. **One caveat travels with that figure and is required:** the authorised rung prices
> every member retiring on the last permissible day, so it is the cheap end of the window and the
> exposure is one-sided dearer — **+7.5%** if retirement dates spread uniformly across the
> permissible dates and **+16.3%** if every member retires at the earliest. There is no local
> cost and no mandate on localities, and a denied member keeps every purchase right current law

## 1. The opposition's best brief, in its own words

Written by the reviewer as the opponent would write it. It is reproduced because a committee will
hear some version of it, and the package is stronger for having answered it in advance.

> *You are asking us to reverse a protection the retirement system itself sought in 2015, while
> renaming the resulting subsidy. Multiplying an old salary by a normal-cost rate and an interest
> factor does not purchase the pension liability generated when a member chooses, near retirement,
> to convert military service into earlier lifetime benefits. We accept that the formula has a
> policy rationale. We reject the assertion that changing its name changes its cost.*
>
> *The proposal also makes an unequal bargain. Veterans with liquid assets and prompt agency
> paperwork get scarce reservations; otherwise identical veterans wait. Before the Commonwealth
> promises a benefit, specify whose obligation survives, reserve its full estimated cost, and fund
> administration. Do not ask us to replace actuarial funding with historical salary records, a
> retirement deadline, and an assurance that another state has a statute.*

## 2. Verdict

**No fatal legal defect was found in a properly funded, narrowly drafted military-service pilot.**
Counts across three reviews — the original draft, the recommended design, and the drafted bill
text: **0 fatal**, and a list of serious items all carried below with their status. "Serious" means
repair before relying on the design or the fiscal headline — not that the General Assembly lacks
authority to enact it.

**The central premise of the first draft of the argument failed.** The claim that the new price
basis is "not a subsidy" does not survive an actuary's reading: a formula price below the cost of
the benefit it creates is still below the cost of the benefit it creates. **That claim has been
removed from every document in this package.** What replaced it is narrower and true — the price
has a principled basis, the gap is bounded by an appropriation, and the Commonwealth is asked to
fund it knowingly.

## 5. Risk by category

### Legal fit
Purchased military service can lawfully provide the eligibility bridge; this is not merely a
benefit-amount purchase. A statutory formula price is legally available in principle, and no
categorical federal prohibition on a subsidised military-service purchase or on proportional credit
was found. A genuine funded admission cap materially controls commitments; the absence of a
Virginia precedent for one is an implementation uncertainty, not evidence of invalidity. **Final
bill text still needs its own single-object and delegation review.**

### Actuarial
**The highest risk in the file is R-A** — the scoring treatment of the retirement condition. Beyond
it: the model values the basic pension only and therefore **understates** cost; plan tier is
inferred rather than observed; and the price basis has never been tested against a VRS quote for a
real member.

### Fiscal uncertainty
All three of the largest drivers are drafting or behavioural choices rather than measurements:
whether electing members actually retire on the deadline (85% of the answer), how long they are
given to complete payment (83%), and how long the retirement deadline is (55%). The interest rate,
which earlier drafts of this analysis treated as the dominant dial, is a sixth-order driver of the
level. Participant counts rest on judgment multipliers because 69% of the state veteran-status
field is unknown. **Nothing in this package is reliable beyond two significant figures.**

### Administration
VRS prices a discrete § 51.1 change at $104,100–$374,500 to implement. A subsidy reservation queue
is **not** something VRS is known to operate today; the package assumes it must be built and funded.
No implementation quote has been obtained — which is why the draft carries a separate enactment
clause requiring VRS to report on exactly that by 1 December 2027, whether or not the act is
reenacted. A short pilot that costs more to administer than it
delivers in benefit is a legitimate reason for a subcommittee to decline, and the honest answer is
to fund administration explicitly and keep the mechanism simple.

### Equity
Three distinct objections, all with force: *why only veterans* (the cliff is identical for eight
other purchase categories); *why only Plan 1 and Plan 2* (the Hybrid plan is 54% of active
members); and *why only members near retirement* (the mid-career veteran the bill is named for gets
nothing for twenty years). The package answers all three in document 06 and concedes where the
answer is a scoring choice rather than a principle.

### Stakeholder opposition
The decisive actor is VRS. A § 51.1-142.2 bill VRS wants passes unanimously; a bill VRS scores as
an assumption change dies. Local government opposition is removed entirely by the state-employee
scope. Employee associations are likely to ask for broadening rather than to oppose — and
broadening is what makes the bill unscoreable.

### Unintended incentives
Accelerating retirements in a workforce where service retirements are already running above average
is a genuine objection, and it uses the Commonwealth's own data. The contractor bar and the
reporting requirement exist for this reason. **There is no backfill control** — subsection J was
dropped on 11 October 2026 — so this objection is answered by reporting and by the contractor bar
and not by a provision that limits refilling. The package does not claim the measure is a retention
tool.

### Messaging vulnerabilities
Three claims would lose on contact and have been removed from every document: "it is not a
subsidy"; "it pays for itself"; and "Virginia is the only state" stated without the bound of the 20
states actually verified.

### Missing evidence
No official VRS quote for any member; no implementation estimate from VRS for the reservation
queue; no count of VRS members who are veterans with purchasable service; and no verified
enactment provenance for the South Carolina provision the fallback design is modelled on.

## 6. What survived review

- No structural necessity to reprice the eight other purchase categories.
- Purchased military service provides a real eligibility bridge.
- A formula price is legally available; no categorical § 415(n) bar was found.
- A genuine funded admission cap materially controls commitments.
- Removing the "already eligible" pathway removes an obvious windfall channel; settling at
  retirement improves on unsecured post-retirement debt.
- State-only scope genuinely avoids imposing costs on teachers or localities, provided the operative
  text and the employer-cost allocation preserve it.
- The price-grid arithmetic reproduces independently to within 0.0000036 of a percentage point, and
  the distinction between a 4% and a 6% statutory rate is material and correctly characterised.
