> **Reprice military-service purchase off the service instead of the calendar — total normal cost on the member's first 36 months of state pay plus 6.00% statutory interest, no election window, state agency employees only — and appropriate $3.0 million as a hard cap on the Commonwealth's share for a four-year pilot beginning 1 July 2029.** At 6.00% the member pays **1.42× the approximate
> normal cost** of a year of VRS service credit and **43% of the current-law price**, so the veteran
> keeps most of the benefit and the Commonwealth is not selling service credit below the cost VRS
> itself states. On the authorised basis the pilot is **18.4 elections over four years, range 9.2 to
> 30.7** — measured on § 51.1-142.4 C as option (c) with the no-advance bar of clause (vii) in the
> unreduced subdivision A 1 cohort, reached through § 51.1-153 A, B 2 or B 3. **That count is an
> upper bound, not a measurement of the current text:** clause (viii) of
> "eligible member" is in the live draft and in no committed model run, and it is a further screen,
> so it can only refuse more. Each election certifies a mean **$106,253** of subsidy as
> § 51.1-142.4 D 2 certifies it — median $106,405, 90th percentile $152,558 — which is the quantity
> D 2 caps. **The trust's own net cost per election is a different and smaller quantity, $71,813 in
> present value on the authorised 12-month rung**, because D 2 certifies the reservation on the
> earliest qualifying retirement date and the retirement window does not move it. **Two of the
> bill's own limits turn members away before money does:** § 51.1-142.4 C refuses any election that
> would not advance the member's unreduced retirement eligibility date, and a **$170,000** per-member
> ceiling refuses the most expensive 4.74% of that cohort — and the bill still brackets `[$100,000]`,
> so the ceiling figure is directed but not yet in the text. On modelled demand, at the central
> estimate the $3,000,000 funds every election the bill admits, and at the top of the range the
> appropriation binds and rations. **The pilot's estimated net cost to the trust is $1.3 million in
> present value, range $0.66M to $2.2M — about $71,813 per participating member** — and with the
> one-time $375,000 implementation allowance the headline cost to the Commonwealth is **$1.7
> million**. **One caveat travels with that figure and is required:** the authorised rung prices
> every member retiring on the last permissible day, so it is the cheap end of the window and the
> exposure is one-sided dearer — **+7.5%** if retirement dates spread uniformly across the
> permissible dates and **+16.3%** if every member retires at the earliest. There is no local
> cost and no mandate on localities, and a denied member keeps every purchase right current law
> already gives them.

## 1. The political read, in one paragraph

This bill does not need a broad coalition. It needs a subcommittee chair with no reason to say no.
The decisive actor is **VRS**: a § 51.1-142.2 bill the system wants passes unanimously, and a bill
the system scores as a plan-wide assumption change dies. Veteran framing wins the floor and the
Governor; it has never won a money committee. In 2023 the strongest possible veteran framing — four
free years of military service for teachers — was tabled 8-0 in House Appropriations subcommittee.
**Use the framing to win the room you can win, and arithmetic to win the room that decides.**

## 2. The gate that has already closed

The Joint Leadership Council of Veterans Service Organizations set its 2027 package on **10 June
2026** and ranked it on **16 September 2026**. The eight published 2027 initiatives contain no VRS
or military-service-purchase measure. **There is no path to a numbered JLC 2027 initiative for this
bill.**

That changes the sequencing, not the policy:

- **2027** — a patron's bill through a money-committee member. Individual veterans organisations
  may testify in their own name without a JLC initiative number.
- **2028** — the JLC cycle proper: proposals to member organisations over winter 2026–27,
  Legislative Committee screening around April 2027, full Council vote around June 2027.
  Initiative 8 in the current package is a retirement-income tax exemption, so a retirement subject
  is not out of character for the Council.

## 3. The decision path

Retirement bills in Virginia go **straight to the money committees**. There is no policy committee
for Title 51.1 in either chamber.

| Step | Venue | What that venue has done with comparable bills |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Drafting with the Division of Legislative Services | 2027 prefiling opened 20 July 2026; prefile draft deadline 13 January 2027 |
| 2 | **VRS impact statement under § 30-19.1:7** | Filed within about two weeks of prefiling in every 2026 case observed |
| 3a | **House Appropriations → Compensation and Retirement subcommittee** | Cost-neutral § 51.1-142.2 bill reported 8-0. Cost-bearing bills: continued |
| 3b | **Senate Finance and Appropriations**, reported from full committee | Continued four VRS benefit bills in the last session, each unanimously |
| 4 | Appropriation Act amendments to Item 483 (VRS administration) and Item 469 (contribution rates) | Both were named in VRS's 2026 statements on comparable bills |
| 5 | Floor, then the Governor | The 2026 purchase expansion that a previous Governor vetoed was signed |

**The clock.** 2027 is a 30-day short session convening 13 January. In the 2026 long session, Senate
Finance disposed of a comparable bill by 3 February; the short-session equivalent is roughly the
**last week of January 2027**. There is no room for a bill that needs explaining. **Complexity is
the enemy** — the original draft's two pathways, 12-month purchase window, six-month retirement
condition and clawback were four moving parts more than a subcommittee absorbs on a voice vote.
The recommended design has one price, one condition and one cap for that reason.

**The competition.** Several VRS benefit bills and two Line of Duty Act bills are already sitting in
the money committees, each continued at least once. Our bill joins a queue that the committees are
inclined to dispose of as a batch. The way out of the batch is to be visibly different in kind: a
capped appropriation that repairs a defect, not another occupational expansion.

## 5. Sequencing

**Phase 0 — before anyone outside the team sees anything (now to 31 October 2026).**

1. Decide the rate and cap pair, and confirm Pathway A only. *(Decision memo §8, item 1.)*
2. Get an answer, or at least a question on the record, on whether the retirement condition triggers
   a plan-wide assumption change. **Nothing else matters as much.**
3. **Set the retirement deadline.** It is the largest cost control in the drafter's hands — **32% on
   the priced ladder** between the drafted version and a 24-month deadline — and it is the one lever
   that changes the VRS scoring conversation without changing what the veteran pays. **Say the 32%
   precisely:** it is a modelled reduction in present-value cost to the trust with the participant
   count held constant, not a demonstrated cut in programme expenditure or in the appropriation, and
   a longer deadline may change who elects.

**Phase 1 — VRS first, not veterans first (November to December 2026).**

4. The **12 November 2026** Benefits and Actuarial Committee meeting is the last before session.
   VRS receives written comment and does not take public comment at meetings. **Recommendation:
   nothing goes to VRS until the retirement-condition question in Phase 0 has an answer**, because
   VRS's first read becomes its § 30-19.1:7 statement.
5. The message to VRS is: *bounded, funded, administratively simple, and on your timeline.* Ask for
   technical review, not endorsement. Offer to fund implementation and to accept a 1 July 2028
   effective date.

**Phase 2 — patrons (November 2026 to early January 2027).**

6. Companion bills, prefiled the same week. Drafts with the Division of Legislative Services by
   **30 November 2026**; realistically mid-December at the latest for a short session.
7. The message to a patron leads with what the bill does **not** do: no teachers, no localities, no
   Hybrid plan, no uncapped liability, no savings claim.

**Phase 3 — the coalition (December 2026 to January 2027).**

8. The **17 December 2026** JLC meeting cannot adopt a 2027 initiative, but it is where individual
   organisations learn the measure exists and decide whether to testify in their own name.
9. Employee associations are more useful for their bill tracker and their lobbyists in January than
   for a legislative day that falls after the short session's decision point.

**Phase 4 — the 2028 cycle, in parallel and regardless of the 2027 outcome (January to July 2027).**

10. Prepare a position paper in the Council's own template and find a member organisation willing to
    present it. Target the Legislative Committee screening around April 2027 and the full Council
    vote around June 2027.
11. Tie the initiative to the Department of Veterans Services strategic plan, which is the stated
    screening criterion.

**Phase 5 — the fallback that needs no score (available throughout).**

12. If the scoring question comes back badly, pivot to a **study or a VRS reporting requirement**
    rather than losing a bill on an unrecorded voice vote. A study costs almost nothing to score
    and creates an official record for 2028. Losing in subcommittee leaves no record at all.

| **VRS as a non-objecting agency** | Decisive | Accept a 2028 effective date, fund implementation, accept a hard cap, and restructure anything that triggers an assumption change | **Yes — the highest-value trade available, and it should be made before any other** |
| **Veterans service organisations** | Testimony, member letters, credibility with the Military and Veterans Caucus, and the route to a 2028 initiative | Essentially free in 2027; in 2028, the Council's template and competition for one of five slots | Yes |
| **Secretary of Veterans and Defense Affairs** | Access to the Governor's package; a signal to the money committees | Reframe as workforce and transition policy rather than as a pension benefit | Yes — the reframe is free and helps everywhere |
| **Local governments** | Removes the unfunded-mandate objection entirely | Exclude political subdivision employees | **Yes — already taken** |
| **State employee associations** | Lobbying presence, a bill tracker, retiree mobilisation | They will want the measure broadened, or paired with a cost-of-living or health-credit ask. Either enlarges the score. | Yes, but only unpaired. **Do not accept a paired COLA amendment.** |
| **Hybrid plan members and their advocates** | Removes the largest equity objection | Including the Hybrid plan opens the eligible population to the majority of active members and destroys the capped-pilot argument | **No. Answer this one with honesty, not with scope.** |

| **Teachers** | Scale | Imports the $2.7 billion scoring profile | **No** |

## 7. Message matrix

### To veterans service organisations
1. **Same service, several times the price.** A veteran hired at 24 buys four years at approximate
   normal cost. A veteran hired at 34, after a full enlistment, pays about three times as much for
   identical service.
2. **Nobody told them.** The 24-month clock runs from first hire, before most mid-career hires have
   had a benefits conversation. This is a notice failure the Commonwealth created.
3. **It is bounded and honest.** Four years, a hard cap, a sunset, annual reporting. We are not
   claiming it pays for itself, and we are not asking for teachers or the Hybrid plan.

### To fiscal conservatives
1. **Closed population, binding cap, automatic sunset.** Maximum exposure is knowable in advance,
   which is more than can be said for the occupational expansions the committees keep continuing.
2. **The member pays — and pays more than VRS's own stated cost of a year of service credit.** This
   is a price change on a voluntary purchase, not a grant.
3. **Subsidies already exist in VRS pricing, and VRS's own actuary calls them normal.** The June
   2026 factor study documents embedded early-retirement subsidies and confirms Virginia's factors
   are within common practice. The question is not whether Virginia subsidises; it is whether this
   subsidy is capped and disclosed. Ours is both.

### To the workforce and HR audience
1. **Veterans are 6% of the classified workforce and most of the field is unknown.** The pilot's
   reporting requirement would be the Commonwealth's first systematic data on this.
2. **It is a recruiting signal for mid-career hires**, not only a retirement benefit. Average
   new-hire age is 39.1; a veteran weighing a state job against a federal or contractor job is
   comparing total compensation.
3. **Address the hard version head-on.** DHRM's own data says service retirements are above average
   and the acute problem is first-five-year attrition. So do not sell this as retention of the
   near-retirement cohort. Sell it as a recruitment signal and as an orderly, capped, reported way
   to handle a retirement wave that is coming anyway — with an evaluation designed to test whether
   the payroll offset is real.

### To VRS
1. **Funded, not unfunded.** We accept the fiduciary standard — enhance benefits only if they can be
   funded up front — and we are asking the money committees for the appropriation, including
   implementation.
2. **Administratively simple and on your timeline.** One price, one condition, one cap, one report.
   We will take 1 July 2028 and we want help cutting any feature that drives implementation cost.
3. **No retirement-timing assumption change.** We have read the HB 1306 statement. Tell us what
   design avoids an assumption change and we will draft to it.

## 8. What not to do

- **Do not lead with the veteran framing in a money committee.** It has a documented 8-0 losing
  record there.
- **Do not let the bill be amended to add teachers, localities or the Hybrid plan** without the cap
  being re-sized in the same motion. Each of those changes the score by an order of magnitude.
- **Do not accept a lower interest rate without reopening the cap clause in the same motion.** At
  4.00% the member pays less than the original draft asked and the Commonwealth pays more.
- **Do not let anyone describe the interest rate as the cost control.** It is not. If a committee
  wants the cost down, the answer is a longer retirement deadline, which costs the veteran nothing.
- **Do not claim savings.** The auditor record is unanimous and it will be read back.
- **Do not approach VRS before the retirement-condition question has an internal answer.** Their
  first read becomes their statement.
