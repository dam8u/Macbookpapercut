## 1. §0.3 — keep F-1's deletion. Four reasons, in the order that decides it

**The question.** Edit 1's saving sentence carried *"required to act upon a request **or an application**"*.
Pass 18's F-1 says delete the three words. Keeping them leaves the same six words — *"acts upon the
request"* — with a **fixed** meaning in the first half of M 2's retention sentence and an **open** one in
the second half **of the same sentence**. The price of deleting them is that M 2's duty to act upon the
application is fixed at **first act + 15**: day 22 on the ordinary facts, against a reconsideration
decided day 85.

**1. The defect is a drafting defect, not a policy choice.** The words are the drafter's own, added in a
saving clause, not text the board decided. One sentence cannot carry two meanings of one phrase. That is
the kind of thing a drafting pass exists to find, and when it finds one the answer is to fix it.

**2. The alternative reading is worse for the member on the exact metric Edit 3 exists to fix.** Undeleted,
the duty falls due on the open reading at **day 100** against a retention ending **day 37** — Edit 3 would
**enlarge** the self-defeat gap it was adopted to cure from 15 days to **63**. Keeping the clause does not
preserve a member-favourable reading; it preserves an ambiguity whose member-favourable branch defeats the
board's own edit.

**3. The reviewer's condition is explicit and I am not going to overrule it on a point of drafting.**
Pass 18: *"I would not apply Edit 3 without this deletion."* Edit 3 is a board decision. The choice is
between Edit 3 working and Edit 3 half-working.

**4. The alternative on the table costs a board decision.** Rejecting meant **Edit 3 comes out of the
patch** and card (k-ii) is abandoned, leaving M 2's live 15-day self-defeat gap in place. That is a worse
outcome than day 22 and it answers a question the board already answered.

**What I accept with it, and disclose.** Day 22 is a real sequencing defect: the Board decides the
**application** before it decides the **reconsideration** whose outcome the application's retained filing
date exists to preserve. Pass 18's card B, confirmed, structural, and not cured by a longer retention.
**It is one site in enacted §1** — D 3's parallel duty is keyed to the confirmation and does not move by a
day. It goes into the memo's risk section, §5.

**There was no third option, and I checked the obvious candidate.** Re-keying M 2's application duty to the
decision — the move Edit 2 makes for the notice — puts the duty at day 100 while Edit 1's deeming keeps
Edit 3's retention first-act-keyed at day 37, which re-opens the gap at 63 days. Inside Edit 1's deeming
the retention is first-act-keyed, so the application duty must be first-act-keyed too for the sentence's
two halves to work together. **Day 22 is the price of a coherent sentence.**

## 3. The six cards, disposed

### 3.1 Card 1 — the no-decision residual. **Take [MOO-42](/MOO/issues/MOO-42) §7 item 124. Gate.**

**Accepted, and the drafter's ranking is right: this is the one addition to take.** Edit 2 keys the
backstop's only deadline to a *decision*, and no provision of § 51.1-142.4 requires the Board ever to give
one. Store revision 59 made that worse, not better: a reconsideration the Board never completes now
produces **no case decision at all**, by two express exclusions, and no judicial review.

**Item 124's deemed denial on the 91st day is the cure, for three reasons.** It gives Edit 2's 15 days a
guaranteed start without adding a duty to M 2's 90-day sentence; it cannot pull the notice forward of an
actual decision, because it only arises where the Board has **not** completed the reconsideration, so
cards (g) and (k) stay closed; and it is **already drafted and already recommended** for an independent
reason. Pass 18's card C does the same work with a larger footprint. **Card C is the fallback and fires
only if item 124 is declined.**

**This is a gate on application, not a card to carry forward.** Edit 2 is a board decision and this
residual is a consequence of it; applying Edit 2 while the residual is open puts the start date of the
member's only deadline in the Board's gift.

### 8.1 The change, and what it buys

Edit 3's READ text becomes *"retains its filing date for the purposes of subdivisions D 4 and D 5 until
**120 days** after the Board acts upon the request."* **One character more than revision 5's Edit 3 —
+15, not +14 — and the patch becomes +698.** The drafter re-verifies both figures; neither is taken from
me.

**On the ordinary facts the retention now ends day 127 against a reconsideration decided day 85.** It
covers the decision with **42 days to spare**, where 30 days expired **48 days short**. The purpose of
the retention is delivered, and [MOO-163](/MOO/issues/MOO-163) prices that at **$0 at the central
estimate**, because D 2's $3,000,000 cap does not bind at 67.4% utilisation.

### 8.2 What it breaks, and this is mine to disclose

**At 30 days, Edit 3's retention was word-identical to M 2's tolling endpoint.** Revision 5 §1.3 records
that *"30 days after the Board acts upon the request"* then appeared twice in M 2 and nowhere else in the
bill, and that pass 18 tested the identity and endorsed it: *"under Forst the identity is what secures the
same meaning for both."*

**At 120 days the identity is gone.** The retention outlives the tolling by 90 days, and there is a new
**day 37 to day 127 window** in which the application keeps its D 4 / D 5 filing date while the clock is
no longer tolled.

**My recommendation's *"uses a figure already in the subsection"* pointed at the cap clause's 120-day
aggregate, not at the 30-day endpoint, and I did not put the broken identity in front of the board.** It
is disclosed here and it is the first item in the drafter's note at [MOO-176](/MOO/issues/MOO-176) §2,
which asks for the divergence to be stated as deliberate, for the day 37 to day 127 window to be priced,
and for what carries Edit 3 now that the *Forst* identity does not. **It does not reopen the figure.** If
the divergence proves to be a defect rather than a choice, it returns to the board **as a card with
options**, not as a drafter's or my own re-decision.

### 8.3 Card 4 — F-6's cap is now a paired item, and still declined
