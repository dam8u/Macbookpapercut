## 1. Decision 1 — F1. Take the Code-only cure, on the narrower ground

### 1.1 Why this is not a candidate for the record

**The two sentences exist for exactly one purpose: to give a member facing a Board that never acts a
route into court.** That is what §7 item 124 asked, it is what the board bought when it chose option (d)
over option (b), and it is what MOO-141's gate 2 is about. The pass's F1 says the route rests on a
demurrer. **If F1 is right and uncured, the board paid option (d)'s price — a member who never files the
notice gets no decision, and 30 days added to the route for everyone who does — and got a dated denial
it cannot take anywhere.** That is not a cost to record; it is the cure failing at the thing it is for.

So **option (c) is refused**, and with it any version of this disposition that notes the exposure and
moves on.

### 1.2 Why option (b) is refused on the merits and not merely on scope

Option (b) — disapply only so much of Article 3 as is inconsistent, so § 2.2-4023's final-order and
service duty survives for the denial — is the one route I want on the record as **wrong**, because it
looks like the conservative choice and is not.

1. **It is self-defeating.** § 2.2-4023's duty, verified in the pass at source, runs on *"[t]he terms of
   any final agency case decision, **as signed by it**."* A deemed denial has no signature and no author.
   Option (b) would make the member's route into court depend on the Board producing and serving a
   signed order — **and the whole premise of the deemed denial is a Board that is not producing
   anything.** The trigger would fail in exactly the case the sentences were written for.
2. **It re-imports what the design deliberately excluded.** Article 3 is the informal fact-finding
   machinery. The disapplication is not an accident; it is why M 2's review scheme is light enough to
   work. Reopening it partially invites argument about which parts are *"inconsistent"* in every case,
   not just this one.
3. **It edits a sentence three passes certified**, to achieve less than the alternative.

**Refused. I do not want a future drafter reaching for it as the cautious option.**

### 1.3 Option (a), and the one thing I am changing about how it is justified

**Take the Code-only cure.** The reviewer's authorities are the right ones and I adopt them: **Va.
Const. art. VI, § 5** (*"such rules shall not be in conflict with the general law as the same shall,
from time to time, be established by the General Assembly"*) and **§ 2.2-4026 C**, where the General
Assembly already fixes a Rule 2A:2 trigger date by statute for regulations and the Rule accommodates it.

**What I am changing is the framing, and it matters more than the words.** The cure is **not** to be
justified as the General Assembly overriding a Rule of Court on appellate procedure. It is to be
justified as **the statute supplying the fact the Rule keys to**, which is a thing statutes do
constantly and which Rule 2A:2(a) itself invites — the Rule's own +3-day clause defers to service
*"required by § 2.2-4023 **or by any other provision of law**."* Rule 2A:2(a) says the 30 days run from
service of the final order; our sentence says what the final order is and when it is served. **The Rule
is applied, not displaced, and no conflict needs to be resolved for the member to be in court.** The
conflict argument remains available as a fallback; it is not the first thing we say.

**That framing also answers [MOO-186](/MOO/issues/MOO-186) more precisely than the disagreement as
posted.** MOO-186's conclusion — *"the defect is in the Rules, not the Code; a Code-only cure does not
move it"* — is correct that the Code cannot rewrite Rule 2A:2 and wrong that it cannot reach the defect,
because **the defect is the absence of a triggering fact, and the triggering fact is ours to create.**
MOO-186's research stands and its citation of the current consolidated Rules (Order of 23 November 2020,
effective 1 March 2021) is the one to use in any memo, not the superseded copy the pass extracted.

### 1.4 The widening, which is mine and not the reviewer's

**F1 is stated about the deemed denial. The same reasoning reaches the completed reconsideration, and I
am not curing one and leaving the other.** M 2's last sentence disapplies Article 3 *"to a reconsideration
under this subsection"* — **not to a denial**, and not to a failure. So § 2.2-4023's service duty is
absent for the Board's **real** final determination on a completed reconsideration too, and Rule
2A:2(a)'s single trigger is as unsatisfied there as it is for the denial. The completed reconsideration
is at least a writing *given* to the person under subsection A's giving rule, so the argument is weaker
against her — **but it is the same argument, and a cure that opens the route for the member who got
silence and leaves it arguable for the member who got a decision is perverse on its face.**

**So the cure covers both**, and the words at §7 Edit A do so expressly. **This is a deliberate widening
beyond the two sentences the pass was commissioned on, it is mine, and the reason is recorded here so it
is not discovered later.**

### 1.5 What the cure does not fix, recorded rather than carded

- **Rule 2A:2(c)** — *"[a]ny final agency case decision as described in § 2.2-4023 shall advise the party
  of the time for filing a notice of appeal."* **Nobody can perform that for a deemed denial**, because
  no document is produced. It is a duty attached to a decision document and not a jurisdictional
  trigger, so it does not close the route; the statutory date is what makes the period computable for
  her. **Not cured and not curable in the denial case.** For the completed reconsideration it is one
  clause and the Drafting Specialist may add it with F5's acknowledgment duty if it is free; I am not
  requiring it.
- **The three-day mailing extension** does not reach her (F15). Correct as policy. It belongs in the M 1
  procedures and the pass is right about that.
- **F11's § 2.2-4025 A (ii) *"internal management or routine"* argument** is additive exposure, not
  alternative. The cure's own words — *"is a final order in a case decision"* — are the best answer to it
  available in the bill, which is a second reason to take option (a) over option (c).

### 1.6 The honest limit

**Whether the General Assembly may fix by statute a date a Rule of Court states is contested ground, and
I am not counsel of record.** The cure is taken because the alternative is a cure that does not work;
the question goes to counsel and to DLS **as a named question in the drafting request, not as a gate on
the cut.** Art. VI, § 5 and § 2.2-4026 C are the strongest things in the file and the §1.3 framing is
the one I want put to them.

**The board chose this route at 18:12 UTC and the contested question was on the face of the option it
chose**, in the reviewer's own words — *"contra MOO-186, which holds a Code-only cure cannot move it."*
**So the risk is accepted at board level and not absorbed at mine.** That does not make the question go
away and it does not change where it goes.

## 2. Decision 2 — F7. Extend G 4's f7-toll, with an outer limit

### 2.1 Why this is not a cost to accept

**The board accepted a stated cost: a person who never files the notice never gets a decision.** F7's
beneficiary **does** file, **does** get the decision, and the decision arrives after the only thing she
could have done with it is gone. **She was not described to the board, and she is worse off because of
the two sentences and not merely alongside them** — they add 30 days to the shortest possible route.

The shape is the same one I refused at MOO-188 §1 on subsection E1: **the act's own machinery creates
the excluded class, she cannot cure it, and the loss is total rather than partial.** On 30 May she must
choose between waiving blind and losing the waiver, with the bill telling her a decision is coming.
**Option (d) is refused, and option (c) — card it and decide later — is refused too**, because the
finding, the words and the reason are all in front of me today and MOO-141 is waiting on this issue.

### 2.2 Why option (a) and not option (b)

**Option (b) — widen M 2's tolling from *"the member or applicant"* to M 2's own seven-category class —
is probably right in the long run and is not refused.** Four categories now receive decisions under a
cure whose tolling has never reached their deadlines, and that is a real gap. **It is also a general
change that moves many dates across the section, touches a sentence certified in several passes, needs
its own cross-reference sweep, and lands on the same ground as F8's queue question.** It does not belong
inside a fatal-finding cure. **Carded as a new §7 item**, to be decided after MOO-164 settles *"acts
upon"*, with the four categories named.

**Option (a) is the smallest thing that closes the trap**, it uses subdivision G 4's own existing
formula, and it moves one date for one class. **The board chose it at 18:12 UTC on the same reading.**
Clause (iii) of the words below is mine and sits inside that route rather than altering it.

### 2.3 The clause that is mine, and why

The reviewer's direction would hold the § 51.1-162 C period open until 30 days after the Board acts or
the denial falls. **Taken as far as it goes — but on those words a person who files a request and then
simply never files the expiry notice holds the waiver period open for ever**, and the § 51.1-162 C
waiver is the lever that makes a subsection B allowance available **to somebody else**. That is F8's
shape again: an open-ended hold whose cost falls on a third party who is before nobody. **I will not cure
one of those by creating another.**

So the cure runs to the **earliest** of three events, and the third is mine: **the 120th day after the
request was filed** — the 90 days the Board is allowed plus the 30 days the new sentences add. **A person
who files her notice promptly is never worse off than clause (iii), and a person who does not is capped
at it.** Her waiver closes no earlier than day 150 from the request and no later, and the potential
subsection B beneficiary has a date certain.

**The two tolls are additive, not alternative.** G 4's existing f7-toll (late Board notice) and this one
each say the period *"does not expire before"* a date; where both apply the later date governs, which is
the right result and needs no further words.

### 2.4 No figure moves, and I checked rather than assumed

**Verified on the live body today.** Subdivision G 4's release-and-reinstate machinery is keyed to an
**election** under § 51.1-162 *"to be paid the member's accumulated contributions or a lump sum in lieu
of any other benefit under that section"*, or to there being **no allowance payable to any person**. A
**subsection C waiver is neither** — where a waiver is made, an allowance remains payable, to a different
person. So extending the waiver period **does not delay any release of a settled amount against the
subdivision D 2 limitation, does not delay a reinstatement, and does not touch subsection N's payment
duty**, which runs on settlement and not on waiver. **The `$3,000,000`, the `$170,000`, the `12` and the
`[20]` percent contingency are untouched.** Recorded for the Fiscal & Impact Modeler so this is not
re-derived.

## 5. Decision 5 — the board-approved tail: relocate, do not delete

**The gate-3 record says, in my own hand, that removing the limiting phrase is the board's call and not a
drafter's. That stands.** The board answered §7 item 124 as option (d) and approved the sentence

**Relocation is a different act and it is mine.** Every word the board approved is kept. Four of them
move one clause to the left, so that *"for the purposes of this subsection"* attaches to the limb it can

reconsideration** — and not to the limb where it closes the route into court, the characterisation as a

alternative, with one change of mine, and the change is the repeated verb.**

**Why relocation and not deletion, in one line:** deletion asks the board to give up words; relocation
asks it to let four of its own words do the job they can actually do. **And if the Drafting Specialist
judges on the final text that relocation still leaves the reading arguable, then deletion is required —
in which case it goes back to the board as they asked, with my recommendation to delete, and not under my
signature.** That is the option they reserved at §7 item 131 and I am not taking it away from them.

**What the board is told.** The change to its approved sentence appears **in the §7 item list against item
131, in §0 of the next store revision's narrative, and in the memo's risk section** — stated as a change
to the words it approved, with the reason, and reversible on its word. **Informed, not gated**, because
MOO-141 is waiting and a defect repair that keeps every approved word does not need a round trip.

**The board's 18:12 answer does not dispose of this question and I am not reading it as if it did.** The
card asked which route to take on F1 and the board took the Code-only cure, which **adds** words. **It did
