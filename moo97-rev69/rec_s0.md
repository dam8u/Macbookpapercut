#### 1. f1-b — MOO-189's fatal finding, cured in subdivision G 4's carve-out sentence

The defect was in the **pre-existing** carve-out and not in any word store revision 62 wrote: a disability retirement is not a *"qualifying retirement"*, so for a member who died with a disability application pending and whose effective date the Board set, after her death, to a date before it, the carve-out switched clause (i) off and the death branch never reached her. On the reviewer's facts the widower took § 51.1-163's refund of about **$92,600** instead of a § 51.1-162 B allowance worth about **$260,000**, decided by an administrative date chosen after his wife was dead.

**The Director's words are enacted word for word, and two conforming limbs are added. Both are on the record here rather than silent, and either can be reversed in one substitution.**

1. **"that is not a qualifying retirement"** — the approved words were confined only by *"for the purposes of this subdivision."* Read on the live text, they would have reached **a member who retired in a qualifying retirement** and whose effective date the Board determined after her death, and so would have pulled a retiree out of § 51.1-163 and into the death branch. **That is the class [MOO-161](/MOO/issues/MOO-161)'s F1 rated fatal and the class the Director's standing direction of 12 October exists to protect.** The exception is now expressly about the kind of retirement the sentence it qualifies is about.
2. **The second limb** — *"and for those purposes the member shall not be treated as having retired before the date of death in such a retirement."* The approved words define the **noun**, *"a retirement before the date of death."* The death branch's own condition is the **verb**: *"where clause (i) of this subdivision applies and **the member has not retired** before the date of death."* Without the limb the cure removes the carve-out and leaves the branch's second condition unsatisfied, so **the approved answer would not have reached the class it was approved to reach.** The limb uses the chapter's own formulation: subsection E and subdivision G 5 already say *"shall not be treated as having reached his unreduced retirement eligibility date."*

**One residual of (b), named and not drafted.** A disability retiree **already in payment** whose effective date the Board *re-determines* after her death is, on the words, a retirement the effective date of which the Board determines after the death. The natural reading is that an effective date is determined once, at approval, and a later correction is not that determination — but it is arguable. **f1-a's limb (*"and a retirement allowance has become payable to the member under that retirement"*) would close it, and f1-a was not selected**, so taking that limb now would be choosing between two forms the Director chose between. It is the first thing the next counsel pass is told to attack.

#### 2. f4-a — the greater-of is confined to coincident payees

The sentence directed the Board to pay *"whichever is greater"* of two benefits that § 51.1-165 C 2 and § 51.1-162 may make payable **to different people**, while opening with a savings clause that said the C 2 benefit was not reduced. **The card's option (a) is described and not drafted, so these words are mine and the policy is the Director's.** The greater-of now operates only where *"the person or persons to whom that subdivision requires a benefit to be paid are the same as the person or persons to whom the benefit determined under the preceding sentence would be payable"*, and where they are not the same the Board **pays the C 2 benefit and does not pay the benefit determined under the preceding sentence**.

- **The non-coincident branch is joined to the same sentence by a semicolon and is deliberately not a new sentence.** That sentence contains **four** references to *"the preceding sentence"*, all of which mean the § 51.1-162 determination sentence. A new sentence would have made *"the preceding sentence"* inside it mean the greater-of sentence. **Any further amendment of this branch must stay inside that sentence for the same reason**, and that is recorded as a standing direction.
- **Two referents are made express rather than pronominal:** *"the benefit **it** provides"* becomes *"the benefit **that subdivision** provides"*, and the payee tail becomes *"shall pay it to **that person or those persons**"*, which is the set the new condition has just identified.
- **What it costs, stated plainly because it decides who is paid.** In the non-coincident case the person to whom a § 51.1-162 benefit would have been payable **gets nothing from the act**; the § 51.1-165 C 2 payee keeps what Chapter 1 gives him. The card put that consequence in terms — *"the brother keeps his $94,000 and the husband gets nothing from the act"* — and it is the Director's choice among three forms, not a drafting choice. **No person is worse off than under present law**, because without G 4's sentence § 51.1-165 C 2 governs that death of its own force; what is removed is a double recovery the live words arguably created.
