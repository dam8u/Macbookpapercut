# MOO-200 — Counsel pass on store revision 67's nineteen substitutions

**Internal risk analysis, not legal advice. Nothing in this pass clears the draft. I am not counsel of record and no part of this is an opinion the Lab or the Commonwealth may rely on.**

Read at source on 12 October 2026, all at `law.lis.virginia.gov`: §§ 51.1-124.3, 51.1-153, 51.1-162, 51.1-163, 51.1-165, 51.1-165.01, and Article IV, Section 12 of the Constitution of Virginia. Bill text read from the live `bill-text-s8` document, revision id `db67d2bb-9cb2-4476-99e1-8281e689d066`, **store revision 68** (the record-only revision above 67; the enacted §1 slice is unchanged). All nineteen substitutions verified present exactly once in the §1 slice, and A4e once in §2, by count.

**Count: 0 fatal, 5 serious, 7 moderate, 6 minor, 12 null results.**

**Most serious: F1.** B1's narrowing left § 51.1-165.01 C's lump-sum reduction in force, and B2 now compels the Board to certify in writing that no § 51.1-165.01 option limits the death benefit. On the better reading of § 51.1-162 B that statement is wrong, and it goes to every participant.

**You are wrong about both of the things you asked to be wrong about.** B2's premises hold as you stated them, but the word *limits* carries more than *extinguishes* does, and § 51.1-165.01 C is where it breaks (F1). And f7-a can bar the Commonwealth's payment indefinitely — the election it waits on has no deadline anywhere in the Code (F2). The fiscal test in §4 says how often that second one actually fires: about once every seven pilots.

## 1. The strongest case against, in the opposition's voice

> This is a $1.4 million pilot that now reaches into the Commonwealth's death-benefit law. Four years ago the pitch was a discount on buying military service. Today the bill amends § 51.1-162 — the section that pays the widow of a state trooper killed on duty — and it does so to patch a hole the pilot itself dug. The patch is one clause, inserted mid-sentence, cross-referencing a section that this same bill causes to expire on 1 July 2033, after which § 51.1-162 will tell every survivor in the Commonwealth to go read a statute that no longer exists. The sponsors will say that is a technical matter for the Code Commission. It is not technical to the person reading the statute.
>
> And look at what the bill asks the Retirement System to put in writing before it will take a member's money. Four hundred words of statutory construction, authored by this committee, that the Board must certify as law to a 58-year-old corrections officer deciding whether to spend $30,000. Some of it is contestable. At least one clause of it is, on the better reading, false — the bill tells her that no partial lump-sum election limits her survivor's benefit, while § 51.1-165.01 says in terms that any optional payment of her allowance is based on the reduced allowance, and § 51.1-162 expressly disapplies only one modifier and not that one. When she dies before she retires and her husband's monthly allowance arrives $667 light with no lump sum ever paid, the Commonwealth's defence will be that the General Assembly told the Board to say it. That is the opposite of the protection this subdivision was written to give. Strike the pilot, or at minimum strike everything in it that purports to tell a member what the death-benefit sections mean, and let the Board administer the Code as the Code reads.

## 2. Findings

### F1 — serious — compelled misstatement of law / narrowed displacement

**Exact words at fault.** B2: *"that no option elected under § 51.1-165.01 … limits that benefit"*. B1: *"Notwithstanding so much of subdivision C 1 of § 51.1-165 and of subsection F of § 51.1-165.01 as requires benefits to be paid in accordance with § 51.1-163"*.

**Authority.** § 51.1-165.01 C: *"The monthly amount of the service retirement allowance otherwise payable to such member making the election shall be reduced on an actuarially equivalent basis to reflect the payment of such lump-sum distribution. **Any optional payment of the retirement allowance, pursuant to § 51.1-165, shall also be based upon such reduced retirement allowance.**"* § 51.1-165.01 D: the lump sum *"shall be paid at the same time that the first monthly annuity payment is paid to such member."* § 51.1-165.01 F, first sentence: the election is void only *"if the member dies prior to the Board receiving written notification of the member's effective date of retirement."* § 51.1-162 B: *"The retirement allowance shall equal the decreased retirement allowance that would have been payable under the joint and survivor option…"* and *"When determining the allowance that would have been payable to the member had the member retired on the date of his death, the provisions of subdivision A 4 of § 51.1-155 shall not apply."*

**What is wrong.** B1 deliberately narrowed the notwithstanding to the § 51.1-163 **routing** limb of § 51.1-165.01 F. It therefore leaves § 51.1-165.01 **C** — the reduction limb — in full force. § 51.1-162 B's measure is a hypothetical joint-and-survivor option computation, and § 51.1-165.01 C says any optional payment pursuant to § 51.1-165 is based upon the reduced allowance. § 51.1-162 B then names exactly one modifier it disapplies (§ 51.1-155 A 4) and names no other — the ordinary inference is that other modifiers apply. So a partial lump-sum election does, on the better reading, reduce the § 51.1-162 B death allowance. B2 compels the Board to certify the contrary, without qualification, as a condition of taking the member's money.

**Failure scenario.** Member, age 60, average final compensation $80,000, 30 years of creditable service including the four purchased. Unreduced allowance under § 51.1-155 A 1 is $3,400/month. She elects the § 51.1-165.01 C 3 partial lump sum (36 × $3,400 = $122,400) and a joint-and-survivor option, files her notice of retirement, and dies three weeks before her effective date. § 51.1-165.01 F does not void the election — the Board had the notification. B1 displaces the § 51.1-163 routing. G 4 routes her husband to § 51.1-162 B. On the reading above, his allowance is computed on the PLOP-reduced allowance: roughly $785/month of actuarially equivalent reduction, so $2,615 instead of $3,400, and a 100% survivor continuation of about **$2,223/month instead of $2,890 — $667/month, $8,004 a year, about $200,000 over a 25-year survivorship** — and the $122,400 lump sum is never paid to anybody, because § 51.1-165.01 D pays it with the first monthly annuity payment and there is none. He holds a Board certification, signed before his wife paid a dollar, telling him no § 51.1-165.01 option limits his benefit. Loser: the surviving spouse. Second loser: the Board, defending a certification the act compelled it to give.

**Fix direction.** Add to G 4 that for the purposes of § 51.1-162 the member is treated as not having elected a lump-sum distribution under § 51.1-165.01 where no lump-sum distribution has been paid, and conform the B2 clause to say so rather than asserting that no § 51.1-165.01 option limits the benefit.

### F2 — serious — no terminus on the Commonwealth's payment (f7-a)

**Exact words at fault.** A1: *"The Commonwealth shall not pay an amount settled under subdivision G 4 on the death of a member before the Board has determined … **whether the person entitled to it elects** under § 51.1-162 to be paid the member's accumulated contributions or a lump sum in lieu of any other benefit under that section."*

**Authority.** § 51.1-162 B, penultimate sentence: *"**If the person elects in writing**, the amount of the member's accumulated contributions or lump sum payment shall be paid to him exclusively, in lieu of any other benefits under this section."* The subsection fixes **no deadline** for that election, and the reduction it imposes is *"by the amount of any retirement allowance previously received by **the member**"* — not by amounts the survivor has received. G 4's release sentence operates only *"Where no retirement allowance is payable under § 51.1-162 on the member's death"*. Subdivision D 2: *"A certified subsidy that has been settled shall not be released … and the amount settled shall remain charged against the limitation."*

**What is wrong.** Yes — this bars the payment for ever, on the ordinary facts. Where an allowance **is** payable and the survivor simply takes it and never elects, the Board can never make the determination A1 waits on, because the election stays open for the survivor's lifetime. The amount is then simultaneously (i) settled, (ii) charged against the $3,000,000 limitation and unreleasable, (iii) owed to the retirement system by N's first sentence, and (iv) unpayable by N's second. The hole is not symmetric with the one f7-a cured: the cured problem was a payment made too early and recoverable; this one is a payment never made and the trust carrying it.

**Failure scenario.** Member dies in service in March 2031. Certified subsidy $74,685 (MOO-183 central). G 4 requires the Board to credit the purchased service and pay the § 51.1-162 B allowance to the surviving spouse, who takes the monthly allowance and never makes a § 51.1-162 B election. The Commonwealth never pays the $74,685. The retirement system has credited four years of service credit and received nothing for the subsidised part. The $74,685 stays charged against the cap — 2.5% of the program's capacity — for the spouse's lifetime. Losers: the VRS trust fund, for $74,685; and, at the margin, the applicant on the subdivision D 5 waiting list who is turned away for want of capacity the act will never release.

**Fix direction.** Give the hold a terminus — bar the payment only until the earlier of the Board's determination and the ninetieth day after the G 4 notice is given, and treat a late election as a case for the reinstatement machinery G 4 already has.

### F3 — serious — § 51.1-162 C is left pointing at an expired section

**Exact words at fault.** A4c's inserted clause: *", except as subdivision G 4 of § 51.1-142.4 provides,"*, read against enactment 3.

**Authority.** Enactment 3 expires § 51.1-142.4 on 1 July 2033 and deals with every other loose end — including, at clause (iv), the precise device needed here: *"upon the expiration of the amendments made by this act to the undesignated paragraph of subsection B of § 51.1-142.2 …, that paragraph shall read as it read immediately before the effective date of this act."* Nothing in enactment 3 says anything about § 51.1-162.

**What is wrong.** The § 51.1-162 C amendment is **permanent**; the section it cites is not. From 1 July 2033 the Code of Virginia will contain, in a death-benefit section of general application, an exception keyed to a subdivision of a section that has expired. The drafter plainly knew the cure — clause (iv) is it — and did not extend it to the section the act added to its own footprint on 12 October 2026.

**Failure scenario.** August 2033. A deputy's widow reads § 51.1-162 C, sees *"except as subdivision G 4 of § 51.1-142.4 provides"*, and cannot find § 51.1-142.4 in the Code. She cannot tell whether the ninety days is ninety days. No dollar amount; the cost is a curative bill in the 2034 session and a section of the Code that misinforms every reader of it for as long as it stands.

**Fix direction.** Add a clause to enactment 3 in the form of clause (iv): upon the expiration, § 51.1-162 shall read as it read immediately before the effective date of this act, except as clause (i) provides for elections accepted before that date.

### F4 — serious — B5 imports a qualifier that can swallow the benefit the act routes to

**Exact words at fault.** B2/B5: *"that, to the extent required by § 401(a)(37) of the Internal Revenue Code, as amended or renumbered, and the regulations thereunder applicable to governmental plans, subsection B of § 51.1-162 provides a retirement allowance on the death of a member in service only where no benefit is payable under subsection C of that section…"*

**Authority.** § 51.1-162 B opens with exactly those words, so the quotation is accurate — verified verbatim. 26 U.S.C. § 401(a)(37) is the HEART Act survivor-benefit rule: it requires additional death benefits only where the participant *"dies while performing qualified military service."*

**What is wrong.** F7 was right that the certification omitted the qualifier. Enacting it has a cost the finding did not price. Read literally, the opening words make the whole of § 51.1-162 B operative only *"to the extent required by § 401(a)(37)"* — which, for a member who dies at her desk rather than on active duty, is to no extent at all. The likely legislative intent was to qualify the inserted phrase *"including a member performing active duty military service"*, not the subsection; but the words sit at the head of the subsection, and B5 now has the **Board** reciting them, in writing, to the member, as the Board's statement of what § 51.1-162 B provides. G 4's entire death branch routes to § 51.1-162 *"as on the death of a member in service"*. The act's own certification is the best evidence available to a litigant that the Board does not consider the subsection to operate on an ordinary death.

**Failure scenario.** Member dies of a heart attack in 2031 having never served on active duty during membership. Her husband claims the § 51.1-162 B allowance, about $2,890/month. The Board denies, citing the opening words of § 51.1-162 B and its own certification, and pays only the § 51.1-162 A refund of accumulated contributions — say $62,000 once. Loser: the surviving spouse, for the difference between $62,000 and a lifetime allowance worth roughly $400,000 in present value. Even if he wins on reconsideration under M 2, the act handed him the fight.

**Fix direction.** Either drop the quoted qualifier from the certification and leave the plain description that stood before B5, or state the qualifier and add that the Board treats § 51.1-162 B as providing the allowance on any death in service.

### F5 — serious — B10 makes the § 51.1-162 C waiver period indefinitely revivable, and nothing can be recovered

**Exact words at fault.** B10: *"and where the Board after giving that notice determines a person's entitlement or non-entitlement to any benefit payable on the member's death differently … it shall give that person a further notice"*, read with G 4's existing *"Where the Board gives **a notice required by this subdivision** on the death of a member later than 30 days after the death, the period stated in subsection C of § 51.1-162 within which a waiver must be made does not expire before the thirtieth day after the notice is given to the person entitled to make it."*

**Authority.** § 51.1-162 C expressly contemplates late determinations: *"If no compensation is finally awarded under the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act due to legal proceedings or otherwise …, the Virginia Workers' Compensation Commission shall determine whether the member's death was from a cause compensable…"*. Subsection F of § 51.1-142.4: *"no amount shall be collected, offset, or withheld from any member, retiree, or beneficiary on that account"*; G 4: *"No amount shall be collected, offset, or withheld from any member, retiree, survivor, beneficiary, or estate of a member…"*

**What is wrong.** The extension sentence is keyed to *"a notice required by this subdivision"* — any of them — not to the first notice on the death. B10 creates an open-ended duty to give further notices whenever the Board re-determines an entitlement. Every further notice that reaches the person entitled to waive, given more than 30 days after the death, restarts the § 51.1-162 C waiver period. There is no cap and no outer date. Because the act forbids collection or offset of anything already paid, any switch between the § 51.1-162 B and C branches after payment has begun produces an unrecoverable overpayment.

**Failure scenario.** Member dies in service in 2031. The Board determines no § 51.1-162 C benefit is payable and begins paying the surviving spouse the § 51.1-162 B allowance, $2,890/month. In 2033 the Workers' Compensation Commission determines the death was compensable. The Board must re-determine, gives the spouse a further notice under B10, and the waiver period revives for 30 days from that notice. Whichever branch is superseded has by then paid about **$69,000** that the act forbids the Board to recover. Loser: the trust fund, for the whole of the superseded branch's payments, with no cap on how long after the death this can happen.

**Fix direction.** Key the extension sentence to the notice first required by G 4 on the death, and add an outer limit — the waiver period shall not in any event expire later than a stated number of days after the death — and add a reconciliation rule for a switch between § 51.1-162 B and C after payment has begun.

### F6 — moderate — the named assumption set is not an identifiable set

**Exact words at fault.** A2: *"the actuarial assumptions and factors the Board **uses** to determine actuarial equivalence under § 51.1-165"*.

**Authority.** § 51.1-124.3 already defines the term the clause is reaching for: *"'Actuarial equivalent' means a benefit of equal value when computed upon the basis of **actuarial tables adopted by the Board**."* That definition governs Chapter 1, and so governs § 51.1-165 and § 51.1-142.4 alike. § 51.1-165 A does not assign the Board the task of "determining actuarial equivalence" — it says the member shall *"receive the actuarial equivalent of the retirement allowance otherwise payable to him"* and that the election *"shall be subject to the approval of the Board."*

**What is wrong.** Three defects in one phrase. (a) It keys the basis to what the Board *uses* rather than what it has *adopted*, so there is nothing to publish, nothing to adopt, and nothing a reviewing court can find; the Code's own formulation ("adopted") is an act with a record. (b) It attributes to § 51.1-165 a determination that section does not assign to the Board. (c) Every option in § 51.1-165 converts an allowance into another allowance; none converts an allowance into a lump sum. The comparison A2 governs can have a lump sum on one side (see F7), and the lump-sum factors live in § 51.1-165.01, not § 51.1-165. So the named set does not contain the factor the named comparison needs.

**Failure scenario.** A G 4 death where subdivision C 2 applies. The Board must compare two benefits "on the basis of actuarial equivalence … determined on the actuarial assumptions and factors the Board uses to determine actuarial equivalence under § 51.1-165." Its actuary reports that § 51.1-165 option factors are a family of allowance-to-allowance conversion factors and that no § 51.1-165 factor discounts a lump sum. The Board improvises — most likely with the valuation discount rate — and its choice is a reviewable determination under M 2 with no statutory standard against which it can be defended. Loser: whichever of the two payees the improvised basis puts second.

**Fix direction.** Use the Code's defined term: compare the two *"on the basis of actuarial tables adopted by the Board"*, or *"as actuarial equivalents within the meaning of § 51.1-124.3"*.

### F7 — moderate — the greater-of comparison changes no amount for anyone

**Exact words at fault.** A2 in full, and the sentence it sits in: *"the Board shall pay the benefit it provides or the benefit determined under the preceding sentence, whichever is greater…"*

**Authority.** § 51.1-165 C 2 routes to one of two things: § 51.1-163, or § 51.1-162 B where the contingent annuitants *"are the same person or persons who would receive a monthly benefit under subsection B of § 51.1-162 and the monthly benefit under such subsection would be greater."* § 51.1-163 pays *"any excess of his accumulated contributions as of the effective date of his retirement, over the total retirement allowances received by him"* — a lump sum, nothing more. § 51.1-162 B pays a lifetime allowance only to a surviving spouse, minor child or parent, as does C 2's clause (iv).

**What is wrong.** You asked whether dating the § 51.1-163 side at the death changes any amount the Code would otherwise produce for anyone. **No — for anyone, in either branch.** Branch one: C 2 routes to § 51.1-163, so the comparison is a lump sum of accumulated contributions against a lifetime allowance. Program eligibility (clauses (v)–(vii) of "eligible member") confines participants to members at or near their unreduced date, so the allowance side wins on any plausible basis — a $62,000 refund against an allowance worth four to five hundred thousand in present value. Branch two: C 2 routes to § 51.1-162 B, and the G 4 benefit *is* § 51.1-162 computed on the member's creditable service including the purchased credit, so both sides are the same number. The clause is surplusage. The death-dating refines a figure that never wins, and the named assumption set (F6) is a basis for a comparison whose outcome it cannot change.

**Failure scenario.** No loser, which is the point. The exposure is the opposite kind: the opposition reads the subdivision aloud and asks what the actuarial-equivalence comparison is for, and the honest answer is nothing. Surplusage in a benefit provision invites the argument that the drafters did not know what the cited sections do.

**Fix direction.** Either strike the greater-of comparison and the dating together, or keep them as belt-and-braces and fix F6, but say in the drafter's note that the clause is not expected to be operative.

### F8 — moderate — the inserted clause's placement is ambiguous as to what it qualifies

**Exact words at fault.** *"by written notification to the Board within ninety days after the death of the member**, except as subdivision G 4 of § 51.1-142.4 provides,** in order to make available a retirement allowance…"*

**Authority.** Article IV, Section 12 of the Constitution of Virginia requires the amended section to be *"reenacted and published at length"*; the clause is therefore read as part of the published section by a survivor who has never seen § 51.1-142.4.

**What is wrong.** Set off by commas, the clause is a parenthetical interrupting the whole predicate *"shall be entitled to waive his rights to the allowance by written notification to the Board within ninety days after the death of the member."* It is therefore available to be read as qualifying the **right to waive** ("is entitled to waive, except as G 4 provides"), or the **purpose** ("except as G 4 provides, in order to make available…"), as well as the ninety days. Today only the third reading matters, because G 4 does nothing but extend; but § 51.1-162 C will outlive § 51.1-142.4 (F3), and a reader of § 51.1-162 C alone cannot tell whether the cross-reference enlarges the right or cuts it down. The act's own plain-English row at A4e compounds it: it says G 4 *"gives her 30 days"*, which states a substitute period, whereas G 4's words are a floor (*"does not expire before the thirtieth day"*) and never shorten the ninety.

**Failure scenario.** Death on 1 March; the Board gives the G 4 notice on 10 April, day 40 — late, so G 4's floor is 10 May, earlier than the Code's day 90 of 30 May. The survivor, or a claims clerk, reads *"within ninety days after the death of the member, except as subdivision G 4 … provides"* as meaning that G 4 supplies the operative period where the notice is late, and treats 10 May as the deadline. She waives on 20 May, inside the Code's ninety days, and is told she is out of time. Loser: a surviving spouse whose waiver would have moved the family from the § 51.1-162 C allowance (33⅓% of an $80,000 average final compensation, $26,667 a year, where she qualifies for Social Security death benefits) to the § 51.1-162 B allowance of about $34,680 a year — **about $8,000 a year for life**.

**Fix direction.** Make it a separate sentence in § 51.1-162 C — "The ninety-day period stated in this subsection is extended as subdivision G 4 of § 51.1-142.4 provides." — or attach it directly to the period: *"within ninety days after the death of the member, or within such longer period as subdivision G 4 of § 51.1-142.4 provides,"*.

### F9 — moderate — "all periods so excluded" is the wrong unit

**Exact words at fault.** A3: *"provided that the aggregate of all periods so excluded shall not exceed 90 days."*

**Authority.** The same sentence's clause (i): *"no second or subsequent request for reconsideration of the same determination or of the same failure, and no second or subsequent request under that definition, tolls any period."* M 2's deeming sentence makes *"[e]very determination, certification, redetermination, recertification, reduction, cancellation, rescission, restoration, crediting of service credit, placement on a waiting list, expiration of a reservation, denial of an election, and refusal to accept an election"* separately reviewable. M 2's deemed-denial path caps the Board's inaction at 90 days plus a notice plus 30 days.

**What is wrong.** Clause (i) already prevents a member from manufacturing tolling by re-filing on the **same** determination. The 90-day aggregate cap therefore bites only across **different** determinations — that is, it penalises the member for the Board's lateness on matters she had a statutory right to have reconsidered separately. The right unit is per request. With the deemed-denial path used diligently, the excluded period per request is about 30 days, so the 90-day aggregate is exhausted at the **fourth** distinct first-time request.

**Failure scenario.** Member has four distinct adverse determinations — not an eligible member under D 4, the clause (ii) amount, a recertification under D 6, and the certification's failure to state a required matter. She files one first-time request on each, files the expiry notice promptly each time, and the Board runs 30 days past its 90 on each. Excluded: 90 days, cap reached. The fourth 30 days counts against the 120-day tolling aggregate; her final qualifying retirement date arrives while the fourth reconsideration is live. She does not retire in a qualifying retirement; G 2 reduces her credited service to what her payments bought at standard cost. On the MOO-183 central case she paid roughly a quarter of the standard cost, so **about three of her four purchased years are taken back** and the $74,685 subsidy is released. She has a clause (iii) argument — "error of the Board" — but that term is defined in G 4 only for two specific statement-and-notice failures, and she is now litigating whether missing a statutory deadline is an "error."

**Fix direction.** Make the exclusion per request rather than aggregate, or cap the number of tolling requests instead of the Board-delay exclusion.

### F10 — moderate — no date by which the Commonwealth must pay

**Exact words at fault.** Subsection N, first sentence: *"**Upon the settlement** of a certified subsidy under subdivision G 1 or G 4, the Commonwealth shall pay the amount settled…"*, read against A1's second sentence.

**What is wrong.** "Upon the settlement" was the only timing rule in N. For a G 4 death settlement, A1 now defers the payment to an event with no date, and nothing supplies a date once that event occurs. This is not a true conflict — the specific carve-out controls the general — but the result is a duty of the Commonwealth with no due date and no reviewable failure-to-pay window keyed to anything. M 2 gives a remedy for *"a failure … to make a payment within a period required by this section"*; for this payment there is now no period.

**Failure scenario.** The Board determines on 1 June 2032 that the § 51.1-162 allowance is payable and that no § 51.1-162 B election has been made within the G 4 notice period. Nothing in the act requires the Commonwealth to pay the $74,685 on any date. The retirement system carries it. Loser: the trust, for the time value — on $74,685 at the Board's assumed return, about $5,200 a year of forgone earnings.

**Fix direction.** "The Commonwealth shall pay the amount settled no later than 60 days after the settlement or, where the second sentence of this subsection applies, no later than 60 days after the Board makes the determinations required by that sentence."

### F11 — moderate — a non-member's window still runs from a document he never receives

**Exact words at fault.** B4: *"the second sentence of subdivision M 2 does not apply to such a failure **where the person adversely affected is the member**;"* read with D 1's *"or, where that person is not the member, no later than 30 days after the date on which the notice required by subdivision G 4 is given to that person"*.

**What is wrong.** B4 is directionally right — restoring the deeming sentence for non-members gives a payer a route. It does not close the gap, because both of the non-member's routes are keyed to documents a non-member may never receive. D 1's non-member window runs from a **G 4 notice**, which exists only *"Where the member dies."* The restored deeming route runs, under M 2's first sentence, from *"the notice of the determination"* — and the certification is given *"to the member"*. So a non-member adversely affected by a failure to state in a certification, in a case where the member does not die, has a window that opens on a document given to someone else and closes 30 days later.

**Failure scenario.** The Department of Corrections pays $12,000 of a member's cost from its appropriation under subdivision B 4. The certification fails to state the amount payable by the agency — a matter D 1 requires via subdivision B 4 c. The agency never receives the certification. It discovers the defect eight months later when the refund under D 6 comes back short. Its 30 days expired seven months earlier. Loser: a state agency, for $12,000, with no reconsideration route and no case decision to take to the circuit court under § 2.2-4026.

**Fix direction.** For a person who is not the member, run the D 1 window from the earlier of the G 4 notice and the date the Board gives that person notice of the certification, and require the Board to give the certification to any state agency from whose appropriation an amount was paid.

### F12 — minor — a late determination that is never noticed has no window

**Exact words at fault.** B8: *"or, where the Board has made the determination, given the notice, or made the payment late, **within 30 days after the notice of the determination is given** or, in the case of a failure to give a notice or to make a payment, within 30 days after the Board gives the notice or makes the payment,"*

**What is wrong.** B8 fixed the notice and payment cases. The determination case is now trigger-identical to the ordinary branch at the head of the sentence ("within 30 days after the notice of the determination is given") and therefore adds nothing — and leaves a gap. The open-ended branch runs only *"at any time before the Board makes the determination"*; once the determination is made, it closes. The late branch waits on a notice. A determination made late and never noticed falls between them: the person can seek review of the failure to give the notice, but not of the determination's substance.

**Failure scenario.** The Board makes a D 6 recertification 100 days late and never gives the notice D 6 requires. The member learns of it from her payment schedule. Her open-ended window closed when the Board made the determination; her 30-day window has not opened and may never. Loser: the member, who must sue for the notice before she can seek review of the recertification.

**Fix direction.** Add: "or, where the Board has made a determination late and has not given notice of it, at any time before it gives that notice."

### F13 — minor — B9 drops § 51.1-162 A's qualifier on the reduction

**Exact words at fault.** B9: *"the payment of the member's accumulated contributions, **reduced by the amount of any retirement allowance previously received by the member**, to the person the member has designated…"*

**Authority.** § 51.1-162 A: *"This amount shall be reduced by the amount of any retirement allowance previously received by the member **under this chapter or the abolished system**."*

**What is wrong.** The certification states the reduction without the Code's limiting words, so it is broader than the Code: it reads as a reduction for any retirement allowance from any source. Academic for a G 4 member, who has received none — but it is a compelled written statement of law and it overstates a deduction against a survivor's payment.

**Fix direction.** Add "under this chapter or the abolished system."

### F14 — minor — B11 makes the bar derivative and loses subsection L's audience

**Exact words at fault.** B11: *"**No person whom the definition of "eligible member" in subsection A excludes**, including a teacher, an employee of a political subdivision, and a member of a retirement system established under Chapter 2 …, is eligible to make an election under this section."*

**What is wrong.** Two things. (a) The definition excludes not only by its final sentence but by each of clauses (i)–(viii), several of which are time-dependent; subsection L states the bar with no time anchor. The anchor exists elsewhere — subdivision D 4 determines eligibility *"as of the filing date of the applicant's complete application"* — so the better reading is sound, but L on its face invites the argument that a member who later ceases to satisfy a clause was never eligible and her accepted election is void. (b) Subsection L exists to tell localities and school boards that the act does not reach them; its first sentence says so for duties and costs, and its second sentence used to say so expressly for teachers and local employees. It now says so by cross-reference to a definition in another subsection. The reader this subsection is written for — VML, VACo, the Commission on Local Government — must now follow a cross-reference to get the comfort the subsection was drafted to give.

**Fix direction.** "No person who is not an eligible member, determined as of the filing date of that person's complete application, is eligible to make an election under this section," and restore the express naming of teachers and local employees as a second sentence.

### F15 — minor — the title names an object the bill does not contain

**Exact words at fault.** A4a's title line: *"relating to the Virginia Retirement System; purchase of prior active duty military service credit by state employees; cost; pilot program; **post-retirement employment**; reports."*

**Authority.** Article IV, Section 12: *"No law shall embrace more than one object, which shall be expressed in its title."*

**What is wrong.** "post-retirement employment" occurs exactly once in the whole document — in the title. The vice the single-object rule guards against is a title narrower than the act, so a title broader than the act is not a constitutional defect; but it is a defect DLS will strike, and it tells a reader the bill does something it does not.

**Fix direction.** Strike "post-retirement employment" from the title, or restore the provision it refers to.

### F16 — minor — a float-formatting artifact in the §1 editorial note

**Exact words at fault.** *"and **§0.0000000000000000e** records what came out with it."*

**What is wrong.** A record pointer has been rendered as a floating-point number. The editorial note is expressly not part of the bill, so nothing legal turns on it, but the note is the reader's index into the §0 record and this entry points nowhere.

**Fix direction.** Restore the intended §0 record number.

### F17 — minor — "obtained" is undefined and the giving rule does not reach it

**Exact words at fault.** D 1: *"The Board shall give the certification required by this subsection no later than 15 days after the confirmation required by subdivision 3 is **obtained**."*

**What is wrong.** Subsection A's giving rule, as widened by B6, covers *"a written statement, notice, or certification **required of the Board**"*. The subdivision D 3 confirmation is required of the Director of the Department of Planning and Budget, not of the Board, and it is "obtained" rather than "given." So a period that runs against the Board runs from an event the act does not date. The Board can date it from its own internal logging.

**Failure scenario.** The Director signs the confirmation on 1 March; the Board logs receipt on 20 March and gives the certification on 2 April. On the Board's dating it is on time; on the Director's it is 17 days late, and the lateness is what triggers D 1's extension of the final qualifying retirement date. Loser: the member, who loses 17 days of extension she cannot prove she is owed.

**Fix direction.** Run the 15 days from the date the Director or his designee acts upon the Board's request, or define "obtained" by reference to the giving rule.

### F18 — moderate — three different expressions for the actuarial basis in one act

**Exact words at fault.** "Certified subsidy": *"using the actuarial assumptions adopted by the Board for the most recent actuarial valuation of the retirement system"*. A2: *"the actuarial assumptions and factors the Board uses to determine actuarial equivalence under § 51.1-165"*. And § 51.1-124.3's *"actuarial tables adopted by the Board"*, which the act never invokes.

**What is wrong.** Three formulations for one kind of thing, in a section that is read alongside a chapter that already has a defined term. Valuation assumptions and option factors are genuinely different sets and the act is right to distinguish them, but it distinguishes them with ad hoc language rather than with the Code's vocabulary, and the reader cannot tell whether the difference is deliberate. A court construing A2 will ask why the drafter used the chapter's defined term nowhere and invented two descriptions instead.

**Fix direction.** Define one term in subsection A — e.g. "option factors" — by reference to § 51.1-124.3, and use the defined terms consistently.

## 4. Fiscal assumption challenge

**The load-bearing assumption.** MOO-183's central case: **$74,685 per participant**, about **18.9 participants**, aggregate about **$1.41M** on the authorised rung. Everything about the severity of F2 depends on how often a G 4 death actually happens, because F2's failure mode strands exactly one certified subsidy per occurrence.

**The test.** F2 fires on an in-service death of a member whose election has been accepted and who has not retired, where a § 51.1-162 allowance is payable and the entitled person never makes the § 51.1-162 B election. The last condition is the common case, so essentially every G 4 death fires it. Exposure: acceptances run from 1 July 2029 to 1 July 2033; each participant is exposed from acceptance to retirement — payment within 12 months under subsection E, then up to 12 months to the final qualifying retirement date — so roughly **2 participant-years each**. At the central count that is about **38 participant-years**. Pre-retirement mortality for VRS state employees in the 55–62 band is of the order of **0.4% a year**.

**The result.** **Expected G 4 deaths over the whole pilot: about 0.15.** F2 is real, certain in mechanism, and fires in roughly **one pilot in seven**. When it fires it strands **$74,685** — **2.5% of the $3,000,000 limitation** — permanently and unreimbursed, and the same arithmetic caps the exposure of F1, F4 and F5, each of which also needs a G 4 death.

**What that means for the ranking.** The tail findings (F1, F2, F4, F5, F6, F7, F9) are low-frequency. The **high-frequency** exposure is the compelled certification, which is given to **every one of the ~19 participants, before any of them pays a dollar** — and F1, F4 and F13 are all defects in that certification. That is why F1 and not F2 is the most serious finding, even though F2 is the one that strands money.

**Reruns.** MOO-183's model was not rerun; no finding of mine changes an input to it. The arithmetic above uses its published central figures and a mortality rate I did not take from the team's work — if the Fiscal & Impact Modeler has a VRS pre-retirement mortality rate for this cohort, substitute it; the conclusion is insensitive to anything between 0.2% and 1.0% a year, which spans 0.08 to 0.38 expected deaths.

## 6. What I checked and found sound — stated as null results

- **N1.** The § 51.1-162 reproduction is character-identical to the official LIS text read 12 October 2026, apart from the inserted clause and the omitted historical-citation line. Machine diff, not eyeball.
- **N2.** Article IV, Section 12 requires reproduction at length, verbatim as quoted in the issue. Reproducing § 51.1-162 is **necessary**, not optional, once the act amends it, and the act does it correctly and in Code numerical order after § 51.1-142.4.
- **N3.** **No serious single-object objection.** The title leads with "relating to the Virginia Retirement System"; a conforming amendment to the waiver period for a death benefit payable on a participant's death is germane to the pilot. Virginia practice construes the object broadly. The only title defect is **F15**, which runs the other way.
- **N4.** Enactment 4's reenactment condition reaches the § 51.1-162 amendment automatically, because the amendment sits in the first enactment. Adding § 51.1-162 created no new contingency problem. Enactment 2's 1 July 2029 date likewise applies to it without amendment.
- **N5.** **B1's narrowing is correct.** § 51.1-165 C 1's limbs 1, 2 and 4 and § 51.1-165.01 F's first sentence should all survive; only the § 51.1-163 routing limbs should be displaced. Not displacing the null-and-void limb is right — it is protective of the member, and displacing it would have left an option election operative on a death the member never notified.
- **N6.** All four of your B2 premises hold at source. The failure is in the verb, not the premises.
- **N7.** B5's quoted § 401(a)(37) prefix, B5's designation-and-precedence limb, and B9's predeceased-designee trigger and offset are each accurate against § 51.1-162 A and B as published.
- **N8.** **B6/B7's line is in the right place.** The three occurrences that needed the giving rule are the ones that now have it: D 1's own review window runs from the giving of the certification, and the three-day mailing extension now reaches it. The eleven left alone name particular documents required by the "final qualifying retirement date" definition, which the giving rule already reaches as statements and notices.
- **N9.** All nineteen READ strings occur **exactly once** in the enacted §1 slice, and A4e's row once in §2, at the live document. Verified by count, not by assertion.
- **N10.** I tested the retroactive-notice window against G 4's condition. A member who dies after a back-dated effective date of retirement but before filing the § 51.1-153 notice **has not retired**, so G 4's death branch reaches her, and § 51.1-165 C 1's first limb voids any option election she had made. This is a different route from the carded F1 and it comes out sound.
- **N11.** **No Dillon's Rule exposure.** The act imposes no duty, cost, reporting obligation or administrative act on a county, city, town, other political subdivision, local school board or local retirement system, and subsection L says so in terms. No local power needs an express grant because none is exercised.
- **N12.** **A1/f7-a has a virtue worth recording.** Where the member had elected the straight life option under § 51.1-165 A 1 and no § 51.1-162 allowance is payable to anyone, G 4's release-and-restore sentence releases the settled amount — but only *"to the extent the amount settled has not been paid to the retirement system under subsection N."* It is f7-a's hold that keeps the Commonwealth from paying first and having nothing to release. That case works, and it works because of the clause **F2** attacks. F2's fix must preserve it.
