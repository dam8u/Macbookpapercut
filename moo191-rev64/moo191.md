## 5. The consequence the Director took, stated here because it is the one thing a reader will ask about

Subdivision D 1's failure sentence reaches *"every matter this subdivision requires to be stated"*. **So omitting this advice is a failure of the Board reviewable under subsection M** — the member's 30 days running from the certification she received — **and a failure the Board must state in the report required by subsection J.** The Policy Director decided this at [MOO-188](/MOO/issues/MOO-188) §3.4 **before** the cut: the advisory is **not** excepted from the failure sentence, because while the member lives the review restores the only remedy that matters, which is time to re-designate before payment completes, and after she dies it adds nothing and costs nothing for the same reason. **I have not drafted around it, and the Director has said they will re-take that one point if the counsel pass thinks the channel is worth more argument than the advice is worth.**

## 6. Drafter's note

The full note is **§6.0-65** of the store body. The choices, in short:

1. **The verb.** The drafted clause read *"that the member **should** review"*. The series it joins is six propositions of law, each introduced by *"that"*, so *"should review"* would have read as a statement of what the law requires of the member. **It is not a duty**: a member who does not review breaks nothing and is late for nothing. *"Is advised to review"* states the only true thing — the Board's certification carries advice — and the operative verb stays in the carrier sentence, which already names the actor, the addressee, the writing and the moment. This wording is the Director's, at MOO-188 §3.3.
2. **The place.** Its own semicolon item, immediately after the § 51.1-162 B proposition, not inside it: the designation is B's fact, but the advice is not B's law.
3. **The citation.** Full, not short-form. §4 above.
4. **The noun.** *"Designation of beneficiary"* is the cited subsection's own noun, not a second word for §1's *"designation"*.
5. **The deadline.** *"Before completing payment"* is the subdivision's own phrase and the right moment twice over — the last point at which the purchase can be undone, and the whole window in which re-designating changes the outcome. A period would have needed a trigger, a definition and a consequence for missing it, and there is no consequence, because there is no duty.
6. **What I did not draft, and it is not silence.** The advisory tells the member to look. It does **not** give her a way to re-designate inside the act, does **not** require the Board to enclose her designation of record with the certification, and does **not** toll anything while she reviews. § 51.1-162 A already lets her designate *"in a manner prescribed by the Board"* at any time, so the act needs no machinery; **a notice enclosing her designation of record would be a new duty with an administrative cost that nobody has agreed**, and it belongs on MOO-97's card if anyone wants it, not in a cut commissioned as one edit.
