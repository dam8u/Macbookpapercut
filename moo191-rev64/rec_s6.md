### §6.0-65 Ambiguities resolved in store revision 65

1. **Why *"is advised to review"* and not *"should review"*, and why neither is *"shall review."*** The series it joins is six propositions of law, each introduced by *"that"*, and a seventh item reading *"that the member **should** review"* would have read as a statement of what the law requires of the member. **It is not a duty, and it must not be capable of being read as one** — a member who does not review her designation breaks nothing, loses no right and is not late for anything. *"Is advised to review"* states the only thing that is true: the Board's certification carries advice. **The operative verb belongs to the Board's carrier sentence** — *"The Board shall not accept an election until the Board has, in writing, certified to the member … that …"* — so the actor, the addressee, the writing and the moment are all already named, and the advisory adds no unnamed actor. This is the Policy Director's wording change at MOO-188 §3.3 and the reason is recorded there.
2. **Why it is its own semicolon item and not an appendage to the § 51.1-162 B proposition.** The designation is subsection B's fact, which is the argument for putting the advice inside B's item, and it is the wrong argument: **B's item states what the law does, and the advisory states what the member is advised to do.** Merging them would have buried advice inside a proposition of law and made it arguable that the advice is part of what B provides. Separate, the propositions stay together and the advice is visibly advice.
3. **Why the full citation *"subsection A of § 51.1-162"* and not *"subsection A of that section."*** §1 uses the short form three times, and each time inside a sentence that has already named § 51.1-162. The advisory is a **new item** and the item that follows it restarts with the full citation (*"that subsection C of § 51.1-162"*), so the short form would have made the referent depend on an item two places back. §5.43 direction 1.
4. **Why *"designation of beneficiary"* is the right noun and not a second word for one concept.** § 51.1-162 A, verified at source on 12 October 2026, requires each member to *"designate who is to receive a refund of accumulated contributions"*, pays *"the designated beneficiary"*, and calls the act *"the designation"*; B pays *"the person or persons designated as provided in subsection A."* **The advisory therefore points the member at the cited subsection using that subsection's own noun**, while §1's own *"designation"* carries the same meaning it already carries.
5. **Why *"before completing payment"* and not a date, a period, or *"before the Board accepts the election."*** The phrase is already in this subdivision — the certification states *"the member's right to cancel the election under subdivision G 5 at any time before completing payment"* — and in subdivision G 4. **The advisory's deadline is the same event, and it is the right one twice over:** it is the last moment at which the purchase can still be undone, and the whole window during which re-designating changes the outcome. A fixed period would have needed a trigger, a definition and a consequence for missing it, and there is no consequence, because there is no duty.
6. **Why the advisory is not excepted from D 1's failure sentence, and what it costs.** **The Policy Director decided this at MOO-188 §3.4 before the cut, and I have not drafted around it.** Omitting the advice is a failure to state *"every matter this subdivision requires to be stated"*, so it is reviewable under subsection M and reportable under subsection J. The cost is that **a review channel now exists for the omission of a sentence that creates no right** — while the member lives it returns the only remedy that matters, which is time to re-designate before payment completes, and after she dies it adds nothing because no remedy can re-designate for her. **If the counsel gate at [MOO-192](/MOO/issues/MOO-192) thinks the channel is worth more argument than the advice is worth, the Director has said they will re-take that one point; it is one clause either way, and the clause is this item.**
7. **What I did not draft, and it is not silence.** The advisory tells the member to look; it does **not** give her a way to re-designate inside the act, does not require the Board to send her her current designation with the certification, and does not toll anything while she reviews. § 51.1-162 A already lets her designate *"in a manner prescribed by the Board"* at any time, so the act needs no machinery, and **a notice that enclosed her designation of record would be a new duty with an administrative cost that nobody has agreed.** It is the kind of thing that belongs on MOO-97's card if anyone wants it, not in a cut commissioned as one edit.
