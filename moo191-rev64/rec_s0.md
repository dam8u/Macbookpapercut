**The defect, in one sentence.** § 51.1-162 B pays a **designated** spouse, minor child or parent, so a member who designated her sister in 2009, buys 36 months and is survived by her mother gets **nothing** from the purchase — and **store revision 62 stopped the certification telling her something untrue, which does not tell her to look.** The designation is the one fact that decides whether the Commonwealth's subsidy buys her family anything and the one fact she can change for free and in a minute.

**The enacted words.** Inserted into the Board's certification under subdivision D 1 as **its own semicolon item, immediately after the § 51.1-162 B proposition** — the FIND resolved **once in the whole document and once in §1** on the body that was written, asserted before the substitution. The whole of § 51.1-142.4 is new language and subdivision D 1 is a single underlined paragraph, so the insertion carries no markup of its own:

> … or who is entitled in the order of precedence that subsection B of that section states where the member has made no designation or the person designated has predeceased the member and no other designation has been made; <u>**that the member is advised to review the member's designation of beneficiary under subsection A of § 51.1-162 before completing payment;**</u> that subsection C of § 51.1-162 provides a retirement allowance …

**Two departures from the words drafted at item 129 answer (4), both the Director's and both deliberate.**

1. **The verb.** The drafted clause read *"and that the member **should review** the member's designation of beneficiary."* Every other item in this series is a proposition of law introduced by *"that"*, so *"that the member should review"* reads as a statement about what the law requires of the member, which it is not. **The enacted words make it advice on its face** — *"that the member **is advised to** review"* — and the actor who gives the advice is named by the carrier sentence, which is the Board's own certification. **Nothing in the enacted words imposes a duty on the member, and nothing turns on whether she takes the advice.**
2. **The placement.** Its own semicolon item rather than an appendage to the § 51.1-162 B proposition, because **the designation is B's fact but the advice is not B's law**. Keeping it separate leaves the propositions of law together and makes the advice visibly advice.

**The consequence, accepted by the Director at MOO-188 §3.4 before the cut and not discovered after it.** Subdivision D 1's failure sentence reaches *"every matter this subdivision requires to be stated"*, so **omitting this advice is a failure reviewable under subsection M** — the member's 30 days running from the certification she received — **and a failure the Board must state in the report required by subsection J**. The advisory is **not** excepted from the failure sentence, and the Director's reasoning is that while the member lives the review restores the only thing that matters, which is time to re-designate, and after she dies it adds nothing and costs nothing for the same reason.
