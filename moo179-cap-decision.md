## 2. Why — the chaining price, and the one figure that did move

### 2.1 The decline's premise survives the chain, and it survives it analytically

**The reason the cap was declined was never the length of the retention. It was that there is no
money-rationed queue to cap.** At the central estimate D 2's $3,000,000 ceiling does not bind —
**67.4% utilisation** — so every election the bill admits is funded whatever the order, and queue
position decides nothing for anyone.

**MOO-170 shows the chain does not reach that.** Three findings carry it, and the first two are
derivations rather than draws:

- **The $204,000 high-take-up bound is unchanged, and there is no chain length in it.** The bound is the
  cap-fill residual argument and it already quantified over *any* ordering of a fixed cohort against the
  fixed pool. A chain of any length — including an unbounded one — is only another ordering. The sweep's
  largest observed cell is **$332, 0.163% of the bound**: agreement, and falsifiable.
- **It is still lapse, not cost.** D 2's $3.00M is a self-executing ceiling, not an appropriation.
  Reordering changes which reservations fill it, never how much is authorised. The appropriation line
  stays **$0 exactly**.
- **A chain cannot add demand.** It is the same member filing again about the same election. So chain
  length is not a second route to the cap binding. **The cap binds on cohort size, and cohort size is
  decided upstream at MOO-49's clause (vi) — not here.**

**That is trigger 1, answered in the negative, on the limb the board just widened.** The 120-day figure
was in the model as the live case, with 30 days kept as a sensitivity.

### 2.2 The one thing that moved, stated at full strength

**The displacement count rises, and it rises a lot: 0.14 → 2.80 members mean over the 48-month pilot,
roughly 21×, with 11 members in the worst draw.** MOO-170 did not bury that behind the word "fraction"
and I am not going to either. **It is the only quantity chaining changes, and it is the strongest fact
against the decision I am taking.** Four things answer it.

**First, it is a count of members who move, not of members who lose — and on the branch that matters the
mover gains.** The three construction branches rise for opposite reasons:

- **`removed`** — the chain **funds** the reconsideration applicant: her retention now outlasts pool
  exhaustion, so she is no longer dropped. The 2.80 is **her gain**, and the members who move against her
  are those behind her in a queue that was already going to exhaust.
- **`back_of_queue_asis`** — the chain displaces **her**. MOO-170 names this the artefact branch: at long
  chain lengths it relegates a member who never actually lost the retained date.
- **`back_of_queue_gated`**, the faithful generalisation — **flat at 0.00 at every chain length including
  unbounded.**

**Second, and this is what decides it: the cap would reverse the board's own decision in substance.** On
the branch where a third party bears a cost — `removed` — what the chain does is keep the reconsideration
applicant's filing date alive long enough to be funded. **That is precisely the protection the board
bought at 14:15 when it chose 120 days over 30.** A cap that cuts the chain takes it back from the same
member, by the same mechanism, without the board being asked. **I will not do that on a quantity that is
$0 at the central estimate.**

**Third, the figure is still inside the resolution of the count it sits in.** The participant count is
known only as "18, range 9 to 30" — a band 21 members wide. The high cell's cohort is 35, and 2.80 is
**8.0%** of it. The high cell is itself an upper bound (MOO-71 §3). So: 2.80 members **on the worst branch
of the worst cell, against a count known to roughly ±9 members.** Narrower than at MOO-163, and I record
it as narrower.

**Fourth, the loss is of priority, not of the purchase.** MOO-162 §3.4 stands: **D 5 preserves every
subsection B standard-cost right.** The displaced waiting-list applicant that MOO-125 maps as cost-bearer
loses queue position, not the right to purchase.

### 2.3 Question 3 — the dimension, and why it matters for the text

**Days per request is disposed of by construction.** MOO-170 re-ran the saturated cell at the 33-day,
40-day and 120-day link and got **identical** results — flips 2.57 / 2.56 / 0.00 by branch, the same
dollars — because the horizon-filling chain and the unbounded limit do not use the link length at all.
**Link length decides only how fast the chain saturates, not what it saturates at.** So the clause (ii)
dimension — aggregate days — has **no fiscal warrant whatsoever**, at any figure.

**Number of requests is the only dimension in which anything moves**, and only in members, not dollars:
the count climbs monotonically with k and saturates around k = 44, where the chain fills the pilot. **So
if the cap ever revives, the live limb is clause (i) and the dead limb is clause (ii).** §3.

**Honest statement of the limit.** The long-chain *dollar* deltas are not resolved: every one of the 12
seed-test cells sits inside its own 95% Monte Carlo interval and the seed-to-seed swing reaches $421,
larger than half the printed deltas. **This decision therefore does not rest on them.** It rests on the
analytic bound, which uses none of the draws, and on the member counts, which are one-sided by
construction and not seed artefacts.

## 3. The form question, answered for the record and not enacted

**Nothing goes into the operative text.** But MOO-179's question 2 was asked so the answer exists when the
cap next comes up, and the chaining dimension now settles it. Pass 18's text was:

> …, except that (i) no second or subsequent request for reconsideration of the same determination
> continues the retention and (ii) the aggregate of all retention under this sentence shall not exceed
> 120 days.

**On the board's figure, clause (ii) is dead either way it is read.** At 120 days per request the first
request exhausts a 120-day aggregate by itself. So:

- read **outside** pendency, clause (ii) bites only on the second request — which is exactly and only what
  clause (i) does, in more words and with a number that invites the other reading. **Surplusage, and the
  worse-drafted of the two.**
- read **inside** pendency, the aggregate reaches into the pendency period and **claws back part of the 120
  days the board has just granted.** That is not a cap on chaining; it is a reduction of the board's
  figure, taken without a card.

**So the smallest form that survives the collision is clause (i) alone**, and it is also the only limb
with a dimension the price can speak to (§2.3). **If the cap is ever revived, clause (ii) at 120 days does
not come with it.** A different aggregate — a multiple of 120 — would be a new figure, and a new figure is
a board card, not a drafter's or my own re-pitch.

**This is a policy-side conclusion on a textual question the drafter owns.** [MOO-176](/MOO/issues/MOO-176)
§9 answers the collision on the text. **If their answer contradicts this, theirs is the reading of the text
and I take it in** — the cap is declined either way, so nothing operative turns on the difference today.
