# MOO-169 — Counsel pass on store revision 61 (thirteen substitutions in § 51.1-142.4 G 4, D 1 and M 2)

**INTERNAL RISK ANALYSIS. NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** I am not counsel of record. Nothing in this document clears
`bill-text-s8` for introduction, and nothing here is a legal opinion the Lab or the Commonwealth may rely on.
Pre-decisional and internal.

**Verdict:** the cure to MOO-161 F1 works against the defect it was aimed at, and the citation premise behind the
deletion holds. But the *confinement* half of the cure creates a new forfeiture in a different class of member, and
the narrowed M 2 class removes review from two people who need it. **1 fatal, 9 serious, 9 moderate, 4 minor, and 12 null results.**

## 1. The strongest case against, in the opposition's voice

> *You have now written the same subdivision four times and you still cannot tell a state employee what her family
> gets if she dies. Look at what revision 61 actually does. It takes a sentence that protected every member who died
> after you took her money, and it shrinks that protection down to a member who dies inside a window the Board
> itself controls the opening of — after the Board has received a notification the member cannot sensibly file until
> the Board gives her a statement, and before a date the Board has the power to move. Outside that window the act is
> silent, and silence here means § 51.1-162 A: a refund of contributions. So the warehouse worker you lay off in
> June, the one your own bill gives ninety days to retire, is out of service, has not filed her notice because she is
> waiting for your statement, and dies in July — the Commonwealth settles her subsidy on the day she dies, pays it
> under subsection N, credits four years of military service to a dead woman's record, and hands her widow a check
> for her own contributions. That is the identical injury counsel found fatal in revision 58 and again in revision
> 45. You did not cure it. You moved it.*
>
> *And then you took away the remedy. In the same revision you created a conservator as the person who must be
> written to for a minor child, you struck from the reconsideration class every person who is only an addressee —
> which is exactly what a conservator is. You made standing turn on the Board having already decided against the
> claimant, so the Board can defeat review by never deciding. And because your own subsection M makes a case
> decision the product of reconsideration, the person you shut out of reconsideration can never get to a circuit
> court at all. Meanwhile you compel the Board, in writing, before it may take a member's money, to state six
> propositions of law. Three of them were wrong last revision. Three of them are wrong this revision — in a
> direction that tells her she has rights § 51.1-165 C 2 does not give her, and never tells her the one thing that
> decides whether her purchase is worth anything: who she named as her beneficiary.*

## 2. Findings

### FATAL

**F1 — E1's window withdraws the in-service waiver from the separated member who has not yet filed her § 51.1-153
notice, and gives her survivor a contributions refund.**

Exact words at fault (E1): *"where clause (i) of this subdivision applies **and the member dies after the Board has
received the notification of the member's effective date of retirement required by subdivision D 1 and before that
date**, the Board shall determine the benefit payable under this subdivision under § 51.1-162 as on the death of a
member in service … and for the purposes of § 51.1-162 the requirement that the member be in service shall not
apply."*

Authority, quoted:

- § 51.1-162 B: *"if a member dies **in service** … a retirement allowance shall be paid …"*
- § 51.1-162 A: *"If a member dies before retirement, and if no benefits are payable under subsection B, the amount
  of his accumulated contributions shall be paid …"*
- § 51.1-153 E: *"The effective date of retirement shall be **after the last day of service of the member**, but
  shall not be more than 90 days prior to the filing of the notice of retirement."*
- § 51.1-153 C: *"For the purposes of this subsection, any requirements as to the member being in service shall not
  apply"* — the Code treats a separated member as **not** in service, and **"in service" is nowhere defined in
  § 51.1-124.3**.
- The act's own words, G 4: *"If the member incurs an involuntary separation after the Board accepts the election …
  the service credit purchased under the election shall vest … if the member retires in a qualifying retirement no
  later than 90 days after the date of the involuntary separation"* — the act itself puts this member out of service
  for up to 90 days and more.
- D 1: *"**Nothing in this subdivision requires the member to file that notice any period before the effective date
  of retirement stated in it.**"* The act expressly tells her she need not file early.

Failure scenario. A VRS Plan 2 member, 58, buys 30 months of military credit for $21,400 under an accepted election;
the Commonwealth's certified subsidy is $148,000. Her agency abolishes her position on 30 June 2030 — an involuntary
separation. Under G 4 she has until 30 September 2030 to retire in a qualifying retirement, and her final qualifying
retirement date is extended accordingly. She has not yet received the Board's written statement of her final
qualifying retirement date, so she has not filed the § 51.1-153 notice; D 1 tells her she need not. She dies on 18
July 2030, survived by her husband. Clause (i) applies (she died after acceptance; she did not retire in a
non-qualifying retirement), so the credit vests, the Board credits 30 months, and the certified subsidy settles *"on
the earliest of (a) the date of the member's death"* — 18 July — whereupon subsection N obliges the Commonwealth to
pay $148,000 to the system. But E1's window never opened: the Board never received a notification of an effective
date of retirement. So the Board has no direction to determine the benefit under § 51.1-162 "as on the death of a
member in service," and the in-service requirement is **not** waived. She was not in service on 18 July. § 51.1-162 B
is therefore unavailable; § 51.1-162 A pays her husband her accumulated contributions. Her survivor's benefit is a
refund. At **store revision 58 and at store revision 60** the sentence was unconditional on notification and she
would have had the § 51.1-162 B allowance. **The cure took it from her.**

This is MOO-161 F1's injury — forfeiture of the death benefit the purchase was supposed to improve — relocated from
the retiree to the separated pre-filing member. Plaintiff: the husband, as beneficiary and as personal representative.
Remedy sought: mandamus/declaratory relief that § 51.1-162 B governs, after reconsideration under M 2, in the circuit
court identified under Rule 2A:2 (in practice the Circuit Court of the City of Richmond).

Why fatal and not serious: the class is defined by the act's own machinery, not by member error; the member cannot
act (she is dead); the Commonwealth pays the subsidy anyway; and the loss is the whole benefit the subdivision exists
to deliver.

Fix direction. Delete the notification window and condition the sentence on the member not having retired —
e.g. *"where clause (i) of this subdivision applies and the member dies before the effective date of any retirement
of the member"*. Clause (i) already excludes the member who retired in a non-qualifying retirement, and with
*"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply"* deleted the retiree case cannot come back: § 51.1-162 A and B both require death
before retirement or in service, so the in-service waiver alone cannot reach a retiree.

### SERIOUS

**F2 — The window is keyed to a notification *"required by subdivision D 1"*, which imports D 1's timing; D 1
expressly contemplates a late-filed notice, and § 51.1-165 C 1's trigger is receipt of *any* written notification.**

Exact words: E1's *"the notification of the member's effective date of retirement **required by subdivision D 1**"*,
read against D 1's *"A notice filed within the period allowed by this subdivision satisfies § 51.1-153
notwithstanding that it is filed after the effective date of retirement stated in it."*

Authority: § 51.1-165 C 1: *"in all cases where the death of the member occurs prior to the effective date of
retirement but after the Board has received **written notification of the member's effective date of retirement**,
benefits shall be paid in accordance with the provisions of § 51.1-163 and the requirement that the member be in
service shall not apply."*

Failure scenario. The member files her notice 41 days after the Board's statement — 11 days late — stating an
effective date of 1 November. She dies 20 October. The Board takes the view that the notification she filed was not
the one "required by subdivision D 1" (it was outside D 1's period), so E1's window did not open and the
notwithstanding limb never engaged. § 51.1-165 C 1's third sentence then governs of its own force, because the Board
*did* receive written notification: benefits are paid in accordance with § 51.1-163, i.e. a refund of the excess of
accumulated contributions. The same facts, with a timely notice, produce a § 51.1-162 B allowance. An 11-day filing
slip costs the family the allowance — and D 1 does not say a late notice is ineffective; it says a timely one is
effective notwithstanding retroactivity, which invites exactly this inference.

Fix direction. Use the Code's own trigger and drop the cross-reference: *"after the Board has received written
notification of the member's effective date of retirement."*

**F3 — E12 makes standing turn on the Board having acted, so the Board can defeat review by not acting; and because
M 2 makes the case decision the product of reconsideration, exclusion from reconsideration forecloses the circuit
court.**

Exact words (E12): *"person who claimed any benefit payable on the death of a member **and whom the Board determined
is not entitled to it**, or employer that paid on a member's behalf."*

Authority: § 51.1-124.3: *"'Beneficiary' means **any person entitled to receive benefits** under this chapter."*
M 2: *"Upon completion of a reconsideration under this subsection, the final determination issued by the Board shall
constitute a case decision as defined in § 2.2-4001."* § 2.2-4026 A: *"Any person affected by and claiming the
unlawfulness of any regulation or **party aggrieved by and claiming unlawfulness of a case decision** … shall have a
right to the direct review thereof."*

Two holes, both concrete:

(a) *Claimed, never determined.* The decedent's adult daughter writes to the Board claiming the death benefit as
designated beneficiary. The Board never determines her claim. Her ground is M 2's own failure limb (*"a failure of
the Board to make a determination … within a period required by this section"*), but she is not a "beneficiary"
(she is not in fact entitled if her claim is bad — the very question in dispute), and she is not a person "whom the
Board **determined** is not entitled," because the Board determined nothing. She is outside the class. The limb that
exists for Board inaction is unavailable to the person Board inaction hurts.

(b) *Determined not entitled, never claimed.* The Board identifies the surviving spouse itself and writes to her
under G 4 as a person entitled; it later determines, on the agency's record, that the marriage was void and she is
not entitled. She never "claimed" anything — the Board volunteered her — so she does not satisfy the claimed-and-
denied limb, and she is not a "beneficiary" because the determination says she is not entitled.

Both are then shut out of court as well, not by E13 but by the architecture: no reconsideration means no case
decision, and § 2.2-4026 A gives review only of a case decision.

Fix direction. Drop the adverse-determination qualifier — *"person who has claimed any benefit payable on the death
of a member"* — and add *"or person to whom this section requires a notice to be given in respect of a benefit
payable on the death of a member."*

**F4 — E12/E13 strip the fiduciary addressee that E2 created in the same revision.**

Exact words (E2): *"to each person entitled to any benefit payable on the member's death or, where that person is a
minor or is under a disability, **to that person's guardian, conservator, committee, or other fiduciary**"*, read
against E12's replacement of *"other person to whom this section requires a notice to be given"*.

Failure scenario. The decedent's 9-year-old son is entitled under § 51.1-162 B. His court-appointed conservator
receives the G 4 notice on 1 March and files a written request for reconsideration in her own name on 20 March,
disputing the computation. The Board rejects the request because a conservator is none of: member, retiree,
beneficiary (she is not entitled in her own right), applicant, personal representative of an estate, denied
claimant, or employer that paid. The 30 days run out on 31 March. At store revision 60 she was squarely in the class
as a person to whom the section required notice. The revision that made her the addressee is the revision that took
away her standing to act on what she was sent.

Fix direction. Add to both class lists *"or fiduciary of, or person to whom this section requires a notice to be
given in respect of, a person in any of the foregoing classes."*

**F5 — E10's deferred period leaves the living member no workable period, and collides with M 2's second sentence,
which still deems the certification a determination.**

Exact words (E10): *"is a failure of the Board for the purposes of subsection M; a person adversely affected by it
may seek review under subsection M **no later than 30 days after the date on which the notice required by
subdivision G 4 is given to that person**."* G 4's notice duty opens *"**Where the member dies**, the Board shall
give written notice …"*.

Authority: M 2, second sentence (unamended): *"Every determination, **certification**, redetermination,
recertification, … under this section is a determination of the Board for the purposes of this subsection, whether or
not another provision of this section expressly provides for review of it."*

Failure scenario (the living member). The Board's certification omits the § 51.1-165 C 2 proposition. The member
notices the omission three months later, after paying $19,000, and files for reconsideration. Under E10 her period
has not begun and never will — she is alive, so the G 4 death notice will never be given. Under M 2's second
sentence the certification is a determination and her 30 days expired long ago. Two windows, inconsistent, no
priority rule; the Board may rely on whichever defeats her. At store revision 60 the certification was a
determination with a clock that ran from the writing she actually received. **The person most adversely affected by
an incomplete certification is the living member, and E10 gives her nothing.**

Fix direction. Give the member an express period running from the giving of the certification *or* from the date she
files written evidence of the omission, whichever is later, and state which of the two characterisations governs.

**F6 — E11's third window is empty almost everywhere it applies.**

Exact words (E11): *"or, in the case of a failure to state a matter, **within the period provided by the provision
that requires the matter to be stated**."*

Only one provision in §1 now provides such a period: D 1, via E10. Subsection C's statement duties, subdivision D 2's
over-cap notice, the definition of *"final qualifying retirement date"*, G 4's death notice and rescission notice,
and the G 4 reinstatement notices provide **no** period for seeking review of a matter **omitted** from them. G 4's
own rider is expressly about a determination *stated* in the notice: *"the period within which such a person may seek
review under subsection M of a determination **stated in that notice** runs from the date the notice is given."*

Failure scenario. The G 4 death notice omits *"the computation of it"* — the computation of the survivor's benefit.
The survivor has a ground (E11's new limb) and no window: the provision that requires the computation to be stated
provides no period. The Board treats the request as untimely for want of any period; the survivor argues the period
is unlimited. Nobody can say which is right from the text.

Fix direction. Make limb three read *"within 30 days after the certification or notice is given or, where the
provision that requires the matter to be stated provides a longer or later period, within that period."*

**F7 — E6's restoration is keyed to payment under subsection N, and subsection N has no deadline. Whether the
$3,000,000 headroom comes back is therefore an unregulated administrative choice.**

Exact words (E6): *"shall release the amount settled under this subdivision and shall restore it to the amount
available under the limitation stated in the first sentence of subdivision D 2 **only to the extent the amount
settled has not been paid to the retirement system under subsection N**."*

Authority: subsection N: *"**Upon the settlement** of a certified subsidy under subdivision G 1 or G 4, the
Commonwealth shall pay the amount settled to the retirement system from an appropriation made for that purpose"* —
no period, no deadline, no actor named for the timing. G 4 settles *"on the earliest of (a) the date of the member's
death"*, while the amount is not redetermined until up to 90 days after the Board **learns** of the death (E5), and
the survivor's election under § 51.1-162 B may come later still.

Failure scenario. Two identical deaths. In the first, the Commonwealth pays under N before the survivor elects
contributions in lieu; nothing is released, and $148,000 stays charged against the cap forever for a benefit nobody
received. In the second, the payment has not gone out; $148,000 is released and restored. The next applicant on the
D 5 waiting list is admitted in the second case and refused in the first. She is an "applicant" and so may seek
review under M 2 — of a decision for which the act supplies no standard.

Fix direction. Either bar payment under subsection N until the Board has determined whether an allowance is payable
under § 51.1-162, or require the system to repay the Commonwealth and restore the cap where the settled amount has
already been paid.

**F8 — E6's reinstatement does not recharge the reinstated amount against subdivision D 2 and does not trigger the
excess machinery, so commitments can exceed $3,000,000 silently.**

Exact words (E6, second sentence): *"the Board shall reinstate the amount released, together with any contingency
amount released with it, no later than 30 days after the determination is reconsidered or reviewed, and the rule of
this subdivision that no certified subsidy is settled more than once does not prevent the settlement of an amount so
reinstated."*

Contrast G 4's general reinstatement sentence, which does what E6's does not: *"the Board shall reinstate it,
together with the contingency amount released with it, … and **the reinstated reservation and contingency amount
shall be charged against the limitations in subdivision D 2 notwithstanding that the amount available to be reserved
is thereby exceeded**"*, followed by notice to the Chairmen, notice to every waiting-list applicant, a confirmation
request, certification of the excess to the Governor, and *"shall accept no further election until the excess is
eliminated."* E6's release is of a **settled amount**, not of a reservation, so none of that machinery is engaged.

Failure scenario. The Board determines no allowance is payable, releases $148,000, restores it to the cap, and admits
the next waiting-list applicant on that headroom. On reconsideration the spouse establishes entitlement. E6 requires
the $148,000 to be reinstated and settled — but nothing recharges it against the first-sentence limitation, no excess
is declared, the Chairmen are not told, waiting-list applicants are not told, and elections keep being accepted.
Aggregate commitments stand at $3,148,000 under a $3,000,000 bar that D 2 says is *"self-executing."*

Fix direction. In E6's second sentence, adopt the general sentence's charging rule and excess consequences by
reference.

**F9 — E7 misstates § 51.1-165 C 2 by dropping its identity condition and by adding a payee rule C 2 does not
contain. This is a proposition of law the Board must state in writing before it may accept the member's money.**

Exact words (E7): *"that where the member dies while in service and subdivision C 2 of § 51.1-165 otherwise applies,
that subdivision provides the greater of the benefit payable in accordance with § 51.1-163 and the benefit payable
under subsection B of § 51.1-162, **each payable to the person entitled to it under the provision under which it is
determined**."*

Authority — § 51.1-165 C 2, in full on the point: *"then benefits shall be paid in accordance with the provisions of
§ 51.1-163. **However, if such contingent annuitant or annuitants are the same person or persons who would receive a
monthly benefit under subsection B of § 51.1-162** and the monthly benefit under such subsection would be greater
than the benefit provided in accordance with the provisions of § 51.1-163, then retirement benefits shall be paid in
accordance with the provisions of subsection B of § 51.1-162."*

C 2 does **not** provide a greater-of at large. It provides § 51.1-163, and switches to § 51.1-162 B **only where the
contingent annuitant and the § 51.1-162 B payee are the same person or persons.** Where they differ, C 2 pays under
§ 51.1-163 alone — so the words *"each payable to the person entitled to it under the provision under which it is
determined"* describe a mechanism the Code does not have, and could not have, because the comparison exists only
when the payees coincide.

Failure scenario. The member elects a joint and last-survivor option naming her 24-year-old daughter as contingent
annuitant (permissible where § 51.1-165 A 2's actuarial test is met) and is survived by her husband, who would take
under § 51.1-162 B. She dies in service after notification. The certification told her the greater of the two
benefits is payable "to the person entitled to it under the provision under which it is determined," so she
understood that her husband would receive the § 51.1-162 B allowance if it were larger. Under C 2 the however-clause
does not apply, and C 2 pays under § 51.1-163 to her designated beneficiary. (The act's own greater-of in G 4 may
reach the § 51.1-162 B figure for her — see F3/F10 and the carried finding below — but the writing the Board gave her
described the Code wrongly, and she made an irrevocable option election on it.)

Fix direction. Restate the proposition with C 2's identity condition, and separate the Code's rule from the act's
greater-of in G 4.

**F10 — The § 51.1-162 B proposition (not restated this revision) omits the one fact that decides whether the
purchase buys anything: the designation of beneficiary.**

Exact words (D 1, proposition four): *"that subsection B of § 51.1-162 provides a retirement allowance on the death
of a member in service **only where the member is survived by a spouse, a minor child or a parent** and no benefit is
payable under subsection C of that section."*

Authority — § 51.1-162 B: *"a retirement allowance shall be paid **to the person or persons designated as provided
in subsection A of this section if the person is the member's (i) surviving spouse, (ii) minor child, or (iii)
parent(s)**. **If no designation has been made, or if the death of the designated person occurs prior to the death
of the member and another designation has not been made**, a retirement allowance shall be paid in the following
order of precedence …"* And § 51.1-162 B opens *"**To the extent required by § 401(a)(37) of the Internal Revenue
Code** … if a member dies in service …"*.

Survival by a qualifying relative is necessary but **not sufficient**. If the member has designated a living person
who is not a spouse, minor child or parent, B's first sentence does not pay (the designee does not qualify) and B's
second sentence does not engage (a designation has been made and the designee is alive). No allowance is payable
under B at all; § 51.1-162 A pays the accumulated contributions to the designee.

Failure scenario. The member designated her sister in 2009 and never changed it. She buys 36 months of credit for
$26,000 with a $160,000 certified subsidy, dies inside E1's window, and is survived by her mother. The certification
told her § 51.1-162 B provides an allowance where the member is survived by a parent. No allowance is payable: her
sister takes the accumulated contributions under § 51.1-162 A. The purchased credit and the Commonwealth's subsidy
produce nothing, and the certification is what led her to believe otherwise. The act never requires the Board to tell
her to review her designation — the single act that would have made the cure work for her.

Fix direction. Add to the proposition the words *"to a person so surviving whom the member has designated under
subsection A of that section, or, where the member has made no designation or the designated person has
predeceased the member, in the order of precedence that subsection provides"*, and add a compelled statement
advising the member to review her designation of beneficiary before completing payment.

### MODERATE

**M1 — E5's *"that date"* has no referent where the subsidy was settled under subdivision G 1.** E5: *"The Board
shall, no later than 90 days after **that date** or, where that date is the date of the member's death, no later than
90 days after the date on which the Board learns of the death, redetermine …"*. *"That date"* comes from the
preceding sentence, which operates only on an election *"that is **not settled under subdivision 1**"*. A member who
files a retroactive notice under D 1 (effective date 1 April, filed 1 June) retires in a qualifying retirement, so
the subsidy settles under G 1 — and if she dies on 15 June, clause (i) still applies and G 4's redetermination duty
still speaks, with no date to run from. Fix: say *"after the date on which the certified subsidy is settled under
subdivision 1 or under this subdivision, as the case may be."*

**M2 — E6's *"together with any contingency amount released with it"* is a stranded reference.** E6's first sentence
releases only *"the amount settled"*; contingency amounts are released by G 4's redetermination sentence and by
subdivision D 7, not by E6. Nothing is ever "released with" the settled amount under the preceding sentence. Fix:
point the words at the release that actually carries a contingency amount, or delete them.

**M3 — E6's release has no *"to the extent not already released"* rider and can be required twice of the same
amount.** The two limbs are *"no later than 60 days after the election"* and *"where no such allowance is payable to
any person, no later than 60 days after the Board determines that no such allowance is payable."* Sequence: the Board
determines no allowance is payable and releases; on reconsideration a spouse establishes entitlement (E6 reinstates);
the spouse then elects contributions in lieu (limb one fires again on an amount already released once). Fix: add
*"to the extent not already released and not reinstated."*

**M4 — E2 overstates both § 51.1-162 rights it compels the Board to describe.** E2 requires notice *"of any right
under subsection C of § 51.1-162 **of a person entitled to any benefit payable on the member's death** to waive the
allowance."* § 51.1-162 C gives the waiver only to *"**Any beneficiary entitled to the entire amount of a retirement
allowance** under the provisions of this subsection … in order to make available a retirement allowance under the
provisions of subsection B of this section."* Where two minor children share the C allowance, neither is entitled to
the entire amount and neither may waive — yet the Board must tell both they may. The same defect in the next clause:
the § 51.1-162 B election belongs to the person to whom the B allowance is payable (*"If the person elects in
writing…"*), not to *"such a person"* read back to *"a person entitled to any benefit."* Fix: track the Code's
qualifiers.

**M5 — D 1's first proposition tells the member more than the act does about § 51.1-165 C 1 and § 51.1-165.01 F.**
Exact words: *"the benefit payable on the member's death is determined under subdivision G 4 and under § 51.1-162 as
on the death of a member in service and **not under subdivision C 1 of § 51.1-165 or under subsection F of
§ 51.1-165.01**."* The notwithstanding displaces only C 1 **so far as it sends benefits to § 51.1-163** and *"so much
of subsection F of § 51.1-165.01 as requires benefits to be paid in accordance with § 51.1-163."* Both provisions
continue to operate in their first sentences: § 51.1-165 C 1: *"The election of any one of the options stated in this
section shall be null and void if the member dies prior to the Board receiving written notification of the member's
effective date of retirement"*; § 51.1-165.01 F: *"The election of any one of the options stated in this section
shall be void if the member dies prior to the Board receiving written notification …"*. A member reading the
certification may believe her option election and her partial lump-sum election survive her death. They do not. Fix:
*"not under the provisions of subdivision C 1 of § 51.1-165 or subsection F of § 51.1-165.01 that require benefits to
be paid in accordance with § 51.1-163."*

**M6 — E8 omits § 51.1-162 C's in-service condition, and the omission bites in exactly the class of F1.** E8 states
C *"provides a retirement allowance, where the member's death results from a cause compensable under the Virginia
Workers' Compensation Act …"*. The Code: *"If a member dies **in service** from a cause compensable under the
Virginia Workers' Compensation Act …"*. For the separated member of F1 — the one outside E1's window and so without
the in-service waiver — C does not apply at all, and the certification says it does. Fix: add *"in service"*.

**M7 — E4's agency death-notice duty has no consequence, and its report trigger presupposes the knowledge the notice
was meant to create.** Exact words: *"A state agency that employs or last employed a member whose election the Board
has accepted shall give the Board written notice of the member's death no later than 30 days after the agency learns
of it, and the Board shall state every failure of a state agency to give that notice **of which the Board becomes
aware** in the report required by subsection J."* If the agency never reports, the Board does not learn of the death,
so it never becomes aware of the failure, so nothing is reported — the loop closes on itself. And the duty runs on
*"the agency that employs or last employed"*: a member who left state service for a private employer may have a last
state agency that never hears she died. Fix: add an independent duty on the Board to identify deaths from vital
records or a death-master-file match at stated intervals, and deem the Board to have learned of the death on the
date of a match.

**M8 — the tolling exclusion in M 2 is an uncapped channel, operated by the Board rather than the member.** M 2:
*"the aggregate of all tolling under this subsection shall not exceed 120 days, **excluding any period after the
expiration of the 90 days allowed by this subsection and before the Board acts upon the request**."* The 120-day cap
is real as to the member (see the F16 note in §5 below), but every day of Board delay past 90 days tolls without
limit, extending the member's final qualifying retirement date and keeping the certified subsidy charged against the
cap for as long as the Board does not act. Fix: cap the excluded period too, or make the member's final qualifying
retirement date run again once the 90 days expire.

**M9 — E13's narrowing of the judicial-review class is either inert or an implied repeal.** § 2.2-4026 A grants
direct review to any *"party aggrieved by and claiming unlawfulness of a case decision"*, and the act disapplies only
Articles 3 and 4 and the deemed-approval sentences of § 2.2-4021 B and C (M 3). So E13 either adds nothing (the APA
right survives) or silently withdraws an APA right from aggrieved parties, which would need express words. Either way
the operative narrowing is E12's, through the exhaustion route in F3. Fix: state expressly that the enumeration does
not limit § 2.2-4026.

## 4. The load-bearing assumption, tested

The issue states *"No figure moves, and nothing needs re-running."* **No runnable model was provided with this pass**,
so I tested the assumption analytically rather than rerunning anything, and I say so plainly rather than implying a
model run.

**The load-bearing assumption is that a certified subsidy which buys no benefit comes back to the $3,000,000 cap.**
E6 was written to make that true. It is true only *"to the extent the amount settled has not been paid to the
retirement system under subsection N"*, and subsection N fixes no time for payment while G 4 settles the subsidy
**on the date of death**.

Test. Take the figures the cap itself implies: a $3,000,000 aggregate limitation and a $170,000 per-election ceiling
(D 2), so an admitted cohort of roughly 17 to 18 elections at the ceiling and more at the average. Suppose one
election in the cohort ends in a death where no § 51.1-162 allowance is payable (F1's class, or F10's
non-qualifying-designation class).

- *If N is paid after the Board determines no allowance is payable* — the assumption holds: the amount is released,
  the cap is restored, and the next waiting-list applicant is admitted. No figure moves.
- *If N is paid on or shortly after settlement* — which is what subsection N's own words direct (*"Upon the
  settlement"*) — **nothing is released and nothing is restored.** The cap carries a permanent charge for a benefit
  nobody received. At the $170,000 ceiling, each such death permanently removes one election from the cohort, a
  **5.6% to 5.9% reduction in admissions per occurrence**; at a $148,000 average charge, roughly one admission per
  occurrence as well. The program's admitted cohort — the thing every figure in the package prices — shrinks by one
  member for each of these deaths, and nothing in the act or in the J report flags it as a reduction in capacity
  rather than a use of capacity.

So the figures do not move only on an assumption about payment timing that the act leaves entirely unregulated. That
is F7. It is not a modelling error; it is a drafting gap with a fiscal consequence, and the party who pays for it is
the next applicant on the D 5 waiting list, who appears in no stakeholder map I was given.

## 5. The carded findings: which became more urgent, and the two declinations

**F3 (the greater-of's measure and payee) — materially more urgent, and I would not let E7 stand without it.**
Answering the Director's question 3 directly: the greater-of is now **performable in form** — with
*"§ 51.1-163 shall not apply"* deleted, C 2 can produce a benefit, so MOO-161 F2's objection is answered. But:

1. In the central case the comparison is **vacuous**: where C 2's however-clause applies, C 2 pays § 51.1-162 B, and
   the purchased credit is already credited under G 4, so C 2's figure and the G 4 figure are the same number.
2. In the **only** case where the comparison bites — where C 2 pays under § 51.1-163 — the two sides are a lump sum
   and a monthly allowance, **payable to different people**, with no measure and no payee rule. Worse, § 51.1-163's
   own measure has no content in a pre-effective-date death: *"any excess of his accumulated contributions **as of the
   effective date of his retirement**, over the total retirement allowances received by him"*, where that effective
   date never arrived.
3. E7 now **compels the Board to state this greater-of in writing to every applicant before it may accept her
   money**, and E12 now defines what remedy the losing claimant has. The act makes a promise in a form it cannot
   perform.

My recommendation to the Director: either supply the measure and the payee rule in the same revision as E7, or delete
the G 4 greater-of and provide instead that where § 51.1-165 C 2 applies the Board pays the § 51.1-162 B benefit
computed on the member's creditable service including the purchased credit — which is what C 2's own
however-clause prefers whenever the payees coincide, and the only case the greater-of adds is the one that cannot be
computed.

**F7 (the § 51.1-162 C waiver period) — more urgent, and now a contradiction on the face of the act rather than a
gap.** E2 compels the Board to state the C waiver right *"and … the period stated in that subsection within which the
waiver must be made"* — 90 days **from the death**. E4 gives the agency 30 days from when it learns; G 4 gives the
Board 60 days from when **it** learns to credit; E2 gives it 30 days after that to give the notice; E5 runs the
redetermination from when the Board learns. **Every clock in the revision runs from the Board's knowledge; § 51.1-162
C's runs from the death.** On the act's own deadlines the notice of a 90-day right can be given 120 days or more
after the death, and § 51.1-162 is not amended by this act. The act now guarantees in its own text that it can
destroy the right it compels the Board to describe.

**F10 (sister systems), F11's express disclosure, F14's supplemental notice, M 2's lateness-after-cure** — no change
in urgency from what was enacted. Not re-reported.

**F16's declination (the tolling lever) — right on its own terms, with one qualification.** M 2 does cap the member's
lever: *"(i) no second or subsequent request for reconsideration of the same determination or of the same failure …
tolls any period"*, and *"(ii) the aggregate of all tolling under this subsection shall not exceed 120 days."* Note
that (i) bars repeats of the *same* determination only, and M 2's second sentence now makes a long list of acts
reviewable determinations, so serial requests on *different* determinations do toll — but the 120-day aggregate holds
them. The declination stands. The uncapped channel that remains is **Board-operated**, not member-operated, and I
report it as M8 rather than as a revival of F16.

**F21's declination (the § 51.1-142.2 asymmetry) — the stated ground no longer holds.** The declination rests on the
premise that curing F21 *"widens the class this revision narrows."* F3 and F4 show the narrowing overshot in two
places. The two should be decided together: if E12 is repaired as F3 and F4 suggest, the ground for declining F21
disappears. I am not re-reporting F21; I am saying the reason given for declining it has been undermined by the
revision it was declined in favour of.

## 6. What I checked and found sound — null results

- **N1. The deletion is safe against the deeming routes.** Three provisions, and only three, deem § 51.1-163 to reach
  a pre-effective-date death. Two are displaced by the notwithstanding; the third (§ 51.1-165 C 2) is deliberately
  preserved and expressly protected by G 4. **I looked for a fourth across all 97 sections of Chapter 1 and there
  is none.**
- **N2. No route through §§ 51.1-207 / 51.1-218.** Closed by the exclusion of Chapter 2, 2.1 and 3 members from
  *"eligible member."*
- **N3. No separate route through § 51.1-165.01 C 2.** Subsection F governs every C option, and the displacement is
  cast at F, not at C.
- **N4. § 51.1-163 cannot reach the pre-effective-date death of its own force.** MOO-161's N1 confirmed on the live
  text. The premise of the cure is correct, and deleting the disapplication does **not** resurrect § 51.1-163 for a
  member inside the window.
- **N5. The never-filed member who dies *in service* is not harmed by the narrowing.** § 51.1-165 C 1's first
  sentence voids her option, § 51.1-165.01 F's first sentence voids her lump-sum election, § 51.1-162 then applies of
  its own force, and G 4 still credits the purchased service. The harm in F1 is confined to the member who is **out
  of service** and has not filed.
- **N6. The cancelling member (G 5) does not break the settlement rules.** D 7 releases the reservation on
  cancellation *"in accordance with subdivision D 7"*, and G 4 bars settlement *"after the reservation for the
  election has been released in full in accordance with subdivision D 7 and has not been reinstated."* No
  double-settlement, no stranded subsidy.
- **N7. E6 cannot reinstate an amount never released, and cannot restore to a limitation never charged.** Sentence
  two is expressly conditioned on *"an amount released under the preceding sentence"*, and D 2 keeps the settled
  amount charged: *"the amount settled shall remain charged against the limitation in the first sentence of this
  subdivision."* The collision with *"no certified subsidy is settled more than once"* is expressly handled by E6's
  closing words. Three of the Director's four arithmetic questions come back clean; the fourth is M3.
- **N8. E3 is sound.** The per-person reading of the J-report hook now matches the per-person notice duty E2 creates.
- **N9. E2's split of the waiver from the election is correct on the periods.** § 51.1-162 C states 90 days;
  § 51.1-162 B states no period. Rev 60's conditional rider (*"where § 51.1-162 states a period"*) was the right
  diagnosis and the split is the right cure.
- **N10. All thirteen substitutions are in the live document, and the replaced words are gone from §1.**
- **N11. The G 4 greater-of is performable in form.** Its inputs both exist now. Its defects are measure and payee
  (F3, carded), not impossibility.
- **N12. No constitutional tripwire found in these thirteen substitutions.** I looked for single-object, special
  legislation, delegation without standards, retroactivity, takings and compelled speech. Subsection M 1's rulemaking
  is bounded by an enumerated subject list; subsection L keeps the scheme off localities, so Dillon's Rule is not
  engaged; the Program is prospective and voluntary. The only constitutional-adjacent exposure is the due process
  question lurking in F3 and M9 — a person with a claimed entitlement to a death benefit who is given no path to a
  case decision — and I state it as exposure, not as a conclusion.
