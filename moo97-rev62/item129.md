129. **NEW IN STORE REVISION 62, from [MOO-169](/MOO/issues/MOO-169) — seven answers drafted in full and enacted nowhere.** Each is one substitution against the live text of store revision 62 and each is drafted word for word at [`moo169-disposition`](/MOO/issues/MOO-97#document-moo169-disposition) §7. **Four of the seven decide who bears a loss and I have picked none.** Items 60, 106, 107, 126 and 127, and the five unenacted answers at item 122, are unaffected and stay open.
    - **(1) F7 — subsection N fixes no time for payment, so whether a subsidy that buys no benefit comes back to the $3,000,000 cap is an unregulated administrative choice. Serious, and the reviewer's §4 shows it is the load-bearing fiscal assumption of the whole package.** Subdivision G 4 settles the subsidy *"on … the date of the member's death"*; subsection N says the Commonwealth shall pay *"upon the settlement"*; the release enacted in store revision 61 operates *"only to the extent the amount settled has not been paid."* So on subsection N's own words **nothing is ever released**, the cap carries a permanent charge for a benefit nobody received, and at the $170,000 per-election ceiling each such death **permanently removes one election from the admitted cohort — a 5.6 to 5.9 percent reduction in admissions per occurrence.** Two forms, alternatives. **(a) Bar the payment until the benefit is known**: *"The Commonwealth shall not pay an amount settled under subdivision G 4 on the death of a member before the Board has determined whether a retirement allowance is payable under § 51.1-162 on the member's death and, where such an allowance is payable, whether the person entitled to it elects under § 51.1-162 to be paid the member's accumulated contributions or a lump sum in lieu of it."* **(b) Repay and restore**: *"Where an amount settled under subdivision G 4 has been paid to the retirement system under this subsection and no retirement allowance is payable under § 51.1-162 on the member's death, the retirement system shall repay that amount to the Commonwealth no later than 60 days after the Board determines that no such allowance is payable, and the Board shall restore it to the amount available under the limitation stated in the first sentence of subdivision D 2."* **(a) delays the Commonwealth's cash and keeps the money out of the trust fund; (b) takes money back out of the trust fund after settlement, which is what [MOO-96](/MOO/issues/MOO-96)'s F-3 and Va. Const. art. X, § 11 bear on.** Routed to [@Fiscal & Impact Modeler](agent://428b53bf-f49e-4505-a607-3560a26b3d50) as the cohort question, not as a new figure.
    - **(2) The measure you approved has one incomputable side, and the cure is one clause.** f3-a compares the two benefits *"on the basis of actuarial equivalence as of the date of the member's death."* One side is the § 51.1-163 benefit, and § 51.1-163 measures *"any excess of his accumulated contributions **as of the effective date of his retirement**, over the total retirement allowances received by him"* — **in a pre-effective-date death that date never arrived.** Drafted, in the same sentence: *"and for the purposes of that comparison the benefit payable in accordance with § 51.1-163 shall be determined as though the effective date of the member's retirement were the date of death."* **It fixes an amount, which is why it is not enacted.** The alternative that dissolves the problem instead of solving it is **f3-c**, which you did not select: confine the greater-of to the case where § 51.1-165 C 2 itself pays under § 51.1-162 B, in which case the comparison is between two figures of the same kind. **f3-c takes a right from the designated beneficiary; answer (2) does not.**
    - **(3) F5's discovery rule — the living member's 30 days run from a certification she cannot yet know is incomplete.** Store revision 62 removed the contradiction: there is now one window, and it runs from the certification she actually received. **It did not make the window useful to her**, which was the reviewer's point. Drafted, in subdivision D 1: *"or, where the person adversely affected is the member, no later than 30 days after the date on which the member files with the Board written evidence of the omission, whichever is later."* **It is an open-ended limitation period and that is a policy setting, not the resolution of a contradiction.**
    - **(4) F10's designation-review advisory — the one compelled statement that would make the purchase work for the member who is about to lose it.** § 51.1-162 B pays a **designated** spouse, minor child or parent. A member who designated her sister in 2009, buys 36 months for $26,000 with a $160,000 certified subsidy and is survived by her mother gets **nothing** from the purchase, and nothing in the act tells her to look. Store revision 62 corrects the proposition so it no longer misleads her; it does not advise her. Drafted, appended to that proposition: *"and that the member should review the member's designation of beneficiary under subsection A of § 51.1-162 before completing payment."* **It is a new compelled statement and was not agreed.** It is also, on the reviewer's analysis, the cheapest thing on this card.
    - **(5) M7 — the agency death-notice duty closes on itself.** The Board must report every agency failure *"of which the Board becomes aware"*; if the agency never reports, the Board never learns of the death, so it never becomes aware of the failure, so nothing is reported. And the duty runs on *"the agency that employs or last employed"*, so a member who left state service for a private employer may have a last state agency that never hears she died. Drafted, in subdivision G 4: *"The Board shall, no less often than quarterly, compare the records of members whose elections it has accepted against the records of deaths maintained by the State Registrar of Vital Records and against any index of deaths maintained by an agency of the United States to which the Board has access, and the Board is deemed to have learned of a member's death on the date of a match."* **It is a new duty with an administrative cost, and the vital-records citation must come from [@Statutory Researcher](agent://d2a2feeb-989e-40ec-9cf6-496e7633daa6) before it is drafted into the act** — I have not cited a section I did not read.
    - **(6) M8 — the 120-day tolling cap binds the member and not the Board.** Subdivision M 2 caps aggregate tolling at 120 days *"excluding any period after the expiration of the 90 days allowed by this subsection and before the Board acts upon the request."* **Every day of Board delay past 90 days tolls without limit**, extending the member's final qualifying retirement date and keeping the certified subsidy charged against the cap for as long as the Board does not act. The reviewer is careful that this is **Board-operated, not a revival of the member's lever**, which [MOO-154](/MOO/issues/MOO-154)'s F16 declination correctly closed. Two forms, alternatives. **(a) Cap the excluded period**: *", provided that the aggregate of all periods so excluded shall not exceed 90 days"*. **(b) Restart the clock**: *"and the running of the member's final qualifying retirement date resumes upon the expiration of the 90 days allowed by this subsection notwithstanding that the Board has not acted upon the request."* **(b) is the stronger discipline and it can cost the member her qualifying retirement through no fault of her own; (a) cannot.**
    - **(7) The conforming amendment to § 51.1-162 C — and it changes the act's footprint.** f7-toll, enacted in this revision, provides that *"the period stated in subsection C of § 51.1-162 within which a waiver must be made does not expire before the thirtieth day after the notice is given."* **That is the act's first operative override of a period stated in a section this act does not amend**, and a reader of § 51.1-162 C will not learn of it from § 51.1-162 C. The conforming amendment is one clause — *"except as subdivision G 4 of § 51.1-142.4 provides"* — but drafting it **adds § 51.1-162 to the amended sections, changes the title line and changes the first enactment clause.** The act has amended one section and added one since revision 21 removed § 51.1-155 on your direction, and widening that is yours, not mine. **Not drafted.** Either way the override must travel in the drafting request to Division of Legislative Services, because a drafter's note is not enacted.
