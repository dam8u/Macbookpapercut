The denominator

  • 81,274 active participants in the VRS State Employees plan; average annual compensation $77,215; average age 46.9; average total service 11.0 — VRS Annual Actuarial Valuation, June 30, 2025, pp. 49–50.
  • Plan-tier split derived from the published age × total-service table using statutory boundaries (Plan 1 ≥ 15.0 years of service, Plan 2 11.5–15.0, Hybrid < 11.5): Plan 1 20,872 · Plan 2 8,122 · modeling denominator 28,994. Hybrid 52,280 is excluded by G15.
  • Board decision, 12 Oct 2026: 177,807 stays as the system-wide context figure only. It appears nowhere in this arithmetic.

Pathway pools, computed cell by cell from that table

  • Incentive — 6,254 already eligible for unreduced retirement (Plan 1 5,928 at $84,616 · Plan 2 326 at $87,242).
  • Bridge — 2,396 can reach the threshold with ≤ 48 months purchased (Plan 1 only, $83,222, average 24.0 months needed).
  • G9's "within 4 years of the threshold" screen gives 5,177 — 2.2× too many, because purchased service adds service, not age. A member whose only path is Plan 1's age-65 rule or Plan 2's age-67 rule cannot be bridged at any price. Decision 3, 12 Oct 2026: the purchasable screen is adopted; 5,177 is retired as a Bridge figure and kept here only so the difference can be audited.
  • Plan 2 Bridge participation is structurally zero. A Plan 2 member holds at most 15.5 years of service, so Rule of 90 needs age 74.5 — and anyone that old already qualified at 67 under the age rule, which puts them in the Incentive pathway. Decision 5, 12 Oct 2026: confirmed. Plan 2 stays legally eligible for the Bridge and is scored at zero for the whole pilot, and the Plan 2 proximity assumptions of 12% and 13% are withdrawn in favour of the derived 4.0% and 6.7%.

Prices and benefit rules

  • Approximate normal cost, State/Teacher/Local Non-Hazardous Duty, effective 1 July 2025: Plan 1 12.50% of salary, Plan 2 10.74% (Hybrid 6.68%; hazardous duty 23.78% / 19.15%; judicial 31.97% / 29.67%). Charged on creditable compensation or AFC at purchase, whichever is higher — VRS Purchase of Prior Service Cost Rates.
  • Plan 1 unreduced: "age 65 with at least five years of service credit or at age 50 with at least 30 years"; multiplier 1.7%; AFC = highest 36 consecutive months — Plan 1 Handbook.
  • Plan 2 unreduced: "normal Social Security retirement age with at least five years of service credit or when your age and service credit equal 90"; multiplier 1.65% on service purchased on or after 1 January 2013; AFC = highest 60 consecutive months — Plan 2 Handbook.
  • 48-month active-duty maximum, 180 consecutive days, no military pension at purchase — VRS Types of Service.

Employer costs

  • State Employees employer DB contribution rate, FY2027–28: 11.07% = employer normal cost 4.34% + administrative expense 0.24% + amortization of UAAL 6.49%. UAAL $6.05 billion — VRS valuation, p. 12.
  • Only 4.58 of those 11.07 points is a real saving. The amortization component is a percentage-of-payroll charge against a fixed dollar liability. Cutting payroll does not cut the liability by a cent — it cuts the dollars collected against it, and the rate must rise later. Booking 11.07% as avoided cost would record a timing cost as a saving. The scalable multiplier used here is 1 + 4.34% + 0.24% + 7.65% FICA = 1.1223.

Veteran share

  • 6% of the classified state workforce, DHRM EPR Reports as of 30 June 2024 — DHRM to Senate Finance and Appropriations, 21 January 2025, slide 8. Treat as a floor: this is the same field V1 found reads "unknown" for 69% of members.
  • Virginia context: veterans were 369,000 of a 4,528,987 civilian labour force in 2025 — 8.1% — BLS Table 6A and FRED LAUST510000000000006A.
What this model does not do — and must not be read as doing
  • Nothing here is a VRS actuarial result. Ledger 1 deliberately stops at the member payment and expresses the trust-fund cost as the ratio k. That is the G13 discipline, not a gap.
  • The cost of earlier benefit commencement is not quantified. The Bridge accelerates retirement by up to 24 months. Pricing that needs mortality, COLA, forgone accrual and forgone member contributions — VRS work, not ours.
  • The Incentive has no eligibility acceleration at all. That cohort is already eligible, so the purchase raises the benefit amount and nothing else. The G5 true-up is the only mechanism tying the Incentive to a retirement outcome, and whether it is collectable is a written question for VRS counsel.
  • Administration cost is not quantified (G14). V1's $375,000 does not carry over — it priced a queue and a certified-subsidy reservation that no longer exist.
  • Not in the workforce ledger: health insurance (per-head, so it saves only on unfilled positions); leave payout at separation, which is a one-time cost against the ledger; vacancy and training cost during transition; the risk that a senior post cannot be filled at 85% in a tight market.
  • Assumptions, labelled per G23: any veteran share above the recorded 6%; the qualifying-service share; the service-based tier proxy; Plan 2 normal Social Security age of 67; uniform distribution inside the published age and service bands; salary as a proxy for AFC; k as a ratio rather than a computed value.
  • Scope: the VRS State Employees (non-hazardous-duty) plan only. State Police (1,902), Virginia Law Officers (7,459) and Judicial (473) are excluded. Decision 4, 12 Oct 2026: excluded, and the exclusion is stated expressly in the bill — their eligibility rules differ and their approximate-normal-cost price rates run roughly 1.9× and 2.6× the non-hazardous rate.
