     how much earlier the design PERMITS retirement; nobody has measured how much earlier
     members actually retire.  There is no published distribution of chosen retirement
     dates inside a VRS window.  Analyst judgment, no anchor, and it is the single biggest
     unevidenced input in the exchange - the same item MOO-183 §5 flagged second.

[8] EXTERNAL SANITY CHECK
================================================================================================
The exchange's one externally anchorable input is the replacement-salary differential,
because that is where every dollar of agency-level saving has to come from.  The engine's
register records the central 18% as JUDGMENT between two published anchors, and the LOWER
anchor is the only one that is a measured salary gap: VRS valuation p.50, average salary at
age 35-39 ($74,012) against age 55-59 ($83,081) = 10.9% lower.  So the check is: run the
exchange at the published 10.9% instead of the judged 18% and see what survives.

Check 1 - at the published-anchored 10.9% differential, central frictions:
  net workforce effect $30,430 per participant, covering 42.4% of the authorised anchor
  against 88.5% on the judged 18%.  DISAGREEMENT, and it is material:
  the only published salary gap available cuts the coverage figure by 52%.
  The register's own note says why the published figure is a floor rather than the answer -
  it is CROSS-SECTIONAL (two age bands in one year), so it understates an incumbent-versus-
  new-hire gap, and DHRM publishes no incumbent/new-hire salary pairs by role.  That
  reasoning is sound and I am not overturning it.  What the check establishes is that the
  18% is not anchored, that the gap between the anchored floor and the judged central moves
  the headline by 52%, and that a hostile reader who insists on published figures only
  gets 42% coverage, not 88%.  Print the range, never the point.

Check 2 - the sign of the tier effect on the AGENCY's books.  At the central 18% on a
  $104,181 incumbent the annual salary differential is $18,752 and the annual LOADED
  differential is $19,682.  Had the successor carried the incumbent's own load
  rate it would be $22,672, so the tier change destroys $2,990 a year of the
  saving - the Hybrid successor pays up to 3.5% of pay in DC contributions the Plan 1
  incumbent does not, on top of the same blended employer DB rate.  So on the agency's own
  books replacing a Plan 1 incumbent with a Hybrid hire at the SAME salary costs MORE, and
  every dollar of agency-level saving has to come out of the salary gap alone.  The model
  already has that sign right, against the direction a 'turnover savings pay for it' claim
  usually assumes.  Agreement, and it is the one structural check that comes out in the
  model's favour.

Check 3 - against the engine's own default.  The published engine's central acceleration
  assumption is 6 months; this design MEASURES 44.9.  The measured figure is
  7.5x the assumption.  DISAGREEMENT, and it is
  the disagreement that matters most in this document.  The engine's 2/6/14-month ladder
  was set for a GENERAL early-retirement offset; this bill's Pathway A cohort buys an
  average 1.61 years of credit that advances unreduced eligibility by nearly four years.
  Those are not the same quantity, and anyone who reads the 6-month default as a bound on
  the measured 44.9 will conclude the workforce leg is seven times smaller than the model
  says.  The measured figure is the right one for THIS cohort; the gap is a reason to
  distrust the LEVEL of the workforce leg, not its sign, and it is why §6 reports a range
  from -18% to 213% and not a point.

Not checkable at all, and all three are data requests to VRS and DHRM:
  - every participant count here (no published count of § 51.1-142.2 purchases exists);
  - the retirement-date distribution inside a VRS window;
  - the actual salary of the actual replacement for an actual accelerated retiree.
