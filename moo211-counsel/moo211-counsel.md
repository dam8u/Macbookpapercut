## 2. Your three findings, tested

### Finding 1 — F3 does not close the subsection M channel. **AGREED. Take form M-a.**

Your reasoning holds and I verified each step mechanically on the live §1.

| Step in your reasoning | Verified |
|---|---|
| M 2's first sentence reaches *"a failure of the Board to state in a certification or notice a matter this section requires to be stated in it"* | 1× in §1. D 1 requires the advice; D 1 is in § 51.1-142.4. The omission is inside M 2's own words |
| M 2's clock contemplates another provision giving *"a longer or later period"* | 1× in §1. The clause presupposes a provision that **extends**, so D 1 supplements M 2 |
| Five other *"for the purposes of subsection M"* clauses, all declaratory | *"for the purposes of subsection M"* = **6× in §1**; *"is a determination of the Board for the purposes of subsection M"* = **5× in §1**. Your count is exact |
| M 2's second sentence operates *"whether or not another provision of this section expressly provides for review of it"* | 1× in §1 |

**The decisive point, and it is one you did not put in the record.** D 1's sentence expressly disapplies
M 2's second sentence for the member. *You cannot disapply a provision that does not apply of its own
force.* The disapplication is only intelligible on the premise that M 2 reaches the failure
independently of D 1. The drafter's own words therefore foreclose the contrary reading.

**The contrary reading, stated at its best and why it loses.** Its best form is not expressio unius; it
is that the specific governs the general (*Dodson v. Potomac Mack Sales & Serv., Inc.*, 241 Va. 89 (1991)
— *"when one statute speaks to a subject in a general way and another deals with a part of the same
subject in a more specific manner, … where they conflict, the latter prevails"*) together with the
presumption against surplusage (*Hubbard v. Henrico Ltd. P'ship*, 255 Va. 335 (1998) — *"every part of a
statute is presumed to have some effect and no part will be considered meaningless unless absolutely
necessary"*), both re-read for this pass — on
which reading the exception must do *something*, and the only thing left for it to do is to make D 1
exhaustive within its domain. It loses for a structural reason that is cleaner than any canon:

> **D 1 can add to subsection M by defining; it cannot subtract from subsection M except by amending
> subsection M.** A deeming clause (*"X is a failure to state … for the purposes of subsection M"*)
> supplies content to M 2's own words and is effective — that is precisely what M 2's own second sentence
> does for *"determination."* An exception written into a conferring sentence withdraws only what that
> sentence confers. The exception is in the wrong provision to do the job it was taken for.

So the exception is not nugatory — it withdraws D 1's clock and D 1's disapplication — and the contrary
reading is strong enough to produce litigation and not strong enough to rely on. **It should not be left
alone.**

**A second independent route the record does not name, and it inverts the provision.** By carving the
advice failure out of the whole sentence, D3c also carved it out of *"the second sentence of subdivision
M 2 does not apply to such a failure where the person adversely affected is the member."* So for the
advice failure, M 2's second sentence **applies in full**: the certification is itself *"a determination
of the Board for the purposes of this subsection, whether or not another provision of this section
expressly provides for review of it."* A member whose certification omits the advice can therefore plead
the certification as a reviewable determination, on a clock running from notice of the determination —
a channel the member whose certification omits one of the six propositions of law does **not** have,
because for her the second sentence is disapplied.

**The member whose certification omits the least legally substantive item now has the widest channel of
any member in the subdivision.** That is the opposite of F3's purpose and it is a second reason the
clause has to move to M 2.

**Which form. M-a.** M-b's generic hook (*"a matter that the provision of this section requiring it to be
stated expressly excepts from this subsection"*) buys a cross-reference by description, costs +42 in D 1
as well, and — the point against it that matters — it creates a **standing invitation**: after M-b, any
future exception written anywhere in the section silently withdraws subsection M review, with no reader
of subsection M able to see which matters are excepted without auditing the whole section. M-a keeps the
exception visible in the provision that confers the channel, where a reader of subsection M looks. Take
M-a. *(Correct its arithmetic first — see m1.)*

### Finding 2 — the F3 residue. **The residue is real. The drafted scope is right; the instruction is wrong.**

You asked whether the residue is theoretical or whether a three-way split inside one item is the lesser
evil. **Neither. There is no residue to cure, because the drafted words are correct and your statement of
the scope is under-inclusive by two.**

Your direction to item 133 was that the misstatement limb reach *"the six propositions of law and not the
advisory."* After F4 and F1/F2/F6/F9, item 7 carries **one advisory and two propositions of law** — the
power to re-designate and the no-fault clause. The certification series therefore contains **eight
propositions of law and one advisory**, not six and one. The drafted scope — *"every matter other than the
advice"* — is exactly right: it reaches all eight and excepts the one. **The asymmetry you identified is
an artefact of counting the propositions as six.** Correct the record's description of the direction;
leave the drafted words alone. **No renumbering to (i)–(vii) is needed, and the three-way split is a
solution to a problem that does not exist.**

**And item 133's own premise is overstated — which lowers the priority of F7.** Item 133 says the failure
sentence and M 2 *"are framed on absence. Neither reaches a matter that is stated and wrong."* Most
misstatements decompose into omissions. The required matter is *"that the Board advises the member to
review the member's designation of beneficiary."* Item 133's own worked example — a template rewritten to
*"Your beneficiary designation on file with VRS will apply to this purchase"* — **does not state that
matter at all.** It is an omission, it is inside D 1's failure sentence and inside M 2's limb, and item
133 uses it to prove a gap it does not fall into. The same is true of the six propositions: each is
required *with its qualifiers*, so dropping *"only to a surviving spouse, minor child, or parent"* from
the § 51.1-162 B proposition is a failure to state the required matter.

**The real F7 gap is additive misstatement only** — the Board states the required matter correctly and
adds something false beside it (*"and your designation will lapse if you do not act before completing
payment"*). That is narrower than item 133 describes, it is rarer than a template rewrite, and it is
worth knowing before you spend 253 characters on it.

## 3. Findings, ranked

### S1 — SERIOUS — review channel — F3's exception is in the wrong provision, and it inverts the subdivision

- **Provision:** subdivision D 1, failure sentence; subdivision M 2, first and second sentences.
- **Words:** *", other than a failure to state that the Board advises the member to review the member's designation of beneficiary,"* (116 chars, 1× in §1).
- **Harm:** two. (a) A survivor loses the subdivision G 4-notice clock and is left with M 2's default — 30 days from a certification given to the member. (b) By carving the advice failure out of the whole sentence, the exception also carves it out of the disapplication of M 2's second sentence, so a member complaining of the omitted advice can plead the certification as a reviewable determination while a member complaining of an omitted proposition of law cannot.
- **Failure scenario:** member files a complete application 3 Sept 2029; certification given 14 Oct 2029 and, through a template rewrite, carries no designation-review advice. The member has designated her adult son under § 51.1-162 A. She dies in service 2 Feb 2033. Because a designation was made and the designated person survived, subsection B's order of precedence never opens and the son does not qualify under B(i)–(iii); her husband receives nothing under B and the son receives accumulated contributions under A. The husband first learns of any of it from the subdivision G 4 notice given 11 Apr 2033. He files for reconsideration 2 May 2033. The Board denies for untimeliness: D 1's G 4-notice clock was withdrawn by the exception and M 2's default clock expired 13 Nov 2029 — **1,266 days before he had any document to read.**
- **Litigation surface:** plaintiff the surviving spouse; APA appeal of the Board's case decision under § 2.2-4026, notice of appeal and petition under Rule 2A:2, **venue in the circuit court of the county or city where she resides** (§ 2.2-4026 → § 2.2-4003 → § 8.01-261 subdivision 1 a, verified); pleaded as (1) M 2's clock cannot be read to bar a claimant before accrual, (2) the certification is a determination under M 2's second sentence, which D 1 does not disapply for this failure, and (3) due process under Va. Const. art. I, § 11 as a reason to prefer (1). The Commonwealth's answer is that D 1's deeming is exhaustive — the reading you asked me to test. **It is the Board that decides the reconsideration in the first instance**, so the Board picks its own construction and the survivor funds the appeal.
- **Authority:** the text of M 2's first and second sentences; the disapplication clause in D 1, which presupposes M 2's independent operation; *Dodson v. Potomac Mack Sales*, 241 Va. 89, 94 (1991) and *Hubbard v. Henrico Ltd. P'ship*, 255 Va. 335, 340 (1998) for the contrary reading at its best.
- **Cure:** **form M-a**, drafted at §7 item 135, with the count corrected. FIND (**128** chars, **1× in §1**): `or by a failure of the Board to state in a certification or notice a matter this section requires to be stated in it, may obtain`. Insert after `stated in it`: `<u>, other than a failure to state that the Board advises the member to review the member's designation of beneficiary</u>`. **+115 net of markup, +122 with the `<u>` tags** — not +116.

### S2 — SERIOUS — effective date / sunset — subsection M's failure-to-state limb is not continued past 1 July 2033. **Not on the sweep's list.**

- **Provision:** enactment clause 3, clause (v).
- **Words:** *"and subsection M of that section as it applies to such a determination or to a failure to make such a determination or to give such a notice"* (140 chars, 1× in §1).
- **Harm:** clause (v) continues subsection M past the § 51.1-142.4 expiry for three things — a determination, a failure to make a determination, and a failure to give a notice. **It does not continue subsection M for a failure to state a matter in a certification or notice.** That is the limb S1 is about, and it is the limb whose harm surfaces last, because it surfaces on the member's death. Clause (i) expressly continues members' rights and periods *"as though those provisions had not expired"*, and clause (iii) continues the subsection J reports out to a possible December 2053 — so the drafter plainly contemplated a long tail and continued the wrong limb of M across it.
- **Failure scenario:** election accepted 6 June 2033, three weeks before the sunset. The certification omits the § 51.1-162 B proposition's *"only to a surviving spouse, minor child, or parent"* qualifier — an ordinary omission, inside D 1's deeming, nothing to do with the advice. The member dies in service 19 Mar 2036. The Board gives the subdivision G 4 notice 2 May 2036, correctly, under clause (v). The survivor seeks reconsideration of the omission. **Subsection M expired on 1 July 2033 as to failures to state**, and clause (v) did not bring that limb forward. There is no reconsideration, therefore no case decision under § 2.2-4001, therefore no Article 5 judicial review. Her only route is an original equity suit against the Board.
- **Sharper variant, and the one I would expect first:** the omission is in the **subdivision G 4 notice itself** — it fails to state her right under § 51.1-162 B to elect accumulated contributions or a lump sum in lieu. Clause (v) continues M for a failure *to give* that notice but not for a failure to state a matter *in* it. After 1 July 2033 **the content of the one document a survivor ever receives is unreviewable.**
- **Authority:** the text of enactment clause 3, clauses (i), (iii) and (v); subsection M 2's first sentence, which separates the four grounds clause (v) only partly reproduces.
- **Cure:** FIND (**140** chars, **1× in §1**) `and subsection M of that section as it applies to such a determination or to a failure to make such a determination or to give such a notice`; insert after `such a notice`: `<u>, or to a failure of the Board to state in a certification or notice a matter that section requires to be stated in it</u>` — **+118**. This is an enactment-clause edit and touches no operative text.

### S3 — SERIOUS — accuracy / spillover — *"at any time before the member's death"* is wrong after retirement, and item 7 enacts a gloss on a section of general application

- **Provision:** subdivision D 1, item 7 of 7, second proposition.
- **Words:** *"that the member may make or change that designation under subsection A of that section at any time before the member's death"*.
- **Harm, part one — the proposition is wrong for most of the member's life.** § 51.1-162 is captioned *"Death before retirement"*, and A's designation is of who receives the refund *"in the event of the death of the member prior to retirement."* Subsection F requires every member whose election is accepted to retire in a qualifying retirement. So for the ordinary case — the member retires and dies years later — the A designation governs nothing, and the power to change it is a power to change a dead letter. The certification tells her the opposite, in the register of a statutory proposition, at the one moment she is attending to it. **F2 cured the false implicature that the window shuts at payment by creating a false one that it stays open until death.** The accurate terminus is retirement, not death.
- **Failure scenario:** member certified 2029, retires 2034 under a straight-life option elected under subdivision A 1 of § 51.1-165. In 2041 she divorces, and remembering the certification's words she files a new § 51.1-162 A designation naming her daughter. She dies 2046. § 51.1-162 does not apply — she did not die before retirement — and her straight-life election left no death benefit. The daughter receives nothing. The member did exactly what a certification the General Assembly compelled told her she could do, and it accomplished nothing; the thing she needed to attend to was the § 51.1-165 option, which the certification mentions only to warn that it *"may leave no benefit payable"*.
- **Harm, part two — the gloss travels.** The act amends and reenacts § 51.1-162 on its face. Enacting, in § 51.1-142.4 D 1, that a member *"may make or change that designation under subsection A of [§ 51.1-162] at any time before the member's death"* puts a legislative construction of A into the Code. § 51.1-162 A applies to the whole VRS membership, not to the pilot cohort. In an unrelated designation dispute — a non-pilot member's survivor, no military service, no pilot — counsel on either side will cite D 1 as the General Assembly's own reading of A. **You correctly identified this proposition as an inference from A's structure; enacting it converts the inference into a statutory gloss of general application, and the gloss is wrong for retired members.** Neither the non-pilot cohort nor the Board's non-pilot designation function is in the stakeholder map.
- **Authority:** § 51.1-162 caption and subsection A, third sentence (*"in the event of the death of the member prior to retirement"*); § 51.1-142.4 F; §§ 51.1-163, 51.1-165, 51.1-165.01 as the provisions that actually govern death after retirement.
- **Cure, and it does not reverse any decision you took:** keep the words and add the limitation. FIND (**108** chars, **1× in §1**) `may make or change that designation under subsection A of that section at any time before the member's death`; insert after `death`: `<u>, and that that designation determines the payment of a benefit under that section only where the member dies before retirement</u>` — **+127**. The alternative — replacing *"before the member's death"* with *"before the member retires"* — is shorter but reverses F2's terminus decision, so I do not recommend it.

### S4 — SERIOUS — compliance arbitrage — nothing requires the certification to be readable, and F5 was the provision that would have

- **Provision:** subdivision D 1, the *"shall also state"* sentence.
- **Measured on the live text:** that sentence is **3,447 characters and 576 words, one sentence, seven undesignated items**. D 1 as a whole is 8,676 characters.
- **The cheapest nominal compliance path, which is what a well-resourced regulated party will take:** reproduce D 1's own words verbatim as a block. It states every matter D 1 requires, so there is no failure to state, nothing for the subsection J report, and nothing for subsection M — and it is the **safest** path precisely because block quotation is the one method that cannot get a proposition wrong. D 1 imposes no plain-language duty, no prominence duty, no separate-document duty, no acknowledgment and no signature as to item 7. (The one acknowledgment D 1 does require is *"of the member's obligations under subsections E, F, and G"* — which does not reach item 7.)
- **Failure scenario:** VRS adopts a certification template in 2028 under enactment 5's authority, consisting of a one-page summary and an appendix headed *"Statutory disclosures required by § 51.1-142.4 D 1"* carrying D 1's sentence verbatim. Every member receives it. The designation-review advice is the final clause of a 576-word sentence on page 4. The 2032 subsection J report shows **zero** failures to state. Read-rate is not measured because nothing requires it to be. F1's harm — a designation that defeats both of subsection B's branches — occurs at the same rate as before the advisory existed.
- **Why this is the finding that decides whether the rest mattered:** the MOO-188 → MOO-191 → MOO-208 line took four findings and 590 characters inside D 1 to give the advisory an actor, an object, a terminus and a no-fault clause. Every one of those is a property of *words on a page the member may never read*. **F5 — the acknowledgment form — is the only candidate in the whole series that addressed delivery rather than wording, and it is held with an express direction that it not be drafted.** I am not reopening that; I am recording that it is now the load-bearing decision in the series, and that S4 is the cost of it.
- **Cure direction, if you want one that is not F5:** a single sentence in D 1 — the certification's statements under this subdivision shall be given in a separate writing, in plain language, and the Board shall state in the subsection J report the method by which they were given. That is one duty, one actor, one report line, and it does not require a member acknowledgment.
- **Authority:** none needed; this is enforcement reality, not law. The evidence is the measured length of the sentence and the absence of any delivery, format or prominence duty anywhere in D 1 or subsection M 1.

### S5 — SERIOUS — maintenance trap — D 1 freezes descriptions of four other Code sections, with no *"as it then reads"* device, and the same sentence shows the drafter knew the device

- **Provision:** subdivision D 1, items 1 to 6 of the *"shall also state"* sentence.
- **Words, as an example:** *"that, to the extent required by § 401(a)(37) of the Internal Revenue Code, as amended or renumbered, … subsection B of § 51.1-162 provides a retirement allowance on the death of a member in service only where no benefit is payable under subsection C of that section and only to a surviving spouse, minor child, or parent whom the member has designated under subsection A of that section…"*
- **The internal inconsistency, counted:** *"as amended or renumbered"* occurs **2× in §1** and both are the Internal Revenue Code. *"as it reads"* and *"as then in effect"* occur **0× in §1**. The six propositions describe §§ 51.1-162 (B and C), 51.1-163, 51.1-165 (A 1, A 2, A 3, C 1, C 2) and 51.1-165.01 (F) **by content**, with no equivalent device for any of them.
- **Harm:** the moment any described provision is amended, D 1 compels the Board to state the old content. A Board that states the new content commits *"a failure to state … every matter this subdivision requires to be stated in it"* — M-reviewable under D 1's deeming and reportable under subsection J. **The statute compels the Board to choose between misstating the law and committing a reportable, reviewable failure.**
- **Failure scenario, and the window is already open:** the first enactment is effective 1 July 2029 and must be reenacted by the 2028 Session, so three sessions sit between today and the first certification. The 2028 Session adds a surviving registered domestic partner to § 51.1-162 B's qualifying classes. The act is reenacted without conforming D 1 — nobody looks, because D 1 is not a provision of § 51.1-162. From 1 July 2029 every certification VRS issues must state that B reaches *"only"* a spouse, minor child or parent. A domestic partner designated under A, told by a state certification that she cannot qualify, does not pursue her claim.
- **Authority:** D 1's own use of *"as amended or renumbered"* for the IRC and nowhere else; art. IV, § 12's second sentence, which is why §§ 51.1-142.2 and 51.1-162 are reenacted at length here and why an intervening-session amendment to § 51.1-162 and this act's published text would conflict on reenactment.
- **Cure:** one sentence in D 1, immediately before the failure sentence. FIND (**106** chars, **1× in §1**) ` A failure to state in the Board's certification every matter this subdivision requires to be stated in it`; insert immediately before it: ` <u>A statement this subdivision requires of the effect of a provision of § 51.1-162, of § 51.1-163, of § 51.1-165, or of § 51.1-165.01 is a statement of that provision as it reads on the date the certification is given, and a statement that conforms to that provision as it then reads satisfies this subdivision.</u>` — **+310**. This cures M2 as well, at no extra cost.
- **Pre-existing**, from store revisions 62 and 67. Revision 70 did not create it. Revision 70 is the revision that makes it worth raising, because it takes the number of compelled statements about other sections from six to seven and puts *"death benefits; designation of beneficiary"* in the title, which is where a reader is now told to look for them.

### M4 — MODERATE — delegation / the member-duty argument — the no-fault clause says *"this subdivision"* where the act elsewhere says *"this section"*. **This is the answer to "attack it."**

You asked me to attack the position that *"advises"* imposes nothing and the no-fault clause closes the argument. **The first half is right and the second half is not.**

*"Advises"* is the Board's verb in the Board's carrier sentence; the actor, addressee, writing and moment are all the Board's; nothing in item 7 is addressed to the member. I could not construct a reading on which the enacted words themselves impose a duty, and I tried the obvious route — D 1's immediately preceding sentence requires the member to file *"a written acknowledgment of the member's obligations under subsections E, F, and G"*, which does **not** reach item 7. **Holding F5 is what keeps the advice duty-free. If F5 is ever taken, item 7 must be expressly excluded from the acknowledgment**, or the member will have acknowledged an obligation to review.
