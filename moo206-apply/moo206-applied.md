## 1. C2 — the approved text, as it is to be printed from today

### 1.1 The text

**Verified at store revision 68 (12 October 2026).** Source: Policy Director, MOO-205 §2.4,
reproduced verbatim. **This supersedes MOO-140 §4 for every artifact dated at store revision 68.**

> **A member whose purchase has not settled, and who has no disability application pending, can wait
> months with no retirement allowance at all, and the catch-up payment carries no interest.** On the
> face of subdivision G 2 the Board may pay her no retirement allowance until the cutback determination
> takes effect. The clock starts at the earlier of her final qualifying retirement date and the date she
> retires in a retirement that is not a qualifying retirement — a date the Board does not choose. From
> that date the Board has 60 days to decide and 60 days to give her written notice, and the
> determination may not take effect for a further 30 days, or 33 where the notice was mailed. **That is
> up to 120 days at zero allowance where the Board meets each of its own deadlines.** It runs longer in
> three cases. The 120-day limit yields expressly where the Board gives its notice late, and the
> determination then takes effect 30 days — 33 if mailed — after whenever the notice comes. It yields on
> the same terms where the Board does not give the notice at all, and in that case the bill sets **no
> outer bound** on how long she waits. And a reconsideration request of her own stops every one of these
> clocks for up to a further 120 days, with no allowance while it runs. Her refund follows within 60 days
> after the determination takes effect. Her first payment is made whole from her effective date of
> retirement, but it carries no interest for the months she waited.
>
> This sits two subsections below subsection F's promise that *"no amount shall be collected, offset, or
> withheld from any member, retiree, or beneficiary on that account."* Subsection F answers that
> juxtaposition in terms, and it answers it against the member: it provides that *"[n]othing required or
> permitted by subdivision G 2 … including any deferral of payment of a retirement allowance that
> subdivision G 2 requires until a determination under that subdivision takes effect … is a collection,
> offset, or withholding for the purposes of this section,"* and that *"no amount payable to the member
> or to any survivor, beneficiary, or estate of the member shall be reduced by reason of any such
> deferral or application."* So the two provisions do not read as a contradiction on the face of the
> bill. Subsection F is what makes the wait lawful, and what it preserves is her money and not her time.
> The wait itself is the exposure.
>
> The provision has been repaired repeatedly and the memo should say so. The old deadline ran from the
> final qualifying retirement date and could fall years out; the first payment is now made whole from
> the effective date of retirement; the member's allowance is no longer withheld while a disability
> application of hers is pending; the determination can no longer take effect inside her own
> reconsideration period; and the Board's own delay in acting on a reconsideration request can no longer
> extend the tolling it would otherwise earn. **The window survives the repairs**, and that is what
> belongs in front of a committee.

## 4. What I did not do, and why — four flags, none of them a guess

### 4.1 The ten documents cannot take either edit in place. `v2.0` is frozen, and `v2.1` is not commissioned. **This one is the Policy Director's.**

Rule 1 of the file-output convention freezes a version folder **once `VERSION.md` exists in it**.
`v2.0/VERSION.md` was written on 12 October 2026, the `VERSIONS.md` row is in, and `CURRENT` is a
symlink to `v2.0`. So the folder is published-and-frozen, and **editing the ten documents in place
would break the convention in the one direction it exists to prevent**: a reader who quotes `v2.0`
today and quotes it again next week would get two different documents under one version label.

`VERSIONS.md` already lists **`v2.1` as "not cut, and not yet commissioned as a folder"**, carrying
the two Fiscal items delegated as [MOO-199](/MOO/issues/MOO-199). **Both of today's edits are the
same shape as those** — they move printed text, not a recommendation — and they travel naturally in
the same pass:

| Edit held for `v2.1` | What it needs |
|---|---|
| **C2 into the risk section**, in the §1 text with its citation line and its MOO-164 caveat | The ten documents' risk register is the **R-series** (R1–R11, R-A to R-G). The C-series must-appear items — **C1, C2, C4, C5, C6, C12, C13, C14, C15, C16, C17, C18** — are **in no artifact at all** yet. Building C2 in means building §8 of the memo, which is a build, not a line edit |
| **The verbatim phrase into every class-1 site**, and the extended form into every class-2 site | The ten documents name the cohort in their own words — *"the bill's cohort"*, *"the bill's own narrower cohort"*, *"the full A + B cohort"*. The word *"cohort"* occurs **87 times** across the ten, and **I have not classified those 87**: many are not class-1 sites at all (*"a defined cohort"* of another state's members, *"the superseded cohort"*, *"cohort construction"*), and the §3.3 rule is keyed to hits of the phrase, which is what I was asked to classify. Deciding which of the 87 must carry the mandated phrase is the insertion pass itself, and it is an editorial pass across all ten, then PDF, DOCX and zip rebuilds and a 30-artifact byte comparison |

**I am not starting a `v2.1` folder on my own initiative, and I am not editing a frozen one.** Say the
word and both edits go in one pass with the MOO-199 items, on the same basis, with the usual
exact-match assertions and the recommendation-sentence re-hash.
