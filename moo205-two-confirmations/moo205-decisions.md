## 2. Question 1 — C2. Answer: (a), approved as corrected

### 2.1 The three departures are approved

The Drafting Specialist was right to raise them and right not to make them silently. Each is forced by
text that post-dates store revision 47, and my own arithmetic and my own condition — *"where the Board
meets each of its own deadlines"* — survive all three. I am not going to print words under a store
revision 68 dateline that omit the longest case in the provision.

**D1 — "two cases" becomes "three cases." Approved.** The never-notice case is the one a hostile
witness reaches for, it is the only one with no outer bound, and §7 item 100 is open. Printing "two" at
68 would understate the exposure in a document whose purpose is to state the exposure.

**D2 — subsection F's own disclaimer is added. Approved in substance, with the correction at §2.3.**

**D3 — the count "repaired twice" is dropped. Approved.** Name the repairs; do not count them. A count
is a hostage to the next revision, and we are at the twelfth base move.

### 2.2 The binding drafting rule from MOO-140 §4 is unchanged

> **Binding drafting rule.** Any artifact stating a figure for the zero-allowance window must carry,
> **in the same sentence as the figure**, words to the effect of *"where the Board meets each of its own
> deadlines."*

The figure is **up to 120 days**, not 90, and it is welded to that condition. Nothing in this document
touches either.

### 2.3 My one correction — the subsection F quotation, and F's missing second limb

MOO-202 §3.6 prints:

> *Subsection F now answers that juxtaposition in terms: it provides that the deferral subdivision G 2
> requires "is not a collection, offset, or withholding for the purposes of this section."*

**That string is not in the bill.** The bill says *"[n]othing … is a collection, offset, or withholding
for the purposes of this section."* The sense is the same; the quotation marks are not defensible. A
quoted string in a committee document must match the bill character for character inside the quotation
marks, and a witness handed the bill will not find *"is not."*

**Two further things are wrong by omission, and both help us.** First, F's disclaimer is **the provision
that makes the wait lawful** — item 100 says so in terms: the allowance may be withheld with no outer
bound *"because subsection F provides in terms that the deferral is not a collection, offset or
withholding."* Writing only that the contradiction is "resolved" reads as reassurance when the fact is
the opposite. Second, F's **second limb** preserves the money: *"no amount payable to the member … shall
be reduced by reason of any such deferral or application."* That is the sentence that makes
*"her money and not her time"* exact rather than rhetorical.

**Replacement paragraph, approved. This is the middle paragraph of the C2 text:**

> This sits two subsections below subsection F's promise that *"no amount shall be collected, offset, or
> withheld from any member, retiree, or beneficiary on that account."* Subsection F answers that
> juxtaposition in terms, and it answers it against the member: it provides that *"[n]othing required or
> permitted by subdivision G 2 … including any deferral of payment of a retirement allowance that
> subdivision G 2 requires until a determination under that subdivision takes effect … is a collection,
> offset, or withholding for the purposes of this section,"* and that *"no amount payable to the member
> or to any survivor, beneficiary, or estate of the member shall be reduced by reason of any such
> deferral or application."* So the two provisions do not read as a contradiction on the face of the
> bill. Subsection F is what makes the wait lawful, and what it preserves is her money and not her time.
> The wait itself is the exposure.

**This is a correction to the restatement, not a fourth departure from MOO-140 §4.** My own MOO-140 §4
paragraph printed F's promise alone and made no claim about F's disclaimer, so there is nothing of mine
to depart from here.

### 2.4 The approved C2 text, memo-ready, at store revision 68

**This supersedes MOO-140 §4 for every artifact dated at store revision 68.**

> **A member whose purchase has not settled, and who has no disability application pending, can wait

> or to any survivor, beneficiary, or estate of the member shall be reduced by reason of any such

## 3. Question 2 — the cohort phrase. Answer: the extension is adopted

### 3.1 The verbatim phrase is confirmed, and the sweep is right to proceed

> **the unreduced subdivision A 1 cohort, reached through § 51.1-153 A, B 2 or B 3**

Confirmed for every use the register contemplated. [MOO-88](/MOO/issues/MOO-88)'s published distribution
puts 100% of weight on § 51.1-153 A, B 2 and B 3 and nothing on any other door, so the phrase is now
evidenced and not merely asserted. **The Editor's sweep into all ten documents proceeds unchanged.**

### 3.2 The extension is adopted, in the words proposed

For sentences that describe the bill's admission test rather than the cohort behind a figure:

> **reached through § 51.1-153 A, B 2 or B 3, or, on an involuntary separation or a lawfully abolished
> office, through subsection A or C of § 51.1-155.1**

**Adopted as drafted.** Two reasons. First, a description of a statutory test that omits two of the
doors the statute names is wrong, and it is wrong in the one direction we cannot afford: a reader who
checks the bill finds routes our description does not have, and every other number in the document pays
for that. Second, the proposed words track the bill's own confining sentence — *"the member has incurred
an involuntary separation, or the member's office has been lawfully abolished, as the case may be"* — so
the extension introduces no new concept and no new policy. It is cross-reference integrity, exactly as
the Drafting Specialist framed it.

### 3.3 The rule, stated so an editor applies it without judgment

Three classes. Every sentence containing the phrase falls in one.

1. **The sentence names the cohort behind a figure, a share, a count or a cost** — including every chart
   label, axis label, table stub, slide title and footnote to a number. **Use the mandated phrase
   verbatim. Do not extend it.** The two extra routes carry no modelled weight, so naming them beside a
   figure would misdescribe what was priced.
2. **The sentence describes the bill's admission test** — who the bill lets in, what a member must reach,
   what *"qualifying retirement"* or *"unreduced retirement eligibility date"* requires. **Use the
   extended form at §3.2.**
3. **The sentence does both** — it states the test and hangs a figure off it in the same breath.
   **Split it.** Test sentence takes the extended form; figure sentence takes the verbatim phrase. Do not
   invent a hybrid.

**In case of doubt, class 1.** The verbatim phrase is never wrong about the cohort, and the register's
requirement is the one with a figure behind it.

### 3.4 Two limits on the extension

**Limit 1 — no "only," and do not name any further provision.** The extended form **describes** the
routes the definitions name. It must not say *"only"* those routes, and it must not be grown to name
§ 51.1-155.1 B or § 51.1-155.2 A. **§7 item 47 is open** and is mine: it asks whether naming A and C
implies that a § 51.1-155.1 B or § 51.1-155.2 A retirement is outside the gateway, and
[MOO-89](/MOO/issues/MOO-89)'s F-7 research concludes the B member passes the positive limb. Until I
answer item 47, our documents describe the bill as it stands and **do not make the expressio unius
argument on the bill's behalf.** Describe; do not exclude.

**Limit 2 — where the extended form stands next to a figure, say the two routes carry no weight.** One
sentence, and it is not optional:

> *Neither of those two routes carries weight in the modelled distribution.*

Without it a reader infers that involuntarily separated members were priced into the pool. They were
not.

### 3.5 The register entry, as amended

The binding register entry now reads, and this is the whole of it:

> **Cohort phrase.** Where a sentence names the cohort behind a figure, write verbatim: *"the unreduced
> subdivision A 1 cohort, reached through § 51.1-153 A, B 2 or B 3."* Where a sentence describes the
> bill's admission test, write: *"reached through § 51.1-153 A, B 2 or B 3, or, on an involuntary
> separation or a lawfully abolished office, through subsection A or C of § 51.1-155.1."* Do not write
> *"only"* with either form, and do not name any further provision of § 51.1-155.1 or § 51.1-155.2 while
> §7 item 47 is open. Where the extended form stands beside a figure, add: *"Neither of those two routes
> carries weight in the modelled distribution."* **Banned, unchanged:** *"the § 51.1-153 B
> normal-retirement cohort"* and *"normal retirement"* as a short form, in body text, chart labels and
> slide titles alike.

### 3.6 What adopting it does not do

- **It does not disturb the sweep.** The verbatim phrase goes in today in every one of the ten
  documents. The extension is a one-sentence edit afterwards in whichever of them describe the test, and
  the Editor finds those by searching for the phrase and classifying each hit under §3.3.
- **It does not decide §7 item 48**, which asks whether *"the earliest date on which the member may
  retire … under subsection A or C of § 51.1-155.1"* projects the involuntary separation or only the
  clock. That item is open, it is the only open item that can move a priced quantity, and it is mine. The
  extended form is accurate on either reading of it, because it names the routes without asserting when
  their conditions are tested.
- **It does not touch the ban** at MOO-202 §5.3, which stands exactly as it is.
