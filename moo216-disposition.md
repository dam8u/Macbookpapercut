## 1. What R1 is, in the fewest words that are still true

The pass was asked to find the case where the two deemings fix different dates, or to confirm they
cannot. **It found the case, and the case needs one real act of the Board, not two.**

My §3.2 said 164-7's deemed date *"necessarily precedes any real act of the Board upon the same
request, because the deeming's own trigger is the absence of such an act up to that date."* For
164-7's limb 2 that is true. **For limb 1 it is false**, and the reason is in the base text, which
neither patch amends:

> *"Where the Board does not act upon a request for reconsideration within the 90 days allowed by
> this subsection, the person who filed the request **may file with the Board written notice that the
> period has expired**. Where the Board does not act upon the request **within 30 days after that
> notice is filed**, the request is denied on the first day after the expiration of those 30 days…"*

That is a **windowed** no-act predicate. The window opens only when the filer files the notice, and
**an act of the Board before the window opens is invisible to it**. The filing right is given on a
condition stated as a completed fact and nothing untrues it. The drafter writes an express continuing
condition eleven lines earlier in the same subdivision — *"at any time before the Board makes the
determination"* — and did not write one here.

| | |
|---|---|
| 1 Mar | reconsideration filed |
| 30 May | the 90 days expire; the Board has not acted |
| **2 Jun** | **the Board actually acts — one real act** |
| 5 Jun | the filer files the expiry notice |
| 6 Jul | the request is deemed denied, and *"that denial constitutes the final determination of the Board upon the reconsideration and a case decision as defined in § 2.2-4001"* |

**164-7 deems the Board to act, and to last act, on 6 July. Edit 1 deems it to act, and to last act,
on 2 June. Two sentences, the same two deemed facts, 34 days apart, no priority rule in either.**
MOO-215's F4 was right on its own terms. All five base facts are now counted in my own kit (§3).

**And §3.2 was inconsistent with my own §3.4**, which withdrew the old requirement 1 precisely because
*"Edit 1's deeming is not confined to the multi-act case."* §3.4 is the one that is right. §3.1's test
— whether the Board acts more than once **and** a residual class obtains — was the wrong test.

## 2. The rulings on R1, R2, R6 and R10, which are one ruling in four places

### 2.1 What survives, and what I withdraw

**Withdrawn, in full:**

- §3.2's premise that the date 164-7 deems **necessarily** precedes any real act upon the same request.
- §3.2's sub-claim that *"a deemed act is an act for Edit 1's purposes, because 164-7's confinement
  clause makes the deeming operate for every provision measuring a period from the Board's acting, and
  Edit 1's sentence is such a provision."* **R2 is right: Edit 1's sentence is not such a provision.**
  It measures no period and fixes no date by reference to a month. It is referent-**supplying**, not
  referent-using.
- §3.2's conclusion that the two deemings *"agree by construction."*
- §3.3's statement that adding R-6's duty to Edit 1's exception **moves no date**. On R1's facts it
  moves R-6's deadline from 21 July to **17 June** — earlier, so that on 6 July the Board is 19 days
  late with a notice nobody could have known was owed. That is MOO-215 F1's vice, relocated from Edit
  2's class test into Edit 1's exception, in the revision that cured it in Edit 2. **The pass is right
  that the reversal is what imports R-6's deadline into an unresolved conflict.**

**Surviving, and untouched by R1:** §3.1's finding that the exception is **operative and not
surplusage**. R1 attacks the reason the reversal is costless; it does not attack the reason the
reversal is needed. **So the reversal of my 01:32 ruling stands — on §3.1's ground alone, and with its
price now stated instead of denied.**

**R1 and R2 are a dilemma and I accept it as one.** Either a deemed act counts for Edit 1, and R1's
two dates conflict; or it does not, and Edit 1 has no referent in the pure no-act case. On neither horn
is "they agree" established. **A rule that answers both horns is therefore not optional tidying. It is
the condition of the patch shipping.**

### 2.2 The narrowing, because the record should be exact about which clock diverges

R1(2) says G 4's pendency window gets two end dates. G 4 ends the window *"on the date on which the
Board **first** acts upon it."*

- **If 164-7's deeming is read as exclusive** — it fixes *the* dates on which the Board acts for every
  provision in its confinement class — then for G 4 the Board's only acting date is 6 July and Edit 1
  gives 2 June. **Two end dates, 34 days apart. The pass is right.**
- **If it is read as non-exclusive** — it adds a deemed act without unmaking the real one — then G 4's
  *"first acts"* is 2 June on both texts, and the divergence moves one provision over to **subsection
  M's tolling end**, which 164-1 keys to *"the Board acts upon the request"* with a 30-day floor and a
  48-day cap and which can take 6 July.

**On the first reading G 4 fights Edit 1. On the second, G 4 fights M's tolling. Either way two of this
item's own clocks restart on different days — which is the exact defect MOO-141 was opened to fix.**
I do not have to choose the reading, and I am not choosing it: the finding holds on both, and that is
why it is fatal rather than arguable. Counted in my kit: both base occurrences of *"first acts upon"*
are in G 4's single pendency sentence, once for the disability application and once for *"such a
request"* — which is the tolling-class request, so the two ends move together.

### 2.3 Requirement 4 has fired. Here is my answer — the priority, resolved expressly, in two limbs

I set requirement 4 so that this came back to me rather than being settled in a drafter's note. It has
come back, and I am not sending it onward as a question.

> **Limb 1 — the Board's own duties. Where a period within which the Board is required to do anything
> under this section runs from the Board's acting, or last acting, upon a request, it runs from the
> *latest* of the dates on which the Board acts or is deemed by any provision of this section to act
> upon that request.**
>
> **Limb 2 — the member's periods and clocks. A date on which the Board is deemed under subdivision
> M 2 to act upon a request is a date on which the Board acts upon it for the purposes of subsection
> A's deeming *only where the Board does not in fact act upon the request*.**

**Why limb 1.** A duty must never be capable of having been breached before the obligor could know it
was owed. That is MOO-215 F1's principle applied to timing instead of class membership, and it is the
same principle R4 vindicates against my own Edit 4. On R1's facts R-6's deadline is **21 July** — which
is where it sat before the reversal. **So the reversal moves no Board deadline after all: by rule,
not by construction.** The claim §3.3 made falsely is made true by enacting it.

**Why limb 2.** It answers R2's horn directly: in the pure no-act case there is no real act, so the
deemed date counts and Edit 1's *"first acts upon"* has a referent. And it stops a deemed date
competing with a real one, so G 4's window and M's tolling end on the same day — **2 June on R1's
facts, on either reading of 164-7**. That is MOO-141's object, reached rather than restated.

**What this costs, stated rather than buried.**

1. **It resolves a priority and it chooses dates.** Requirement 2 is withdrawn. The cross-reference is
   no longer a signpost; it is substantive, and it is longer than a signpost.
2. **It is a decision about whose clock the Board gets.** Limb 1 gives the Board the later date for its
   own compliance; limb 2 gives the member the earlier real act for her clocks. **Those point in
   opposite directions on purpose**, and the purpose is that neither party is bound by a date it could
   not have known. I am content to defend that in a memo and in a committee.
3. **It makes the cross-reference a blocking ask on MOO-164, not a tidy-up.** It still goes in 164-7
   and not in Edit 1 — subsection A comes first, so the later sentence refers back without a forward
   reference. Unchanged from 01:32.
4. **It disposes of §3.2's "wrinkle"** (164-7 deems a *last* act and a real act can follow it) rather
   than routing it: under limb 1 the later real act governs for duties, and under limb 2 the deemed
   last act is inert wherever a real act exists. **That routing to the Drafting Specialist is
   withdrawn and replaced by this.**

### 2.5 R10 — I am the owner, and the repair is the cheapest cure for R1

R1's engine is a defect in the base: M 2's deemed-denial sentence operates on a purely temporal
condition, so it can deem denied a reconsideration the Board has **actually decided**, including one
decided in the member's favour, and the deemed denial *is* the final determination and a case decision.

5 June.

**Three things follow, and I own all three.**

1. **It is mine, not the board's and not the Drafting Specialist's to dispose of.** The sentence is the
   enacted product of the board's option (d) at store revision 63 — the member-triggered denial. A

   gap inside it. **So no card goes to the board on my motion. If the Drafting Specialist judges that
   the repair changes option (d)'s substance, that is a finding and it goes to the board, not to me.**
2. **If the repair lands, R1's scenario disappears from the base** and §3.2's withdrawn premise becomes
   true by enactment. That is the cheapest cure available and it is why this "minor" finding is routed
   at high priority.
3. **Revision 10 must be safe on today's base regardless.** So §2.3's rule lands whether or not the
   base repair lands, and the two are not conditioned on each other.

Also to the Stakeholder Analyst: R10 gives the **state agency from whose appropriation an amount was
paid on a member's behalf** a second, independent scenario in which it is the litigant — and unlike the

## 5. R4, R5, R7, R8, R9 — the drafting and record consequences

**R4 — accepted.** Edit 4 keys its class to the **outcome** of the expiry sentence, and the expiry
sentence turns on *"the date that would otherwise apply"*, which the Board can move later by granting
the later date the request asked for. **A Board can defeat the duty retrospectively by acting late and
granting.** This is the one provision revision 9 drafted fresh and it reproduces F1's vice in the
mirror. **Direction taken:** key the trigger to the affirmative fact — the Board's not having acted
upon the request within the period, in the expiry sentence's own class (*a request the filing of which
tolled the running of the member's final qualifying retirement date under subsection M*). Drafting
Specialist. **One thing to watch and report back on:** on the affirmative trigger the notice can state
a date a later Board grant supersedes; I read R-6's chapeau as picking that up on the later act, and
**if the Drafting Specialist reads it otherwise, that is a finding back to me.**

**R5 — accepted, and I adopt the general-words direction now, unconditionally.** Extending a closed
list does not open it; on the *Forst* reasoning this record relies on throughout, a five-family
enumeration implies **more** strongly against the nine of M 2's thirteen reviewable classes that stay
outside it and the seven further express Board duties outside both lists. **The enumeration goes.** In
its place, general words: *the deeming does not determine the date by which the Board is required to do
anything under this section*. It adds no exception, it keeps both carve-backs, **it is shorter than the
enumeration**, it closes the family permanently, and the deeming keeps its real work — the member's
dates and clocks, M's tolling, and subdivision B 4 f.

- **F7 therefore closes differently than revision 9 said.** The cure is general words, not three more
  families. My 17:26 finding of 12 October stays closed; the mechanism changes.
- **This cheapens what revision 9 asks of MOO-164.** §3.3's ask — 164-7's saving taking Edit 1's three
  duty families byte for byte — is **replaced** by the same general words in both bodies. **Byte-identity
  of the two saving bodies is still the deliverable, and it now costs fewer characters than revision 9's
  version of the ask.**
- **The deliberately-failing-later check must be re-pointed, not fixed.** `verify_r72.py` holds the two
  saving bodies at **(1, 1)** today and expects **(2, 0)** once MOO-164 takes the three families. With
  the enumeration gone the target changes: **both bodies carry the general words, byte for byte.** I am

  re-pointing it now would assert a target for text that is not yet cut.
- **The Statutory Researcher's question still goes out**, because R5's severity turns on it: does M 2's
  *"for the purposes of this subsection"* limiter confine those thirteen as the pass reads it? **It no

**R7 — accepted.** *"referred to in that sentence"* modifies **the request**, not **the period**, and a
